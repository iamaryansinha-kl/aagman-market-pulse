Wipro Q2 Profit Dips 1.4% to Rs 3,201 Crore on Weak Bookings

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Net profit fell 1.4% YoY to Rs 3,201 crore and declined 4.7% sequentially.
  • •- Gross revenue rose 9.1% YoY to Rs 24,752.7 crore, but slipped 1.1% from the June quarter.
  • •- IT services revenue grew just 0.3% YoY to $2,611.5 million; margins came in at 16.5%.
  • •- Total bookings fell 22.9% QoQ in constant currency; large deals dropped 58.6% to $674 million.
  • •- Q3FY27 guidance implies sequential IT services growth of -1.5% to +0.5%.
  • •- Operating cash flow was a bright spot at Rs 5,300 crore, 166% of net profit.

Wipro's September-quarter results landed on Thursday with the quiet thud investors have grown accustomed to: net profit down 1.4 per cent year on year at Rs 3,201 crore and off 4.7 per cent sequentially, gross revenue up 9.1 per cent year on year at Rs 24,752.7 crore but down 1.1 per cent from the June quarter — a print that beat the street's modest expectations yet did nothing to dispel the questions hanging over the company's growth engine.

The core IT services business tells the tighter story. Segment revenue grew just 0.3 per cent year on year to $2,611.5 million, and 0.6 per cent in constant currency; on a sequential basis it was flat. Operating margins of 16.5 per cent contracted 0.2 percentage points from a year earlier, though they expanded 0.5 percentage points quarter on quarter — a small mercy chief financial officer Aparna Iyer highlighted, noting the company defended profitability while continuing to invest in its business and workforce.

The booking trend is what will worry the street most. Total bookings fell 22.9 per cent sequentially in constant currency to $2,595 million, and large-deal bookings — the contracts that move annual revenue run-rates — collapsed 58.6 per cent quarter on quarter to $674 million. For a company that has spent three years promising a turnaround powered by its consulting-led, AI-powered strategy, the shrinking large-deal pipeline is a credibility problem: chief executive Srini Pallia insisted strategic investments are beginning to deliver new markets, clients and expanded relationships, with artificial intelligence a long-term growth driver for technology and business transformation, but the order-book arithmetic is running ahead of the evidence.

Guidance confirmed the caution. For the December quarter, Wipro expects IT services revenue between $2,571 million and $2,625 million — implying sequential constant-currency growth of anywhere between minus 1.5 per cent and plus 0.5 per cent. Even the top of that band is unimpressive against a sector where peers are posting mid-single-digit growth, and it positions Wipro to potentially cede more ground in the league tables of Indian IT.

The demand environment does part of the explaining. Clients in Wipro's key verticals — particularly financial services and technology — have been cautious with discretionary technology spending, and the company's consulting-heavy model is more exposed to that caution than the managed-services-heavy models of its larger rivals. What the September quarter did not answer is when the AI pivot Pallia describes starts showing up in the top line: agentic-AI and transformation deals are being signed across the industry, but Wipro's flat revenue and shrinking bookings suggest its share of that spending remains limited. Management's message, unchanged across several quarters, is that the strategy requires patience; the market's patience, judging by the stock's year-to-date performance, is running thin.

Investors will watch three things from here. First, whether December-quarter bookings recover from the $674 million large-deal trough — a second weak print would harden the narrative of structural share loss. Second, whether margins can hold near 16.5 per cent while the company keeps funding AI capability-building and consulting capacity. Third, capital deployment: with operating cash flow at 166 per cent of profit, the scope for larger buybacks or special dividends exists even if growth does not return quickly. Cash, at least, is one part of this story Wipro cannot be faulted on.

There were genuine bright spots beneath the headline. Operating cash flow surged 61.3 per cent sequentially to Rs 5,300 crore — a remarkable 166 per cent of quarterly net profit — giving the company ample firepower for dividends and buybacks even in a soft demand environment. Earnings per share rose 4.2 per cent year on year to Rs 3.23, flattered partly by capital-structure effects, and voluntary attrition of 13.8 per cent on a trailing basis sits within the industry's comfortable band. The margin defence, up half a percentage point sequentially, suggests cost discipline is holding even as growth stalls.

The workforce numbers, at least, show no sign of distress: attrition at 13.8 per cent is well below the levels that characterised the great IT reshuffle of the early 2020s, and the company has been hiring selectively in consulting and AI roles even as it manages utilisation elsewhere. For employees as much as investors, the next two quarters of deal-flow data will decide whether Wipro's pivot is finally real.

The market's verdict was politely indifferent. Shares closed Thursday at Rs 174.05, up 1.82 per cent on the day as part of the broad IT rally, but the stock remains down roughly 35 per cent year to date at a market capitalisation of Rs 1.72 lakh crore — a discount that prices in years of under-delivery. The contrast with Thursday's other major IT print was stark: while Tech Mahindra announced 33 per cent profit growth, a bonus issue and twelve straight quarters of margin expansion, Wipro offered flat growth, shrinking bookings and a guidance range that straddles zero. For India's third-largest IT services company, the turnaround remains, as it has for several seasons now, a story for next quarter.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT WERE WIPRO'S Q2FY27 RESULTS?
Net profit fell 1.4% YoY to Rs 3,201 crore; gross revenue rose 9.1% YoY to Rs 24,752.7 crore, but IT services revenue grew only 0.3% YoY to $2,611.5 million.
WHAT IS THE Q3 GUIDANCE?
IT services revenue of $2,571-2,625 million, implying sequential constant-currency growth of -1.5% to +0.5%.
HOW DID BOOKINGS PERFORM?
Total bookings fell 22.9% QoQ in constant currency, and large-deal bookings dropped 58.6% to $674 million.
WAS THERE ANY POSITIVE NEWS?
Operating cash flow jumped 61.3% QoQ to Rs 5,300 crore — 166% of net profit — and margins expanded 0.5 percentage points sequentially to 16.5%. GLOSSARY: - Large-deal bookings: New contracts above a threshold size (typically $30 million or more for Indian IT firms) signed in a quarter — a leading indicator of future revenue. - Constant currency: Financial figures restated to remove the effect of exchange-rate movements. - Operating cash flow: Cash generated from core business operations; a ratio above 100% of profit indicates high earnings quality. - Sequential (QoQ) growth: Change versus the immediately preceding quarter, as opposed to the same quarter last year.
