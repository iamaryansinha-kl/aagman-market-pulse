UAE Exits OPEC: Brent Crude Dips to $104 as Oil Markets React

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/UAE announces OPEC exit effective May 1, 2026; Brent crude dips 0.36% to $104.02 while WTI falls 0.61%
  • 02/ING Think warns exit is a "big blow to OPEC"; UAE is third-largest producer at 3.4 million bpd pre-war
  • 03/Strait of Hormuz blockade remains the dominant near-term price driver; ING sees Brent at $92 in Q4 2026

The United Arab Emirates announced it will exit OPEC and OPEC+ effective May 1, 2026. The decision, confirmed by official WAM news agency, focuses on "national interests" and long-term energy strategy.

Crude oil futures edged lower on Wednesday morning. July Brent futures traded at $104.02 per barrel, down 0.36%. June WTI futures were at $99.32, down 0.61%.

On the Multi Commodity Exchange (MCX), May crude futures fell 0.72% to ₹9,417. June futures slipped 0.45% to ₹8,985 during the initial hour of trade.

"The UAE's exit from OPEC is a significant move and will be a big blow to OPEC. It's the highest-profile exit from OPEC in recent years," said Warren Patterson, Head of Commodities Strategy at ING Think, and Ewa Manthey, Commodities Strategist.

Prior to the Iran war, the UAE pumped 3.4 million barrels per day. That represented roughly 12% of total OPEC output, making it the group's third-largest producer behind Saudi Arabia and Iraq.

The UAE's production capacity stands at 4.85 million barrels per day. It aims to reach 5 million by 2027. However, its 2024 output averaged only 2.95 million bpd due to strict OPEC quota constraints.

The exit timing was carefully planned. ING Think noted that announcing during a period of significant supply disruption limits the immediate market impact. A normal-period announcement would likely have triggered sharper price drops.

In the near term, the Strait of Hormuz blockade remains the dominant price driver. With no signs of an imminent restart in oil flows, ING Think has revised its forecasts higher. It now expects ICE Brent to average $104 per barrel in Q2 2026 and $92 in Q4 2026.
