Bajaj Finance Q2 Profit Up 29% but Stock Slips on Fee Miss

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Bajaj Finance reported Q2 net profit of Rs 6,367 crore, up 29% year-on-year.
  • •- Net interest income rose 24% to Rs 13,335 crore; assets under management grew 26% to Rs 5.85 lakh crore.
  • •- The stock fell about 3% after fee income came in below expectations, causing a marginal miss on operating profit.
  • •- 360 One WAM upgraded the stock to Buy from Hold, citing a raised FY27 growth guidance of 26-27%.
  • •- Jefferies kept Buy but cut its target to Rs 1,230; Nomura held Buy at Rs 1,270.
  • •- Management flagged marginal NIM headwinds in a rising rate environment.

Bajaj Finance printed the growth and got punished for the garnish. India's largest non-banking financial company reported a 29% year-on-year jump in net profit to Rs 6,367 crore for the July-September quarter, on net interest income that rose 24% to Rs 13,335 crore and assets under management that expanded 26% to Rs 5.85 lakh crore. The franchise added 4.42 million customers in three months, taking the total to 128.85 million, and booked 13.45 million new loans, up 11% from a year earlier. The stock still fell roughly 3% on Wednesday morning to Rs 929.20, because the quarter's flaw was not in the lending engine but in the fee line: lower-than-expected fee income produced a marginal miss on pre-provision operating profit and, per Nomura, profit about 3% behind consensus.

The brokerages' response mapped neatly onto their priors. Nomura maintained Buy with a target of Rs 1,270, roughly 33% above the previous close, noting that credit costs at 1.56% came in below its estimate and that growth momentum across segments was healthy; it trimmed EPS estimates by 2-3% to account for management's indication of marginal net interest margin headwinds in a rising rate environment, and models return on assets and return on equity of 4.2% and 19.9% over FY28-29, assuming the capital raising completes within FY27 itself. Jefferies stayed Buy but cut its target to Rs 1,230 from Rs 1,280, flagging two watch items: stressed loans rose slightly, driven by gold loans — which management characterised as negligible in impact while affirming overall asset quality — and regulatory clarity on flexi-loans and banca fees is pending, with mitigating steps underway. Jefferies still pencils in 25% profit compound annual growth over FY26-29 and keeps Bajaj Finance among its top picks.

The pattern across these notes deserves emphasis: nobody downgraded, and two of three targets sit 24-37% above the market price. This is what disagreement looks like when the fundamentals are not in dispute — estimates move by single-digit percentages, ratings hold, and the argument migrates to questions of capital structure and regulation. Bajaj Finance has spent two years as the market's valuation whipping boy precisely because its operating performance kept outrunning the concerns; Wednesday's dip after a 29% profit jump suggests that pattern is not finished.

The most telling call came from the domestic side. 360 One WAM upgraded the stock to Buy from Hold, target Rs 1,150, explicitly on the back of management raising its FY27 growth guidance to 26-27% from 22-24% earlier — an upgrade, notably, delivered alongside lower estimated return on equity: factoring in the capital infusion and warrant conversion over FY27-28 produces a higher book value but an RoE of 20% versus 22% earlier. The brokerage's logic is that the recent correction had already priced the dilution, leaving the guidance upgrade as the unpriced fact. For a stock down 12% over one year and roughly 2% in 2026, the upgrade reframes the debate: the question is no longer whether growth is slowing but whether shareholders will tolerate the capital-raising bill required to fund it.

Context matters here: Bajaj Finance has been compounding at this pace for the better part of a decade, and its valuation de-rating over the past year has tracked two forces — rising competition in secured lending from banks, and the regulatory scrutiny of fee practices that this quarter's miss made tangible. Wednesday's numbers said neither force has dented the engine yet; the stock's reaction said investors need more than one quarter of confirmation. With 128.85 million customers and a loan book approaching Rs 6 lakh crore, the company is now large enough that even its guidance upgrades move consensus for the entire NBFC sector.

That trade-off is the real story of this quarter. Bajaj Finance's growth machine — 26% AUM expansion in a system growing at low teens — is intact, but funding that growth in a rising-rate world means equity issuance, and equity issuance means dilution; management's own NIM caution confirms the cost environment is tightening. The flexi-loan and banca fee regulatory overhang adds a second variable that fee-income sensitivity this quarter made concrete: when roughly a quarter of the loan book's economics flow through fees rather than interest, regulatory reclassification of those fees moves the P&L independently of credit performance. None of this argues against the franchise; three Buy ratings and targets of Rs 1,150-1,270 against a Rs 929 stock argue the market is paying for yesterday's dilution fears while the growth guidance just moved the other way. The stock's reaction on Wednesday says the fee miss will take another quarter or two of clean prints to wash out — and the raised guidance gives management the runway to deliver them.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT WERE BAJAJ FINANCE'S Q2 NUMBERS?
Net profit rose 29% year-on-year to Rs 6,367 crore, NII rose 24% to Rs 13,335 crore, and AUM grew 26% to Rs 5.85 lakh crore, with 13.45 million new loans booked.
WHY DID THE STOCK FALL DESPITE THE PROFIT GROWTH?
Fee income came in below expectations, causing a marginal miss on pre-provision operating profit; management also flagged marginal NIM headwinds from rising rates.
WHAT ARE BROKERAGES SAYING?
Nomura and Jefferies maintained Buy with targets of Rs 1,270 and Rs 1,230 respectively; 360 One WAM upgraded to Buy at Rs 1,150, citing raised FY27 growth guidance of 26-27%.
WHAT ARE THE KEY WATCH ITEMS?
Regulatory clarity on flexi-loans and banca fees, slight stress in gold loans, and the dilution impact of the planned capital raise and warrant conversion. GLOSSARY: - Net interest income: The difference between interest earned on loans and interest paid on borrowings. - Assets under management: The total value of loans and investments a lender manages. - Pre-provision operating profit: Operating profit before setting aside money for bad loans. - Warrant conversion: The exercise of rights to buy shares at a preset price, increasing share count.
