PL Capital Sets Nifty Base Case Target At 27,080; Flags Geopolitical Risks, Crude Pressures

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/PL Capital sets Nifty 12-month base case target at 27,080, valuing the index at 17.5x FY28 EPS of Rs 1,551
  • 02/The Nifty has fallen 6.6% over the past three months due to sustained FII redemptions amid geopolitical instability
  • 03/Risks include higher crude oil prices, potential El Niño impact on monsoons, and global supply chain disruptions

PL Capital has set a base case target of 27,080 for the Nifty. The valuation assumes a 17.5x P/E multiple applied to FY28 earnings per share of Rs 1,551.

The brokerage notes the Nifty has dropped 6.6% over the last three months. Foreign institutional investors have been net sellers throughout this period.

The current market trades at 17x one-year forward earnings. This represents a 12.4% discount to the 15-year historical average of 19.4x.

Geopolitical tensions in West Asia remain the primary overhang. The crisis has pushed Brent crude prices past $100 per barrel.

India imports roughly 85% of its total oil requirements. The spike could increase the annual import bill by over $70 billion.

The brokerage expects Nifty EPS growth of just 4% for FY26. However, the medium-term outlook shows a 15% CAGR over FY26-28.

"Geopolitical tensions along with the sharp increase in oil prices have made the global macroeconomic environment highly uncertain," said Amnish Aggarwal, Co-Head of Institutional Equities at PL Capital.

GDP growth is currently around 6.5% and could dip to 6%. Inflation may rise above 5% due to higher crude and supply chain costs.

El Niño risks loom with rainfall forecast at 94% of the Long Period Average. This could hurt kharif output, reservoir levels, and rural demand.

Near-term earnings remain resilient. PL Capital projects Q4FY26 revenue growth of 11.3%, EBITDA growth of 6.3%, and PBT growth of 5.7% for its coverage universe.

Sectors preferred include capital goods, defense, healthcare, banking, telecom, and metals. Caution is advised on IT services, exports, cement, chemicals, and oil & gas.
