India GDP Growth to Slow to 6.7% in FY27 on Oil Shock, Waning Momentum: BMI

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/BMI forecasts India's GDP growth to decelerate to 6.7% in FY2026-27, down from 7.7% in FY2025-26
  • 02/Crude oil surged to USD 105/barrel on May 11; Brent at USD 90 could shave 0.4-0.7 percentage points off GDP
  • 03/Vehicle registrations and electricity data show momentum already fading; El Nino monsoon adds further downside risk

India's economic growth will likely decelerate to 6.7 per cent in FY2026-27. BMI, a Fitch Group firm, released the forecast on Monday, May 11, 2026.

The 6.7 per cent projection marks a sharp drop from 7.7 per cent in FY2025-26. BMI revised the current fiscal year's estimate upward by 0.1 percentage points to 7.7 per cent.

The slowdown stems from waning momentum and an oil price shock triggered by the Iran conflict. Crude prices jumped to USD 105 per barrel on Monday after the US rejected Iran's peace proposal.

Brent Crude at USD 90 per barrel could reduce India's GDP growth by 0.4 to 0.7 percentage points. BMI said India's economy is among Asia's most sensitive to energy price swings.

Vehicle registrations grew only 9 per cent year-on-year in April. That is down sharply from 23 per cent growth recorded in the January-March quarter.

Electricity generation rose 2.7 per cent year-on-year last quarter. But March consumption climbed a mere 0.9 per cent, confirming the loss of economic momentum.

BMI also warned that restricted energy and food supply will slow consumption growth. India's weather department predicts below-normal monsoon rainfall due to El Nino this year.

The International Monetary Fund estimates a typical El Nino shock cuts India's GDP by 0.1 per cent. BMI believes this could further offset momentum carried over from FY2025-26.
