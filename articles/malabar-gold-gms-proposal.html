Malabar Gold & Diamonds Seeks GMS Reforms to Reduce Import Dependency

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Malabar Gold & Diamonds submits proposal to government recommending strategic enhancements to the Gold Monetisation Scheme
  • 02/India imports 700-800 tonnes of gold annually while households hold 25,000-35,000 tonnes of idle domestic gold
  • 03/Company recommends reducing minimum deposit from 10 gm to 1 gm and integrating organised jewellers into GMS framework

Malabar Gold & Diamonds has submitted a proposal to the Government seeking reforms to the Gold Monetisation Scheme. Chairman M.P. Ahammad presented the recommendations to Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman and Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal.

The proposal aims to mobilise idle domestic gold into the formal economy. It focuses on recycling, exchange, re-use, and monetisation of existing gold holdings.

India imports nearly 700-800 tonnes of gold annually. This creates significant foreign exchange outflows and pressure on the current account deficit.

Indian households and institutions hold an estimated 25,000-35,000 tonnes of gold. Much of this remains economically idle in the form of jewellery, coins, and bars.

"With appropriate policy support and active integration of the organised jewellery sector, the Gold Monetisation Scheme can emerge as a highly effective mechanism for mobilising idle gold into the formal economy." - M.P. Ahammad, Chairman, Malabar Group.

The proposal recommends reducing the minimum deposit quantity from 10 gm to 1 gm. It also seeks flexible redemption options in gold weight or cash.

Other suggestions include lower lock-in periods, improved liquidity options, and a jeweller-assisted collection framework. This would operate under bank and regulatory supervision with digital tracking.

Mobilising even 1-2 per cent of India's domestic gold holdings could release 600-700 tonnes. This equals a substantial portion of annual import demand.
