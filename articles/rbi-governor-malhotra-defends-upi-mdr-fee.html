RBI Governor Malhotra Defends UPI MDR: 0.4% Fee Won't Dent Volumes

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra said the 0.4% merchant discount rate on UPI payments above Rs 2,000 is a "small fee" that will not have a "major impact" on transaction volumes
  • 02/The Supreme Court declined to grant an interim stay on the fee and issued notices to the government, the RBI and NPCI; the legal challenge remains pending
  • 03/Separately, banks raised nearly USD 133 billion in diaspora deposits through the FCNR(B) scheme under a concessional swap facility, underscoring macro fundamentals

Reserve Bank of India (RBI) Governor Sanjay Malhotra on October 7 defended the newly introduced merchant discount rate on UPI payments. He described the 0.4% levy as a "small fee" that will not materially slow transaction volumes.

The governor spoke to reporters at RBI headquarters in Mumbai. His comments came hours after the central bank's policy review, where the repo rate was hiked by 25 basis points to 5.50% and rate cuts were taken off the table.

Last month, the government permitted banks and payment providers to charge MDR on UPI transactions. Payments above Rs 2,000 now attract a 0.4% fee, ending the long-standing assumption that UPI would remain free for all users.

The fee carries real weight for merchants. On a Rs 10,000 payment, the charge works out to Rs 40. Transactions at or below the Rs 2,000 threshold continue to carry no charge.

"As of now, we do not see any drop in volumes. And I don't personally think that a small fee will have a major impact on the volumes," Malhotra said.

The MDR restores an income stream for the payments ecosystem. Banks and prepaid payment instrument issuers have absorbed UPI's running costs for years, and part of the fee supports National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI), which operates the network.

The levy, however, is contested. The Supreme Court on October 7 refused to grant an interim stay on the 0.4% MDR and issued notice to the government, the RBI, NPCI and other respondents. The challenge will now be heard on merits.

The governor also used the press interaction to underline the health of the banking system. He said banks' credit growth will remain strong and aid economic activity.

The RBI is watching non-bank lenders closely. Malhotra said the central bank is conscious of the potential for an adverse asset-quality impact at non-banks amid the current liquidity flush, though he does not expect such an eventuality. System liquidity, he added, will not stay in such a high surplus for long.

On the external front, the FCNR(B) scheme delivered a striking number. Banks raised nearly USD 133 billion in diaspora deposits under the scheme, which carried a concessional swap facility from the RBI.

Malhotra said the figure illustrates the strength of India's macro fundamentals. He expressed confidence that banks will deploy the funds prudently.

The governor acknowledged that external headwinds caused a dip in flows. He described current account pressure as temporary and said he is confident the balance of payments will return to surplus soon.

Malhotra declined to answer questions on the RBI's reported rejection of Tata Sons' request to surrender its NBFC licence. The matter is tied to a conflict between Tata Sons' owners and management over upper-layer NBFC listing norms.

WHAT MDR HAS THE GOVERNMENT ALLOWED ON UPI TRANSACTIONS?
FROM LAST MONTH, UPI TRANSACTIONS ABOVE RS 2,000 ATTRACT A MERCHANT DISCOUNT RATE OF 0.4%. PAYMENTS AT OR BELOW RS 2,000 REMAIN FREE OF ANY CHARGE.
WHAT DID RBI GOVERNOR SANJAY MALHOTRA SAY ABOUT THE FEE'S IMPACT ON UPI VOLUMES?
SPEAKING ON OCTOBER 7, MALHOTRA SAID, "AS OF NOW, WE DO NOT SEE ANY DROP IN VOLUMES. AND I DON'T PERSONALLY THINK THAT A SMALL FEE WILL HAVE A MAJOR IMPACT ON THE VOLUMES."
WHAT HAS THE SUPREME COURT DECIDED ON THE CHALLENGE TO THE UPI MDR?
THE SUPREME COURT REFUSED TO GRANT AN INTERIM STAY ON THE 0.4% MDR AND ISSUED NOTICE TO THE GOVERNMENT, THE RBI, NPCI AND OTHER RESPONDENTS. THE CHALLENGE REMAINS PENDING AND WILL BE HEARD ON MERITS.
WHAT DID THE RBI DECIDE ON INTEREST RATES AT THE OCTOBER 7 POLICY?
THE RBI HIKED THE REPO RATE BY 25 BASIS POINTS TO 5.50%. RATE CUTS ARE OFF THE TABLE FOR NOW, THE GOVERNOR SIGNALLED HOURS AFTER THE ANNOUNCEMENT.
HOW MUCH DID BANKS RAISE UNDER THE FCNR(B) SCHEME?
BANKS RAISED NEARLY USD 133 BILLION IN DIASPORA DEPOSITS UNDER THE FCNR(B) SCHEME, WHICH CARRIED A CONCESSIONAL SWAP FACILITY FROM THE RBI. MALHOTRA CALLED THE FIGURE PROOF OF INDIA'S STRONG MACRO FUNDAMENTALS. GLOSSARY: Merchant Discount Rate (MDR): The fee a merchant pays to accept a digital payment, shared among the bank, the payment app, and network operators. Zero-MDR on UPI meant these players earned nothing on UPI transactions. UPI (Unified Payments Interface): India's instant real-time payment system run by NPCI, processing billions of transactions monthly across banks and payment apps. FCNR(B) Deposits: Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank) deposits — dollar-denominated deposits Indians abroad hold with Indian banks. The RBI's concessional swap window let banks raise nearly $133 billion cheaply. Balance of Payments (BoP): The ledger of all money flowing in and out of a country — trade, investment, transfers. A surplus means more money entering than leaving. Current Account: The trade-and-transfers slice of the BoP — imports, exports, remittances. Malhotra called the current pressure on it "temporary." Upper-Layer NBFC: Non-banking finance companies above an RBI size threshold, required to list on stock exchanges — the norm at the centre of the Tata Sons dispute the governor declined to discuss.
