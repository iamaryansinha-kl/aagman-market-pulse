MRPL, ONGC and OPaL Form Joint Venture to Integrate Petrochemicals Marketing Operations

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

Mangalore Refinery and Petrochemicals Ltd (MRPL) informed stock exchanges on Monday, April 27, 2026, that the Board of Directors of its parent company, Oil and Natural Gas Corporation Ltd (ONGC), has approved the formation of a joint venture company for integrated petrochemicals marketing. The shareholding structure will be split 50:25:25 among ONGC, MRPL and ONGC Petro additions Ltd (OPaL) respectively. MRPL will contribute ₹12.5 crore towards the equity share capital of the new entity.

The proposed JV is subject to clearance from the Department of Investment and Public Asset Management (DIPAM), which operates under the Union Finance Ministry. ONGC currently holds 88.58 percent of MRPL, making this an intra-group consolidation rather than a third-party partnership. The structuring as a separate legal vehicle reflects a deliberate effort to ring-fence the marketing operations while maintaining promoter control.

The strategic rationale centres on production economics and pricing power. Pooling the marketing operations of three group entities into a single platform is expected to reduce logistics costs, allow grade optimisation across product types, and create a consolidated sales channel. The JV will also produce speciality grades and explore third-party sales in petrochemical categories where India currently relies on imports.

OPaL operates a world-class petrochemical complex at Dahej in Gujarat, commissioned in March 2017 by Prime Minister Narendra Modi. The complex houses the largest standalone dual feed cracker in South-East Asia, with capacity to produce 1,100 KTPA of ethylene and 400 KTPA of propylene. Its polymer plants include 2×360 KTPA LLDPE/HDPE swing units, one 340 KTPA dedicated HDPE unit and one 340 KTPA PP unit, giving it total polymer capacity of 1.5 MMTPA and chemical capacity of 0.5 MMTPA.

In March 2025, OPaL received final approval to exit the Dahej Special Economic Zone and operate as a Domestic Tariff Area (DTA) unit, a move designed to enhance its competitiveness in the domestic market. ONGC has already infused ₹18,365 crore into OPaL to lift its stake to 95.69 percent from 49.36 percent previously, as part of a broader financial restructuring to improve the subsidiary’s capital structure and debt-equity ratio.

MRPL’s share price fell 7.02 percent to ₹173.3 on April 27, 2026, from the previous close of ₹186.38, giving the company a market capitalisation of approximately ₹30,390 crore. The stock trades at a P/E of 17.05 within a 52-week range of ₹120 to ₹212.31. The decline was driven primarily by the company’s FY26 results showing a steep profit contraction and the board’s decision to skip a final dividend, having already distributed an interim dividend of ₹4 per share in March 2026.

ONGC shares were trading at around ₹285.5 on the same day, with a market capitalisation of roughly ₹3.59 lakh crore. The parent company also approved an additional ₹265 crore investment for a northeast gas grid project and appointed a new Chief Financial Officer in the same board meeting. Brent crude was trading near $108 per barrel following the closure of the Hormuz Strait, a development that adds pressure to refining margins across the sector.

The equity outlay of ₹12.5 crore is immaterial to MRPL’s balance sheet, but the operational implications could be meaningful if the JV achieves the stated goals of cost reduction and revenue enhancement. Whether the structural logic converts into realised margin improvement will depend on execution speed, governance independence from parent entities, and how quickly the new company can establish commercial traction in a market where OPaL’s polymer market share already dropped to 11 percent in FY24 from 12 percent in FY23.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT IS THE SHAREHOLDING STRUCTURE OF THE NEW JOINT VENTURE?
THE JV WILL BE HELD 50 PERCENT BY ONGC, 25 PERCENT BY MRPL, AND 25 PERCENT BY OPaL. THIS MEANS ONGC RETAINS CONTROLLING INTEREST WHILE BOTH SUBSIDIARIES PARTICIPATE EQUALLY IN THE REMAINING HALF.
HOW MUCH WILL MRPL INVEST IN THE JOINT VENTURE?
MRPL WILL CONTRIBUTE ₹12.5 CRORE TOWARDS THE EQUITY SHARE CAPITAL OF THE NEW COMPANY. THIS IS A RELATIVELY SMALL OUTLAY COMPARED TO MRPL’S OVERALL BALANCE SHEET.
WHAT REGULATORY APPROVAL IS REQUIRED BEFORE THE JV CAN PROCEED?
THE DEAL IS SUBJECT TO CLEARANCE FROM THE DEPARTMENT OF INVESTMENT AND PUBLIC ASSET MANAGEMENT (DIPAM), WHICH OPERATES UNDER THE UNION FINANCE MINISTRY. THIS IS STANDARD FOR PUBLIC SECTOR UNDERTAKINGS.
WHAT ARE THE MAIN BUSINESS OBJECTIVES OF THE JV?
THE JV WILL INTEGRATE PETROCHEMICALS MARKETING ACROSS THE GROUP COMPANIES. IT AIMS TO REDUCE COSTS, INCREASE REVENUE THROUGH IMPROVED PRICING MECHANISMS, OPTIMISE LOGISTICS, ENABLE GRADE OPTIMISATION, PRODUCE SPECIALITY GRADES, AND PURSUE THIRD-PARTY SALES IN IMPORT-DEPENDENT SEGMENTS.
WHY DID MRPL’S SHARE PRICE FALL ON APRIL 27, 2026?
MRPL SHARES DECLINED 7.02 PERCENT TO ₹173.3, PRIMARILY DUE TO FY26 RESULTS SHOWING A STEEP PROFIT CONTRACTION AND THE BOARD’S DECISION TO SKIP A FINAL DIVIDEND. THE JV ANNOUNCEMENT ITSELF WAS NOT THE MAIN DRIVER OF THE PRICE DROP.
