118-Year-Old Calcutta Stock Exchange Plans Comeback with New Trading Platform

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

One of India's oldest stock exchanges is trying to rise from the ashes.

The 118-year-old Calcutta Stock Exchange (CSE), which has been dormant for years after losing almost all trading volumes to the National Stock Exchange and Bombay Stock Exchange, is planning a comeback with a new trading platform. The revival effort represents a bold attempt to restore the historic exchange's relevance in India's financial markets.

The CSE has a storied history. Founded in 1908, it was once among the largest stock exchanges in India, serving as the primary trading venue for companies in eastern India. The exchange thrived for decades, with trading floors bustling with brokers and investors. But the advent of electronic trading and the rise of NSE in the 1990s gradually siphoned volumes away, leaving the CSE a shadow of its former self.

The exchange has been technically operational but largely inactive. While the CSE maintained its licence and some regulatory compliance, trading volumes dwindled to negligible levels as brokers and investors migrated to the NSE and BSE platforms. The exchange's physical trading floor, once a symbol of Kolkata's financial prominence, fell silent.

The comeback plan centres on technology. The CSE intends to launch a new trading platform that could offer specialised services, regional focus, or innovative products that differentiate it from the NSE and BSE. The details are still emerging, but the strategy appears to be about carving out a niche rather than competing head-on with the dominant exchanges.

Regional focus may be the differentiator. Eastern India, including West Bengal, Odisha, Jharkhand, and the northeastern states, has a large population but relatively low penetration of capital market participation compared to western and southern India. A revived CSE with a regional focus could tap into this underserved market, offering local language support, regional investor education, and products tailored to eastern India's industrial base.

The regulatory hurdle is significant. SEBI has strict criteria for operating stock exchanges, including capital requirements, technology standards, risk management systems, and investor protection measures. The CSE will need to demonstrate that it can meet these standards and offer a compelling value proposition that justifies its existence alongside the NSE and BSE.
