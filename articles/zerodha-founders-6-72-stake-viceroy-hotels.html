Zerodha Founders Buy 6.72% Stake in Viceroy Hotels

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Nithin Kamath and Nikhil Kamath acquired a 6.72% stake in Viceroy Hotels during the September quarter through Kamath Associates and Nksquared
  • 02/The hotel company exited insolvency in 2023 after creditors were owed over Rs 1,150 crore, and resumed trading on NSE and BSE in April 2024
  • 03/Viceroy is raising around Rs 105 crore via a rights issue at Rs 115 per share and approved a Rs 206-crore acquisition in December 2025

Zerodha founders Nithin Kamath and Nikhil Kamath have picked up a 6.72% stake in Viceroy Hotels. Shareholding disclosures show the acquisition was made during the September quarter.

The investment was routed through two entities: Kamath Associates and Nksquared. Both entities disclosed the stake under their respective names.

Viceroy Hotels shares rose 2.18% to Rs 128.80 on Tuesday following the disclosure. The stock reaction was modest but positive.

This is an insider-style buying signal in a company that has only recently come out of insolvency. High-profile investors taking near-7% positions in post-CIRP smallcaps remain rare.

Viceroy Hotels entered the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process after accumulating heavy debt under its previous promoter. Creditors were owed more than Rs 1,150 crore at the time.

Liquidation was a real possibility during the process. Trading in the company's shares was suspended while the insolvency proceedings were underway.

The turnaround began in October 2023. The National Company Law Appellate Tribunal approved a resolution plan submitted by Anirudh Agro Farms.

Fresh capital came in through Viceroy's subsidiary Loko Hospitality. The funds supported the implementation of the resolution plan.

Trading resumed on both NSE and BSE in April 2024 after the resolution plan was fully implemented. The stock has been live for roughly two and a half years since then.

Viceroy currently operates Marriott and Courtyard by Marriott properties in Hyderabad. Both are under the Marriott umbrella, giving the smallcap a branded hospitality profile.

The company is now in the middle of a rights issue. It aims to raise around Rs 105 crore at a price of Rs 115 per share.

The rights issue serves a dual purpose. Alongside raising capital, it is intended to increase public shareholding to meet regulatory requirements after promoter ownership rose following the resolution.

In December 2025, shareholders approved the Rs 206-crore acquisition of SLN Terminus Hotels and Resorts. That asset is a Marriott-branded executive apartments property.

The acquisition is expected to add another asset to Viceroy's portfolio. It extends the company's Marriott-heavy strategy beyond its existing Hyderabad properties.

For investors, the Kamath stake purchase is a data point, not a thesis. Disclosures show the position was built in the September quarter. They do not disclose the intent behind it.

The near-term markers are clear: completion of the rights issue, delivery on the SLN Terminus acquisition, and continued compliance with public shareholding norms.

GLOSSARY: CIRP (Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process): A formal bankruptcy process under Indian law where a company unable to pay debts is restructured or liquidated under National Company Law Tribunal oversight. NCLAT (National Company Law Appellate Tribunal): The appellate authority that hears appeals against orders of the National Company Law Tribunal, including approvals of insolvency resolution plans. Resolution plan: A formal proposal submitted by a bidder to take over an insolvent company, typically involving debt settlement, fresh capital infusion, and a change in ownership or management. Rights issue: A method of raising capital where existing shareholders are offered new shares at a set price, usually at a discount to the market price, in proportion to their holdings. Public shareholding norms: Regulations that require listed companies to maintain a minimum level of public (non-promoter) shareholding, typically 25%, to ensure adequate public float.
