India Fiscal Deficit Apr-Aug: Rs 7.10 Lakh Crore, 41.9% Of FY27 Target

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/India's April-August FY27 fiscal deficit reached Rs 7.10 lakh crore, or 41.9% of the Rs 16.9 lakh crore full-year target, per CGA data
  • 02/The gap is running ahead of last year's pace of 38.1% of target at the same stage, though major tax revenues typically land in the second half
  • 03/Net dated borrowings of Rs 11.7 lakh crore are planned for FY27, with capex and subsidy lines key to the FRBM glide path

India's fiscal deficit stood at Rs 7.10 lakh crore for the first five months of FY2026-27, covering April to August. That equals 41.9% of the full-year budget estimate, according to data released by the Controller General of Accounts (CGA) on September 30, 2026.

The deficit figure measures the gap between the government's total expenditure and its revenue receipts plus non-debt capital receipts. In simple terms, it is the amount the Centre must borrow to fund its spending.

For the full year, the budgeted fiscal deficit is approximately Rs 16.9 lakh crore, or 4.3% of GDP. That compares with 4.4% of GDP recorded in FY26, marking a continued step along the consolidation path.

The current run-rate is slightly ahead of last year's pace. In April-August FY26, the deficit stood at Rs 5.98 lakh crore, which was 38.1% of that year's full-year target at the same stage.

That puts the FY27 ratio about 3.8 percentage points ahead of the comparable year-ago print. The gap between spending and revenue has opened a little faster than it did a year earlier.

On the financing side, the government plans net market borrowings of Rs 11.7 lakh crore through dated securities in FY27. Gross market borrowings, which include repayments of maturing debt, are budgeted at Rs 17.2 lakh crore.

A borrowing programme of that size keeps the deficit's pace under close watch in bond markets. If the gap runs persistently ahead of schedule, it can pressure gilt yields even when the full-year arithmetic remains achievable.

Analysts caution against reading too much into the early-year ratio alone. Major revenue streams — income tax, corporate tax instalments and dividends from state-run enterprises — are concentrated in the second half of the fiscal year.

Because revenue is back-loaded, the deficit-to-target ratio typically looks elevated in April-August and narrows as the year progresses. Markets therefore track the pace against the comparable year-ago ratio rather than a straight-line benchmark.

On expenditure, budgeted capital expenditure for FY27 stands at Rs 12.22 lakh crore, or roughly 3.1% of GDP. That is up from Rs 10.96 lakh crore in the revised estimates for FY26, signalling continued priority on infrastructure spending.

Subsidies remain a risk line. Fertiliser and petroleum subsidies together are budgeted at around Rs 1.83 lakh crore for FY27, and elevated global commodity prices could push the eventual outgo higher than budgeted.

The government has reiterated its commitment to the fiscal consolidation glide path under the FRBM Act. The true test will come in the October-March window, when the bulk of tax revenue arrives and the final deficit trajectory becomes clearer.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT WAS INDIA'S FISCAL DEFICIT IN APRIL-AUGUST OF FY2026-27?
INDIA'S FISCAL DEFICIT FOR APRIL TO AUGUST OF FY2026-27 STOOD AT RS 7.10 LAKH CRORE. THE FIGURE, PUBLISHED BY THE CONTROLLER GENERAL OF ACCOUNTS ON SEPTEMBER 30, 2026, EQUALS 41.9% OF THE FULL-YEAR BUDGET ESTIMATE. THE DEFICIT REPRESENTS THE GAP BETWEEN TOTAL GOVERNMENT EXPENDITURE AND REVENUE RECEIPTS PLUS NON-DEBT CAPITAL RECEIPTS — THE AMOUNT THE GOVERNMENT MUST BORROW TO BRIDGE.
HOW DOES THE FY27 DEFICIT PACE COMPARE WITH LAST YEAR?
IN APRIL-AUGUST FY26, THE FISCAL DEFICIT STOOD AT RS 5.98 LAKH CRORE, OR 38.1% OF THAT YEAR'S FULL-YEAR TARGET AT THE SAME STAGE. THE CURRENT YEAR'S 41.9% RATIO IS THUS RUNNING ROUGHLY 3.8 PERCENTAGE POINTS AHEAD OF LAST YEAR'S PACE, MEANING THE GAP HAS OPENED SLIGHTLY FASTER THAN IT DID A YEAR AGO.
WHY ARE EARLY-YEAR FISCAL DEFICIT RATIOS USUALLY HIGH?
MAJOR REVENUE STREAMS — INCOME TAX, CORPORATE TAX INSTALMENTS AND DIVIDENDS — ARRIVE LARGELY IN THE SECOND HALF OF THE FISCAL YEAR, WHILE GOVERNMENT SPENDING IS SPREAD MORE EVENLY ACROSS THE YEAR. THIS BACK-LOADING MEANS THE DEFICIT-TO-TARGET RATIO TYPICALLY LOOKS ELEVATED IN APRIL-AUGUST AND EASES AS THE YEAR PROGRESSES. MARKETS THEREFORE COMPARE THE CURRENT RATIO WITH THE YEAR-AGO RATIO FOR A TRUER READ.
HOW DOES THE GOVERNMENT PLAN TO FINANCE THE FY27 FISCAL DEFICIT?
TO FINANCE THE FY2026-27 DEFICIT, NET MARKET BORROWINGS FROM DATED SECURITIES ARE ESTIMATED AT RS 11.7 LAKH CRORE, WHILE GROSS MARKET BORROWINGS ARE BUDGETED AT RS 17.2 LAKH CRORE, ACCORDING TO BUDGET DOCUMENTS. THE FULL-YEAR DEFICIT TARGET IS APPROXIMATELY RS 16.9 LAKH CRORE, OR 4.3% OF GDP, DOWN FROM 4.4% IN FY26.
WHAT ARE THE KEY RISKS TO THE FY27 FISCAL MATH?
THE MAIN WATCH ITEMS INCLUDE THE OCTOBER-MARCH REVENUE SEASON, EXECUTION OF THE RS 12.22 LAKH CRORE CAPEX PLAN, AND SUBSIDY OUTGO AGAINST THE BUDGETED RS 1.83 LAKH CRORE FOR FERTILISER AND PETROLEUM. ELEVATED COMMODITY PRICES COULD WIDEN THE SUBSIDY BILL. THE GOVERNMENT REMAINS COMMITTED TO THE FRBM ACT'S FISCAL CONSOLIDATION GLIDE PATH.
