SIP Inflows Dip To ₹31,115 Crore In April On Market Volatility

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/SIP inflows moderated to ₹31,115 crore in April from ₹32,087 crore in March as equity markets turned volatile
  • 02/Equity mutual fund inflows declined 5% to ₹38,440 crore; SIP accounts dipped to 9.65 crore from 9.72 crore
  • 03/AMFI and fund houses say moderation is not a trend; sustained ₹31,000-crore flows signal resilient retail participation

SIP inflows into mutual funds eased to ₹31,115 crore in April. This compares with ₹32,087 crore in the previous month.

The number of contributing SIP accounts dipped to 9.65 crore from 9.72 crore in March. Data from Association of Mutual Funds in India showed the marginal pullback.

However, SIP assets under management rose to ₹16.85 lakh crore from ₹15.11 lakh crore. The gain came despite sharp market swings and continued FPI outflows.

Discontinued SIP accounts stood at 51.21 lakh in April. This was slightly above the 50.71 lakh new SIP accounts opened during the month.

Venkat Chalasani, Chief Executive at AMFI, said the higher stoppages were largely due to Sebi norms. An SIP is classified discontinued if three consecutive instalments are missed.

He added that March inflows were elevated because they included the last four days of February, which were market holidays. AMFI said it is too early to view the moderation as a trend.

Suranjana Borthakur, Head of Distribution at Mirae Asset Investment Managers, said sustained SIP inflows around ₹31,000 crore indicate continued retail participation.

Equity mutual fund inflows declined 5% in April to ₹38,440 crore from ₹40,450 crore. Retail investors stayed committed despite volatility.

A Balasubramanian, Managing Director at Aditya Birla Sun Life AMC, said investor participation reflects growing maturity. Retail investors are increasingly preferring diversified allocation strategies.

Navneet Munot, MD & CEO at HDFC Asset Management Company, said equity flows have held up well. Long-term financial goals are guiding decisions over short-term market noise.

Gold ETFs attracted ₹3,040 crore in April compared with ₹2,265 crore in March. Outflows from silver ETFs moderated to ₹125 crore from ₹684 crore.
