Piyush Goyal Assures Exporters of Sustained Support to Enter New Markets

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal met exporters on Monday and assured sustained government support including facilitation under ongoing schemes and targeted interventions
  • 02/FY26 goods exports from India registered barely 1% YoY growth to $441.78 billion, hit by West Asia crisis, sea route blockades, and rising shipping costs
  • 03/Sectors represented included gems & jewellery, apparel, leather, engineering goods, textiles, pharmaceuticals, and IT; watch for EPM progress and policy reforms

Commerce and Industry Minister Piyush Goyal has assured Indian exporters of sustained government support. The Minister met industry representatives on Monday alongside senior officials from the Commerce Department.

Exporters raised concerns on high compliance costs and stringent testing requirements. MSMEs face particular challenges entering export markets due to these barriers.

Indian goods exports in FY26 registered barely 1% year-on-year growth. Total export value reached $441.78 billion, reflecting prolonged headwinds.

The West Asia crisis has disrupted sea routes and increased shipping costs. Insurance premiums have risen, intensifying uncertainty for exporters.

"The Minister reaffirmed the government's commitment to strengthening a facilitative trade ecosystem through ongoing reforms, targeted support measures and close collaboration with industry, to accelerate export growth and position India as a trusted global supply partner," per the Commerce Department statement.

Discussions highlighted progress under the Export Promotion Mission. EPM is the government's flagship scheme to support exporters and expand active exporter participation.

"The Minister encouraged EPCs to take steps to increase the number of active exporters," the statement noted. Export Promotion Councils are expected to drive outreach.

Sectors represented included gems & jewellery, apparel, leather, engineering goods, cotton textiles, man-made and technical textiles, carpets, handicrafts, agriculture and processed food, pharmaceuticals, and IT and software.
