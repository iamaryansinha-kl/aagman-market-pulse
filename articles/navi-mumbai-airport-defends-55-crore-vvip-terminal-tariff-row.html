Navi Mumbai Airport Defends ₹55 Crore VVIP Terminal in Tariff Row

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/NMIA counters IATA's objection to a ₹55 crore VVIP terminal being included in airport tariff determination framework
  • 02/IATA argues ordinary passengers and airlines should not fund sovereign protocol infrastructure under user-pay principles
  • 03/NMIA cites its concession agreement and regulatory precedent, saying VIP lounges are a standard feature across airports globally

Navi Mumbai International Airport (NMIA) has defended the inclusion of a ₹55 crore VVIP terminal in its tariff proposal. The airport operator filed its counter reply with the Airport Economic Regulatory Authority (AERA) on April 29, 2026.

The submission comes in response to objections raised by the International Air Transport Association (IATA). IATA had challenged the practice of recovering VVIP terminal costs through passenger tariffs.

IATA contends that the VVIP facility will not generate any direct revenue. Requiring airlines and ordinary passengers to fund sovereign protocol infrastructure violates the user-pay and cost-relatedness principles, the association said in its AERA filing.

In its reply, NMIA stated that VIP lounges are a common feature across airports worldwide. Excluding them from tariff determination would disregard both contractual obligations and established regulatory precedent, the airport operator argued.

NMIA pointed to its concession agreement which explicitly provides for VIP lounge facilities as part of the airport's master plan. The provision is not an extraneous or discretionary addition, the counter submission noted.

"The form, scale, and configuration of such facilities are to be determined based on operational, security, and regulatory considerations in consultation with the concerned authorities. The nomenclature used—whether 'VIP lounge' or 'VVIP / protocol facility'—does not alter the functional requirement or the underlying obligation to provide appropriate infrastructure for protocol movement," NMIA said in its reply.

The dispute centres on whether protocol infrastructure costs should be socialised across all passengers. AERA's ruling will set a precedent for how similar facilities are treated at other Indian airports under development.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT IS THE DISPUTE BETWEEN NMIA AND IATA ABOUT?
IATA HAS OBJECTED TO THE INCLUSION OF A ₹55 CRORE VVIP TERMINAL IN NAVI MUMBAI INTERNATIONAL AIRPORT'S TARIFF PROPOSAL. IATA ARGUES THAT COSTS FOR SOVEREIGN PROTOCOL INFRASTRUCTURE SHOULD NOT BE RECOVERED FROM ORDINARY PASSENGERS AND AIRLINES.
WHAT IS NMIA'S DEFENCE FOR INCLUDING THE VVIP TERMINAL IN TARIFFS?
NMIA STATES THAT VIP LOUNGES ARE A COMMON FEATURE ACROSS AIRPORTS GLOBALLY. THE AIRPORT OPERATOR ARGUES THAT EXCLUDING THEM FROM TARIFF DETERMINATION DISREGARDS CONTRACTUAL OBLIGATIONS UNDER ITS CONCESSION AGREEMENT AND ESTABLISHED REGULATORY PRECEDENT.
WHAT ARE IATA'S KEY OBJECTIONS?
IATA CONTENDS THAT THE VVIP TERMINAL WILL NOT GENERATE ANY REVENUE. THE ASSOCIATION SAYS REQUIRING AIRLINES AND PASSENGERS TO FUND SOVEREIGN PROTOCOL INFRASTRUCTURE IS FUNDAMENTALLY INCONSISTENT WITH THE PRINCIPLES OF USER-PAY AND COST-RELATEDNESS.
WHAT DOES NMIA SAY ABOUT THE NOMENCLATURE OF THE FACILITY?
NMIA ARGUES THAT WHETHER THE FACILITY IS CALLED A "VIP LOUNGE" OR A "VVIP / PROTOCOL FACILITY" DOES NOT ALTER THE FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENT. THE UNDERLYING OBLIGATION TO PROVIDE APPROPRIATE INFRASTRUCTURE FOR PROTOCOL MOVEMENT REMAINS THE SAME.
WHAT IS AT STAKE IN THIS AERA PROCEEDING?
AERA'S RULING WILL SET A REGULATORY PRECEDENT FOR HOW PROTOCOL INFRASTRUCTURE COSTS ARE TREATED AT OTHER INDIAN AIRPORTS UNDER DEVELOPMENT. THE DECISION AFFECTS WHETHER SUCH COSTS ARE SOCIALISED ACROSS ALL PASSENGERS OR TREATED SEPARATELY.
