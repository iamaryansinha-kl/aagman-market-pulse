China, India Drive Gold ETF Inflows as 68% of Global Investors Exit

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/China and India have driven positive net inflows in gold ETFs at $9.04 billion and $3.26 billion respectively, WGC data shows.
  • 02/Despite this, 68% of investors have exited physically-backed gold ETFs globally as of April 17, 2026.
  • 03/Gold prices have dropped 15% from their January 29 peak of $5,608 an ounce and currently trade around $4,712.

China and India have kept net investments in physically-backed gold exchange-traded funds positive this year. Data from the World Gold Council shows China leads with $9.04 billion in inflows. India follows at $3.26 billion.

Japan ranks as the sixth-largest net investor, contributing $1.26 billion. For the week ending April 17, global inflows into gold ETFs totalled $63.96 billion.

Outflows during the same period were $43.73 billion. This leaves net investments at $20.23 billion globally. Asia accounted for $14.87 billion in net inflows.

North America and Europe recorded net inflows of $2.06 billion and $2.94 billion respectively. However, in the week to April 17, Asia saw negative flows of $1.5 million.

North America attracted $2.12 billion and Europe $0.96 billion that week. The US led with $2.17 billion, followed by the UK at $0.61 billion.

Gold prices have fallen over 15 per cent from the record high of $5,608 an ounce on January 29. The metal currently trades at $4,712 an ounce in the spot market.

COMEX June futures were quoted at $4,729.51 an ounce. Gold has gained nine per cent year-to-date despite recent volatility.

In March, investors exited gold ETFs fearing Iran war could spur inflation and a stronger dollar. Funds also switched from gold to crude oil during that period.
