Sensex, Nifty Negative Open on May 11 on Austerity Call, Iran Tensions

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/GIFT Nifty at 24,090 signals gap-down opening of ~200 points for Nifty on May 11, 2026
  • 02/PM Modi's austerity call to cut gold, fuel consumption sparks fears of price hikes and economic impact
  • 03/Trump rejects Iran's peace response as "totally unacceptable"; Strait of Hormuz supply risks resurface

Domestic equity markets are set to open on a negative note on Monday, May 11, 2026. Prime Minister Narendra Modi's austerity call has sparked fresh concerns among investors.

The Prime Minister urged citizens to avoid purchasing gold for a year. He also asked people to reduce consumption of petrol, diesel and cooking gas.

Market participants fear the Centre may impose austerity measures. There are concerns about potential hikes in petrol prices that could cascade through the economy.

GIFT Nifty was trading at 24,090 in early morning trade. This indicates a gap-down opening of approximately 200 points from Friday's Nifty close of 24,176.15.

The Sensex had closed at 77,328.19 on Friday, down 516.33 points. The Nifty had shed 150.50 points to end at 24,176.15.

Ponmudi R, CEO of Enrich Money, said fresh geopolitical concerns emerged over the weekend. Donald Trump reportedly dismissed Iran's response to the latest US peace proposal as "totally unacceptable".

The development has brought the Strait of Hormuz back into focus. Broader risks of supply disruption in global energy markets are once again weighing on sentiment.

SBI Securities expects FY27 real GDP growth at 6.6 per cent. The brokerage nowcasts Q4FY26 real GDP growth at closer to 7.2 per cent.

FY26 full-year GDP growth is likely to come in at 7.5 per cent. High-frequency data indicates resilient economic activity despite global headwinds.

Motilal Oswal Financial Research said large-caps are attractive versus Small and Midcap indices. The Nifty-50 is trading at 19.1x forward earnings, roughly 9% below its long-period average.

SMID indices continue to trade at a premium. This suggests a more stretched risk-reward profile in the small and midcap segments.

"If crude remains above $100, it will squeeze corporate margins," Motilal Oswal cautioned. India's market performance remains highly linked to earnings trends and global institutional flows.

Global stocks were mixed in early Monday trade. Korea's Kospi jumped over 4.5 per cent, bucking the broader negative trend.

Crude oil prices remain a key near-term headwind. Crude was quoted at 9,020.00, down 45.00 points, but geopolitical risks threaten to push prices higher.

Gold traded at 152,525.00, up 264.00 points. Silver was at 261,800.00, up 3,260.00 points, as safe-haven demand saw some buying interest.
