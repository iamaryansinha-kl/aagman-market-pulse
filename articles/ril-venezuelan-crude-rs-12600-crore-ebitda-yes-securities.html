Venezuelan Crude To Add Rs 12,600 Crore To RIL EBITDA: YES Securities

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Cheap Venezuelan crude could add Rs 8,400-12,600 crore to Reliance's annual O2C EBITDA through a $2-3 per barrel blended GRM lift, YES Securities estimates.
  • 02/Jamnagar's delayed cokers, hydrocrackers and gasification complex let it monetise heavy Merey-16 grades that other Indian refiners cannot process profitably.
  • 03/YES retains Buy with a Rs 1,660 target price, implying 41.9% upside, but the benefit hinges on licensing and Venezuelan crude holding 25-35% of the slate.

Cheap Venezuelan crude could add as much as Rs 12,600 crore to Reliance Industries Ltd's annual EBITDA, YES Securities estimates. The return of heavy Venezuelan barrels is the key swing factor for the Jamnagar refining complex.

The brokerage estimates that restored Venezuelan volumes plus full optimisation of Jamnagar could lift Reliance's blended gross refining margin (GRM) by $2-3 per barrel. On roughly 70 million tonnes of annual crude throughput, that works out to Rs 8,400-12,600 crore of additional annual O2C EBITDA — about Rs 4,200 crore for every $1 per barrel of GRM expansion.

Jamnagar alone in India can monetise Venezuelan Merey-16. The refinery was designed for extra-heavy grades with API gravity of around 16 degrees and high sulphur, acidity and metals content — barrels that trade at a structural discount to Brent.

Its delayed coking units, high-pressure hydrocrackers and petcoke gasification complex convert those heavy barrels into higher-value middle distillates while limiting low-value fuel oil output. Captive logistics and gasification also let the complex substitute syngas for imported merchant LNG.

Reliance Industries (NSE: RELIANCE) historically processed 300,000-400,000 barrels per day of Merey-16 — worth about $1.30 per barrel in heavy feedstock discount plus another $1.30 per barrel in superior yield configuration. Volumes collapsed to negligible levels in 2021-2023 after US sanctions on PDVSA forced substitution with Russian Urals.

Flows are now returning. YES estimates Indian imports of Venezuelan crude have rebounded to about 250,000-465,000 barrels per day, with Reliance's 2026 intake seen at 250,000-420,000 barrels per day, sourced primarily through licensed international trading houses.

The margin prize is meaningful. Reliance's GRM premium over average Indian PSU refiners ranged from about $3.08 per barrel in FY18 to as much as $7.10 per barrel in FY15, then narrowed after sanctions, discounted Russian crude and fuel-tax changes — inverting against some coastal PSU refiners in FY23. YES models a sustainable premium of around $3 per barrel, below the earlier peak but a clear improvement.

The caveats matter: flows remain subject to regulatory licensing and supply sustainability, the benefit depends on the landed discount to Brent, and the refinery must run at full complex optimisation with Venezuelan crude at 25-35% of the crude slate. YES Securities retains Buy with a target price of Rs 1,660, implying 41.9% upside from Rs 1,170. RIL shares were down around 8% over the past month.

The sensitivity is what makes the estimate striking. At roughly 70 million tonnes of annual crude throughput, every single dollar of blended gross refining margin is worth about Rs 4,200 crore of annualised EBITDA to Reliance. A $2-3 per barrel improvement is therefore not a rounding error — it is a step change in the profitability of the company's largest division.

The reason Jamnagar can chase this when rivals cannot comes down to metallurgy and configuration. Merey-16, with an API gravity of about 16 degrees and heavy loads of sulphur, acidity and metals, is cheap precisely because most refineries cannot run it economically — the crude imposes yield penalties and corrosion costs on less complex plants. Jamnagar's delayed cokers crack the heavy residue into lighter, higher-value distillates; its hydrocrackers upgrade the middle distillates further; and the petcoke gasification complex converts the dregs into syngas, displacing imported merchant LNG and tightening the loop on costs. Integrated captive logistics complete the advantage, letting Reliance land the crude cheaply and move products efficiently.

The recovery has been gradual rather than sudden. Indian imports of Venezuelan crude have rebounded to an estimated 250,000-465,000 barrels per day, and Reliance's own intake this year has climbed to roughly 250,000-420,000 barrels per day — cargoes arriving primarily through licensed international trading houses rather than direct dealings. That structure matters: it keeps the flows dependent on the continuation of authorisations, which is the core risk to the whole thesis.

History also counsels humility on the size of the prize. During the earlier Venezuelan cycle, Reliance's GRM premium over the average Indian PSU refiner ran from $3.08 a barrel in FY18 to as much as $7.10 in FY15 — a gap that narrowed and briefly inverted in FY23 once sanctions and cheap Russian crude levelled the field. YES Securities models a sustainable premium of around $3 a barrel this time, well below the peak, which is a reminder that the new arbitrage is real but not unlimited.

GLOSSARY: Gross refining margin (GRM): The difference between the value of a refinery's output and the cost of its crude feedstock, per barrel. O2C: Reliance's oil-to-chemicals business, which spans refining and petrochemical operations. API gravity: A measure of crude oil density; lower numbers mean heavier, harder-to-refine oil. Delayed coker: A refinery unit that converts heavy residual oil into lighter, more valuable products. Assets under custody (AUC): Securities held by foreign portfolio investors with local custodians, used to track their holdings.
