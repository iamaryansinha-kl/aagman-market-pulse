Kotak Mahindra Bank Shares Fall 5% Despite Q4 Profit Beat, Top Nifty 50 Loser

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Kotak Mahindra Bank shares fell as much as 5% on May 4, 2026, making it the top loser on the Nifty 50 despite reporting a 13% YoY profit rise
  • 02/Q4 FY26 standalone net profit came in at ₹4,027 crore, beating Bloomberg consensus of ₹3,663 crore; NII rose 8% to ₹7,876 crore
  • 03/Brokerages offered mixed outlooks — Morgan Stanley and BofA remain bullish with targets up to ₹500, while Ambit Capital maintains a sell with TP ₹380

Kotak Mahindra Bank Ltd (NSE: KOTAKBANK) shares crashed as much as 5 per cent on May 4, 2026, emerging as the worst performer on the Nifty 50. The stock hit an intra-day low of ₹363 before recovering to trade at ₹375.85, still down about 2 per cent.

The selloff came despite the private lender reporting a 13 per cent year-on-year rise in standalone net profit to ₹4,027 crore for Q4 FY26. The figure beat Bloomberg consensus estimates of ₹3,663 crore.

Net interest income grew 8 per cent YoY to ₹7,876 crore in the March quarter. The bank's net interest margin stood at 4.67 per cent, up from 4.54 per cent in Q3FY26 but below 4.97 per cent in Q4FY25.

Asset quality showed improvement. Gross non-performing assets eased to 1.20 per cent from 1.30 per cent in the previous quarter. Net NPAs dropped to 0.25 per cent from 0.31 per cent quarter-on-quarter.

Provisions fell sharply by 36 per cent QoQ to ₹516 crore, directly supporting the bottom-line beat. The bank's return on assets expanded to 2.14 per cent for the quarter.

The board recommended a dividend of ₹0.65 per equity share. Net advances grew 16 per cent YoY to ₹4.96 lakh crore, while total deposits rose 15 per cent to ₹5.72 lakh crore. The CASA ratio stood at 43.3 per cent.

Brokerages largely acknowledged the earnings beat but offered a mixed outlook. Morgan Stanley retained an overweight rating with a target price of ₹500, calling it a key pick amid macro risks.

BofA maintained a buy with a target of ₹460, noting that asset quality and margins improved and return on assets expanded to 2.1 per cent. Macquarie stayed neutral at ₹460, while Elara Capital maintained a buy but cut its target to ₹473.

Ambit Capital remained cautious, maintaining a sell with a target of ₹380. It flagged concerns over the bank's ability to balance faster growth with strong underwriting.
