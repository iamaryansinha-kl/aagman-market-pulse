Microsoft Stock Plunges 17% in June 2026: Worst Month Since 2000, $570 Billion Wiped Out

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Microsoft shares fell 17% in June 2026, erasing $570 billion in market value — the worst monthly decline since December 2000
  • 02/Investors fear $190 billion AI capital expenditure plan will compress margins while returns take longer to materialise
  • 03/Stock rebounded nearly 6% on Friday after Michael Burry disclosed long-dated call options; trades at 19x forward earnings

Microsoft Corp (NASDAQ: MSFT) has fallen 17% in June 2026. The decline wiped out over $570 billion in market value, according to Bloomberg.

This marks the stock's worst monthly performance since December 2000. The selloff pushed shares to their lowest closing level since 2023.

The drop centres on two concerns: aggressive AI spending and delayed returns. Investors worry that heavy capital expenditures may not pay off quickly enough.

In April, Microsoft guided for $190 billion in capital expenditure for calendar 2026. This exceeds Wall Street expectations of around $154 billion and represents a 60% jump from the prior year.

Q3 FY2026 revenue came in at $82.89 billion, beating estimates of $81.39 billion. Earnings per share were $4.27, ahead of the $4.06 consensus.

Azure revenue grew 39% year-on-year, but the guidance still disappointed investors. Microsoft projected Q4 revenue of $86.7-87.8 billion, below the $87.53 billion consensus.

Operating margins are expected to fall to 44% in Q4, below consensus of 44.6%. Gross margins hit 67.6%, the lowest since 2022.

"Investors are selling first and asking questions later," said Jack Ablin, Chief Investment Strategist at Cresset Wealth Advisors. He noted Microsoft faces pressure from both AI spending and potential disruption to its core software business.

Analyst Brad Reback at Stifel lowered his price target to $400 from $415 on June 25. He cited accelerating capex compressing Azure gross margins and called earnings expectations "too optimistic."

Despite the selloff, Microsoft now trades at 19x forward earnings. This is below the S&P 500's 20x multiple and well under its own 10-year average.

The beaten-down valuation attracted contrarian buyers. Michael Burry, known for betting against the US housing market before 2008, disclosed purchases of long-dated Microsoft call options. The news lifted the stock nearly 6% on Friday, its strongest single-day gain since May 2025.

Consensus estimates still project 17% revenue growth in the current fiscal year. The combined AI-related spending by Google, Amazon, Meta, and Microsoft is expected to reach $700 billion this year.
