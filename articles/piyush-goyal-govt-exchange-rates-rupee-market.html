Piyush Goyal: Govt Doesn't Interfere in Exchange Rates, Rupee Market-Driven

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal stated the government does not interfere in exchange rates, calling them purely market-driven
  • 02/The rupee closed at 95.60 against the US dollar on May 22, rising for a second consecutive session
  • 03/The rupee has become one of the worst-performing emerging market currencies this year due to oil prices and capital outflows

Commerce and Industry Minister Piyush Goyal on Saturday said the government does not interfere in exchange rates. He said these are determined by market forces and influenced by several global factors.

"In fact, if I read correctly, in the last two or three days, again the rupee has appreciated pretty significantly. So, these are market-driven factors," Goyal told reporters in New Delhi.

The rupee rose for the second consecutive session on Friday to close sharply higher at 95.60 against the US dollar. The gain came on softening of crude oil prices and supposed intervention by the Reserve Bank of India.

The rupee's sharp decline has emerged as one of the biggest economic warning signs for policymakers and investors. Once considered among Asia's more stable currencies, it is now one of the worst-performing emerging market currencies this year.

Expensive oil, capital outflows, widening trade deficits and a surging US dollar have pressured the rupee. This toxic mix of factors has eroded confidence in the currency.

Goyal said the government is conscious of promoting exports and reducing import dependence. Steps such as finalisation of nine free trade agreements in recent years will support India's industrialisation.

Import substitution of critical goods, promoting ease of doing business, and decriminalisation of minor offences are other measures. These steps will certainly support India's economic growth trajectory.
