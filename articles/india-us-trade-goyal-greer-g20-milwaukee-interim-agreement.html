India-US Trade: Goyal, Greer Push Interim Pact At G20 Milwaukee

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal held a bilateral with USTR Jamieson Greer on October 1 on the sidelines of the G20 Trade Ministers' meeting in Milwaukee, USA.
  • 02/Goyal called it a "productive discussion" toward the early conclusion of a mutually beneficial interim agreement under the India-US BTA framework.
  • 03/No deal was signed and no date was announced; the BTA is described as roughly 90% complete, with a threatened 100% tariff on Russian oil purchases still in play.

Commerce and Industry Minister Piyush Goyal met US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer in Milwaukee on October 1, 2026. The bilateral took place on the sidelines of the two-day G20 Trade Ministers' meeting.

The United States holds the 2026 G20 presidency. The trade ministers' gathering opened on October 1, IST.

"Productive discussion ... towards early conclusion of a mutually beneficial interim agreement," Goyal said of the meeting. His remarks, carried by ET, framed the talks as a step forward under the India-US Bilateral Trade Agreement framework.

The interim agreement is the first phase of the broader BTA. The two governments have been negotiating the pact for over a year and a half.

Greer used his opening remarks at the meeting to press for a rethink of global trade governance. In a lighter moment, he joked that Goyal is the "trade minister for life".

The remark set the tone for the ministerial sessions. It also signalled how Washington intends to use its G20 presidency year on trade issues.

Goyal is on a US visit from September 29 to October 5, 2026. According to an official PIB statement, he is also engaging American industry, investors and the startup community.

His itinerary includes Milwaukee, Chicago and New York. The outreach runs parallel to the trade negotiations and is aimed at deepening investment and commercial ties.

For New Delhi, the visit doubles as a commerce roadshow. Goyal's meetings with American industry, investors and the startup community run alongside the official G20 programme.

The Milwaukee meeting builds on the February 7, 2026 India-US joint statement. That framework cut the reciprocal tariff on Indian goods from 50% to 18%.

An additional 10% US tariff took effect from July 24, 2026 for several countries, including India. The layered duties have kept sustained pressure on Indian exporters.

US officials have described the BTA as roughly 90% complete. The remaining work centres on the legal detailing and the tariff framework.

New Delhi's core demand is that its final tariff rate be preferential versus competitor countries. In plain terms, India wants a better rate than rival exporting nations receive in the US market.

India also faces a threatened 100% tariff over its purchases of Russian oil unless a deal is reached. That threat has added urgency to the interim agreement talks.

The two sides last held ministerial-level talks in New Delhi in June. The Milwaukee bilateral marks their first in-person engagement since that round.

For now, both capitals are signalling momentum without committing to a signing date. The next markers to watch are the legal text of the interim pact and whether Washington can deliver a preferential rate structure.

Exporters on both sides will track whether the interim text locks in the 18% rate and shields Indian goods from further tariff action. Until a deal is in force, the threatened 100% tariff on Russian oil-linked trade remains a live risk.
