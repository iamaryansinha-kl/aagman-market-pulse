India's Core Sector Output Growth Slows to 4.8% in August as Coal, Gas, Crude Oil Contract

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

India's core sector output growth slowed to 4.8% year-on-year in August 2026 from 5.0% in July, according to provisional data released by the government on Monday.

The eight core industries had recorded 5.4% growth in July based on the provisional estimate. The final July index was revised down to 5.0% from 5.4%, the government said.

Which sectors grew? Five of the eight core industries recorded positive year-on-year growth in August. Cement output rose 12.5%, electricity 11.6%, iron ore 5.5%, steel 3.4% and refinery products 2.6%.

Iron ore, electricity and cement have been the major drivers of overall growth in the Index of Core Industries (ICI) in recent months.

Which sectors contracted? Coal, natural gas, crude oil and fertilisers recorded negative growth during the month.

The contraction in energy commodities — coal, natural gas and crude oil — reflects ongoing supply-side challenges and global energy market dynamics. The fertiliser contraction points to softer agricultural demand or production constraints.

Cumulative performance The cumulative growth of the core sector during April-August 2026 stood at 4.3% on a provisional basis. This compares with 2.4% in the corresponding period of the previous year — a significant improvement.

The final index for July was revised to 120.8 from the provisional estimate of 121.2.

What are the core industries? The eight core industries — coal, crude oil, natural gas, refinery products, fertilisers, steel, cement and electricity — account for 40.27% of the weight of items included in the Index of Industrial Production (IIP).

The ICI is released monthly by the Ministry of Commerce and Industry. It serves as a leading indicator for industrial output, given the large weight of core industries in the IIP.

What it means The slight moderation from 5.0% to 4.8% is marginal and does not signal a significant slowdown. The cumulative growth of 4.3% for April-August, nearly double the 2.4% from a year ago, suggests underlying momentum in the industrial economy.

Cement and electricity continuing to post double-digit growth indicates sustained construction activity and power demand. The contraction in crude oil and natural gas remains a concern for import dependence.
