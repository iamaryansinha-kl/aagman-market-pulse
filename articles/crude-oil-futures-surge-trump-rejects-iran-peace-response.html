Crude Oil Futures Surge 4% as Trump Rejects Iran Peace Response

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/July Brent crude surged 4.10% to $105.44 and WTI jumped 4.52% to $99.73 after Trump rejected Iran's response
  • 02/MCX May crude oil futures traded at ₹9,473, up 4.98% from previous close of ₹9,024 in early Monday trade
  • 03/ING analysts warn oil remains highly sensitive to Iran headlines; China crude imports fell 20% YoY in April

Crude oil futures jumped over 4 per cent in early trade on Monday. The surge followed US President Donald Trump rejecting Iran's response to his peace proposal.

At 9:20 am, July Brent oil futures traded at $105.44, up 4.10 per cent. June WTI crude futures were at $99.73, up 4.52 per cent.

On MCX, May crude oil futures traded at ₹9,473 against the previous close of ₹9,024. The contract surged 4.98 per cent in the initial hour.

June MCX crude futures were at ₹9,149 versus the previous close of ₹8,734. The contract gained 4.75 per cent from Friday's settlement.

In a Truth Social post, Trump said: "I have just read the response from Iran's so-called 'Representatives.' I don't like it — TOTALLY UNACCEPTABLE!"

Iran's response reportedly sought sanctions lifting, an end to the US naval blockade in the Strait of Hormuz, and continued nuclear activity.

Warren Patterson and Ewa Manthey of ING Think said the oil market remains heavily headline-driven. They noted prices remain highly sensitive to Iran noise despite potential fatigue from constant developments.

China's crude oil imports fell 20 per cent year-on-year to 9.4 million barrels per day in April. This marks the lowest level since July 2022.
