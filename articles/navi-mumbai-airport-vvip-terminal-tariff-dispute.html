Navi Mumbai Airport Defends ₹55 Crore VVIP Terminal in AERA Tariff Dispute

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Navi Mumbai International Airport rejects IATA's objection to including a ₹55 crore VVIP terminal in its tariff proposal to AERA.
  • 02/IATA argues ordinary passengers and airlines should not fund sovereign protocol infrastructure, violating user-pay principles.
  • 03/NMIA counters that VIP lounges are standard airport features globally and the concession agreement mandates their inclusion.

Navi Mumbai International Airport Private Limited (NMIAL) has defended its decision to include a ₹55 crore VVIP terminal in its tariff proposal. The airport operator submitted its counter reply to the Airports Economic Regulatory Authority of India (AERA) on April 29, 2026.

The International Air Transport Association (IATA) had objected to the inclusion. IATA contends that costs associated with the VVIP terminal should not be recovered from ordinary passengers.

IATA stated that the facility will not generate any direct revenue. Requiring airlines and passengers to fund sovereign protocol infrastructure would be fundamentally inconsistent with the principles of user-pay and cost-relatedness, the association said in its submissions to AERA.

In its response, NMIA said VIP lounges are a common feature across airports worldwide. The airport operator argued that any suggestion to exclude them from tariff determination disregards contractual obligations and regulatory precedent.

NMIA pointed out that the concession agreement provides for a VIP lounge as part of the airport's facilities. The master plan includes protocol infrastructure as a standard requirement, not an extraneous addition.

"The form, scale, and configuration of such facilities are to be determined based on operational, security, and regulatory considerations in consultation with the concerned authorities. The nomenclature used—whether VIP lounge or VVIP protocol facility—does not alter the functional requirement or the underlying obligation to provide appropriate infrastructure for protocol movement," NMIA said in its counter reply.

The dispute arises during NMIAL's regular tariff determination process for its first control period. AERA had earlier approved ad-hoc tariffs for NMIA, effective from its commercial operations date of December 25, 2025, until March 31, 2026. The airport is currently operating at a Phase 1 capacity of 20 million passengers per annum.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT IS THE DISPUTE BETWEEN NMIAL AND IATA?
IATA HAS OBJECTED TO NMIAL'S INCLUSION OF A ₹55 CRORE VVIP TERMINAL IN ITS TARIFF PROPOSAL TO AERA. IATA ARGUES THAT ORDINARY PASSENGERS AND AIRLINES SHOULD NOT BEAR THE COSTS OF SOVEREIGN PROTOCOL INFRASTRUCTURE.
WHAT IS NMIAL'S DEFENCE FOR INCLUDING THE VVIP TERMINAL?
NMIAL ARGUES THAT VIP LOUNGES ARE A COMMON FEATURE ACROSS AIRPORTS GLOBALLY. THE AIRPORT OPERATOR STATES THAT THE CONCESSION AGREEMENT PROVIDES FOR VIP LOUNGES AS PART OF THE AIRPORT'S FACILITIES, AND EXCLUDING THEM WOULD DISREGARD CONTRACTUAL OBLIGATIONS.
WHAT DOES IATA SAY ABOUT THE USER-PAY PRINCIPLE?
IATA STATED THAT REQUIRING AIRLINES AND PASSENGERS TO FUND SOVEREIGN PROTOCOL INFRASTRUCTURE WOULD BE FUNDAMENTALLY INCONSISTENT WITH THE PRINCIPLES OF USER-PAY AND COST-RELATEDNESS.
WHAT IS THE STATUS OF NMIA'S COMMERCIAL OPERATIONS?
NMIA COMMENCED COMMERCIAL OPERATIONS ON DECEMBER 25, 2025. AERA APPROVED AD-HOC TARIFFS FOR THE AIRPORT UNTIL MARCH 31, 2026, PENDING FINAL DETERMINATION OF REGULAR TARIFFS FOR THE FIRST CONTROL PERIOD.
HOW MUCH IS THE TOTAL INVESTMENT PLANNED FOR NMIA?
NMIAL HAS PROPOSED A TOTAL CAPITAL EXPENDITURE OF APPROXIMATELY ₹57,333 CRORE ACROSS FIVE PHASES. PHASE 1 AND 2 ALONE INVOLVE ₹22,531 CRORE, WITH A DESIGNED CAPACITY TO EVENTUALLY HANDLE 90 MILLION PASSENGERS PER ANNUM.
