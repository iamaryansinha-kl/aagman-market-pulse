Oil Price June 25: Brent Crude Falls Below $73 as Hormuz Resumes

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Brent crude fell below $73/barrel on June 25, first time since February 27, down 42% from April peak of $126
  • 02/Hormuz traffic nearly restored to pre-war levels with 20 million barrels passing in 24 hours per US Energy Secretary
  • 03/Full normalization could take weeks due to demining; depleted global inventories will need time to rebuild

Brent crude has fallen below $73 per barrel for the first time since February 27, 2026. The benchmark August futures settled at $72.40, down $1.40 or 2% on Thursday.

U.S. West Texas Intermediate crude also declined, settling at $69 per barrel. WTI fell $1.20 or 1.6% during the session.

Oil prices have now erased all gains from the Iran war that started on February 28, 2026. Brent hit a peak of $126 per barrel on April 30 and has since dropped 42%.

The slide accelerated after an initial agreement last week to end the U.S.-Israeli war with Iran. Tankers stranded near the Strait of Hormuz have resumed transit through the critical waterway.

U.S. Energy Secretary Chris Wright said at least 20 million barrels passed through Hormuz in the previous 24 hours. He noted oil flows have almost returned to pre-war levels.

Wright cautioned that full normal operations could take a few weeks. Demining work in the area is ongoing, and Oman introduced temporary routes on Wednesday to help coordinate vessel traffic.

Global oil inventories were depleted during the extended disruption and will take time to rebuild. Some shipowners remain wary of operating conditions in the strait and the wider Persian Gulf.

"Disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz could delay a return to stability in global oil markets until 2027" - Amin Nasser, CEO, Saudi Aramco. Prolonged interruptions could affect nearly 100 million barrels of supply each week.
