RBI Withdraws $5.2 Billion in First Net Open Market Debt Sale in 9 Years

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- 01/ The RBI withdrew $5.2 billion through a net open market sale of government securities.
  • •- 02/ It is the central bank's first net OMO debt sale in nine years.
  • •- 03/ The sale is part of a roughly Rs 1 lakh crore bond-sale programme announced this fortnight.
  • •- 04/ The liquidity drain comes as markets price an RBI rate hike as early as October.
  • •- 05/ The benchmark 10-year bond yield has climbed to around 7.08%.

## A nine-year first

The Reserve Bank of India has withdrawn $5.2 billion from the banking system through a net open market sale of government bonds. According to reports, it is the first time in nine years that the central bank has conducted a net OMO sale of debt — a sign of how far the liquidity and rate cycle has turned.

## Part of a larger programme

The sale falls under the roughly Rs 1 lakh crore bond-sale programme the RBI announced for the current fortnight, its largest such liquidity-absorption effort in years. Until now, the RBI had been conducting open market operations largely on the purchase side to keep conditions easy.

Selling bonds pulls money out of the banking system. With system liquidity tightening, short-term rates drift toward the repo rate, and the yield curve bears the pressure.

## Why now

The timing is deliberate. The US Federal Reserve hiked rates by 25 basis points overnight — its first increase since late 2023 — and Wall Street banks including Citi and Deutsche Bank have pulled their RBI rate-hike calls forward to October. Emkay expects a 25 basis-point move in a "shallow" tightening cycle of 50-75 basis points.

Withdrawing excess liquidity through bond sales is the standard first step before an actual rate increase. It lets the central bank test transmission while signalling intent.

## Market reaction

Indian government bonds weakened through the day. The benchmark 10-year yield climbed to around 7.08%, holding near levels that already price some probability of a hike.

The Rs 1 lakh crore programme announced for this fortnight means more supply is coming. Traders say the RBI's switch from buyer to seller of bonds marks a regime change for the debt market after years of abundant liquidity.
