Waaree Energies ₹30,000 Crore Plan: From Solar to Full Energy Stack

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Waaree Energies plans ₹30,000 crore capex over 18-24 months to build a fully integrated clean energy platform beyond solar modules
  • 02/About ₹2,000-3,000 crore already deployed; board approved raising up to ₹10,000 crore via QIP to fund the expansion
  • 03/FY27 operating EBITDA guided at ₹7,000-7,700 crore, up from ₹5,900 crore; Q4 net profit jumped 71% YoY to ₹1,061 crore

Waaree Energies Ltd plans ₹30,000 crore in capital expenditure over the next 18–24 months. Chief Financial Officer Abhishek Pareek said the investment marks a shift from solar modules to a fully integrated energy transition platform. "Waaree is no longer just a solar company," he stated.

The board has approved raising up to ₹10,000 crore through a Qualified Institutional Placement. About ₹2,000–3,000 crore has already been deployed. Nearly 80–85 per cent of the total outlay will flow over the next 18–24 months.

The expansion centres on deep backward integration across the solar chain. Cell capacity will scale from 5.4 GW to 15 GW by December. Ingot and wafer capacity of 10 GW is expected within 12–15 months.

Module manufacturing will reach 28 GW within six months, including 2.5 GW being added in the United States. This builds on an existing base of 25 GW of modules and 5 GW of cells. The company has also taken a strategic equity stake in an Oman-based polysilicon facility.

Beyond solar, Waaree is entering battery manufacturing with 3.5 GW of initial Lithium Iron Phosphate cell capacity. This will scale to 16.5 GW by FY28, with total storage capacity targeting around 20 GW. It is also building 1 GW of hydrogen electrolyser capacity.

The company targets over 90 per cent wallet share across the value chain. Revenue is already diversified: roughly 50 per cent from EPC, 30 per cent from exports, and 20 per cent from B2C. Whole-time director and CEO Jignesh Rathod said the focus is on "deepening value chain integration while scaling next-generation growth engines."

Operationally, module production rose from 7 GW to 12.6 GW in FY26. Quarterly output hit a record 4.2 GW in the March quarter. Consolidated net profit surged 71 per cent year-on-year to ₹1,061 crore in Q4, while revenue more than doubled to ₹8,480 crore.

For FY27, the company has guided operating EBITDA of ₹7,000–7,700 crore, up from ₹5,900 crore in FY26. Government PLI approvals of about ₹2,700–2,800 crore across solar and hydrogen support the build-out. The stock trades on the NSE under the ticker WAAREEENER.
