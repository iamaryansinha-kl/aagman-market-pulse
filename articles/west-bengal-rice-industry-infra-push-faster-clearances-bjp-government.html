West Bengal Rice Industry Seeks Infra Push and Faster Clearances from New BJP Government

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •• West Bengal's rice industry has sought better infrastructure and faster approvals from the new state government
  • •• The industry demands a single-window clearance mechanism and a dedicated Rice Industry Development Board
  • •• India's rice exports rose nearly 19% in 2025 to around 215 lakh tonnes after easing of export restrictions
  • •• West Bengal's premium rice varieties Gobindo Bhog and Tulai Panji enjoy GI-tag recognition

The rice industry in West Bengal has sought better infrastructure and faster approvals from the new BJP government in the state. West Bengal is the country's largest rice-producing state.

Ricevilla Foods CEO Suraj Agarwal said the industry needs improved infrastructure for rice mills and warehouses. Rural areas face poor roads, drainage systems, and delayed electricity connections.

"In many rural districts, electricity connections for new rice mills take months. This causes delays, higher project costs, and harassment," Agarwal said.

Modern storage and logistics infrastructure has become increasingly important. India's annual rice production has crossed 1,500 lakh tonnes. Government rice stocks have exceeded 590 lakh tonnes.

The industry has demanded a single-window clearance mechanism. This would reduce delays and paperwork for licenses and approvals.

Agarwal also pushed for the formation of a dedicated Rice Industry Development Board. This would facilitate coordination among farmers, mill owners, exporters, and government agencies.

India's rice exports rose nearly 19% in 2025. Exports reached around 215 lakh tonnes following the easing of export restrictions.

India is currently the world's largest rice exporter. The country accounts for nearly 40% of global rice trade.

The industry stressed the need for better rail and port connectivity. This would improve exports from eastern India.

West Bengal's premium rice varieties enjoy GI-tag recognition. Gobindo Bhog and Tulai Panji have been witnessing rising demand in domestic and international markets.

"Government support for branding and promotion of Bengal's premium rice varieties can significantly increase exports," Agarwal said.

West Bengal Rice Mills Owners Association chairman Sushil K Choudhury said rice mills in the state are "not in good shape." This is due to large-scale free rice distribution under both central and state government schemes.

"About 3.5 crore beneficiaries receive rice under the state pool. In addition, 6.5 crore beneficiaries receive rice under the central pool. If the state pool is stopped, it will directly have a positive impact on rice mill owners," Choudhury said.

There are around 1,500 rice mills in West Bengal. Nearly 550 are engaged in government procurement. The remaining units are involved in private marketing and exports.

Industry estimates suggest that despite West Bengal contributing nearly 150 lakh tonnes to India's total rice production, only a limited portion reaches export markets directly.
