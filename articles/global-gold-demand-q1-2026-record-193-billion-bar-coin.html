Global Gold Demand Up 2% in Q1 2026: Record $193 Billion on Strong Bar, Coin Buying

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Global gold demand rose 2% YoY to 1,231 tonnes in Jan-Mar 2026, driven by a 42% surge in bar and coin investment to 474 tonnes.
  • 02/The value of demand hit a record $193 billion, up 74% year-on-year, as average gold prices soared 81% to $4,873 per ounce.
  • 03/Central banks added 244 tonnes to reserves and RBI bought 300 kg, while jewellery demand fell 23% to 300 tonnes on high prices.

Global gold demand rose 2% year-on-year to 1,231 tonnes in the January-March quarter. The World Gold Council released its Q1 2026 Gold Demand Trends report on Wednesday.

Bar and coin investment demand surged 42% to 474 tonnes. This was driven by geopolitical tensions and gold's safe-haven appeal.

The average gold price jumped 81% to $4,873 an ounce. This compares with $2,860 an ounce in Q1 2025.

While volumes rose modestly, the value of demand hit a record $193 billion. This marks a 74% increase year-on-year.

"Geopolitical tensions attracted retail investors around the world to gold's price momentum and safe-haven appeal, driving bar and coin demand up 42%," said Sachin Jain, WGC Regional CEO, India.

China led bar and coin buying with demand surging 67% to a record 207 tonnes. This beats the previous quarterly record of 155 tonnes set in Q2 2013.

Other Eastern markets saw gains too. India, South Korea and Japan reported increased bar and coin buying alongside the US and Europe.

US bar and coin demand rose 14%. European demand jumped 50% over the same period.

Jewellery demand fell sharply, dropping 23% year-on-year to 300 tonnes. China saw a 32% decline, India fell 19% and the Middle East dropped 23%.

"Market analysis suggests that some jewellery consumption has moved into bar and coin demand, particularly in markets like China and India where jewellery can act as a proxy investment," Jain added.

Central banks added 244 tonnes to global reserves in Q1. Purchases exceeded both the previous quarter and the five-year average.

The Reserve Bank of India bought 300 kg of gold during the quarter. Turkey, Russia and Azerbaijan saw some official sector selling.

Physically-backed gold ETFs saw holdings increase by 62 tonnes. Asian-listed funds led with 84 tonnes added, though US outflows in March tempered gains.

Total gold supply increased 2% year-on-year to 1,231 tonnes. Mine production hit a new first-quarter record while recycling rose 5%.

"The combination of price momentum and heightened geopolitical risk propelled investment demand, most notably in Asia," said Louise Street, WGC Senior Markets Analyst.

Gold prices peaked above $5,400 an ounce in January before a correction. Street noted geopolitical risk should continue supporting demand, though higher interest rates may pose headwinds in Western markets.
