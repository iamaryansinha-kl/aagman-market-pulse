Rupee Hits Record Low of 95.39 as US-Iran Tensions Spike Oil Prices

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Rupee crashes to all-time low of 95.39 per dollar on May 5, breaching previous record of 95.33 set on April 30
  • 02/US-Iranian strikes in the Gulf push crude above $100; oil-sensitive Asian currencies like rupiah and peso also weaken
  • 03/Foreign investors have net sold over $20 billion of Indian stocks since March; analysts warn of wider current account deficit

The Indian rupee slid to a record low of 95.39 per dollar on Tuesday, May 5, 2026. The currency fell 0.3% on the day.

The fresh low eclipsed the previous all-time low of 95.33 hit on Thursday, April 30. Gulf hostilities have tested a fragile truce between Washington and Tehran.

US-Iranian strikes in the region rattled markets and dimmed hopes for a diplomatic resolution. Crude oil prices stayed above $100 per barrel through the session.

Oil-sensitive Asian peers also weakened. The Indonesian rupiah and Philippine peso declined alongside the rupee as investors priced in prolonged supply risks.

Analysts warn that an elongated Middle East conflict would keep energy prices elevated. This would widen India's current account deficit and hurt macro stability.

Persistently high oil prices could also slow economic growth and stoke inflation. India imports over 80% of its crude requirements, leaving it heavily exposed.

Foreign portfolio investors have dumped over $20 billion worth of Indian stocks between March and May 2026. These sustained outflows amplify currency depreciation pressure.

The rupee faces near-term headwinds until geopolitical stability returns. A weaker currency raises import costs and complicates the Reserve Bank of India's inflation management task.
