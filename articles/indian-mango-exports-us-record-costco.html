Indian Mango Exports to US Hit Record 30% YoY Growth, Costco Debut

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Indian mango exports to the US could jump 30% YoY this season, with last year's full-year figure of 2,188 metric tonnes and $9.6 million already matched by mid-June
  • 02/Costco is selling Indian mangoes for the first time in smaller four-mango packs; retail prices range $45-$55 for a three-kg box
  • 03/Record growth comes despite sharply higher air freight costs from the Iran war; north Indian varieties will extend the season into July

Indian mango exports to the United States are surging to record levels this summer. Premium varieties such as Alphonso and Himayat are driving the demand.

Sales have already matched last year's full-season figures by mid-June. That means 2,188 metric tonnes valued at $9.6 million, according to APEDA chairman Abhishek Dev.

Exports could climb 30% year-on-year this season. The growth comes despite sharply elevated air freight costs due to the Iran war.

The season window is extending further. Good-quality north Indian varieties are expected to reach the US market in July, adding at least two more weeks.

US retail giant Costco is selling Indian mangoes for the first time. The chain is offering smaller four-mango packs instead of the standard nine-to-eleven box format.

Retail prices currently range between $45 and $55 for a three-kg box. Importers are also driving mango vans directly into Indian-dominated neighborhoods.

"Indians don't eat mangoes as just some fruit; we celebrate them," said Yakin Shah, a New Jersey-based importer. His customers line up within hours of WhatsApp announcements.

Demand is concentrated in New Jersey, Dallas, Chicago, Los Angeles and Washington DC. Consumers seek varieties tied to their home states and childhood memories.

Patel Brothers, with over 50 US stores, is a key retail channel. Other chains include Vijetha, Apna Bazar and Apni Mandi.
