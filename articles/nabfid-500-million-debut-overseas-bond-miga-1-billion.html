NaBFID to Raise $500 Million in Debut Overseas Bond, Plans $1 Billion MIGA-Backed Issue

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

NaBFID is going overseas. The National Bank for Financing Infrastructure and Development is set to raise $500 million through a 10-year bond, its debut in the overseas bond market, while separately holding talks with the Multilateral Investment Guarantee Agency for a $1 billion 15-year issue, people familiar with the development said.

The plan is ambitious in scale. NaBFID is targeting around $4 billion in dollar funding by December through a mix of loans and bonds, including the current $500 million issue and external commercial borrowings. The MIGA-backed tranche, expected to carry a World Bank Group guarantee, will help the lender access longer-term overseas funding, a key requirement for financing long-gestation infrastructure projects.

The timing leans on the Reserve Bank of India. NaBFID is tapping the central bank's concessional US dollar-rupee swap facility, operationalised in June to lower hedging costs for eligible overseas borrowings, which provides a concessional swap cost of 1.5% a year with drawdowns permitted until December 31.

The economics, however, have tightened. As US Treasury yields have risen, the cost advantage of dollar borrowing has narrowed. After factoring in the 1.5% swap cost, dollar borrowing costs are now close to domestic bond market rates, though still slightly cheaper. Bank of Maharashtra's recent $500 million five-year bond priced at 130 basis points over US Treasuries with a 6.112% coupon works out to roughly 7.6% including the swap cost, broadly in line with comparable domestic borrowing.

The $500 million 10-year bond will price off prevailing US Treasury yields and the spread at which the market clears, while the MIGA-backed 15-year tranche of around $1 billion is being negotiated separately.

The fund-raising comes a day after a NaBFID-BCG report projected India needs Rs 168-172 trillion of infrastructure investment through FY31, underscoring the scale of funding the development finance institution is expected to help channel. For India's infrastructure push, NaBFID's ability to raise long-dated foreign currency cheaply, and its use of multilateral guarantees to stretch tenors, will matter as much as the headline numbers.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
HOW MUCH IS NABFID RAISING OVERSEAS?
$500 million via a debut 10-year bond, plus around $1 billion through a 15-year MIGA-guaranteed tranche, targeting $4 billion of dollar funding by December.
WHAT IS THE RBI SWAP FACILITY?
A concessional dollar-rupee swap at 1.5% a year for eligible overseas borrowings, operationalised in June, with drawdowns permitted until December 31.
WHY THE MIGA GUARANTEE?
To access longer-term overseas funding at viable pricing, matching the long gestation of infrastructure projects.
