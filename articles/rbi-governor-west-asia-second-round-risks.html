RBI Governor Flags Second-Round Risks From West Asia Conflict

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra warns that prolonged West Asia conflict risks embedding supply shocks into India's general price level via second-round effects
  • 02/West Asia accounts for one-sixth of India's exports, one-fifth of imports, half of crude oil imports, and two-fifths of fertiliser imports and remittances
  • 03/RBI is in data-dependent "wait-and-watch" mode with a neutral stance, keeping the repo rate unchanged at 5.25% in April 2026

RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra has flagged second-round inflation risks from the ongoing West Asia conflict. Speaking at Princeton University on April 21, 2026, he cautioned that what begins as a supply shock could become embedded in the general price level.

"Second-round effects are the real concern. They can materialise if the supply chain disruptions continue for long," Malhotra said. He stressed that monetary policy's primary role is preventing this entrenchment through its influence on inflation expectations.

India's economic exposure to West Asia remains significant. The region contributes about one-sixth of India's exports and one-fifth of its imports. It also supplies roughly half of India's crude oil imports, alongside two-fifths of fertiliser imports and inward remittances.

So far, India has been shielded from sharp fuel price spikes. State-run refiners have absorbed part of the cost pressures, helping keep retail prices relatively stable. However, that buffer may not hold indefinitely.

The RBI kept the repo rate unchanged at 5.25% in its April 2026 MPC meeting. Malhotra said the central bank is in a data-dependent "wait-and-watch" mode, maintaining a neutral stance to preserve flexibility.

"We are, therefore, in wait and watch mode now. We have been maintaining a neutral stance for the last few policy cycles," he said. This approach allows the RBI to respond as inflation-growth dynamics evolve without firm commitments.

Malhotra indicated the RBI would act through its influence on inflation expectations rather than through blunt demand compression. He stopped short of offering firm guidance, underlining that uncertainty in global conditions could still force a shift in either direction.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT ARE "SECOND-ROUND EFFECTS" THAT RBI GOVERNOR MALHOTRA WARNED ABOUT?
SECOND-ROUND EFFECTS REFER TO A SITUATION WHERE INITIAL SUPPLY SHOCKS — SUCH AS RISING ENERGY PRICES FROM THE WEST ASIA CONFLICT — BEGIN FEEDING INTO WAGES, SERVICES, AND CORE PRICES. WHAT STARTS AS A TEMPORARY SUPPLY SHOCK CAN BECOME EMBEDDED IN THE GENERAL PRICE LEVEL, MAKING INFLATION MORE PERSISTENT AND ENTRENCHED.
HOW SIGNIFICANT IS WEST ASIA TO INDIA'S ECONOMY?
WEST ASIA CONTRIBUTES ABOUT ONE-SIXTH OF INDIA'S EXPORTS, ONE-FIFTH OF ITS IMPORTS, HALF OF ITS CRUDE OIL IMPORTS, TWO-FIFTHS OF FERTILISER IMPORTS, AND ALMOST TWO-FIFTHS OF INWARD REMITTANCES. THIS MAKES ANY PROLONGED DISRUPTION IN THE REGION PARTICULARLY SENSITIVE FOR DOMESTIC PRICES AND THE BROADER ECONOMY.
WHAT IS THE RBI'S CURRENT MONETARY POLICY STANCE?
THE RBI IS IN A DATA-DEPENDENT "WAIT-AND-WATCH" MODE WITH A NEUTRAL STANCE. THE REPO RATE WAS HELD UNCHANGED AT 5.25% IN THE APRIL 2026 MPC MEETING. GOVERNOR MALHOTRA SAID THIS APPROACH PRESERVES FLEXIBILITY TO RESPOND AS INFLATION-GROWTH DYNAMICS EVOLVE, WITHOUT MAKING FIRM COMMITMENTS TO THE FUTURE PATH OF POLICY.
HOW HAS INDIA BEEN SHIELDED FROM FUEL PRICE SPIKES SO FAR?
STATE-RUN REFINERS HAVE ABSORBED A PORTION OF THE COST PRESSURES, HELPING KEEP RETAIL FUEL PRICES RELATIVELY STABLE. HOWEVER, THIS BUFFER MAY NOT HOLD INDEFINITELY. WITH REGIONAL ELECTIONS UNDERWAY AND FINANCIAL STRAIN BUILDING ON REFINERS, ANY EVENTUAL PASS-THROUGH OF HIGHER COSTS TO CONSUMERS COULD ADD TO INFLATIONARY PRESSURES.
WHAT DOES THE RBI PREFER OVER AGGRESSIVE RATE HIKES?
GOVERNOR MALHOTRA SIGNALLED THAT THE RBI WOULD PREFER TO ACT "THROUGH ITS INFLUENCE ON INFLATION EXPECTATIONS RATHER THAN THROUGH BLUNT DEMAND COMPRESSION." THIS MEANS THE CENTRAL BANK AIMS TO GUIDE INFLATION EXPECTATIONS JUDICIOUSLY INSTEAD OF SLASHING DEMAND WITH SHARP RATE HIKES THAT COULD SLOW ECONOMIC GROWTH.
