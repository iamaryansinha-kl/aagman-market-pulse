Macquarie: AI Boom Won't Crash at Once, Will Pop in Rolling Bubbles

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Macquarie analyst Viktor Shvets sees AI investment unwinding through sequential rolling bubbles across LLMs, facilitators and applications
  • 02/Global AI investment hit $850 billion in 2026, roughly $500 billion above the pre-AI trend, outpacing railways and dot-com manias
  • 03/Magnificent Seven share of US market cap fell from 36% to 32%, with investors advised to go passive, thematic or day-trade around headlines

Macquarie analyst Viktor Shvets says the AI investment boom will not end in a single crash. It will deflate through a series of rolling bubbles across different segments of the AI ecosystem.

Global AI-related investment is now running at about $850 billion in 2026. That is roughly $500 billion above the pre-AI trend, making it larger and faster than historic manias like railways, canals and the dot-com boom.

Corporations, especially US hyperscalers, are rapidly exhausting internal cash. Debt issuance is expected to reach around $180 billion, with capex-to-revenue ratios climbing above 50%.

Yet annualised AI revenues are already estimated at close to $175 billion. That is enough to cover current operating expenses and depreciation, growing roughly three times faster than previous IT waves.

The Bank for International Settlements has warned that AI exhibits classic bubble characteristics. Extremely rapid capital deployment and increasingly complex off-balance sheet structures likely make current investment figures understated and more fragile than they appear.

Macquarie stresses that adoption is running ahead of typical bubble patterns. A $2 trillion contract backlog and heavy spending on data centres, memory and logic chips are already visible in hard orders rather than just hype.

The so-called Magnificent Seven have fallen from 36% of US market capitalisation to about 32%. Broader indices like the S&P 500 and NASDAQ are showing phases where relative performance periodically shifts as leadership passes between segments.

China's push to commoditise the AI stack poses a major structural threat. China's Z.ai and Tulongfeng systems are now matching the cybersecurity features of leading US model Mythos, with the US technological lead narrowed to around 10-15%.

Macquarie warns that real pressure points are emerging in labour markets. Lower hiring rates, declining education premia and rising social polarisation point to early evidence of AI-related disruption.

The report outlines three broad approaches for investors: day trade around headlines, go passive or go thematic. In an age of extremes with no reset button, investors should expect elevated volatility and serial repricing.
