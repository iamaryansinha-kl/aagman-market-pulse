Puravankara Q2 Sales Bookings Jump 62% to Rs 2,136 Crore

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Puravankara's sales bookings rose 62% year-on-year to Rs 2,136 crore in the September quarter, from Rs 1,322 crore a year earlier.
  • •- Sales area grew 22% to 1.83 million square feet, while average price realisation climbed 32% to Rs 11,651 per square foot.
  • •- The company handed over 977 homes totalling 0.97 million sq ft during the quarter.
  • •- In the first half of the fiscal, Puravankara has delivered 1,722 homes spanning 1.92 million sq ft.
  • •- Managing Director Ashish Puravankara said the focus is on scaling while maintaining discipline in pricing, capital deployment and execution.

Realty firm Puravankara Ltd on Tuesday reported a 62 per cent annual growth in sales bookings to Rs 2,136 crore during the September quarter, powered by sustained demand for its residential properties across the nine cities where it operates.

In a regulatory filing, the company said its pre-sales rose to Rs 2,136 crore in the second quarter of the current fiscal from Rs 1,322 crore in the year-ago period. The growth was volume-led as well as price-led: sales area for the quarter stood at 1.83 million square feet, up 22 per cent annually, while average price realisation increased 32 per cent annually to Rs 11,651 per square foot.

The price number is the one analysts will focus on. A 32 per cent jump in realisation, well ahead of the 22 per cent growth in area sold, indicates that Puravankara is successfully pushing through rate hikes in its markets — a dynamic that has defined India's residential real estate cycle through 2025 and 2026, where demand has remained robust even as prices climbed across Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Mumbai. In a market where construction input costs have also been rising, the ability to lift prices without denting volumes is the clearest signal of brand strength a developer can show.

Delivery momentum stayed strong through the quarter. Puravankara handed over 977 homes totalling 0.97 million square feet to customers in the September quarter, and in the first six months of the fiscal the company has handed over 1,722 homes spanning 1.92 million sq ft — a pace that keeps it on track for one of its strongest delivery years.

"Our focus is on scaling the business while maintaining discipline in pricing, capital deployment and execution," Ashish Puravankara, the company's Managing Director, said in a statement. "The performance supports the company's strategy of investing in differentiated developments in markets having robust demand," he added.

The backdrop is a housing market that has surprised on the upside for several consecutive years. Steady end-user demand, limited new supply in prime micro-markets and gradually improving mortgage affordability have combined to support both volumes and prices for organised developers, and Puravankara's numbers fit that pattern closely.

Puravankara has completed 97 projects totalling 59 million square feet across nine cities: Bengaluru, Chennai, Hyderabad, Coimbatore, Mangaluru, Kochi, Mumbai, Pune and Goa. That footprint keeps the company concentrated in southern markets, where the recovery in housing demand has been the strongest and where it has the deepest brand recognition.

For investors, the quarterly update reinforces the case that residential developers remain among the cleanest plays on India's consumption cycle. Unlike commercial real estate, which carries office-space risk, or retail, which tracks discretionary spending, housing demand has been anchored by genuine end-user buying, and companies that can deliver on time — Puravankara's 977 homes this quarter — have been rewarded with both pricing power and customer trust.

The stock reaction through Tuesday's session will be watched against a broader market that rallied sharply on the day, with the Sensex up over 1,100 points on finance minister Nirmala Sitharaman's capital gains tax remarks. Realty as a sector has been sensitive to interest rate expectations, and with retail inflation at a two-year high of 5.54 per cent, the rate path remains the key swing factor for housing demand heading into the December quarter.

For now, the operating numbers speak plainly: bookings up 62 per cent, realisations up 32 per cent, deliveries tracking at record pace, and management signalling continued investment in differentiated projects across high-demand markets. If the second half holds anywhere near this trajectory, Puravankara is positioned to close the fiscal with one of its best years on record.

The September quarter is traditionally a mixed period for Indian real estate, with the Shradh fortnight typically dampening launches and site visits before the festive season kicks in from October. A 62 per cent bookings jump despite that seasonal drag suggests underlying demand was strong enough to power through the quiet weeks — and sets up the December quarter, traditionally the year's best, as the real test of whether pricing power holds.

Puravankara's city mix is another quiet advantage. The company is anchored in Bengaluru and Chennai, two of the three strongest housing markets in the country over the past three years, with Hyderabad adding a third high-growth leg. Demand in these southern markets has been driven by technology-sector employment and returning office culture, factors that have proved far more durable than the speculative buying that characterised previous property cycles.

On capital deployment, management has been consistent in prioritising projects where the company controls the land and can move quickly from launch to construction — a discipline that shows up in the delivery numbers. Handing over 977 homes in a single quarter requires construction partners and approvals to move in lockstep, and execution at that scale is itself a durable competitive moat in a market where delayed projects have burned buyer trust for a decade.
