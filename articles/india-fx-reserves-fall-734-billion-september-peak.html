India's FX Reserves Fall to $734.6 Billion, $50 Billion Below Peak

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/India's foreign exchange reserves fell for a fourth straight week to $734.6 billion, down about $50 billion from the September 4 record peak of $785.71 billion.
  • 02/The drawdown reflects RBI spot-market dollar sales to smooth the rupee's slide, plus sell/buy FX swaps absorbing surplus liquidity of ₹5.9 lakh crore a day.
  • 03/The rupee still fell nearly 0.5% to 96.8450 per dollar on Wednesday, within sight of its all-time low of 96.96, as the RBI hiked the repo rate to 5.50%.

India's foreign exchange reserves have fallen for a fourth consecutive week. The reserves stood at $734.6 billion, according to a footnote in RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra's monetary policy statement.

The drop works out to about $50 billion in less than a month. Reserves peaked at a record $785.71 billion on September 4, 2026.

The drawdown reflects active intervention by the Reserve Bank of India. The central bank has been selling dollars in the spot market to slow the rupee's decline, rather than defending a fixed exchange rate level.

The RBI is also running sell/buy FX swaps aimed at absorbing surplus liquidity. That surplus averaged ₹5.9 lakh crore daily since the previous MPC meeting. The swap book now does double duty: draining cash and signalling support in the forward market.

The intervention has not stopped the slide. On Wednesday, the rupee fell nearly 0.5% to 96.8450 per dollar, within sight of its all-time low of 96.96.

Three forces are driving the pressure at the same time. Elevated oil prices raise India's import bill, rising US Treasury yields pull capital toward dollar assets, and increased foreign equity outflows directly convert rupees into dollars.

The same day, the RBI hiked the repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.50% and raised its FY27 CPI inflation forecast to 5.2%. "The rupee will find its correct value," Governor Sanjay Malhotra said.

The trade-off is now explicit. Every dollar sold cushions the rupee's fall but shrinks the war chest, while higher rates risk cooling growth. Investors will watch whether the weekly burn rate slows.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT ARE INDIA'S CURRENT FOREX RESERVES?
INDIA'S FOREIGN EXCHANGE RESERVES STOOD AT $734.6 BILLION IN THE LATEST WEEK, ACCORDING TO A FOOTNOTE TO RBI GOVERNOR SANJAY MALHOTRA'S MONETARY POLICY STATEMENT RELEASED ON OCTOBER 7, 2026. THAT MARKS A FOURTH CONSECUTIVE WEEKLY DECLINE. THE PILE HAS SHRUNK BY ROUGHLY $50 BILLION IN LESS THAN A MONTH FROM THE RECORD PEAK OF $785.71 BILLION SET ON SEPTEMBER 4, 2026, A DROP OF MORE THAN 6% FROM THE TOP. EVEN AFTER THE FALL, THE RESERVES REMAIN AMONG THE LARGEST IN ASIA, BUILT UP OVER YEARS OF INTERVENTION ON THE BUY SIDE.
WHY ARE INDIA'S FOREX RESERVES FALLING?
THE DRAWDOWN REFLECTS ACTIVE INTERVENTION BY THE RESERVE BANK OF INDIA. THE CENTRAL BANK HAS BEEN SELLING DOLLARS IN THE SPOT MARKET TO SLOW THE RUPEE'S DECLINE, RATHER THAN DEFENDING A FIXED EXCHANGE RATE LEVEL. IN PARALLEL, THE RBI IS RUNNING SELL/BUY FX SWAPS AIMED AT ABSORBING SURPLUS RUPEE LIQUIDITY, WHICH AVERAGED ₹5.9 LAKH CRORE PER DAY SINCE THE PREVIOUS MPC MEETING. THE SWAP BOOK NOW DOES DOUBLE DUTY: IT DRAINS EXCESS CASH FROM THE BANKING SYSTEM AND SIGNALS SUPPORT IN THE FORWARD MARKET AT THE SAME TIME.
HOW IS THE RUPEE TRADING AGAINST THE DOLLAR?
THE RUPEE FELL NEARLY 0.5% ON WEDNESDAY, OCTOBER 7, TO 96.8450 PER DOLLAR. THAT LEFT THE CURRENCY WITHIN SIGHT OF ITS ALL-TIME LOW OF 96.96. THE PRESSURE COMES FROM THREE SOURCES RUNNING AT THE SAME TIME: ELEVATED OIL PRICES, WHICH RAISE INDIA'S IMPORT BILL; RISING US TREASURY YIELDS, WHICH PULL CAPITAL TOWARD DOLLAR ASSETS; AND INCREASED FOREIGN EQUITY OUTFLOWS, WHICH DIRECTLY CONVERT RUPEES INTO DOLLARS. DESPITE THE RBI'S SPOT SALES, ALL THREE FORCES HAVE KEPT DEPRECIATION PRESSURE ALIVE THROUGHOUT SEPTEMBER AND INTO OCTOBER.
WHAT DID THE RBI DECIDE AT ITS OCTOBER POLICY MEETING?
THE RBI'S MONETARY POLICY COMMITTEE RAISED THE REPO RATE BY 25 BASIS POINTS TO 5.50% AT ITS OCTOBER REVIEW. THE CENTRAL BANK ALSO RAISED ITS FY27 CPI INFLATION FORECAST TO 5.2%, SIGNALING THAT PRICE PRESSURES REMAIN STICKY. GOVERNOR SANJAY MALHOTRA SAID THE RUPEE WOULD FIND ITS "CORRECT" VALUE, INDICATING THE RBI WILL SMOOTH EXCESSIVE VOLATILITY RATHER THAN DEFEND A SPECIFIC LEVEL FOR THE CURRENCY. THE RATE HIKE ADDS A SECOND TOOL, HIGHER RUPEE YIELDS, TO MAKE INDIAN ASSETS MORE ATTRACTIVE TO FOREIGN INVESTORS.
WHAT SHOULD INVESTORS WATCH NEXT?
THE KEY NUMBER IS THE WEEKLY BURN RATE. THE RESERVES FELL ABOUT $50 BILLION IN UNDER A MONTH, AND INVESTORS SHOULD WATCH WHETHER THAT PACE SLOWS OR ACCELERATES IN COMING WEEKS. A FASTER BURN WOULD SIGNAL DEEPER INTERVENTION AND A SHRINKING BUFFER AGAINST EXTERNAL SHOCKS. A SLOWDOWN WOULD SUGGEST THE RBI IS LETTING THE RUPEE FIND ITS LEVEL FASTER. INVESTORS SHOULD ALSO TRACK CRUDE OIL PRICES, US TREASURY YIELDS, AND FOREIGN INVESTOR FLOWS, THE THREE FORCES DRIVING THE CURRENT DOLLAR DEMAND. GLOSSARY: Foreign Exchange Reserves: The RBI's stockpile of foreign currency assets, gold, and IMF position, used to pay for imports, service debt, and intervene in currency markets. Spot Market Intervention: Direct buying or selling of dollars by the central bank to influence the exchange rate — here, dollar sales to slow the rupee's fall. Sell/Buy Swap: A contract where the RBI sells dollars now and agrees to buy them back later, absorbing rupee liquidity today while leaving its reserve position intact at maturity. Import Cover: The number of months of imports a country's reserves can pay for — a standard gauge of reserve adequacy. Drawdown: A decline in reserve levels over a period. A $50 billion drawdown in under a month is unusually steep for India, even in stress episodes.
