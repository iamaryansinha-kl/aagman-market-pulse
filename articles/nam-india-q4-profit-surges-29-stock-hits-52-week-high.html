NAM-India Q4 Profit Surges 29% to ₹385 Crore, Stock Hits 52-Week High

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/NAM-India reports Q4 net profit of ₹385 crore, up 29% YoY from ₹299 crore, beating analyst estimates
  • 02/Stock rallies over 7% to hit 52-week high of ₹1,065; QAAUM grows 3% q-o-q to ₹7.25 trillion with 24 bps market share gain
  • 03/Brokerages raise targets: Emkay sees ₹1,150, JM Financial sees ₹1,080; investors should watch AUM flow momentum and TER regulation impact

Nippon Life India Asset Management Company (NAM-India) reported a net profit of ₹385 crore for the March quarter. This marks a 29 per cent increase year-over-year from ₹299 crore in the same period last year.

The stock rallied more than 7 per cent on Tuesday to hit a 52-week high of ₹1,065 on the NSE. It later pared some gains but remained firmly in the green, trading 5 per cent higher at ₹1,038.70.

The profit growth was supported by steady growth in assets under management and improved yields. Mutual fund QAAUM rose 3 per cent quarter-over-quarter to ₹7.25 trillion.

Market share increased by 24 basis points sequentially. Revenue yield improved to 41.3 basis points, supported by a favourable asset mix shift towards commodity ETFs.

Revenue rose 5 per cent q-o-q to ₹7.39 billion. EBITDA margin expanded 200 basis points q-o-q to 68.6 per cent, reflecting strong operating leverage.

Brokerage HSBC maintained a "hold" rating but raised its target price to ₹890. It noted that yield expansion and controlled operating cost growth led to an earnings beat versus estimates.

JM Financial called it one of the strongest Q4 performances among asset management companies. It raised FY27 and FY28 earnings estimates by around 4 per cent each and expects 18 per cent earnings CAGR, maintaining an "add" rating with a revised target of ₹1,080.

Emkay Global Financial Services retained a "buy" rating with a target price of ₹1,150. It noted that the company plans to offset the 3–4 basis points impact from new TER regulations by passing it on to distributors.
