Tata Group and JSW Group Plow $1 Billion Into EV and Battery Tech

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Tata Group and JSW Group plan to invest nearly $1 billion combined in domestic EV and battery technology R&D
  • 02/Agratas is spending over $400 million on a Bengaluru facility for LFP batteries; JSW Motors will invest at least $500 million over 5-6 years
  • 03/Both moves aim to cut reliance on Chinese battery technology amid tightening global tech transfer rules

Tata Group and JSW Group are investing nearly $1 billion to build domestic capabilities in electric vehicle and battery technologies. The separate R&D initiatives reflect growing urgency among Indian conglomerates to reduce dependence on Chinese suppliers.

Tata's battery unit, Agratas Ltd., is spending more than $400 million on a new R&D facility in Bengaluru. The centre will focus on developing lithium iron phosphate (LFP) and lithium manganese iron phosphate technologies.

Agratas currently relies on China for LFP cells. It already has access to nickel manganese cobalt battery technology sourced from South Korea.

"Our global R&D programme is progressing well, supported by two state-of-the-art labs in Bengaluru and Oxford," an Agratas spokesperson said in an emailed statement. The firm is using advanced equipment and workforce "to drive our next generation of battery innovation."

JSW Motors Ltd., the passenger vehicle arm of Sajjan Jindal's JSW Group, is investing at least $500 million over the next five to six years. The research hub will be located in Maharashtra.

"The goal is to adapt global automotive technology to Indian conditions," said Ranjan Nayak, Chief Executive Officer of JSW Motors Ltd. The centre will localise vehicles developed with global partners and build proprietary software and connected vehicle capabilities.

The investments come as China grows more guarded about sharing critical battery and EV technology. Several Indian firms have faced friction in joint ventures as Beijing tightens tech transfer rules amid the ongoing tariff war with the US.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
HOW MUCH ARE TATA GROUP AND JSW GROUP INVESTING IN EV AND BATTERY TECHNOLOGY?
THE TWO CONGLOMERATES ARE PLANNING TO INVEST NEARLY $1 BILLION COMBINED. TATA'S BATTERY UNIT AGRATAS IS SPENDING MORE THAN $400 MILLION, WHILE JSW MOTORS IS COMMITTING AT LEAST $500 MILLION OVER THE NEXT FIVE TO SIX YEARS.
WHAT IS AGRATAS DEVELOPING AT ITS BENGALURU R&D FACILITY?
AGRATAS LTD. IS BUILDING A NEW R&D CENTRE IN BENGALURU FOCUSED ON DEVELOPING LITHIUM IRON PHOSPHATE (LFP) AND LITHIUM MANGANESE IRON PHOSPHATE BATTERY TECHNOLOGIES. THESE ARE PRODUCTS FOR WHICH AGRATAS CURRENTLY DEPENDS ON CHINA.
WHERE IS JSW MOTORS SETTING UP ITS RESEARCH HUB?
JSW MOTORS LTD. IS ESTABLISHING ITS RESEARCH HUB IN MAHARASHTRA. THE FACILITY WILL FOCUS ON LOCALISING VEHICLES DEVELOPED WITH GLOBAL PARTNERS, BUILDING PROPRIETARY SOFTWARE CAPABILITIES, AND ADVANCING CONNECTED VEHICLE TECHNOLOGY.
WHY ARE INDIAN COMPANIES INVESTING IN LOCAL BATTERY AND EV CAPABILITIES?
CHINA IS GROWING INCREASINGLY GUARDED ABOUT SHARING ADVANCED BATTERY AND EV TECHNOLOGIES AMID THE ONGOING TARIFF WAR WITH THE US. INDIAN FIRMS ARE FACING DELAYS, HIGHER COMPLIANCE BURDENS, AND FEWER GUARANTEES OF ACCESS TO THE LATEST TECHNOLOGY FROM CHINESE PARTNERS.
WHAT BATTERY TECHNOLOGY DOES AGRATAS ALREADY HAVE ACCESS TO?
AGRATAS CURRENTLY HAS ACCESS TO NICKEL MANGANESE COBALT (NMC) BATTERY TECHNOLOGY SOURCED FROM SOUTH KOREA. THE NEW BENGALURU FACILITY WILL HELP IT DEVELOP LFP AND LMFP CAPABILITIES IN-HOUSE TO REDUCE RELIANCE ON CHINESE SUPPLIERS.
