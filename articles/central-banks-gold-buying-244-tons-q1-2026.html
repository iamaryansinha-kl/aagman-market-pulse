Central Banks Scoop Up 244 Tons of Gold in Bumpy First Quarter

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Central banks purchased 244 tons of gold in Q1 2026, the fastest pace in over a year
  • 02/Gold prices peaked near $5,600 in late January before a 12% March correction
  • 03/Poland, Uzbekistan and China led buying while Turkey, Russia and Azerbaijan sold

Central banks added gold at the fastest pace in over a year during the first quarter of 2026. Net official-sector purchases reached 244 tons, up from 208 tons in the previous quarter.

Gold prices swung wildly in early 2026. Bullion hit a record near $5,600 an ounce on January 29, then slumped 12% in March after the outbreak of the US-Iran war. That marked the biggest monthly drop since 2008.

Poland, Uzbekistan and China were the largest reported buyers. Much of the buying remains undeclared and not tracked by IMF statistics.

Turkey, Russia and Azerbaijan were among sellers. They shed an estimated 115 tons over the period. Turkey sold to shield its currency from war effects, while Russia liquidated gold to meet a budget deficit.

Spot gold was trading below $4,600 an ounce ahead of the WGC report. The sharp correction allowed hesitant buyers to enter the market.

John Reade, chief strategist at the World Gold Council, noted the buying opportunity. "That has allowed central banks that might have been hanging back, waiting for exactly this opportunity, to come in and scoop up a load," he said.

Metals Focus Ltd calculates estimated purchases for the WGC using public data, trade statistics and field research. Not all central bank buying is disclosed in official figures.
