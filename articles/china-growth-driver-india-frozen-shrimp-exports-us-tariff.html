China Emerges as Key Growth Driver for India's Frozen Shrimp Exports Amid US Tariff Hit

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/China's imports of Indian frozen shrimp surged 72% year-on-year to 9,016 MT in January 2026, making it the fastest-growing major market for India's seafood sector
  • 02/US exports plunged 36% YoY to 13,888 MT as high reciprocal tariffs disrupted India's largest shrimp market, forcing exporters to pivot eastward
  • 03/India's total shrimp exports reached 50,564 MT in January 2026, up 3% YoY, but value declined 2% to $347 million as raw products replaced value-added shipments

India's frozen shrimp exporters are finding a new lifeline in China. While traditional markets falter, Beijing's appetite for Indian seafood is surging at a pace that few anticipated.

China imported 9,016 MT of Indian frozen shrimp in January 2026, a 72% jump from the same month last year. This makes China the fastest-growing major destination for India's seafood, even as the sector grapples with a sharp contraction in its historically largest market.

The United States, which has long been the cornerstone of India's shrimp export strategy, saw shipments plummet 36% year-on-year to 13,888 MT in January. High reciprocal tariffs imposed by the US government have made Indian shrimp significantly more expensive for American buyers, forcing exporters to absorb costs or seek alternative markets.

The European Union offered some relief, with imports rising 49% to 11,099 MT. But the scale of China's growth stands out. From a relatively modest base, Chinese demand has expanded rapidly, reinforcing its role as an increasingly important alternative market for Indian producers.

India's total shrimp exports reached 50,564 MT in January 2026, marking a modest 3% year-on-year increase in volume. However, the value of these shipments declined 2% to $347 million, reflecting a shift toward raw, lower-value products and away from processed, value-added goods.

Raw L. vannamei, the backbone of India's export portfolio, accounted for 36,765 MT in January, up 3% year-on-year. Value-added exports, which command higher margins, dropped 17% to 4,664 MT as the US tariff environment made premium processed products less competitive.

Raw P. monodon continued its strong growth trajectory, rising 51% year-on-year to 3,670 MT. Wild-caught shrimp saw a modest recovery of 5% to 5,465 MT, though volumes remain structurally lower than in previous years.

The full year of 2025 set a strong baseline, with total exports reaching 796,374 MT, up 9% year-on-year, and value climbing 14% to $5,664 million. The current shift toward China and the EU suggests that 2026 could see a fundamental reorientation of India's seafood trade map.

India is the world's largest exporter of frozen shrimp and the second-largest fish producer overall. Marine products contribute roughly 17% of the country's total agricultural exports, making the sector a significant player in the broader economy. The ability to pivot toward China will be critical for sustaining this contribution.

The broader context includes rising trade tensions globally. India's shrimp sector now faces a similar challenge to Vietnam's, where exporters are also navigating US tariffs while expanding into China and the EU. The next few months will reveal whether China's demand can sustain its current trajectory or if it represents a temporary rebalancing.
