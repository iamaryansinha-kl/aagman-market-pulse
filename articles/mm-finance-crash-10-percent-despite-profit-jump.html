M&M Finance Crashes 10% Despite Profit Jump, Nomura Cuts Target

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Shares of Mahindra & Mahindra Financial Services plunged as much as 10% to a day's low of Rs 368 on the BSE on Thursday.
  • •- The fall came a day after the company reported a 55% rise in standalone net profit to Rs 882 crore.
  • •- Nomura cut its target price to Rs 400 from Rs 415 while retaining a Buy rating, citing tractor-financing asset quality and rising funding costs.
  • •- The brokerage expects credit costs to rise to 1.8% annualised in the second half of FY27 and sees funding costs climbing 70 basis points over five quarters.
  • •- Motilal Oswal maintained a Buy with a Rs 375 target, calling it a steady quarter with healthy disbursement and AUM growth.

The market's verdict on Mahindra & Mahindra Financial Services' blockbuster quarter arrived a day late, and it was brutal. Shares of the rural-focused lender plunged as much as 10% on Thursday to a day's low of Rs 368 on the BSE — this barely twelve hours after the company reported a 55% year-on-year jump in standalone net profit to Rs 882 crore, with disbursements up 22%, credit cost down to 1.6% of average assets and asset quality improving on every metric. A beat of that scale would normally be rewarded; instead, investors chose to sell, and the analyst notes published alongside the fall explain why the market decided the good news was already in the price.

Nomura's note captures the scepticism precisely. The brokerage retained its Buy rating but trimmed its target price to Rs 400 from Rs 415, and the reasoning is a laundry list of second-half risks rather than quarrels with the quarter itself. Nomura has turned cautious on asset quality in the tractor financing segment — the heartland of M&M Finance's wheel business — and expects credit costs to rise to 1.8% annualised in the third and fourth quarters of FY27, compared with 1.7% in the first half. Management's defence is that tractor loan quality is best assessed at the end of the sowing season, when repayments fall due; the market, evidently, is not willing to wait that long for confirmation. That impatience is the tell: with the stock carrying a full valuation at 1.4 times forward book and every lender being marked down for a higher-rate world, investors treated management's wait-and-see framing not as prudence but as an open question they could no longer afford to leave unanswered.

The funding-cost problem is the deeper issue, and it compounds daily as the rate cycle turns. Nomura expects the company's cost of funds to rise 70 basis points over the next five quarters and has cut its net profit estimates for FY27 through FY29 by 1-9% to account for it. Management itself guided that the cost of funds will remain on an upward trajectory, even while pointing to excess liquidity, a floating-rate non-wheel portfolio and securitisation as cushions. A third, quieter overhang sits in insurance: proposed reforms to insurance distribution could hit a business that contributed 35 basis points of average assets in FY26 through the company's broking subsidiary, and management has not yet quantified the potential impact. Uncertainty of that kind is exactly what a falling market prices most harshly.

Motilal Oswal's note, also published on Thursday, shows the divide among the bulls. The brokerage maintained its Buy rating with a target of Rs 375 — implying 26% upside from the day's low, against Nomura's Rs 400 target and its retained 35% upside — and described a steady quarter supported by healthy disbursement and AUM growth, with asset quality improving through a difficult stretch of weak monsoons, El Niño concerns and elevated crude prices. Notably, Motilal Oswal flagged that net interest margin actually declined in the quarter, squeezed by higher liquidity buffers and seasonal trade advances, with management expecting margins to improve only next quarter. The stock's defenders, in other words, are defending growth and asset quality; nobody is defending the margin trajectory, and at 1.4 times one-year-forward book value the shares offered no valuation cushion when the margin question resurfaced.

The episode is a clean case study in what a correcting market does to good earnings. M&M Finance entered Thursday trading at roughly 1.4 times estimated FY28 book value after a strong run, with Motilal Oswal projecting 14% AUM and 18% profit compound growth through FY28 and a 2.2% return on assets — estimates that require the funding-cost rise to stay manageable and the tractor book to hold through the sowing-season test. In a rising market those assumptions get the benefit of the doubt; in a market where midcaps are in correction territory and every NBFC is being re-underwritten for a higher-for-longer rate path, they get discounted before lunch. The 10% fall is less a judgment that the quarter was bad than a judgment that the next three quarters carry more risk than the print revealed — and on a day when the benchmark indices were already falling, that judgment needed no invitation. It is also a reminder that in the current tape, earnings beats have become necessary but nowhere near sufficient: what the market is paying for is visibility on margins and funding costs into FY28, and any company that cannot offer it — however good the headline growth — should expect Thursday's treatment. The next checkpoint arrives at the end of the sowing season, when the tractor book's true asset quality becomes visible; until then, the target-price debate between Nomura's Rs 400 and Motilal's Rs 375 is really an argument about which risk — credit or funding — proves more expensive, and the stock will trade on that arithmetic rather than on the quarter just delivered.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHY DID M&M FINANCE SHARES FALL DESPITE STRONG Q2 RESULTS?
Investors sold despite a 55% profit jump, with Nomura flagging rising credit costs in tractor financing, a 70-basis-point increase in funding costs over five quarters and regulatory risk to insurance distribution income.
WHAT DID NOMURA CHANGE?
It cut its target price to Rs 400 from Rs 415 while retaining a Buy rating, and trimmed FY27-FY29 profit estimates by 1-9%.
WHAT DID MOTILAL OSWAL SAY?
It maintained a Buy with a Rs 375 target, calling the quarter steady, though it noted net interest margin declined due to higher liquidity buffers and seasonal factors.
WHAT SHOULD INVESTORS WATCH NEXT?
Tractor loan asset quality at the end of the sowing season, the margin recovery management has promised for next quarter, and the impact of insurance distribution reforms. GLOSSARY: - Credit cost: Provisions and write-offs expressed as a percentage of average assets. - Price-to-book value: A company's share price relative to the accounting value of its net assets; a standard valuation metric for lenders. - Funding cost: The interest rate a lender pays to raise the money it lends on. - Sowing season: The planting period after which farm loan repayments typically fall due.
