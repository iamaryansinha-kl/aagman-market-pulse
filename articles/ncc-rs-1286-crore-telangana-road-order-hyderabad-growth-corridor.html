NCC Wins Rs 1,286 Crore Telangana Road Order; Stock Jumps 5%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/NCC has received a Letter of Acceptance worth Rs 1,286.03 crore from Hyderabad Growth Corridor Limited for a road project in Telangana.
  • 02/The order covers Package 1 of Radial Road-2, from Budwel on the Outer Ring Road to Shabad on NH-167N, with an 18-month execution timeline.
  • 03/The stock rose 4.89% to Rs 125.57, but remains down 40% over the past year and trades below book value.

NCC has landed a significant order. The construction company received a Letter of Acceptance from Hyderabad Growth Corridor Limited for a road project worth Rs 1,286.03 crore in Telangana, it said in a filing on Friday.

The order is dated October 8, 2026. It covers the construction of Radial Road-2 from the Outer Ring Road near Budwel to Nacharam on NH-167N, specifically Package 1 from Budwel to Shabad.

The execution timeline is 18 months. That puts delivery well inside FY28, adding to the order book of a company that has kept winning work even as its stock has slumped.

The market liked the news. NCC shares rose 4.89% to Rs 125.57 in Friday's session.

The bounce, however, comes off a deep decline. The stock is down 13% over the past three months and 40% over the last year. Its 52-week high stands at Rs 217.25 — the current price is barely half that.

Valuation reflects the pessimism. NCC trades at a price-to-earnings ratio of 10.76 and a price-to-book ratio of 0.94, meaning the market values the company at slightly less than the accounting value of its assets.

The backdrop is a sector under pressure. Road and infrastructure contractors have faced rising input costs, slower receivables from government bodies, and a funding environment made tighter by the Reserve Bank of India's recent rate hike. NCC's 40% one-year decline mirrors that broader derating.

Technicals suggest capitulation rather than confirmation. The stock's Relative Strength Index sits at 22.9 — below the 30 level generally read as oversold — and it trades below all eight of its simple moving averages.

Who is buying at these levels? Promoters marginally raised their stake in the September 2026 quarter, from 23.08% to 23.19%. Mutual funds also added, lifting holdings from 14.13% to 14.38%.

Foreign institutional investors went the other way. FIIs cut their stake from 11.29% to 10.44% during the same quarter — selling into the same weakness that domestic investors were buying.

The order itself is meaningful scale. At Rs 1,286 crore, the contract represents roughly 17% of NCC's current market capitalisation of around Rs 7,515 crore — a single project that moves the needle. For a company of NCC's size, winning one such order a quarter is enough to keep revenue growing even in a slow environment.

For investors, the question is execution. Orders of this size from state development bodies carry payment-cycle and working-capital risks, and NCC's margin track record on large road packages will determine whether this win translates into earnings.

The near-term catalysts are the order-flow disclosures for October and the September-quarter results, where the fresh LoA will start appearing in the reported order book. Hyderabad Growth Corridor's Radial Road programme has multiple packages — watchers will be looking for whether NCC bids for the next ones.

Price-to-book ratio: A valuation metric comparing a company's market value to its book value. Below 1.0 — as with NCC's 0.94 — means the stock trades below the accounting value of its net assets.
