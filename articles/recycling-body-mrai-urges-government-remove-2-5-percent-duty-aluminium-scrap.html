Recycling Body MRAI Urges Government to Remove 2.5% Duty on Aluminium Scrap

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

The recycling industry wants a level playing field.

The Material Recycling Association of India (MRAI) has urged the government to remove the 2.5% customs duty on aluminium scrap imports, arguing that the levy makes domestic recycling uncompetitive and inadvertently encourages primary aluminium production, which is far more energy-intensive and environmentally damaging.

Aluminium recycling is significantly greener than primary production. Recycling aluminium requires only 5% of the energy needed to produce primary aluminium from bauxite ore. This translates to a 95% reduction in carbon emissions per tonne of metal produced. In a world where carbon footprints are increasingly under scrutiny, recycling is not just an economic choice but an environmental necessity.

The 2.5% duty adds cost to scrap imports. India does not produce enough aluminium scrap domestically to meet the demand of its recycling industry. The country relies on imports from the US, Europe, and the Middle East to feed its recycling plants. The duty raises input costs for recyclers, compressing their margins and making recycled aluminium more expensive than primary metal.

Primary aluminium producers benefit from the duty. India has significant primary aluminium capacity, dominated by Vedanta and Hindalco. These companies benefit when scrap imports are discouraged, as it maintains demand for their primary metal. The duty, in effect, subsidises primary production at the expense of recycling.

The circular economy argument is gaining traction globally. The European Union, Japan, and South Korea have all implemented policies that favour recycling over primary production, recognising that resource efficiency and waste reduction are critical for sustainable development. India's recycling industry argues that it should not be penalised for pursuing the same goals.

The government's Make in India and Atmanirbhar Bharat initiatives could align with recycling. Supporting domestic recycling capacity reduces dependence on imported primary aluminium and bauxite, while creating employment in the recycling sector. The 2.5% duty on scrap works against these objectives by making an indigenous industry less competitive.
