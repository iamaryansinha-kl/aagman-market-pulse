India Services PMI Drops to 57.4 in June, Slowest Growth in 17 Months

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/HSBC India Services PMI falls to 57.4 in June from 59.8 in May, marking weakest expansion in 17 months
  • 02/New order growth slows to weakest pace in over 2.5 years; hiring activity remains broadly stagnant
  • 03/Export orders rise at fastest pace in 3 months; input cost inflation eases to 5-month low

India's services sector expanded at its slowest pace in 17 months during June. The HSBC India Services Purchasing Managers' Index (PMI), compiled by S&P Global, fell to 57.4 from 59.8 in May. The reading remained above both the long-run average of 54.4 and the neutral 50.0 mark.

The slowdown was driven by weaker domestic demand and reduced client interest. New order growth slowed to its weakest pace in more than two-and-a-half years. Hiring activity remained broadly stagnant while business confidence weakened to a five-month low.

"The loss of momentum points to more challenging market conditions and weaker demand, particularly at home. Even so, external demand held up well as overseas sales stayed robust and growth reached a three-month high," said Pranjul Bhandari, chief India economist at HSBC.

Despite the domestic slowdown, new export orders rose at the fastest pace in three months. Demand improved from clients in Australia, Belgium, Canada, Germany, Malaysia, Nepal, Oman, Qatar, Singapore, the UAE and the US. This provided a cushion against the broader deceleration.

Input costs continued to rise at the end of the first fiscal quarter. Survey participants cited higher electricity, food, fuel and transportation prices. However, the rate of inflation eased to a five-month low and remained moderate by historical standards.

Consumer services topped the inflation rankings for both input costs and output charges. Despite recording weaker rates of increase than in May, consumer services firms continued to face the highest cost pressures among all service segments.

Reflecting the broader slowdown, the HSBC India Composite PMI Output Index also fell. It dropped to 57.1 in June from 59.3 in May alongside softer sales volumes, slower job creation and more subdued pricing. Both manufacturing and services sectors recorded weaker expansions.

Private sector companies restricted the extent to which they raised selling prices as cost pressures eased. Rates of input price and output charge inflation retreated to five- and seven-month lows respectively. Manufacturers and service providers downgraded their output forecasts for the coming year.
