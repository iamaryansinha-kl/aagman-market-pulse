India April Inflation at 4%: Bank of Baroda Warns Food, Global Risks

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Bank of Baroda expects India's CPI to settle at 4% in April 2026, with risks tilted to the upside
  • 02/BOB ECI rose 1.1% YoY and 0.3% sequentially in April; TOP arrivals declined 12.8% YoY
  • 03/Core inflation expected at ~3.5%; 16 of 20 ECI items saw price increases during the month

India's Consumer Price Index (CPI) is expected to settle at 4% in April 2026. Bank of Baroda's latest report flagged building pressure in food and global commodity prices.

The bank's Essential Commodities Index (BOB ECI) climbed 1.1% on a year-on-year basis in April. On a sequential basis, the index rose 0.3%, marking its fastest pace since August 2025.

"Against this backdrop, we expect CPI to settle at 4% in Apr'26, with risks tilted to the upside," the report stated. Global energy, metals, and food prices are becoming "stickier" due to ongoing war impact.

Arrival statistics for Tomato, Onion, and Potato (TOP) declined by 12.8% in April 2026 compared to the same period last year. The India Meteorological Department has issued heatwave warnings for May 2026 over Gujarat, Maharashtra, and the East Coast.

"With no sign of a formal peace deal in place, the pass through of higher input prices cannot be ruled out," the Bank of Baroda report highlighted. Risks of imported inflation have increased compared to previous months.

Within the BOB ECI, 16 out of 20 items witnessed an increase in their inflation rates during April. The steepest climbs were in tomatoes, onions, pulses, and edible oils including sunflower, mustard, and soya oil.

However, potato and onion still sit in "favourable double-digit deflation." Most pulses remain on a deflationary path, with the exception of masoor dal.

The bank expects core inflation (excluding food and fuel) at approximately 3.5% in April 2026. Core excluding pan, tobacco, gold, silver, and precious metals is seen at 1.8-1.9%, with upside risk from transport and hospitality sectors due to higher ATF and commercial cylinder prices.
