Intex Eyes 5% AC Price Hike as LPG Costs Surge; Industry Hikes Loom

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Intex Technologies is evaluating a 4-5% hike in air conditioner prices as LPG-linked input costs push manufacturing expenses higher
  • 02/Industry-wide price increases are expected from premium brands LG, Samsung and mid-market players Voltas, Lloyd, Godrej and Kelvinator
  • 03/Intex posted FY26 revenue of ₹450 crore, up 36% YoY, and is targeting ₹750-800 crore by FY27 as consumers downtrade to affordable ACs

Intex Technologies is evaluating a 4-5% price increase on air conditioners. Rising LPG-linked input costs are pushing up manufacturing expenses across the sector.

Keshav Bansal, Director at Intex Technologies, said the company is mulling a 5-7% hike. He noted the firm has absorbed much of the cost impact so far, but some pass-through is now necessary.

Commercial LPG prices are nearing ₹1,000 per cylinder. Suppliers of ceramics, glass and metal coatings are passing on higher fuel costs, while plastics and polymer prices have climbed 10-15%.

Industry sources said premium players like LG Electronics and Samsung Electronics are also likely to adjust prices. Mid-market brands including Voltas, Lloyd, Godrej and Kelvinator are expected to follow with calibrated hikes in the coming weeks.

Nomura reported that Voltas and Daikin India already communicated a 7.5-12% price increase for orders invoiced from April 1, 2026. This takes cumulative AC price hikes to about 17-18% over recent months.

The cost pressure is accelerating a shift in demand toward affordable products. Consumers are moving away from premium brands as price gaps widen, a trend Intex is aiming to capitalise on.

In the 1.5-ton 3-star inverter AC segment, value brands are priced at ₹32,000-₹38,000. Premium players like LG, Samsung and Panasonic sit at ₹44,000-₹55,000, a gap of ₹12,000-₹17,000 or roughly 20-35%.

Intex reported revenue of around ₹450 crore in FY26, up about 36% from ₹330 crore in FY25. The company is targeting ₹750-800 crore by FY27, banking on demand shifts in ACs and smart TVs.

The company operates a network of over 25,000 dealers and reaches more than 18,000 pin codes. This distribution strength gives it a solid foothold in Tier 2 and Tier 3 markets.

"For a large section of consumers, price remains the primary decision driver," Bansal said. "As premium products become more expensive, consumers are willing to shift to brands that offer similar features at a more accessible price point."

Intex remains debt-free and cash flow-positive. This gives it the flexibility to absorb part of the cost pressure while peers may have less room to manoeuvre.
