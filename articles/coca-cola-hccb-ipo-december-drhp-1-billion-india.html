Coca-Cola $1 Billion India IPO: HCCB May File DRHP In December

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Coca-Cola is considering filing draft papers (DRHP) in December for HCCB's IPO that could raise about $1 billion (~Rs 9,000 crore), per an ET report on September 30, 2026
  • 02/The IPO would target a valuation close to $10 billion, making it one of India's biggest consumer IPOs, with Kotak, HDFC Group and Citibank as bankers
  • 03/Timeline is not final — the IPO was earlier aimed at summer 2026 and could slip to 2027 if weak monsoon sales dent peak-season volumes

Coca-Cola is weighing a December filing of draft papers for the IPO of its Indian bottling unit, Hindustan Coca-Cola Beverages (HCCB). The Economic Times reported on September 30, 2026 that the DRHP could go in that month.

The proposed IPO would raise about $1 billion, or roughly Rs 9,000 crore. The target valuation is close to $10 billion.

Kotak, HDFC Group and Citibank have been roped in as bankers, according to earlier reports from January 2026. The broader IPO plan has been corroborated since late 2025.

HCCB is Coca-Cola's largest Indian bottling partner. It runs about 14 manufacturing plants across 12 states and 236 districts, serves over 2 million retail outlets, and employs more than 5,200 people.

Its portfolio spans Coca-Cola, Thums Up, Sprite, Maaza, Kinley, Dasani, Georgia Coffee and Schweppes. HCCB posted FY25 revenue of Rs 12,751.29 crore, down 9% year-on-year.

That decline was attributed to refranchising — the sale of manufacturing plants to independent bottlers such as Moon Beverages, Kandhari Global Beverages and SLMG Beverages.

Coca-Cola remains the majority stakeholder after the Jubilant Bhartia Group bought a 40% stake in parent HCCB Holdings for about Rs 12,500 crore in 2023. The IPO fits a global asset-light strategy focused on brands and concentrate.

HCCB is led by chief executive Hemant Rupani, a former Mondelez president for Southeast Asia, who was appointed in July 2025 replacing Juan Pablo Rodriguez. Earlier reports have said the Bhartia family is expected to take a larger strategic role in the company once Coca-Cola reduces its majority holding.

Timing has shifted before: the IPO was earlier targeted for summer 2026, and January reports warned it could slip to 2027 if rains hurt peak-season beverage sales. The offer structure — fresh issue versus offer for sale — is not yet final.

A December DRHP filing would kick off Sebi's review process, keeping a listing within reach across 2026-27. Earlier reports suggested that most of the sale would come from existing shareholders rather than a large fresh issue, though the final structure will only be known once the draft papers are made public.

Context matters: India's soft drinks market is estimated at around Rs 60,000 crore, and Reliance's Campa Cola is undercutting on price with a 200ml pack at Rs 10. For prospective investors, the pitch is India's beverage growth story. The market has been expanding at 6-8% annually on rising spending capacity, a young population and changing lifestyles, even as per-capita soft-drink consumption remains low by global standards. If it happens, the HCCB IPO would follow record precedents — Hyundai Motor India's $3.3 billion listing in 2024 and LG Electronics India's $1.3 billion IPO in 2025.

An HCCB spokesperson earlier declined to comment on the IPO, saying the company remains focused on operational excellence under its realigned leadership team.
