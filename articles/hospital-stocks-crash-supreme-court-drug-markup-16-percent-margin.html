Hospital Stocks Crash Up to 6% as Supreme Court Flags 10x Drug Markup

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Hospital stocks fell up to 6% on Sep 30 after the Supreme Court flagged steep drug markups in corporate hospitals a day earlier
  • 02/A bench cited a cancer medicine supplied to retailers at Rs 2,700 carrying an MRP of Rs 27,000, a 10x markup, and floated a uniform 16% margin on all drugs
  • 03/No order has been passed; the next hearing is on October 12, and investors are pricing in tighter regulation of hospital pharmacy margins

Hospital stocks crashed up to 6% on September 30, 2026. The selloff followed sharp remarks by the Supreme Court on September 29 over medicine markups in corporate hospitals, where a cancer drug supplied to retailers for Rs 2,700 carried an MRP of Rs 27,000.

"This is carnage. Plain and simple," a bench of Justices Vikram Nath and Sandeep Mehta told Solicitor General Tushar Mehta, appearing for the Centre. The court questioned why trade margins differ across medicines.

The bench suggested a uniform 16% margin on all drugs. It also asked the Centre to examine the practice of corporate hospitals requiring patients to buy medicines from their own chemists or specified pharmacies, noting such practices hurt patients and taxpayers, particularly under government treatment schemes.

Apollo Hospitals fell about 5.5% to around Rs 8,185, with an intraday low of Rs 8,161 on the NSE. The stock trimmed its YTD gains to roughly 15%.

KIMS dropped 5.5-5.7% to around Rs 726-728, leaving YTD gains near 18%. Fortis Healthcare declined 4.8-5.4% to around Rs 778-782 and is down about 14% this year.

Max Healthcare fell as much as 6% before recovering to losses of 4.5-5.2%. It was among the top Nifty 50 losers alongside Apollo, and is down roughly 11% YTD. Four of the top five losers on the Nifty 500 were hospital stocks, with Manipal Health Enterprises down 4.47% and Aster DM Quality Care 4.03%.

The weakness was broad across the sector. Rays of Belief declined 4.39%, Artemis Medicare dropped 3.92%, and HealthCare Global fell 3.77%. Rainbow Children's Medicare slipped 2.26% to Rs 1,337.40, Global Health (Medanta) eased 1.72% to Rs 1,391.15, Indraprastha Medical dropped 3.10% to Rs 343.95, and Park Medi World fell 2.57% to Rs 269.40. Yatharth Hospital and Aashka Hospitals also traded lower, by around 4.4% and 3.7% respectively.

The matter is a Public Interest Litigation on drug price fixation and generic mandates. Petitioners argue that price control orders do not cover patented drugs and that 82% of drugs sit outside pricing orders, with the National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority (NPPA) controlling prices of only about 1,000 drugs. The court also flagged the pricing differential between essential and non-essential drugs, expressing concern that drugs and devices classified as "essential" remain outside the scope of price control orders.

A source told Business Today that the proposed changes, if implemented, may have implications for the margins of corporate hospitals. With no order passed yet, Wednesday's selloff reflects the risk of tighter pharmacy-margin regulation rather than any immediate change to earnings.

No order has been passed by the court. The next hearing is scheduled for October 12, and the market reaction reflects the risk of tighter regulation if the 16% uniform margin proposal were implemented.
