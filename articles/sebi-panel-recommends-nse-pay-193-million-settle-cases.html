SEBI Panel Recommends NSE Pay $193 Million to Settle Co-location Cases

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/A SEBI-appointed panel has recommended NSE pay approximately $193 million to settle longstanding legal cases
  • 02/The amount exceeds the ₹13 billion NSE had set aside, signalling higher-than-expected regulatory liability
  • 03/Settlement could clear the path for NSE's much-delayed IPO, which has been stalled for nearly a decade

A Securities and Exchange Board of India panel has recommended the National Stock Exchange pay ₹16 billion. This translates to approximately $193 million at current exchange rates.

The payment would settle longstanding disputes related to governance lapses and unfair access allegations. These cases have hung over the exchange since the co-location scandal surfaced in 2015.

The recommended amount exceeds NSE's own provision. The exchange had earmarked ₹13 billion for regulatory settlements in its financial accounts.

SEBI is expected to issue a formal demand letter to NSE in the coming weeks. The exchange can either accept the settlement or challenge the quantum before the Securities Appellate Tribunal.

NSE is India's largest stock exchange by turnover. It is also the country's biggest unlisted firm with over 190,000 investors holding shares through various employee and investor schemes.

The exchange recently appointed 20 banks to manage its initial public offering. This is the largest banking syndicate ever assembled for an Indian IPO.

Market sources indicate NSE aims to file its draft red herring prospectus by late May 2026. The settlement of regulatory cases is a prerequisite for listing.
