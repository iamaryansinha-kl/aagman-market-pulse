India Bonds May Reverse Gains as US-Iran Peace Deal Uncertainty Persists

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/The benchmark Indian 6.48% 2035 bond yield is expected to drift in the 6.90%-6.95% range on Thursday after closing at 6.9219% on Wednesday
  • 02/The yield posted its biggest single-day decline in four weeks on Wednesday, but oil price volatility threatens to reverse those gains
  • 03/Market participants await Friday's auction of a new 10-year debt issue worth 340 billion rupees

Indian government bonds are expected to surrender some gains in early Thursday trade. Uncertainty around US-Iran peace negotiations has kept Brent crude above $101 per barrel.

The benchmark Indian 6.48% 2035 bond yield is expected to drift within the 6.90%-6.95% range. It closed at 6.9219% on Wednesday after posting its biggest single-day decline in four weeks.

"The continuous back and forth in the U.S.-Iran issue has been a major pain point rather than the war itself," a trader with a private bank said. The trader expects some of Wednesday's gains to get washed away.

U.S. President Donald Trump said it was "too soon" for face-to-face talks with Tehran. A senior Iranian lawmaker dismissed the U.S. proposal as more of a wish list than reality.

Iran is reviewing a peace proposal that would formally end the war. However, key demands around suspending its nuclear program and reopening the Strait of Hormuz remain unresolved.

The Strait of Hormuz carries about one-fifth of the world's oil and natural gas supply. It has remained mostly shut since the war began on February 28.

Market participants also await a new 10-year debt auction on Friday. New Delhi aims to raise 340 billion rupees through this note, which will replace the existing benchmark paper.

India's overnight index swap rates may rise after heavy receiving interest on Wednesday. The one-year OIS ended at 5.90%, the two-year at 6.10%, and the five-year at 6.4950%, all down 14-18 bps.
