FIIs Pour Over $1 Billion into Indian Stocks, Biggest Weekly Buying Since Early 2025

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

Foreign money is flowing back into India.

Foreign institutional investors poured over $1 billion into Indian stocks last week, marking the biggest weekly buying since early 2025 and signalling a significant shift in sentiment toward the country's equity markets. The inflows come despite global volatility, the rout in emerging market technology stocks, and the ongoing conflict in the Middle East, suggesting that global investors are viewing India as a relative safe haven in a turbulent world.

The $1 billion figure is a sharp reversal. For much of 2026, foreign investors had been net sellers of Indian equities, driven by concerns about valuations, global interest rate trends, and the relative attractiveness of other markets. The turnaround suggests that global fund managers are reassessing India's position in their portfolios, either because valuations have become more attractive after the recent correction or because the global alternatives have become worse.

The buying was broad-based across sectors. Financials, led by HDFC Bank and ICICI Bank, saw the strongest inflows as foreign investors bet on the sector's resilience and growth prospects. Consumer discretionary stocks, including Titan, Avenue Supermarts, and Jubilant FoodWorks, also attracted significant buying, reflecting confidence in India's domestic consumption story. Industrials and capital goods companies benefited from the infrastructure spending theme.

The timing is significant. The inflows coincided with the start of the July-September earnings season, with TCS and other IT companies reporting results that were broadly in line with expectations. Foreign investors may be positioning ahead of the earnings season, betting that Indian companies will deliver better results than their global peers in a challenging macroeconomic environment.

The contrast with emerging market peers is stark. While South Korea and Taiwan have seen massive outflows — with the Kospi hitting circuit breakers and Taiwan's tech stocks getting hammered — India has been a net beneficiary. The divergence reflects India's lower exposure to the semiconductor cycle, its stronger domestic demand, and the perception that the Reserve Bank of India's monetary policy is more supportive than that of other central banks.

The sustainability of the inflows is the key question. One week of strong buying does not make a trend, and foreign investors have been fickle in their India allocation in recent years. If global volatility continues, or if India's earnings season disappoints, the $1 billion could reverse just as quickly. But if the inflows are sustained, they could provide a strong tailwind for the market in the second half of 2026.
