Central Bank of India Q2 Profit Rises 21% to Rs 1,469 Crore

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Net profit rose 21% YoY to Rs 1,469 crore from Rs 1,213 crore.
  • •- Net interest income grew 26% to Rs 4,146 crore.
  • •- Total advances grew 30% to Rs 3.81 lakh crore, led by RAM segment growth of 21%.
  • •- Retail loans rose 24.5% to Rs 1.12 lakh crore.
  • •- Net NPAs improved 17 bps to 0.31% from 0.48%.
  • •- Cost-to-income ratio fell to 58.95% from 62.42% a year earlier.

The PSU bank earnings season has produced its share of wreckage this week — IDBI Bank's profit fell 39 per cent, and treasury losses stalked the sector's other income lines. Central Bank of India reported on Saturday from the other side of that divide. The Mumbai-based state-run lender posted a 21 per cent rise in net profit to Rs 1,469 crore for the September quarter, from Rs 1,213 crore a year earlier, on the back of 30 per cent loan growth and an operating efficiency gain that most public-sector peers have found elusive.

The engine was the lending book, plainly. Total advances expanded 30 per cent year-on-year to Rs 3.81 lakh crore from Rs 2.93 lakh crore, with the retail, agriculture and MSME segment — RAM, in banking shorthand — growing 21 per cent. Retail loans alone rose 24.5 per cent to Rs 1.12 lakh crore, a pace that reflects the sector-wide rotation toward secured and granular lending rather than the large corporate exposures that historically defined PSU bank balance sheets. Net interest income grew 26 per cent to Rs 4,146 crore, comfortably ahead of the system's aggregate trend, and total income rose 15 per cent to Rs 11,737 crore.

Asset quality improved at the margin that matters most. Net NPAs fell 17 basis points year-on-year to 0.31 per cent from 0.48 per cent — a level that would have been implausible for this franchise five years ago, when it sat under the RBI's prompt corrective action framework. The cleanup that followed PCA exit was painful, dilutive and slow, which is precisely why the current numbers carry weight: they suggest the repair has matured from a regulatory project into an operating reality.

The efficiency line is the quarter's sleeper detail. The cost-to-income ratio fell to 58.95 per cent from 62.42 per cent a year earlier — a 347-basis-point improvement that flows almost directly to profitability. PSU banks carry legacy branch networks and staffing structures that make cost discipline structurally difficult; compressing the ratio by this much in one year implies genuine operating leverage from the loan growth rather than accounting optics. It also softens the one weak number in the print: other income fell 6 per cent to Rs 1,380 crore, the treasury-and-fees line that the nine-week government-bond slide has punished across the PSU universe. Central Bank of India evidently felt that drag too, but core earnings were strong enough to absorb it.

Set against the day's broader context, the result rounds out a two-speed PSU bank season. On one side: IDBI's profit collapse on one-off and treasury effects. On the other: Central Bank of India compounding at 21 per cent with improving asset quality. Between them sits the sector's uncomfortable truth — the bond-market slide and FCNR-driven deposit churn are hitting individual banks very differently depending on their treasury exposure and liability mix, and the dispersion is widening even as headline credit growth stays above 16 per cent. The RBI's liquidity-draining operations do not distribute pain evenly, and this quarter's PSU results are the distribution map.

For the franchise itself, the trajectory validates the post-PCA strategy of RAM-led granular growth over corporate share-chasing. The 30 per cent loan growth will attract the same supervisory attention that ICICI's 30 per cent will — but from a smaller base, with a state owner, the scrutiny will come with advice attached. The December quarter will test whether the margin holds as the FCNR deposit window reprices and the system's deposit competition intensifies. For now, Central Bank of India has delivered the kind of quarter that the PSU camp needed: profit growth, quality improvement and efficiency in the same filing, with the treasury drag disclosed rather than disguised.

The strategic significance of this franchise's turnaround is easy to understate precisely because it has been so gradual. Five years ago, Central Bank of India was the archetype of the distressed PSU lender — under PCA restrictions, burdened with legacy bad loans, and regularly cited as a consolidation candidate in a shrinking state-bank universe. A 21 per cent profit print with 0.31 per cent net NPAs and sub-59 per cent cost-to-income is not merely a good quarter; it is evidence that the RBI's cleanup-plus-growth model for weak PSU banks can actually complete its arc. That is a more important signal for bank policy than any single earnings number, because it defines what the framework's success case looks like in operating terms: granular lending, brutal cost discipline, and patience through the repair years.

The stock's re-rating, if it comes, will rest on proving that this quarter is cadence rather than cycle. Loan growth of 30 per cent in a system growing at 16 per cent will mean-revert; the question is where it lands, and whether the margin and efficiency gains hold when it does. But after a week when IDBI's 39 per cent profit collapse reminded investors how uneven the PSU recovery remains, Central Bank of India's filing supplied the counterweight — evidence that the repaired end of the PSU spectrum is now producing numbers that compete with, rather than merely apologise to, the private-bank results dominating the weekend.

WHAT WERE CENTRAL BANK OF INDIA'S Q2 HEADLINES?
Net profit rose 21% to Rs 1,469 crore, NII grew 26% to Rs 4,146 crore, and advances expanded 30% to Rs 3.81 lakh crore.
HOW DID ASSET QUALITY MOVE?
Net NPAs improved 17 bps to 0.31% from 0.48% a year earlier.
WHAT DROVE LOAN GROWTH?
The RAM segment grew 21%, with retail loans up 24.5% to Rs 1.12 lakh crore.
WHAT WAS THE SOFT SPOT?
Other income fell 6% to Rs 1,380 crore, reflecting treasury pressure from the bond-market slide. GLOSSARY: - RAM: Retail, agriculture and MSME lending — the granular segments PSU banks have prioritised since balance-sheet cleanups. - Prompt corrective action: An RBI framework imposing growth restrictions on weak banks; Central Bank of India exited it after years of repair. - Cost-to-income ratio: Operating costs as a share of income — the standard efficiency gauge for banks. - Net NPA: Bad loans minus provisions, as a share of advances.
