Sebi Proposes Relaxing Call Recording Rules for Research Analysts Serving Institutional Investors

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Sebi proposes relaxing mandatory call recording requirements for research analysts when interacting with institutional investors, while keeping rules unchanged for retail clients
  • 02/Research analysts would still maintain emails, SMS and written records for institutional clients; current five-year record retention rule continues for retail investors
  • 03/Consultation paper open for feedback; move aims to reduce compliance burden and create a risk-proportionate regulatory framework

Sebi has proposed making telephone call recording optional for research analysts dealing with institutional investors. The consultation paper was released on Monday, May 18, 2026.

Under current Research Analysts Regulations, 2014, analysts must maintain records of all client communications. This includes emails, SMS records and telephone call recordings.

These records must be preserved for five years. Longer retention applies in cases of disputes or regulatory review.

Sebi now proposes amending these rules for institutional interactions only. Call recordings would no longer be mandatory when dealing with institutional investors.

Emails, written records and SMS communications would still need to be maintained for all clients. The full call recording requirement would remain unchanged for retail investors.

The regulator said institutional investors possess specialized knowledge and stronger due diligence capabilities. They are considered better equipped to assess research inputs independently compared with retail participants.

The proposed definition of "institutional investor" would link directly to Sebi's ICDR Regulations. Affected entities include mutual funds, insurance companies, pension funds, banks and qualified institutional buyers.

Sebi noted that research analysts do not provide personalized investment advice or execute trades. The original recording requirement was designed primarily as an investor protection measure for retail participants.

Industry participants had raised concerns about operational complexity and storage costs. Institutional interactions often involve frequent communication across multiple teams and platforms.

The move forms part of Sebi's broader effort to simplify compliance across market intermediaries. Core investor protection safeguards for retail clients will remain intact.
