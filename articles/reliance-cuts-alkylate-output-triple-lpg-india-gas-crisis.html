Reliance Cuts Alkylate Output to Triple LPG Amid India Gas Crisis

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Reliance Industries cuts alkylate output and diverts feedstock to triple LPG production amid India's worst cooking gas crisis in decades
  • 02/India relies on the Middle East for 90% of LPG imports; Strait of Hormuz closure has choked supply routes since March 2026
  • 03/Reliance stock trades at Rs 1,463; shares are down roughly 17% YTD as collapsing refining margins and export duties squeeze earnings

Reliance Industries Ltd (NSE: RELIANCE) has slashed alkylate production at its Jamnagar refinery. The company is diverting feedstock to ramp up LPG output by more than threefold from pre-war levels.

The move comes as India faces its worst cooking gas crisis in decades. The Strait of Hormuz closure amid the Iran war has choked Middle East supply routes that account for 90% of India's LPG imports.

Reliance runs the world's largest single-location refining complex at Jamnagar. The 704,000 barrels-per-day export-focused facility is now operating its alkylation unit at minimum rates.

In March 2026, the federal government directed all refiners to maximise LPG production. New Delhi has cut industrial gas supplies to protect household cooking fuel needs.

"This has been done to bridge part of the gap in loss of LPG imports from Middle Eastern countries," Reliance said in a statement to Reuters on Monday.

The pivot comes at a cost. Reliance's Q4 FY26 net profit fell 13% year-on-year to Rs 16,971 crore despite 12.9% revenue growth. Material costs surged nearly 20% in the quarter.

Benchmark Singapore Gross Refining Margins have collapsed from $40-45 per barrel in early March to negative territory. The government reinstated windfall taxes of Rs 21.5 per litre on diesel and Rs 29.5 per litre on ATF in late March.

Reliance shares closed at Rs 1,463 on May 4, up 2.26%. The stock remains down roughly 17% year-to-date after hitting a 10-month low of Rs 1,301.40 earlier this month.
