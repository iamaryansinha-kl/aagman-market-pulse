Textile Stocks Drop Up to 5% as Trump Signs New Tariff Proclamation

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

Textile stocks came under pressure on Monday after US President Donald Trump signed a new tariff proclamation. Gokaldas Exports and other apparel-linked stocks fell up to 5%.

The specific details of the new proclamation were not immediately clear. However, any shift in US tariff policy has direct implications for Indian textile exporters. Several listed companies derive a substantial portion of their revenue from the US market.

Which stocks are affected? Gokaldas Exports is among the most US-exposed Indian apparel companies. Pearl Global Industries, KPR Mill, Welspun Living, Arvind, Kitex Garments, Indo Count Industries, and Trident also have significant US revenue exposure.

These companies generate between 30% and 70% of their revenue from the US market, according to previous analyses. Any change in tariff rates or trade policy directly affects their margins and pricing power.

A pattern of tariff whiplash Indian textile stocks have been on a roller coaster through 2025 and 2026. In August 2025, Trump slashed tariffs on Bangladeshi goods from 35% to 20%, triggering a 7% slide in Indian textile stocks. India continued to face a 25% tariff on textile exports to the US at that time.

In February 2026, US agreement to zero tariffs on some Bangladesh clothing goods again hit Indian textile stocks. Gokaldas Exports, KPR Mill, and Arvind all declined.

However, in February 2026, the US Supreme Court rejected certain Trump tariffs, triggering a rally in Indian textile stocks. Gokaldas Exports rose as much as 7%.

The repeated shifts reflect the broader uncertainty in US trade policy. Indian exporters face competition from Bangladesh, Vietnam, and other low-cost producers. Tariff differentials of even a few percentage points can shift sourcing decisions by US retailers.

What it means for exporters Indian apparel exporters operate on thin margins. The industry has been lobbying for RoSCTL (Remission of State and Central Taxes and Levies) incentives to continue beyond September 2026. AGOA (African Growth and Opportunity Act) renewal also remains unresolved.

Gokaldas Exports management said in its Q1 FY27 earnings call that the US tariff under Section 301 places India on an equal footing with competing sourcing destinations, while preserving an advantage over China and Vietnam, both subject to a 12.5% tariff. However, any new proclamation could alter that equation.

The company said it is preparing for different policy and trade scenarios while keeping capacity, costs, and customer ties in place.
