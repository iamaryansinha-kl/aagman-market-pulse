Pharma Set for Mixed Q2 as Domestic Growth Offsets US Pressure

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Growth is coming from domestic formulations, specialty products and emerging markets, not the US.
  • •- Sun Pharma is expected to post high-single-digit revenue growth on specialty traction including Leqselvi, Unloxcyt and Winlevi.
  • •- Aurobindo's revenue growth is led by the Lannett acquisition and Europe; EBITDA seen rising in strong double digits.
  • •- Dr Reddy's faces flat revenue on lower Revlimid contribution and Canadian semaglutide supply constraints.
  • •- Lupin's US sales are likely to decline on Mirabegron and Tolvaptan competition; Cipla faces a high US base from Revlimid.
  • •- Torrent Pharma is expected to deliver the strongest growth, aided by JB Pharma consolidation.

India's pharmaceutical majors report September-quarter results over the coming fortnight, and the preview from the ET Intelligence Group sketches a familiar map: a sector whose centre of gravity has shifted decisively away from the United States. Growth this quarter comes from domestic formulations, specialty products and emerging markets, while pricing pressure, generic competition, elevated raw material costs and high US bases weigh on the companies that once depended on American generics for their earnings engine. The quarter, in short, will be mixed — but the mix itself tells the industry's decade-long story in ninety days of numbers.

Sun Pharmaceutical Industries is expected to deliver high-single-digit revenue growth, powered by its three specialty pillars — Leqselvi, Unloxcyt and Winlevi — with global specialty sales, emerging markets and domestic sales each growing in double digits. EBITDA is seen rising in mid-single digits, though margins may soften slightly on higher research spending and raw material costs. The monitorables are the US ramp of the specialty franchise, where subdued growth remains the known weakness, and the pace at which the specialty products scale outside India; Sun's premium valuation has always rested on that ramp being real, and the quarter will add another data point to the argument.

Aurobindo Pharma's growth engine is the Lannett acquisition, with Europe performing strongly and the US business stable; EBITDA is expected to rise in strong double digits with margins holding. The integration of Lannett and the scale-up of its 6-APA and Penicillin-G facility are the two watch items — the antibiotic supply chain Aurobindo is rebuilding in the US is a long-duration bet that this quarter begins to evidence. Torrent Pharmaceuticals is expected to record the strongest revenue growth among the majors, combining the consolidation of JB Pharmaceuticals with healthy core growth across India and the US. Divi's Laboratories, the custom-synthesis and nutraceuticals specialist, should see revenue driven by its custom synthesis business, iodine-based contrast media and nutraceuticals, with inventory trends and geopolitical demand the swing factors.

The other side of the map carries the US scars. Dr Reddy's Laboratories is expected to report flat revenue: weakness in North America, a lower Revlimid contribution, and semaglutide supply constraints in Canada offsetting growth everywhere else, with EBITDA likely to decline and margins contracting on a weaker product mix. Lupin's revenue should grow in high single digits on India and the Visufarma acquisition, but US sales are expected to decline on competition in Mirabegron and Tolvaptan — two generics where exclusivity windows have closed faster than hoped. Cipla's revenue is expected to be flattish despite double-digit domestic growth, pinned by a high US base from Revlimid and a lost South African tender that has weakened its One Africa business.

For investors, the quarter crystallises a sector rotation that has been running for two years: the market has already re-rated domestic-facing and specialty pharma well above US-generic-heavy names, and this quarter's numbers will test whether that premium is earned. The watch list is short. Can Sun's specialty products grow fast enough outside the US to justify the multiple? Can Aurobindo's Lannett integration deliver the synergies the strong-double-digit EBITDA forecast implies? And do Dr Reddy's and Cipla stabilise their US businesses, or does the competition narrative deepen? The answers arrive with the results — and with them, the next leg of the pharma trade.

The sector context frames the quarter further. Indian pharma has spent two years re-rating, and the September numbers land just as the broader market's leadership rotates toward domestic-demand stories — the same rotation that has carried banks and IT. Pharma's version of that trade is cleaner: domestic formulations grow at double digits with pricing freedom, while the US generics business, once the sector's profit engine, now behaves like a mature commodity operation with shrinking exclusivity windows. The companies that saw this earliest — Sun with specialty, Torrent with domestic consolidation, Divi's with contract research — are the ones the preview expects to lead; the ones that optimised for the old model carry the quarter's baggage. Results begin rolling out this week, and the dispersion between the two groups may be the widest the sector has printed in several quarters — a divergence trade in miniature for investors who believe India's domestic consumption cycle outlasts whatever happens to American drug pricing politics.

One practical note for the coming fortnight: pharma results will land in the same window as the Jio IPO subscription period, competing for the market's attention with far less noise. Historically that combination has favoured quality surprises — thin coverage plus heavy index focus means a strong domestic-formulations print can re-rate a stock faster than the fundamentals alone would suggest, while a US miss may be punished less than in a crowded quarter. The preview's message is therefore double-edged for traders but simple for readers: this quarter, India's drugmakers are not one trade but two, split by geography, and the geography — not the molecule — is what separates the leaders from the laggards.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHICH PHARMA COMPANIES ARE EXPECTED TO DO WELL IN Q2?
Sun Pharma, Aurobindo Pharma, Torrent Pharmaceuticals and Divi's Laboratories are expected to be the stronger performers, driven by specialty products, acquisitions and domestic growth.
WHICH COMPANIES FACE PRESSURE?
Dr Reddy's, Lupin and Cipla face US pricing pressure, generic competition and high bases from products like Revlimid.
WHAT IS DRIVING GROWTH THIS QUARTER?
Domestic formulations, specialty products such as Sun's Leqselvi and Unloxcyt, and emerging markets are the primary growth drivers.
WHAT ARE THE KEY RISKS?
Elevated raw material costs, higher R&D spending, US generic competition, and unresolved USFDA observations at select companies. GLOSSARY: - EBITDA: Earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation — a measure of operating profit. - Revlimid: A multiple-myeloma drug whose generic versions have been a major revenue source for Indian pharma. - Semaglutide: A diabetes and obesity drug; supply constraints refer to manufacturing or regulatory limits on its sale. - USFDA observations: Findings from US drug-regulator inspections that can restrict product approvals.
