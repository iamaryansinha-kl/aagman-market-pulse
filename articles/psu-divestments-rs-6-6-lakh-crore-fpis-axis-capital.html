Rs 6.6 Lakh Crore Potential: Axis Capital Says PSU Divestments Could Bring FPIs Back to India

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Axis Capital estimates a 15% cut in government ownership over three years could raise Rs 6.6 lakh crore in divestment proceeds
  • 02/Government holdings in listed companies have risen to Rs 44 lakh crore, roughly four times pre-pandemic levels
  • 03/India has achieved 70% of its FY27 divestment target in the first five months, largely through the LIC offer for sale

Accelerating government divestments could address India's equity market imbalance by expanding the investable universe and reducing incentives for foreign capital outflows, according to a report by Axis Capital.

The brokerage estimates that a 15% reduction in government ownership over the next three years could raise around Rs 6.6 lakh crore. This would expand market free float, support fiscal consolidation, and strengthen capital flows.

Government holdings in listed companies have risen to Rs 44 lakh crore, roughly four times pre-pandemic levels. These holdings are significantly concentrated in banks and non-banking financial companies.

"In India's case, expensive valuations and prolonged earnings disappointment were the primary drivers of foreign selling," Axis Capital said. Strong domestic inflows allowed FPIs to sell while finding buyers in the secondary market.

Foreign capital outflows have persisted despite strong emerging-market performance. The US attracted a record $735 billion in FPI inflows in calendar year 2025. Flows have increasingly favoured China and Brazil.

Fiscal pressures strengthen the case for divestment. Assuming income-tax growth of 12%, versus the implied 18%, would result in a shortfall of Rs 76,200 crore. Higher subsidy spending is adding to the pressure.

The government has already achieved 70% of its FY27 divestment target in the first five months, largely through the LIC offer for sale. More stake sales are in the pipeline, including IDBI.

Axis Capital expects fiscal strains to persist into FY28 with the implementation of the 8th Pay Commission. It argues that closing the gap with sharp expenditure compression would risk undermining growth.

The report, authored by Prateek Ancha, Chief Economist at Axis Capital, said greater supply of shares could help contain valuations, particularly in mid-cap and small-cap segments.
