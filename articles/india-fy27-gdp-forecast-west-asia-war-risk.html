India FY27 GDP Forecast Cut by West Asia War, Says Finance Ministry

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/The Finance Ministry forecasts India's GDP growth at 7-7.4% for FY2026-27, but warns the West Asia war has clouded the macro outlook.
  • 02/The economy grew 7.6% in FY2025-26, among the strongest in recent years, giving a solid base for the new forecast.
  • 03/The IMF projects 6.5% growth for FY27, while the ministry flags supply shocks, inflation, and monsoon risks ahead.

The Finance Ministry has forecast India's real GDP growth at 7-7.4% for FY2026-27. The projection was made in the ministry's monthly economic review published on April 30, 2026.

The outgoing fiscal year delivered GDP growth of 7.6%. This was one of the strongest performances in recent years and encouraged the upbeat forecast.

However, the ministry said the West Asia war has altered the macro outlook. It warned the conflict has introduced a "supply shock" into the economy.

Rising energy prices are pushing input costs higher across sectors. A wide range of downstream industries depend heavily on petroleum, amplifying the pass-through to consumers.

Demand compression is emerging as a key risk. High prices, rising inflation, and slowing activity could weigh on consumption and investment.

Cost-push inflation may intensify as producers pass on higher costs. This threatens margins and consumer purchasing power across the board.

The ministry also flagged weather-related risks from the El Nino Southern Oscillation. Most rainfall districts may see below-normal monsoon, hitting farm output and food prices.

The IMF projects India's FY27 growth at 6.5%, a 0.1 percentage point upgrade from January. It cited strong carryover momentum and reduced US tariffs from 50% to 10%.

Domestic demand, policy support, and public investment remain buffers. But these may be tested if energy and fertiliser supply uncertainty persists.

WHAT ARE THE KEY RISKS TO INDIA'S GROWTH FORECAST?
KEY RISKS INCLUDE WEST ASIA WAR-RELATED SUPPLY SHOCKS, RISING INFLATION, HIGHER ENERGY COSTS, BELOW-NORMAL MONSOON DUE TO EL NINO, AND POTENTIAL DEMAND COMPRESSION.
