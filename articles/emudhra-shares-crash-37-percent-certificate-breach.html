eMudhra Shares Crash 37% in Two Sessions on Certificate Breach

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Shares of eMudhra fell another 20% on Thursday to an intraday low of Rs 345, extending the two-session decline to around 37%.
  • •- The fall followed an urgent advisory from CERT-In, the government's nodal cybersecurity agency, over a compromise of private keys linked to eight intermediate Certificate Authority certificates.
  • •- MeitY said eMudhra's Digital Signature Certificate and eSign services in India remain unaffected and operate on separate infrastructure.
  • •- The affected global TLS business contributes around 1% of revenue, though the company said reputational risks cannot be quantified.
  • •- An independent researcher found the keys could be reverse-engineered, possibly with AI-assisted techniques, and reported the issue to Apple, Google, Microsoft and Mozilla.

For the second consecutive session, shares of eMudhra were locked in freefall. The digital trust and cybersecurity company fell another 20% on Thursday to an intraday low of Rs 345, extending a two-session decline to around 37% and erasing a large slice of a market capitalisation built on being one of India's few trusted custodians of the internet's plumbing. The trigger is a security incident that touches the one thing a certificate authority cannot afford to lose: confidence that its keys are actually secret.

The sequence began on Wednesday, when CERT-In — the Indian Computer Emergency Response Team, the Centre's nodal cybersecurity agency — issued an urgent advisory following a critical security incident involving eMudhra. The company disclosed that private keys linked to eight intermediate Certificate Authority certificates had been compromised. Those intermediate certificates sit one level below the root in the chain of trust that browsers and operating systems use to verify that a website or piece of software is genuine, and a compromise at that level can, in the worst case, allow an attacker to impersonate legitimate services.

The Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology moved quickly to fence off the incident from the company's domestic business. In a series of posts on X, MeitY said it had taken serious note and was working with stakeholders, and stressed that eMudhra's Digital Signature Certificate and eSign services — regulated by the Controller of Certifying Authorities under the ministry — remain unaffected. The impact on Indian websites and applications, the ministry said, had been very limited and for a very short interval. The reason, eMudhra confirmed, is architectural: the DSC and eSign businesses run on infrastructure entirely separate from the global Transport Layer Security business where the incident occurred, and those Indian services continue to operate normally.

The technical explanation for how the compromise happened is both unusual and unsettling. In a regulatory filing, eMudhra said a flaw in its Key Generation Ceremony caused a wrap/unwrap step to produce a second set of weak keys alongside the original strong keys — and it was the weak keys that were published to the browser root stores. The company noted that these roots and issuing CAs had passed independent audits for two years running and had been present in browser trust lists only since mid-2025. Last weekend, an independent researcher discovered that the weak keys could be reverse-engineered, possibly with AI-assisted techniques, and reported the finding to the major root programs: Apple, Google, Microsoft and Mozilla. eMudhra said it is working directly with potentially affected customers to issue replacement certificates so critical services are not disrupted, and continues its root-cause investigation alongside the root programs.

For investors, the commercial arithmetic and the reputational arithmetic point in opposite directions. The affected global TLS business accounts for only around 1% of eMudhra's revenue, which caps the direct financial hit; the company itself said as much in the filing. But the same filing added that the incident could pose reputational risks the extent of which cannot be quantified — and in the certificate business, reputation is the product. MeitY also clarified the regulatory boundary: the global TLS business operates internationally under a global trust framework subject to independent audits, including WebTrust audits by BDO International, and is not audited by the ministry, while the CCA-regulated domestic business is supervised. That division means the fallout will be judged less by Indian regulators than by the four global root programs, whose decisions on whether to distrust or remove eMudhra's roots would determine the international business's future.

The 37% two-session collapse is therefore pricing something more than one percent of revenue: it is pricing the possibility that the hardest asset for a certificate authority to rebuild — the presumption of competence — has been damaged. For context, trust failures in the certificate authority industry have a long history of being terminal rather than temporary — browsers and operating systems have repeatedly distrusted or removed CAs over key-handling failures, and the industry's fundamental rule is that a certificate authority which cannot guarantee its own keys cannot sell guarantees about anyone else's. Whether eMudhra's case reaches that severity depends on what the root-cause investigation finds and how the four root programs assess the remediation, but the market is not waiting for those verdicts before repricing the stock. The company's response so far has followed the textbook: fast disclosure, separation of the affected business, replacement certificates for customers, and cooperation with root programs and the researcher community. What it cannot yet offer is the one thing that moves the stock back up, which is a completed root-cause analysis demonstrating that the weak-key generation was a one-off procedural failure rather than a systemic weakness in how the company generates trust at all. Until that document exists, every tick in the share price will trade on news from Apple, Google, Microsoft and Mozilla rather than on anything eMudhra says about itself.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHY DID EMUDHRA SHARES FALL 37% IN TWO SESSIONS?
The stock crashed after CERT-In issued an urgent advisory over a security incident in which private keys linked to eight intermediate certificate authority certificates were compromised.
IS EMUDHRA'S DIGITAL SIGNATURE BUSINESS IN INDIA AFFECTED?
No. MeitY and the company both said the CCA-regulated DSC and eSign services run on separate infrastructure and continue to operate normally.
HOW MUCH REVENUE DOES THE AFFECTED GLOBAL TLS BUSINESS CONTRIBUTE?
Around 1% of eMudhra's revenue, though the company said reputational risks from the incident cannot be quantified.
WHO FOUND THE VULNERABILITY?
An independent researcher discovered last weekend that the weak keys could be reverse-engineered, possibly with AI-assisted techniques, and reported it to Apple, Google, Microsoft and Mozilla. GLOSSARY: - Certificate Authority (CA): An organisation that issues digital certificates used to verify the identity of websites and software. - Private key: The secret half of a cryptographic pair; if exposed, impersonation becomes possible. - TLS: Transport Layer Security — the protocol that encrypts connections between browsers and websites. - Root program: The trust frameworks run by browser and OS makers that decide which certificate authorities are trusted by default.
