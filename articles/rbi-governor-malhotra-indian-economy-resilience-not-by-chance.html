Indian Economy's Resilience 'Not by Chance': RBI Governor Malhotra

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra told a Princeton University audience on April 18 that India's economic resilience is "not by chance" but built on strong institutions and policy frameworks
  • 02/India's average annual GDP growth of 6.1% over the last decade outpaced the global 3.2% average and regional peers including China and Indonesia
  • 03/RBI maintains repo rate at 5.25% with a "wait and watch" neutral stance amid West Asia crisis risks; GDP growth for 2026-27 projected at 6.9%

RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra said India's economic resilience is "not by chance." Addressing an audience at Princeton University on April 18, he credited strong institutions and policy frameworks for the country's stability.

India's average annual GDP growth of 6.1 per cent over the last decade outpaced the global average of 3.2 per cent. The performance also exceeded regional peers including China and Indonesia.

Malhotra highlighted the Flexible Inflation Targeting framework adopted in 2016 as a primary anchor for stability. Average headline inflation dropped to 4.7 per cent between 2016 and 2025, from 7.4 per cent in preceding years.

Fiscal consolidation has brought the central government deficit down from 9.2 per cent in 2020-21 to 4.4 per cent in 2025-26. The Direct Benefit Transfer system saved an estimated USD 50 billion by early 2024.

"Our pursuit of financial stability is duly reflected in our broader regulatory framework. We have been willing to sacrifice some short term upside for long term growth," Malhotra said. He defended this approach as prudence, not conservatism.

The RBI currently holds the repo rate at 5.25 per cent with a neutral stance. Malhotra described the approach as "wait and watch" amid uncertainty from the West Asia crisis, which supplies half of India's crude oil imports.

India's digital public infrastructure continues to expand. The UPI processed over 22 billion transactions in March 2026 alone. The RBI is now developing the Unified Lending Interface to provide instant credit access for small farmers and businesses.

Forex reserves stood at USD 697.1 billion as of April 3, 2026. GDP growth for 2026-27 is projected at 6.9 per cent. Risks remain tilted to the downside from the ongoing West Asia conflict and El Nino concerns.
