Poonawalla Fincorp Q2 Profit Soars 407% to Rs 375 Crore, NII Up 81%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Poonawalla Fincorp's Q2FY27 profit after tax jumped 406.8% YoY to Rs 375 crore, against Rs 74 crore in the year-ago quarter
  • 02/Net interest income rose 80.6% to Rs 1,380 crore and operating profit surged 126.6% to Rs 877 crore
  • 03/Gross NPA fell 17 bps QoQ and 39 bps YoY; the stock gained 2.15% on Friday but is down 7.6% in 2026

Poonawalla Fincorp Ltd (NSE: POONAWALLA) reported a 406.8% year-on-year jump in profit after tax to Rs 375 crore for Q2FY27. The NBFC had posted Rs 74 crore in the same quarter last year.

Net interest income, the core measure of lending profitability, rose 80.6% YoY to Rs 1,380 crore. It stood at Rs 764 crore in Q2FY26, per an exchange filing.

Total interest income increased 72.3% to Rs 2,416 crore from Rs 1,402 crore a year earlier. Interest expenses grew at a slower 62.2% to Rs 1,035 crore from Rs 638 crore.

That gap between income growth and funding cost growth powered the bottom line. Operating profit more than doubled, surging 126.6% to Rs 877 crore against Rs 387 crore in Q2FY26.

Costs were well controlled. Operating expenses rose 37.5% to Rs 712 crore from Rs 518 crore, far slower than the 72% growth in total interest income. That is operating leverage showing up in the P&L.

Credit costs increased 30.6% to Rs 376 crore from Rs 288 crore. Even with that rise, profitability expanded far faster than risk costs, which is the trade lenders ultimately get judged on.

Asset quality improved during the quarter. Gross NPA declined 17 basis points sequentially and 39 basis points from a year ago. The company reported no accelerated write-offs in Q2.

On the growth side, the NBFC expanded its gold loan branch network to 550 branches. Sourcing channels include direct selling agents, digital platforms, tele-calling and its in-house sales team.

Management pointed to a continued improvement in return on assets, attributing it to enhanced efficiency and sharper capital allocation.

"Q2 marked a decisive inflection point in establishing a resilient and self-sustaining earnings engine through structural improvements," said Arvind Kapil, MD & CEO.

Kapil pointed to four drivers behind the numbers. These include disciplined AUM expansion, richer total realizations via NIM and fee income optimization reflecting "strong pricing power across core and newly incubated verticals," asset quality refinement that has pulled credit costs lower "from an already formidable baseline" and lifted RoA structurally, and a "rapidly maturing proprietary AI layer" accelerating gains across vectors.

The AI comment is notable for a lender still scaling its book. If the proprietary stack genuinely lowers sourcing and underwriting costs, it compounds the operating leverage already visible in the opex numbers.

The market has not yet bought the story. Shares closed Friday at Rs 447.30 on the NSE, up 2.15%, after trading in a range of Rs 436.90 to Rs 450.90. The previous close was Rs 437.90.

Despite Friday's gain, the stock is down 7.60% so far in 2026. Market capitalisation stands at roughly Rs 39,390 crore at the last traded price.

The results land in a difficult tape for Indian equities. Foreign portfolio investor outflows, higher crude oil prices and a strong dollar have kept pressure on the broader market.

What would change the market's mind: sustained RoA improvement, credit costs staying contained as the book grows, and the gold loan buildout converting into AUM without denting asset quality.

The operating leverage is the structural story in these numbers. Net interest income grew 80.6% while operating expenses rose 37.5% — revenue is growing more than twice as fast as the cost base.

That gap is what pushed operating profit up 126.6% to Rs 877 crore. For an NBFC, operating leverage of this order is the difference between a lender that is merely growing and one that is compounding.

Credit costs rose 30.6% to Rs 376 crore, but they grew far slower than net interest income. Combined with the gross NPA improvement, the quarter's earnings quality looks clean rather than flattered by one-offs.

The gold loan network is the clearest physical bet in the numbers. Expanding to 550 branches puts the company deeper into a segment where collateral is liquid and ticket sizes are small.

Distribution runs on four rails: direct selling agents, digital platforms, tele-calling and the in-house sales team. The multi-channel mix matters because gold loan customers often arrive through a different door than the company's other borrowers.

Chief executive Arvind Kapil's invocation of a proprietary AI layer is the quarter's most forward-looking claim. If the AI stack genuinely accelerates underwriting and collections, the RoA improvement the company cites becomes a run-rate question rather than a one-quarter event.

The stock's 7.6% decline this year shows the market has not yet paid for that story. At a market capitalisation of roughly Rs 39,390 crore, the next few quarters will decide whether this quarter marks the inflection Kapil claims it does.

GLOSSARY: Net interest income (NII): The difference between what a lender earns on its loans and what it pays on its borrowings and deposits. Operating leverage: The effect of revenue growing faster than operating costs, which magnifies profit growth. Gross NPA: The share of a lender's total loans that are non-performing, before accounting for provisions. Credit costs: The money a lender sets aside or loses on bad loans in a given period. Return on assets (RoA): Profit measured against total assets; a key efficiency metric for lenders. Gold loan: A loan advanced against gold jewellery as collateral, typically disbursed within minutes.
