Sensex Crashes 1,312 Points on May 11 as US-Iran Tensions Spike Oil to $105

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/BSE Sensex crashed 1,312.91 points or 1.70% to close at 76,015.28 on Monday, May 11, 2026, as US-Iran peace talks collapsed
  • 02/Brent crude oil surged past $105 per barrel after Trump rejected Iran's response as "totally unacceptable," sending MCX crude up 4.98%
  • 03/Nifty 50 plunged 360.30 points to 23,815.85; rupee crashed 139 paise to 94.90 per dollar as 2,894 stocks declined against 1,459 advancers

Indian stock markets collapsed on Monday, May 11, 2026, as escalating US-Iran tensions and a spike in crude oil prices triggered a broad-based selloff. The BSE Sensex tanked 1,312.91 points, or 1.70 per cent, to settle at 76,015.28 after touching an intraday low of 75,957.40.

The NSE Nifty 50 dropped 360.30 points, or 1.49 per cent, to close at 23,815.85. All sectoral indices ended in the red, with banking and financial stocks bearing the heaviest losses.

US President Donald Trump dismissed Iran's response to the latest American peace proposal as "totally unacceptable." Tehran had proposed relocating part of its enriched uranium stockpile while refusing to dismantle its nuclear infrastructure — a position Washington found insufficient.

Brent crude oil futures surged 4.10 per cent to $105.44 per barrel. WTI crude jumped 4.52 per cent to $99.73. On the MCX, May crude oil futures traded at ₹9,473, up 4.98 per cent from the previous close of ₹9,024.

Market breadth remained deeply negative. Of the 4,538 stocks traded, 1,459 advanced while 2,894 declined and 185 remained unchanged. As many as 198 stocks hit 52-week highs, while 27 touched fresh 52-week lows.

The Indian rupee crashed 139 paise to 94.90 against the US dollar in early trade. It opened at 94.97 before inching up to 94.90, down sharply from its previous close, as oil-driven inflation fears mounted.

Prashant Vashisht, Senior Vice President at ICRA Limited, warned that oil marketing companies are incurring substantial losses. "At crude prices of USD 120–125 per barrel, OMCs incur losses of around Rs 1,000 crore per day on auto fuels and domestic LPG," he said.

BMI research said India's economic growth is likely to decelerate to 6.7 per cent in FY27, from 7.7 per cent in FY26, due to waning momentum and the oil price shock. HSBC expects the RBI to hike rates twice in FY27 amid inflation and El Nino risks.
