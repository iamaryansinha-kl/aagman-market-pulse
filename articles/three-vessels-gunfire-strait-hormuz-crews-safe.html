Three Container Ships Hit By Gunfire In Strait Of Hormuz, All Crews Safe

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/At least three container ships hit by gunfire in Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday, maritime security sources and UKMTO confirm
  • 02/Liberia-flagged vessel sustained bridge damage after being hit by gunfire and rocket-propelled grenades northeast of Oman; crew safe, no environmental impact
  • 03/Second Panama-flagged container vessel fired upon about eight nautical miles west of Iran; not damaged, crew safe

At least three container ships were hit by gunfire in the Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday, maritime security sources and the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations said. All crew members across the three vessels remain safe and there was no fire or environmental impact from the incidents.

A Liberia-flagged container ship sustained damage to its bridge after being hit by gunfire and rocket-propelled grenades northeast of Oman. The UKMTO said the master of the vessel reported being approached by an IRGC gunboat and subsequently fired upon. Maritime security sources said three people were onboard that gunboat. The master of the Greek-operated container ship also reported that no radio contact was made prior to the incident and that the vessel had been initially informed that it had permission to transit the Strait of Hormuz.

The UKMTO later reported that a second container vessel had been fired upon about eight nautical miles west of Iran. The Panama-flagged vessel was not damaged and its crew members are safe. Maritime security sources also confirmed that a third container ship was fired upon about eight nautical miles west of Iran while transiting outbound of the Strait of Hormuz. The Liberia-flagged vessel was not damaged but had stopped in the water, with its crew confirmed safe.

Iran has imposed restrictions on ships using the strait, first in retaliation for the US-Israeli bombardment of the country, and then in response to a US blockade of Iranian ports. The waterway typically handled roughly one-fifth of the world's oil and liquefied natural gas supply before the war began on February 28, 2026.

The incidents add to mounting concerns over the safety of commercial shipping in one of the world's most critical energy transit chokepoints. Maritime operators continue to face heightened risks as geopolitical tensions in the region show no signs of de-escalation, with vessel traffic through the strait severely disrupted since hostilities began.
