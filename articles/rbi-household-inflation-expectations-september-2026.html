RBI Survey: Household Inflation Expectations Hit 10% for Next Year

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Households' median one-year-ahead inflation expectation jumped 60 bps to 10% in September, from 9.4% in July; three-month view rose 70 bps to 9.9%
  • 02/Food prices are the anxiety driver: 84.9% of respondents expect food prices to rise over the next three months, and 61.6% expect them to rise faster
  • 03/Expectations now run 480 bps above RBI's 5.2% FY27 CPI forecast, one day after the MPC hiked the repo rate to 5.50% for the first time since February 2023

India's households expect prices to rise at 10% over the coming year — the median reading from the Reserve Bank of India's September 2026 Inflation Expectations Survey of Households, released on October 8. The one-year-ahead expectation rose 60 basis points to 10% from 9.4% in July; the three-month view climbed 70 basis points to 9.9% from 9.2% in the previous round.

"Households' median inflation expectations for the next three months and one year rose by 70 bps and 60 bps to 9.9 per cent and 10.0 per cent, respectively," the RBI said. "The proportion of respondents anticipating a rise in general inflation has inched up for both the time horizons."

Current inflation perception hardened alongside forward expectations. The median respondent perceives inflation at 8.4%, up 60 basis points from 7.8% in July — households already feel prices rising faster than official prints suggest. Over the one-year horizon, 88.9% expect prices to rise, and 67.4% expect them to climb faster than at present.

On the three-month horizon, 81.9% expect price increases next quarter, up from 80.7% in July. Food is the clear anxiety driver: 84.9% expect food prices to rise over the next three months, and 61.6% expect food inflation to accelerate beyond current levels.

The survey covered 5,987 respondents across 19 major cities between September 10 and 20. It is the RBI's standard household gauge of perceived and expected price changes. It lands a day after the Monetary Policy Committee raised the repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.50% on October 7 — the first hike since February 2023. Even as the central bank tightens, households see no relief ahead.

The gap with the central bank's own view is the real story. RBI forecasts CPI inflation at 5.2% for FY27. Household expectations now run 480 basis points above that forecast, a gap that helps explain why the MPC has taken further rate cuts off the table.

Elevated expectations shape behaviour. Households bracing for 10% inflation push for higher wages, feeding pressure into services prices; some bring forward purchases of cars, appliances and homes. Economists call this feedback the wage-price loop, and unmoored expectations are historically hard to pull back down.

One caveat is worth keeping in view: RBI emphasised the 10% figure is the median expectation of surveyed households, not its official forecast — a perception gauge, not a prediction. The outlook hinges on food; if vegetable and cereal prices cool through the winter season, the gap may narrow. With 67.4% expecting faster price rises a year out, re-anchoring is not yet visible in the data.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT DID THE RBI HOUSEHOLD INFLATION EXPECTATIONS SURVEY SHOW FOR SEPTEMBER 2026?
THE MEDIAN ONE-YEAR-AHEAD INFLATION EXPECTATION JUMPED 60 BASIS POINTS TO 10%, FROM 9.4% IN JULY. THE THREE-MONTH EXPECTATION ROSE 70 BASIS POINTS TO 9.9%, FROM 9.2% IN THE PREVIOUS ROUND.
WHY ARE HOUSEHOLD INFLATION EXPECTATIONS RISING?
FOOD PRICES ARE THE PRIMARY DRIVER. 84.9% OF RESPONDENTS EXPECT FOOD PRICES TO RISE OVER THE NEXT THREE MONTHS, AND 61.6% EXPECT FOOD INFLATION TO ACCELERATE BEYOND CURRENT LEVELS. PERCEPTION OF CURRENT INFLATION ALSO HARDENED, WITH THE MEDIAN READING UP 60 BASIS POINTS TO 8.4% FROM 7.8% IN JULY.
HOW DO THESE EXPECTATIONS COMPARE WITH RBI'S OFFICIAL FORECAST?
HOUSEHOLD EXPECTATIONS OF 10% RUN 480 BASIS POINTS ABOVE RBI'S FY27 CPI FORECAST OF 5.2%. RBI NOTED THE 10% FIGURE IS THE MEDIAN OF SURVEYED HOUSEHOLDS, NOT ITS OFFICIAL FORECAST.
WHAT DOES THE SURVEY MEAN FOR RBI'S RATE STANCE?
THE SURVEY WAS CONDUCTED SEPTEMBER 10-20, JUST BEFORE THE MPC'S OCTOBER 7 RATE HIKE TO 5.50% — THE FIRST INCREASE SINCE FEBRUARY 2023. ELEVATED EXPECTATIONS HELP EXPLAIN WHY THE COMMITTEE HAS SIGNALED FURTHER CUTS ARE OFF THE TABLE AND TIGHTENED POLICY DESPITE COOLING HEADLINE PRINTS IN RECENT MONTHS.
WHO WAS SURVEYED, AND WHAT ELSE DID THE DATA SHOW?
THE RBI SURVEYED 5,987 RESPONDENTS ACROSS 19 MAJOR CITIES BETWEEN SEPTEMBER 10 AND SEPTEMBER 20, 2026. PERCEPTION OF CURRENT INFLATION ROSE 60 BASIS POINTS TO 8.4% FROM 7.8% IN JULY, AND 81.9% OF RESPONDENTS EXPECT PRICES TO INCREASE OVER THE NEXT THREE MONTHS. GLOSSARY: Inflation expectations: What households think inflation will be, measured by RBI surveys. Expectations shape spending, saving and wage demands, which is why the central bank watches them. Median: The middle value in a set of responses — half of households expect inflation above it, half below. Basis point (bps): One-hundredth of a percentage point. A 60 bps rise takes the one-year expectation from 9.4% to 10.0%. Repo rate: The rate at which the RBI lends to banks; the MPC raised it to 5.50% on October 7.
