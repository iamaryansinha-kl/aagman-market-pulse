ITC Block Deal: GQG Sells ₹9,395 Crore Stake; Fidelity, MFs Buy

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/A GQG-linked fund sold 36.51 crore ITC shares worth ₹9,395 crore at ₹257.35 apiece in a Thursday block deal on October 8, 2026
  • 02/Fidelity Advisor Overseas Fund bought ₹3,448 crore; ICICI Prudential MF, SBI MF and Nippon India MF together picked up over ₹4,000 crore
  • 03/ITC is down nearly 30% in 2026 on cigarette-tax worries; HDFC Securities says most required price hikes are now in place

ITC Ltd (NSE: ITC) saw a ₹9,395 crore block deal on Thursday, October 8, 2026. GQG Partners Emerging Markets Equity Fund sold 36.51 crore shares at ₹257.35 apiece across multiple tranches, according to block deal data.

Alliance Witan Plc was the other seller, offloading 16.11 lakh shares worth about ₹41 crore. That took the total block deal value to nearly ₹9,437 crore.

Fidelity Advisor Overseas Fund was the largest buyer. It picked up 13.4 crore shares worth roughly ₹3,448 crore — more than a third of the entire offering. ICICI Prudential Mutual Fund bought 9.4 crore shares worth about ₹2,419 crore.

SBI Mutual Fund purchased 3.76 crore shares valued at around ₹968 crore, while Nippon India Mutual Fund bought shares worth nearly ₹696 crore. Mirae Asset, Edelweiss, Kotak Mahindra and Aditya Birla Sun Life mutual funds also bought, alongside ICICI Prudential Life, Bajaj Life, BNP Paribas Arbitrage, Citigroup Global Markets Singapore, Ghisallo Master Fund and Morgan Stanley Asia Singapore.

The trade lands at a weak point for the stock. ITC shares have fallen nearly 30% in 2026, hit by cigarette taxation concerns, sluggish volume recovery and muted investor appetite for defensive consumer names. The stock slid 4.03% to ₹255 on Thursday, the day of the deal itself.

This is a changing-of-the-guard trade. GQG is the value investor famous for buying Adani stocks at the 2023 crisis lows. Its exit from ITC after a 30% crash — absorbed largely by Indian mutual funds and Fidelity — underlines a foreign-versus-domestic divide on the cigarette-taxation overhang.

HDFC Securities has an "Add" rating with a sum-of-the-parts target of ₹300. It values the cigarettes business at ₹137 per share and the other FMCG business at ₹98 per share. The brokerage said ITC chose phased cigarette price increases after the tax hike, except in the KSFT segment, and its channel checks suggest most required price hikes are now in place.

The hotels business, listed separately as ITC Hotels, remains a different story. Jefferies is constructive, citing strong domestic travel demand, weddings, leisure travel and a gradual business-travel recovery. The brokerage also flagged the shift toward an asset-light model — a target of one-third owned and two-thirds managed hotels versus roughly 40/60 now — since managed properties need less capital and earn higher margins.
