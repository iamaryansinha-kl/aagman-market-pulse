Sensex Jumps 1,100 Points on Sitharaman's LTCG, STT Remarks

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Sensex rose over 1,100 points intraday and Nifty gained more than 350 points after FM Nirmala Sitharaman's comments on capital gains taxes.
  • •- Sitharaman said discussions on long-term capital gains tax and securities transaction tax are ongoing, calling the debate healthy.
  • •- Nifty IT rose 5%, led by HCL Tech up 8% after its quarterly results and TCS up 6% after its earnings last week.
  • •- Broader sentiment stayed mixed: smallcaps slipped 0.7% even as 14 of 16 sectoral indices traded higher.
  • •- The FM also said the RBI may take further measures to curb rupee volatility, aiming to stabilise rather than fix its value.

Indian equities rallied sharply in Tuesday's afternoon session after Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman said discussions around long-term capital gains tax and securities transaction tax were ongoing, giving investors a fresh talking point at a time when markets have been looking for relief after weeks of pressure.

Speaking at the Bloomberg New Economy Forum, Sitharaman said Indian and global investors have been commenting on long-term capital gains tax, and that in India the issue is also linked to the transaction tax in the securities market. "LTCG, a lot of people are commenting on, both Indian and foreign investors," she said. "This debate on STT in market and question of treating all asset classes similarly, there is a lot of debate. It is healthy to have this debate."

The market read the remarks as a sign that the government is alive to investor concerns on capital gains taxation and transaction costs. Soon after, the Sensex rose to an intraday high, gaining over 1,100 points, while the Nifty gained more than 350 points. Sitharaman added that India keeps macroeconomic fundamentals strong and that investors need not worry about fiscal discipline.

The rally had a second engine. The Nifty IT index rose 5%, led by HCL Tech, which jumped 8% after reporting strong quarterly results and better profitability metrics, and Tata Consultancy Services, India's largest IT services company, which gained 6% after its quarterly earnings last week.

"The quarterly numbers reported by Accenture, TCS, and HCLTech indicate that the worst of earnings growth concerns for IT companies might be behind us. This is triggering a value-buying opportunity in the sector," said Dharmesh Kant, head of equity research at Cholamandalam Securities.

IT stocks have had a difficult year, with the Nifty IT index still down 23% year-to-date as investors worried about slowing earnings growth and the impact of artificial intelligence on traditional software services. The latest results from TCS and HCL Tech have eased some of those concerns, at least in the near term, and the buying spilled into the wider sector during Tuesday's session.

The capital gains angle adds policy fuel. Foreign investors have consistently flagged India's LTCG regime and STT as friction points, and the finance minister's framing of an active internal debate revived hopes that the tax structure could be reviewed. Sitharaman's parallel comments on the rupee reinforced the sense of an administration engaged on market issues: she signalled that the RBI may take further measures to curb rupee volatility, clarifying that the aim is to stabilise the currency rather than fix its value.

Still, the day's breadth told a more cautious story. Fourteen of the sixteen major sectoral indices traded higher, but smallcap stocks slipped 0.7% after a 1.4% jump in the previous session, and midcaps rose just 0.4%. Investors were buying largecap names and IT heavyweights rather than taking aggressive risks across the broader market.

Overhangs remain. Reports of rapid technological advances by Chinese chipmaker ChangXin Memory Technologies triggered a sell-off in global chip stocks, with South Korea's tech-heavy Kospi slumping 6% and the broader Asian index outside Japan falling more than 2% — moves that could redirect some flows toward Indian equities. Domestically, investors are watching the Reserve Bank of India's rate path after retail inflation rose to 5.54% in September, a two-year high, with sticky inflation keeping the risk of tight monetary conditions alive. Brent crude above $100 a barrel adds to that concern, particularly after a weak monsoon.

For now, though, the combination of an empathetic-sounding finance minister on transaction taxes and a heavyweight earnings season for IT was enough to power the biggest single-session move on the Sensex in weeks. Whether the LTCG debate translates into an actual policy review will be the next test; until then, the market has priced in the possibility. Notably, Tuesday's advance was a largecap-led move in a market where smallcaps have slipped, a pattern that suggests institutional caution even on the day's strongest rally in weeks.

The remarks also landed in a market primed for a policy olive branch. Domestic research has been building the case for months: SBI Research's Ecowrap this week argued for abolishing LTCG on equities altogether, citing persistent foreign portfolio outflows and the drag transaction taxes impose on capital formation. Foreign investors have sold Indian equities heavily through 2025 and 2026, and every major pre-Budget consultation has featured a request to rationalise capital gains taxation.

Tuesday's move also bucked a nervous macro tape: benchmark bond yields have been grinding to three-year highs, September retail inflation printed at a two-year peak, and Brent crude has held above $100. Against that backdrop, a finance minister acknowledging the tax debate — and pairing it with reassurance on fiscal discipline and the rupee — was enough to trigger a squeeze in the most beaten-down corners of the market, IT stocks above all.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT DID THE FINANCE MINISTER SAY ON LTCG AND STT?
Nirmala Sitharaman said discussions around long-term capital gains tax and securities transaction tax are ongoing, that Indian and foreign investors are commenting on LTCG, and that the debate on treating asset classes similarly is healthy.
HOW DID MARKETS REACT?
The Sensex surged over 1,100 points to an intraday high and the Nifty gained more than 350 points soon after the remarks.
WHICH STOCKS LED THE RALLY?
The Nifty IT index rose 5%, led by HCL Tech up 8% after its quarterly results and TCS up 6%, with Infosys and Wipro also joining the advance.
WHAT DID SITHARAMAN SAY ON THE RUPEE?
She said the RBI may take further measures to curb rupee volatility, clarifying that the objective is to stabilise the currency rather than fix its value. GLOSSARY: - LTCG (Long-Term Capital Gains Tax): Tax on profits from assets held beyond a defined period, including listed equities in India. - STT (Securities Transaction Tax): A tax levied on every purchase and sale of securities on Indian exchanges. - Bloomberg New Economy Forum: A global economic conference where policymakers and business leaders discuss macroeconomic issues. - Intraday high: The highest level an index or stock reaches during a single trading session. - Value buying: Purchasing stocks perceived to be trading below their fundamental worth, typically after a decline.
