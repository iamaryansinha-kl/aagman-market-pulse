FPIs Pour Record Rs 39,640 Crore Into Indian G-Secs In June

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Overseas investors bought a record ₹39,640 crore ($4.2 billion) of Indian G-Secs in June so far, shattering the previous monthly record
  • 02/The surge follows tax exemptions on capital gains and expanded FAR access, including 30-year bonds
  • 03/Bloomberg index inclusion expected by year-end, though elevated US Treasury yields remain a risk

Foreign portfolio investors bought a record ₹39,640 crore of Indian government bonds in June so far. This equals approximately $4.2 billion and is the strongest monthly inflow into debt instruments on record.

The previous monthly record stood at ₹22,005 crore, set in August 2024. June 2026 inflows have nearly doubled that figure with days still remaining in the month.

The surge follows coordinated measures by the government and the Reserve Bank of India. Capital gains on eligible sovereign debt investments were exempted, and the pool of securities under the Fully Accessible Route was expanded.

Overseas investors can now buy even 30-year government bonds. This marks a significant step in opening up India's debt market to global capital.

"RBIs measures have alleviated concerns regarding rupee depreciation, while tax exemptions for FPIs have boosted optimism about India's potential inclusion in Bloomberg's global aggregate index," said Sameer Karyatt, MD and head of trading at DBS Bank.

The tax exemption has raised expectations of India's inclusion in Bloomberg's global aggregate bond index. Market participants see this as a key catalyst for sustained foreign inflows.

The rupee hit a record low of 96.96 per dollar in late May. It has since appreciated to 94.40, supported by the policy measures and FPI flows.

The 10-year benchmark yield has eased 20 basis points since the announcements. It closed at 6.76%, according to CCIL data.

"Because the rupee was so volatile and rapidly depreciating, debt investors were averse. But now there is greater confidence and investors think this is a good opportunity," said Abhishek Upadhyay, senior economist at ICICI Securities PD.

The inflows stand in sharp contrast to FY26, when net FPI inflows into FAR bonds totaled just ₹3,546 crore. June alone has seen more than 11 times that amount.

India's foreign exchange reserves stood at $672 billion as of June 12. The bond inflows are expected to add to these reserves.

However, experts caution against extrapolating June's momentum. Elevated US Treasury yields could limit the relative attractiveness of Indian government debt going forward.
