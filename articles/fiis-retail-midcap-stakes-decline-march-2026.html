FIIs & Retail Raise Stakes in 12 Midcaps, But Stocks Crash Up to 41%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/12 BSE Mid-Cap stocks saw both FII and retail investors raise stakes in the December 2025 quarter
  • 02/11 of these 12 stocks delivered negative returns over 3 months, with Coforge crashing 41% from Rs 1,851 to Rs 1,090
  • 03/Only Bayer CropScience bucked the trend, rising 2%; data sourced from ACE Equity as of March 16, 2026

An analysis of the BSE Mid-Cap Index reveals a striking divergence. Foreign Institutional Investors and retail shareholders both raised stakes in 12 midcap stocks during Q3 FY2026.

The December quarter data shows conviction from two very different investor classes. FIIs typically buy after deep research. Retail investors often follow the smart money.

Yet the broader market punished nearly all of them. 11 of the 12 stocks delivered negative returns over the past three months. Some crashes were severe.

Coforge Ltd (NSE: COFORGE) led the decline, crashing 41%. The stock fell from Rs 1,851 to Rs 1,090. FII holding still rose to 34.53% from 34.09%.

OLA Electric Mobility Ltd (NSE: OLAELEC) dropped 37%, from Rs 37 to Rs 23. Retail holding surged to 21.21% from 17.33%, while FII holding climbed to 4.08% from 3.24%.

ACC Ltd (NSE: ACC) fell 22%, from Rs 1,772 to Rs 1,379. Suzlon Energy Ltd (NSE: SUZLON) matched that decline, dropping from Rs 53 to Rs 41.

Whirlpool of India Ltd (NSE: WHIRLPOOL) declined 10% despite FII holding rising to 11.89% from 11.12%. Retail holding there jumped sharply to 7.00% from 5.37%.

Only Bayer CropScience Ltd (NSE: BAYERCROP) escaped the selling. The stock rose 2% from Rs 4,409 to Rs 4,512. Both FII and retail holdings inched up marginally.

The data, compiled by ACE Equity and reported on March 16, 2026, reflects a volatile phase. Rising global uncertainties and cautious sentiment drove a sharp midcap selloff.
