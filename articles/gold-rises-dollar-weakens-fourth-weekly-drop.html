Gold Rises 1.3% as Dollar Weakens; Fourth Weekly Drop Looms

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Spot gold rises 1.3% to $4,077.64 per ounce as the U.S. dollar weakens following inflation data
  • 02/Prices remain down 2.1% for the week, heading for a fourth consecutive weekly decline
  • 03/Traders cut September rate hike odds to 59%; TD Securities warns energy strength may pressure gold further

Spot gold rose 1.3% to $4,077.64 per ounce by 1:35 p.m. EDT on Friday. U.S. gold futures for August delivery settled 1.2% higher at $4,096.30 per ounce.

The U.S. dollar eased from recent highs after the Federal Reserve's preferred inflation gauge was released on Thursday. The Personal Consumption Expenditures Price Index surged 4.1% in the 12 months through May, matching economists' forecasts in a Reuters poll.

Traders are now pricing in a 59% chance of a U.S. rate hike in September. That is down from an earlier expectation of 64%, according to CME Group's FedWatch Tool.

Higher interest rates reduce the appeal of non-yielding bullion. They tend to boost bond yields and increase returns on interest-bearing assets, making gold less attractive.

"Gold is seeing a modest rebound after coming under selling pressure earlier this week," said Jim Wyckoff, market analyst at American Gold Exchange. Spot gold had hit a seven-month low earlier in the week.

TD Securities said in a note that gold's inverse relationship with higher oil prices and a stronger U.S. dollar means sustained energy market strength could put further downward pressure on the precious metal in the months ahead.

Gold started trading at a premium in India this week for the first time in a month and a half. Demand in China, the world's top consumer, remained subdued.

Among other precious metals, spot silver rose 2.2% to $59.12 per ounce. Platinum gained 2% to $1,632.80 and palladium jumped 2.5% to $1,213.87, though all three were still headed for weekly declines.
