BHEL Shares Extend Rally After Q4 Profit Jumps 2.5x, Brokerages Split On Valuation

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/BHEL Q4FY26 standalone net profit jumps to ₹1,282.68 crore from ₹504.05 crore YoY, a 2.5x increase
  • 02/Stock hits 52-week high of ₹399, extends rally for second session; brokerages divided on valuation at ~51x FY27 earnings
  • 03/Order inflows at ₹75,900 crore in FY26; JM Financial sees ₹60,000-70,000 crore annual inflow visibility for FY27-FY28

Shares of Bharat Heavy Electricals Ltd (NSE: BHEL) extended gains for the second straight session on May 5, 2026. The stock rose nearly 3% in early trade to ₹388.40 from the previous close of ₹377.05.

The rally follows BHEL's strong Q4FY26 earnings reported on May 4, 2026. The stock had surged over 7% on the results day and hit a 52-week high of ₹399 during intraday trade.

BHEL reported standalone net profit of ₹1,282.68 crore for the quarter ended March 2026. This compares with ₹504.05 crore in the same period last year, reflecting a sharp year-on-year jump of over 2.5 times.

For the full financial year FY26, profit after tax climbed to ₹1,577.95 crore from ₹512.97 crore in FY25. The board has recommended a final dividend of ₹1.40 per share.

Consolidated revenue for Q4FY26 stood at ₹12,300 crore, up 37% year-on-year and ahead of estimates, according to JM Financial. Gross margin expanded 100 basis points to 34.8%, while EBITDA margin improved to 14.2% from 9.2% a year ago.

Excluding estimated forex gains and provisions, the adjusted EBITDA margin stood at around 12.6%. This indicates genuine underlying operational improvement beyond one-off items.

Operating cash flow rose sharply to ₹5,800 crore from ₹2,200 crore in the year-ago period. Better working capital management supported the cash generation, according to the company.

BHEL booked orders worth ₹75,900 crore during FY26, taking its total order book to ₹2,40,000 crore. Revenue from the power segment rose to ₹9,509.85 crore in Q4 from ₹6,192.41 crore a year ago.

Brokerages remain sharply divided on the stock. Morgan Stanley maintained its overweight rating and raised its target price to ₹444 from ₹304 earlier, saying the turnaround remains intact.

"Better revenue execution and sustained power margins are key catalysts. Improving macro positioning within India's capex and power cycle," Morgan Stanley said in its report.

JM Financial reiterated its buy rating and raised its target to ₹435 from ₹393. The brokerage cited stronger gross margins, higher interest income from improved cash flows, and sustained annual order inflow visibility of ₹60,000-70,000 crore for FY27 and FY28.

On the bearish side, Kotak Securities maintained a sell rating with a target of ₹140. CLSA kept its underperform rating with a target of ₹282, flagging expensive valuations at around 51x FY27 earnings and weak core gross margins.

JPMorgan also remains bearish with an underweight rating and target of ₹220. The brokerage called the sharp outperformance a good exit opportunity, noting the best of the thermal power plant ordering cycle may already be behind.
