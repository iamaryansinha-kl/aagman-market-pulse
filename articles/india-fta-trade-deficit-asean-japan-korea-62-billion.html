India's Early FTAs Left a $62 Billion Annual Deficit: Trade Gap With ASEAN, Japan, Korea Balloons

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- 01/ India's trade deficit rose 381% with ASEAN, 318% with Japan and 268% with South Korea between 2007-09 and 2023-25.
  • •- 02/ The combined deficit with the three averaged about $62 billion annually over the three years to 2025.
  • •- 03/ Exports to ASEAN reached $38 billion in 2025-26, while imports climbed to nearly $90 billion.
  • •- 04/ Only 20-30% of India's eligible exports use FTA preferences, versus 60-70% for inbound shipments.
  • •- 05/ Trade experts say the fix is reviews focused on non-tariff barriers and utilisation, not abandonment of FTAs.

## One-way traffic

About 15 years after India opened its doors wider to ASEAN, Japan and South Korea through free trade agreements, the traffic has moved decidedly in one direction. Exports to all three have grown — but imports have grown much faster.

According to a GTRI comparison of trade flows in 2007-09 and 2023-25, India's trade deficit rose 381% with ASEAN, 318% with Japan and 268% with South Korea. The deficit with the rest of the world rose 142% over the same period.

Over the three years to 2025, India's combined trade deficit with the three averaged about $62 billion annually.

## The export-import gap

FIEO Director General Ajay Sahai spelled out the asymmetry. Exports to ASEAN rose from about $19 billion in 2008-09 to around $38 billion in 2025-26 — while imports climbed from $26 billion to nearly $90 billion.

With South Korea, exports stand at around $6 billion against imports above $21 billion. With Japan, exports edged up from $5.1 billion in 2010-11 to about $6 billion, while imports rose from $8.6 billion to more than $21 billion.

## Why the deals skewed

India entered its early FTAs with relatively high tariffs; its partners were largely low-tariff economies. India's trade-weighted MFN tariff is around 12.6%, while average tariffs are close to zero in Singapore and below 4% in Japan and Malaysia.

GTRI's Ajay Srivastava illustrates with a pencil: eliminating a 2% tariff abroad gives an Indian exporter a $2 advantage on a $100 sale; eliminating a 30% Indian tariff gives the partner a $30 advantage. The concessions India gave away were simply worth more.

Non-tariff barriers compound the gap. Standards, certification, SPS and TBT measures have constrained effective market access for Indian exporters.

Then there is utilisation. Only around 20-30% of India's eligible exports use FTA preferences, versus 60-70% for exporters shipping into India — partly because where foreign tariffs are already near zero, the paperwork is not worth the saving.

## The domestic side

Some of the repair work sits at home. Indian manufacturers face duties on raw materials like steel and aluminium even when finished products enter at low or zero tariffs under FTAs — an inverted duty structure that penalises value addition. High input, financing, logistics and compliance costs blunt the benefit of zero tariffs abroad.

Notably, Srivastava points out that in 15 years there has been no comprehensive study of what India gained or lost from its FTAs — the first requirement for course correction. FIEO has proposed country- and sector-specific roadmaps and an annual performance dashboard tracking utilisation, market share, MSME participation and domestic value addition.

## FAQ Q: How much has India's trade deficit with ASEAN, Japan and Korea grown? A/01/ By 381%, 318% and 268% respectively between 2007-09 and 2023-25, versus 142% with the rest of the world.

Q: What is the annual deficit with the three? A/02/ About $62 billion on average over the three years to 2025.

Q: Why did the FTAs benefit partners more? A/03/ India's tariffs were far higher, so tariff elimination handed partners bigger price advantages; Indian exporters also face non-tariff barriers and use only 20-30% of available preferences.

Q: Should India abandon these FTAs? A/04/ No — experts advocate reviews targeting non-tariff barriers, rules of origin, inverted duty structures and utilisation.

## KEY TAKEAWAYS - Deficits with ASEAN (+381%), Japan (+318%) and Korea (+268%) dwarf the 142% rise elsewhere. - ASEAN imports hit nearly $90 billion against $38 billion of exports in 2025-26. - Low outbound FTA utilisation (20-30%) and non-tariff barriers are the quiet killers. - Nobody has formally studied what India gained or lost from its FTAs in 15 years.
