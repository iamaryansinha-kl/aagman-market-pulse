NSE Cautions Investors: Overseas ETFs Trade 65-80% Above NAV

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/NSE on Wednesday cautioned investors as some international ETFs traded at premiums of roughly 65%-80% to their underlying NAV at various points in September
  • 02/Indian mutual funds have exhausted regulatory overseas investment limits, blocking fresh ETF units and removing the arbitrage mechanism that normally closes price-NAV gaps
  • 03/ETF price bands will be linked to the previous day's NAV from April 1, 2027; investors buying at steep premiums face the risk of abrupt price falls

The National Stock Exchange on Wednesday cautioned investors against rising risks in international ETFs. Several such funds are trading at significant premiums to the net asset value of their underlying overseas holdings.

The premiums widened sharply in recent weeks. Publicly available market data shows some ETF prices surged even as the NAVs of their underlying overseas holdings remained broadly stable, with certain ETFs trading roughly 65%-80% above underlying value at various points in September.

NSE attributed the divergence to supply constraints and regulatory changes. Indian mutual funds have exhausted their regulatory overseas investment limits, restricting the creation of fresh ETF units. With no fresh units, the arbitrage that normally keeps an ETF's price near its NAV cannot work, so the premium persists.

The demand behind the squeeze is easy to understand. Over the past year, the Nifty 50 fell about 8% and the Sensex about 9%, while the Nasdaq-100 gained roughly 24%. Retail investors have chased that performance gap through global funds, and the flow shows no sign of slowing.

Money has followed in size. Assets in mutual fund schemes investing abroad rose nearly 55% year-on-year to about ₹485 billion in August 2026, AMFI data shows. Investor folios in such schemes are up more than 40%.

From April 1, 2027, ETF price bands will be linked to the previous day's NAV. The change could materially alter how international ETFs trade on the exchange, anchoring daily limits to underlying value. For products currently trading far above NAV, the shift could compress the room for premium-driven price spikes.

The clog in direct channels is already pushing issuers toward alternate routes. On Wednesday, Marcellus-VanEck launched a GIFT City multi-asset route, a move partly driven by the fact that direct overseas channels are congested.

NSE's warning to investors was direct. Those buying at steep premiums may face an abrupt price fall that is entirely unrelated to the performance of the underlying securities.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHY DID NSE CAUTION INVESTORS ABOUT INTERNATIONAL ETFS?
ON WEDNESDAY, OCTOBER 7, 2026, NSE WARNED THAT SEVERAL INTERNATIONAL ETFs ARE TRADING AT SIGNIFICANT PREMIUMS TO THE NAV OF THEIR UNDERLYING OVERSEAS HOLDINGS. PUBLICLY AVAILABLE MARKET DATA SHOWS SOME ETFS TRADED ROUGHLY 65%-80% ABOVE UNDERLYING VALUE AT VARIOUS POINTS IN SEPTEMBER, EVEN AS THE NAVS OF THOSE HOLDINGS REMAINED BROADLY STABLE. NSE SAID INVESTORS BUYING AT SUCH ELEVATED PRICES MAY FACE AN ABRUPT PRICE FALL UNRELATED TO THE UNDERLYING SECURITIES.
WHY ARE INTERNATIONAL ETFS TRADING AT SUCH STEEP PREMIUMS TO NAV?
THE PREMIUM COMES FROM A SUPPLY CONSTRAINT. INDIAN MUTUAL FUNDS HAVE EXHAUSTED THEIR REGULATORY OVERSEAS INVESTMENT LIMITS, WHICH RESTRICTS THE CREATION OF FRESH ETF UNITS. NORMALLY, WHEN AN ETF'S PRICE RISES ABOVE ITS NAV, AUTHORISED PARTICIPANTS CREATE NEW UNITS AND SELL THEM, AN ARBITRAGE THAT KEEPS THE PRICE IN LINE. WITH FRESH UNIT CREATION BLOCKED, THAT ARBITRAGE CANNOT FUNCTION AND THE PREMIUM PERSISTS.
WHAT REGULATORY CHANGE IS COMING ON APRIL 1, 2027?
FROM APRIL 1, 2027, ETF PRICE BANDS WILL BE LINKED TO THE PREVIOUS DAY'S NAV INSTEAD OF THE PREVAILING MARKET PRICE. THIS CHANGE COULD IMPACT HOW INTERNATIONAL ETFs TRADE ON THE EXCHANGE, AS THE DAILY TRADING RANGE WILL BE ANCHORED TO THE FUND'S UNDERLYING VALUE RATHER THAN WHERE THE STOCK LAST TRADED.
HOW BIG IS THE DEMAND FOR OVERSEAS INVESTING BY INDIAN INVESTORS?
ASSETS IN MUTUAL FUND SCHEMES INVESTING ABROAD ROSE NEARLY 55% YEAR-ON-YEAR TO ABOUT ₹485 BILLION IN AUGUST 2026, ACCORDING TO AMFI DATA. INVESTOR FOLIOS IN SUCH SCHEMES ARE UP MORE THAN 40%. THE DEMAND HAS BEEN FUELLED BY A PERFORMANCE GAP: OVER THE PAST YEAR, THE NIFTY 50 FELL ABOUT 8% AND THE SENSEX ABOUT 9%, WHILE THE NASDAQ-100 GAINED ROUGHLY 24%.
WHAT IS THE RISK FOR INVESTORS BUYING THESE ETFS AT STEEP PREMIUMS?
THE RISK IS THAT THE PREMIUM, RATHER THAN THE UNDERLYING INVESTMENTS, BECOMES THE SOURCE OF LOSSES. IF THE PREMIUM COLLAPSES — FOR EXAMPLE, IF OVERSEAS LIMITS ARE RAISED OR UNIT CREATION RESUMES — THE ETF PRICE CAN FALL SHARPLY EVEN WHEN THE UNDERLYING SECURITIES HAVE NOT MOVED. NSE HAS URGED INVESTORS TO EXERCISE CAUTION AND CHECK THE FUND'S NAV BEFORE BUYING, AND TO COMPARE THE MARKET PRICE AGAINST THE INDICATIVE NAV BEFORE PLACING ORDERS. GLOSSARY: ETF (Exchange-Traded Fund): A fund that trades on an exchange like a stock, tracking an index or basket of assets. Its price should stay close to the value of its holdings, or NAV. NAV (Net Asset Value): The per-unit value of a fund's underlying holdings. An ETF trading above NAV is at a premium; buying at a steep premium risks a fall even if the underlying performs well. Premium: The percentage by which an ETF's market price exceeds its NAV. NSE flagged overseas ETFs trading at 65-80% premiums in September. Overseas Investment Limits: Regulatory caps on how much Indian mutual funds can invest abroad. With limits exhausted, fresh ETF units cannot be created, blocking the arbitrage that normally keeps prices aligned with NAV. Price Bands: Exchange-set limits on how much a security's price can move in a session. From April 1, 2027, ETF price bands will be linked to the previous day's NAV.
