Systematix sees India inflation at 6-7%, RBI terminal repo at 6.5-7%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Systematix Institutional Equities estimates India's inflation could reach 6-7% in the coming quarters, well above what the RBI currently acknowledges
  • 02/The brokerage says restoring a real rate of at least 1% needs a terminal repo of 6.5-7%, i.e. 100-150 bps of further hikes from the current 5.50%
  • 03/RBI's own household survey shows inflation expectations at 9.1% in September 2026; the report warns the 10-year bond yield could top 7.5%

India's inflation could surprise on the upside and reach 6-7% in the coming quarters, Systematix Institutional Equities said in a report. The brokerage argues that inflation risks are significantly higher than the Reserve Bank of India acknowledges.

The RBI's household survey shows inflation expectations averaging 9.1% in September 2026, well above headline inflation and still rising. The central bank's inflation diffusion index shows price pressures spreading across a wider range of categories — inflation is becoming broad-based.

Input costs for non-financial companies rose around 40% year-on-year in Q1FY27, even as crude oil prices climbed 45%. Gross margins contracted by 650-800 basis points, as companies passed on only part of the cost increase to consumers.

The report also flagged inconsistencies in the RBI's own narrative. The central bank attributed rising fuel inflation largely to an unfavourable base effect, while simultaneously acknowledging supply-side risks from a deficient southwest monsoon, El Nino conditions and volatile international oil prices.

Government subsidies on food, energy and fertilisers, along with under-recoveries at oil marketing companies, have cushioned the inflationary impact of the West Asia conflict. "As this fiscal support wanes, pipeline inflation is likely to accelerate in the coming months and add to the existing momentum," the report said.

On monetary policy, Systematix views the RBI's 25-basis-point repo rate hike to 5.50% and its shift towards calibrated tightening as the beginning of a new rate-hike cycle. Real interest rates remain negative against elevated inflation expectations.

"Inflation could surprise to the upside and reach 6-7% in the coming quarters. Restoring a real rate of at least 1% would require further hikes and a terminal repo rate of 6.5-7%, or 100-150 bps above the current 5.5%," the report said.

Strong growth gives the RBI room to tighten. Q1 growth came in at 7.8%, prompting the central bank to lift its FY27 GDP projection to 7.1% from 6.7%. It also revised its Q3FY27 inflation projection up to 6% from 5.9% — which Systematix argues still understates the risk.

The read-across for markets is stark. Continued inflation and tightening could hit equity valuations, while input costs and borrowing expenses squeeze corporate earnings. The 10-year government bond yield, already around 7.24% at the time of the report, could rise above 7.5%.

The household data is where Systematix's case is strongest. When the central bank's own survey shows consumers expecting prices to rise 9.1 per cent — well above the official headline number and climbing — the anchor that monetary policy depends on starts to look loose. Inflation expectations that detach from reality are costly to recapture, and they feed directly into wage negotiations and pricing behaviour.

The corporate evidence points the same way. Input costs for non-financial companies jumped about 40 per cent year-on-year in the first quarter of FY27 as crude rose 45 per cent, yet gross margins compressed by 650 to 800 basis points. Companies absorbed most of the shock rather than passing it on. That restraint delays inflation rather than preventing it — every deferred price increase is pipeline pressure waiting for an outlet.

The report also zeroes in on a quiet subsidy buffer. Government support on food, energy and fertilisers, together with under-recoveries at oil marketing companies, has been masking the full inflationary effect of the West Asia conflict. "As this fiscal support wanes, pipeline inflation is likely to accelerate in the coming months and add to the existing momentum," the report warned. In other words, part of today's inflation has been prepaid by the exchequer, and the bill arrives when the support unwinds.

Set against that, the RBI's own posture looks measured to a fault. The central bank raised its FY27 GDP growth projection to 7.1 per cent on the back of a strong 7.8 per cent first quarter, and nudged its Q3FY27 inflation projection up to 6 per cent from 5.9 per cent. Systematix reads even those upward revisions as understating the risk. Its rate math is blunt: with inflation expectations at 9.1 per cent, the 5.5 per cent repo rate implies deeply negative real rates, and restoring a real rate of at least one per cent would require a terminal repo of 6.5 to 7 per cent — another 100 to 150 basis points of tightening from here.

For markets, the report sketches an uncomfortable combination: higher rates pressuring equity valuations while input costs and borrowing expenses squeeze corporate earnings at the same time. Its warning that the benchmark 10-year bond yield — around 7.24 per cent when the report was written — could push above 7.5 per cent looked prescient within hours, as the yield touched 7.37 per cent on Monday. Whether or not the RBI ultimately travels as far as Systematix predicts, the brokerage has staked out one of the clearest roadmaps yet for where India's new tightening cycle could end.

GLOSSARY: Terminal repo rate: The level of the policy rate at which a central bank is expected to stop hiking in a cycle. Real interest rate: The policy rate adjusted for inflation — what borrowers pay and savers earn in purchasing-power terms. Inflation expectations: The rate of price increase households or businesses anticipate, which shapes wage and pricing behaviour. Gross margin: Revenue minus the direct cost of goods sold, expressed as a share of revenue. Inflation diffusion index: A measure of how broadly prices are rising across categories; a rising reading signals broadening pressure.
