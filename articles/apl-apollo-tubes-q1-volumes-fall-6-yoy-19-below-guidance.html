APL Apollo Tubes Q1 Volumes Fall 6% YoY, 19% Below Guidance on Weak Demand

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/APL Apollo Tubes Q1FY27 volumes fall 6% YoY and 19% sequentially to 744,823 tonnes, well below 875,000-tonne guidance
  • 02/Weak domestic demand, Dubai disruptions due to West Asia war, and steel price volatility dragged volumes
  • 03/JM Financial and Nuvama cut FY27/28 estimates; EBITDA per tonne to be closely watched

APL Apollo Tubes Ltd began the new financial year on a sombre note. The structural steel tubes company released its Q1FY27 business update, with volumes falling 6% year-on-year and 19% sequentially to 744,823 tonnes. This was well below the 875,000-tonne guidance given during the Q4FY26 earnings call.

For perspective, Q4FY26 and FY26 volumes had grown 9% year-on-year and 10.6%, respectively. What hurt Q1FY27 volume growth? Dull domestic demand and lower utilisation at its Dubai operations due to the impact of the West Asia war.

Volumes under the flagship APL Apollo brand declined 11% year-on-year and accounted for 76% of total volumes. Roofing products remained steady, while the SG Premium brand posted a sharp 315% year-on-year increase, albeit on a low base. UAE volumes plunged 46% as utilization levels took a hit. During the Q4FY26 earnings call, the management had said its Dubai operations were running at about 40% utilization.

The slowdown had been building for months. During the Q4FY26 earnings call, the management said rising hot-rolled coil (HRC) prices had prompted dealers to delay purchases in anticipation of lower prices, leading to channel de-stocking. Geopolitical disruptions hurt the Dubai business, while elevated steel prices, labour shortages and temporary fuel-related issues affected domestic operations.

"While APL has guided for 15–20% year-on-year volume growth for FY27E, we believe that the weak Q1FY27 performance increases the likelihood of a downward revision to its full-year volume guidance," said JM Financial Institutional Securities in a 1 July report.

Nuvama Institutional Equities added: "The ongoing HRC price correction is likely to keep volumes subdued in Q2FY27 too. Given a volume miss, likely weak Q2FY27 with falling HRC prices and destocking, we are cutting FY27/28/29E EPS by 3%/6%/6% and target multiple to 33x (from 35x)."

Investor attention is now shifting to profitability. Anubhav Gupta, APL's chief strategy officer, said during the Q4FY26 call: "Our focus right now is to protect our profitability and margins when we know that volume prediction becomes challenging in this environment."

When APL reports its Q1FY27 earnings, EBITDA per tonne will be closely watched. JM Financial expects EBITDA per tonne to remain broadly stable at around ₹5,500. For comparison, EBITDA per tonne stood at ₹5,522 in Q4FY26 and ₹5,160 for FY26.

Meanwhile, the management plans to increase structural steel capacity to 8 million tonnes by FY28. The expansion includes new greenfield facilities in Gorakhpur, Siliguri and Malur, along with de-bottlenecking of existing mills. APL is targeting EBITDA growth of 20-25% in FY27, supported by a higher contribution from value-added products. APL's shares are down about 7% so far in 2026.
