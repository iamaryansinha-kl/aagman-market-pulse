Triveni Power Transmission Debuts on BSE, NSE After Demerger

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Triveni Power Transmission Ltd listed 10.48 crore equity shares on the BSE and NSE after its demerger from Triveni Engineering and Industries.
  • •- Eligible shareholders received one TPTL share of face value Rs 2 for every three Triveni Engineering shares held as of the July 22, 2026 record date.
  • •- The NCLT-approved scheme became effective on May 19, 2026, with an appointed date of April 1, 2026.
  • •- The demerged gearbox and power transmission business reported FY26 revenue of about Rs 340 crore with a PBIT margin near 35% and an order book of Rs 467 crore.
  • •- Vice chairman and MD Tarun Sawhney said the listing creates sharper business focus, transparent performance evaluation and independent growth.

Triveni Power Transmission Ltd, the demerged power transmission business of Triveni Engineering and Industries, began trading as an independent listed company this week. The company listed 10.48 crore equity shares on the Bombay Stock Exchange and the National Stock Exchange, completing a restructuring that separates the high-speed gearbox maker from its parent's sugar and distillery operations.

The listing follows a composite scheme of arrangement approved by the National Company Law Tribunal. The scheme became effective on May 19, 2026, with April 1, 2026 fixed as the appointed date from which the businesses were carved apart. Under the arrangement, eligible shareholders of Triveni Engineering received one equity share of Triveni Power Transmission for every three shares they held in the parent company as of the record date of July 22, 2026. The new shares carry a face value of Rs 2 each and were credited automatically to eligible shareholders.

"The demerger and independent listing create a platform for sharper business focus, more transparent performance evaluation and independent growth," said Tarun Sawhney, vice chairman and managing director of TPTL, in the company's listing statement. TPTL manufactures high-speed gearboxes and power transmission equipment for industrial use, and is part of the Triveni Group, which is best known for its sugar business.

The numbers attached to the demerged business give investors their first clean look at what the standalone entity looks like. The power transmission business reported revenue of about Rs 340 crore in FY26, with a profit before interest and tax margin of roughly 35%, according to company disclosures compiled during the demerger process. Its order book stood at Rs 467 crore as of March 31, 2026.

Those economics explain the strategic logic of the split. A gearbox business earning a mid-thirties PBIT margin has a very different valuation profile from a sugar and distillery operation whose earnings swing with cane prices, ethanol policy and commodity cycles. As part of Triveni Engineering's consolidated numbers, that quality was bundled into a holding whose headline revenue was dominated elsewhere. Triveni Engineering reported consolidated revenue of Rs 6,290.5 crore in FY26, up 10.6% year on year, with consolidated debt of Rs 2,148 crore at March 31, 2026 and a long-term credit rating of AA+ stable reaffirmed by ICRA after the scheme approval.

Post-demerger, the two vehicles are cleanly separated. Triveni Engineering and Industries retains the sugar, distillery and allied agri-businesses, along with water and wastewater operations. Triveni Power Transmission takes the high-speed gearing and power transmission franchise, with its own order book, customer relationships and export footprint. Brokerage commentary around the record date put the combined shareholder base at more than 95,000 investors who now hold interests in both listed entities.

When Triveni Engineering traded ex-demerger in July, the stock price adjusted mechanically to reflect the removal of the transmission business, a routine feature of such splits. The listing now lets the market price each business on its own earnings trajectory rather than as a blended conglomerate.

For TPTL, the near-term questions are order inflows and export momentum. The FY26 order book of Rs 467 crore against revenue of about Rs 340 crore gives reasonable revenue visibility, but the business has seen quarters where delayed order finalisation and export shipment timing softened results, as management itself noted in the parent's FY26 earnings commentary. The inquiry pipeline, management said at the time, remained strong.

For Triveni Engineering, the separation simplifies the equity story into a sugar, ethanol and distillery franchise, where policy tailwinds around ethanol blending and distillery volumes have driven recent profitability. The distillery business delivered its highest-ever production and sales volumes in FY26 with revenue of Rs 1,550 crore, supported by lower maize costs and better feedstock availability.

Demerger-led listings have become a familiar pattern in Indian markets as conglomerates move to unlock value through pure-play structures. The Triveni split follows that template: two distinct operating businesses, distinct margin profiles, and now distinct listed vehicles. For shareholders, the immediate task is tracking two price series instead of one. For the companies, it is delivering on the sharper focus that the restructuring was designed to buy.

The listing also completes a process that took longer than initially signalled. Management had guided that TPTL would list by the end of August 2026, with the record date expected within weeks of the scheme's approval; the actual listing came in October after the routine sequence of record date, allotment and exchange approvals. Such delays are common in NCLT-driven demergers and carry no operational implication for either business.

The group context matters too. The Triveni Group already has experience with separately listed engineering businesses, having listed its turbine franchise years earlier, so investors have a reference point for how the group manages focused industrial entities. Whether TPTL earns a valuation re-rating as a pure-play will depend on order inflow conversion and export traction over the coming quarters, not on the listing event itself.

GLOSSARY: - Demerger: The separation of a company's business division into an independent listed entity, with shares distributed to existing shareholders. - Record date: The cutoff date that determines which shareholders are eligible to receive shares under a corporate action. - Appointed date: The accounting effective date from which the demerged business is treated as belonging to the new entity. - PBIT margin: Profit before interest and tax expressed as a percentage of revenue, measuring core operating profitability. - High-speed gearbox: Precision mechanical equipment that transmits and regulates rotational power in industrial machinery. - NCLT: The National Company Law Tribunal, which approves corporate restructuring schemes in India.
