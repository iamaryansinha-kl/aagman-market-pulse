India Oil Trade Deficit to Widen in FY27 as Crude Imports Surge 60%: Crisil

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/India's oil trade deficit is set to widen sharply in FY27 as crude imports cross 300 million tonnes and prices surge
  • 02/Crisil expects Brent crude to average USD 90-95/barrel in FY27, up from USD 70.3 in FY26
  • 03/Current account deficit forecast to rise to 2.2% this fiscal from an estimated 0.8% in FY26

India's oil trade deficit is heading into risky territory. A Crisil report titled "Oil's not well" warns that the deficit will widen significantly in FY27.

India relies on imports for over 85% of its annual crude oil requirement. This heavy dependence leaves the economy exposed to global price swings.

Oil imports have climbed steadily from nearly 190 million tonnes in FY14 to well above 300 million tonnes in FY26. That is a surge of roughly 60% over twelve years.

Meanwhile, exports of refined petroleum products have remained largely flat. They even declined for two straight fiscals from FY24 onward.

Crisil expects Brent crude prices to average between USD 90-95 per barrel in FY27. This compares to an average of USD 70.3 per barrel in FY26.

The report forecasts India's current account deficit to widen to 2.2% of GDP this fiscal. Last fiscal, the deficit stood at a modest 0.8%.

"Consequently, the oil trade deficit in dollar terms rose, despite crude oil prices trending down in that period," Crisil noted. This marks a break from the past pattern where lower prices narrowed the gap.

Pressure on remittances from West Asia is an added concern. Together with a larger oil bill, this threatens India's external balances in the year ahead.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT IS THE CURRENT STATUS OF INDIA'S OIL TRADE DEFICIT?
ACCORDING TO THE CRISIL REPORT, INDIA'S OIL TRADE DEFICIT HAS STARTED WIDENING AGAIN AFTER HISTORICALLY MODERATING DURING PERIODS OF LOWER CRUDE PRICES. THE DEFICIT IS EXPECTED TO WIDEN SHARPLY IN FY27.
HOW MUCH OF INDIA'S CRUDE OIL NEEDS ARE MET THROUGH IMPORTS?
INDIA IMPORTS MORE THAN 85% OF ITS ANNUAL CRUDE OIL REQUIREMENT, LEAVING THE ECONOMY HEAVILY EXPOSED TO GLOBAL PRICE VOLATILITY AND SUPPLY DISRUPTIONS.
WHAT IS THE TREND IN INDIA'S OIL IMPORT VOLUMES?
INDIA'S OIL IMPORTS ROSE FROM NEARLY 190 MILLION TONNES IN FY14 TO WELL ABOVE 300 MILLION TONNES IN FY26. EXPORTS OF REFINED PETROLEUM PRODUCTS, HOWEVER, REMAINED RANGE-BOUND AND DECLINED FOR TWO STRAIGHT FISCALS FROM FY24 ONWARD.
WHAT CRUDE OIL PRICE DOES CRISIL EXPECT IN FY27?
CRISIL EXPECTS BRENT CRUDE PRICES TO AVERAGE BETWEEN USD 90-95 PER BARREL IN FY27, SIGNIFICANTLY HIGHER THAN THE USD 70.3 PER BARREL AVERAGE RECORDED IN FY26.
HOW WILL THIS IMPACT INDIA'S CURRENT ACCOUNT DEFICIT?
CRISIL FORECASTS INDIA'S CURRENT ACCOUNT DEFICIT TO WIDEN TO 2.2% OF GDP IN THE CURRENT FISCAL, UP FROM AN ESTIMATED 0.8% IN FY26. THE WIDENING IS DRIVEN BY A RISING OIL TRADE DEFICIT AND PRESSURE ON REMITTANCES FROM WEST ASIA.
