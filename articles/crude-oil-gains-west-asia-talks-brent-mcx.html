Crude Oil Gains on West Asia Talks: Brent at $102, MCX Up 0.65%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/July Brent crude rises to $102.02, up 0.74%, and June WTI climbs to $95.77, up 0.73% on May 7
  • 02/MCX May crude futures trade at ₹9,076, up 0.65%, as Trump says Iran wants a West Asia agreement
  • 03/US crude inventories fell 2.3 million barrels for week ending May 1, supporting prices further

Crude oil futures traded higher on Thursday morning, May 7, 2026. July Brent oil futures were at $102.02, up 0.74 per cent. June WTI crude futures stood at $95.77, up 0.73 per cent.

On the Multi Commodity Exchange, May crude oil futures traded at ₹9,076. This was up 0.65 per cent from the previous close of ₹9,017. June MCX futures were at ₹8,767, up 0.49 per cent.

US President Donald Trump said he believed Iran wanted an agreement. He posted on Truth Social about ending "Epic Fury" and opening the Hormuz Strait. The comments came as markets tracked West Asia peace talks.

Ebrahim Rezaei, a member of Iran's parliament security committee, dismissed reports of a nearing deal. He called the Axios reports "more of a wish list than a reality." This tempered optimism around an immediate resolution.

The US Energy Information Administration reported a drop in crude inventories. US commercial crude oil inventories fell by 2.3 million barrels for the week ending May 1. Gasoline and distillate stocks also declined.

Total motor gasoline inventories decreased by 2.5 million barrels. They are now 4 per cent below the five-year average. Distillate fuel inventories fell 1.3 million barrels, about 11 per cent below average.

Total petroleum products supplied averaged 20.3 million barrels per day over the past four weeks. This was up 2.6 per cent from the same period last year. Demand data suggests steady US consumption.

Motor gasoline supplied averaged 9 million barrels per day, up 1 per cent year-on-year. Distillate fuel supplied rose 3.5 per cent. Jet fuel product supplied was down 6.2 per cent.
