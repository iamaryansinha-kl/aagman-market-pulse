GST Council May Withdraw IGST Exemption on Gold, Silver Imports

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/The GST Council is likely to consider withdrawing the IGST exemption on gold, silver and platinum imports by banks and nominated agencies at its October 7 meeting
  • 02/Imports of these metals currently attract a 3% IGST, but banks and government-nominated agencies have been exempt since 2017
  • 03/Gold imports rose 3.38% to USD 17.47 billion in April-August 2026-27, while silver imports fell 8.81% to USD 1.74 billion

The GST Council is likely to consider withdrawing the Integrated GST (IGST) exemption on gold, silver and platinum imports by banks and nominated agencies. The proposal is expected to come up at the Council's meeting on October 7, sources said.

The Council, which comprises finance ministers of the Centre and the states, will weigh ending a tax break that has been in place since 2017. The exemption was originally granted to facilitate trade that was then tightly canalised.

Under the current structure, imports of gold, silver and platinum attract a 3% IGST. However, this tax is exempt when the metals are imported by banks and nominated agencies.

These nominated agencies are entities periodically listed by the government for the purpose. The exemption has effectively given this channel a tax advantage over other market participants.

Sources said that after the withdrawal, bullion exchanges and these agencies will be on the same footing in terms of taxation. That would remove a disparity built into the system nearly a decade ago.

Precious metal imports have been a persistent concern for the government. They lead to an outflow of foreign currency and dent India's forex reserves.

For a country that imports the bulk of its gold, even modest changes in import volumes matter for the currency. A rising import bill increases demand for dollars and puts pressure on the rupee.

India is the world's second-largest gold consumer after China. Gold imports are mainly driven by demand from the jewellery industry.

The canalised nature of bullion trade was the original justification for the 2017 exemption. Banks and nominated agencies were the designated importers at the time, and the waiver was meant to keep that channel smooth.

The government has already moved to discourage such imports this year. In May, it hiked the import duty on gold and silver to 15% from 6%.

The duty on platinum was also raised, to 15.4% from 6.4%. Both steps were aimed at curbing non-essential imports.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi has given a clarion call to avoid gold purchases. The appeal comes amid stress on the rupee, which has faced pressure from rising import bills after the Iran-US-Israel war.

The latest trade data shows why the issue remains on the government's radar. Gold imports in April-August 2026-27 grew 3.38% to USD 17.47 billion.

Silver imports moved in the opposite direction during the same period. They declined 8.81% to USD 1.74 billion in April-August 2026-27.

If the Council takes up the proposal as expected, the October 7 meeting will be the first major test of the plan. A decision would align the tax treatment of imports through banks and nominated agencies with the rest of the bullion market.

The proposal is still at the consideration stage. Whether the Council clears it on October 7, and how quickly any change would take effect, will be known only after the meeting.

Market participants will be watching the Council's deliberations closely. Any change would affect how gold, silver and platinum enter the country through the canalised channel.
