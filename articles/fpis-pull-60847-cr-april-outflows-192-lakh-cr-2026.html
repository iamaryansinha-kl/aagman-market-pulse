FPIs Pull ₹60,847 Cr in April; Outflows Hit ₹1.92 Lakh Cr in 2026

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Foreign Portfolio Investors withdrew ₹60,847 crore in April 2026, pushing total outflows to ₹1.92 lakh crore in the first four months
  • 02/The 2026 outflows have already exceeded the full-year 2025 outflow of ₹1.66 lakh crore, according to NSDL data
  • 03/DII buying of around ₹1.7 lakh crore year-to-date and expected Nifty earnings growth of 16% CAGR may provide a cushion

Foreign Portfolio Investors pulled out ₹60,847 crore ($6.5 billion) from Indian equities in April 2026. The relentless selling was driven by escalating geopolitical tensions and global macroeconomic uncertainties.

Total FPI outflows in the first four months of 2026 have surged to ₹1.92 lakh crore. This already exceeds the ₹1.66 lakh crore outflow recorded in the entire calendar year 2025.

FPIs were net sellers in all months of 2026 except February. They withdrew ₹35,962 crore in January, saw an inflow of ₹22,615 crore in February, then reversed sharply with a record ₹1.17 lakh crore outflow in March.

"April began with heavy selling as escalating tensions in the Middle East pushed crude oil prices higher, reviving concerns around global inflation." - Himanshu Srivastava, Principal Manager Research at Morningstar Investment Research India.

Elevated crude oil prices crossing $100 per barrel, a weakening rupee toward ₹92 against the US dollar, and reduced expectations of near-term rate cuts weighed on emerging market sentiment, including India.

"This is a textbook risk-off reaction to escalating US-Iran tensions. India's Nifty valuation of around 21 times price-to-earnings appears expensive amid global uncertainty." - Vaqar Javed Khan, Senior Analyst Fundamental at Angel One.

If the Iran ceasefire holds and WTI crude falls below $90 per barrel, FPI flows could stabilise. Strong domestic institutional investor buying of around ₹1.7 lakh crore year-to-date and an expected Nifty earnings growth of 16% CAGR between FY26 and FY28 provide support.

However, renewed tensions around the Strait of Hormuz or a spike in US 10-year bond yields above 4.5% could trigger further selling pressure. Domestic flows may provide a cushion, but global risks remain.
