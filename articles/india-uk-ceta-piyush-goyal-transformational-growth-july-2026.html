India-UK CETA: Piyush Goyal Pushes Transformational Growth as Deal Takes Effect July 15

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal is in the UK on a 3-day visit to review CETA implementation, which comes into force on July 15, 2026
  • 02/The India-UK bilateral trade deal covers £48 billion in annual trade and aims to liberalize 99% of UK tariffs and 90% of Indian tariffs
  • 03/The Double Contribution Convention takes effect alongside CETA, allowing Indian temporary workers in the UK to save 25% of salary into PF at 8.25% interest for up to 5 years

Commerce and Industry Minister Piyush Goyal is in the UK on a three-day visit. He is reviewing preparedness for the India-UK Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement (CETA), which takes effect on July 15, 2026.

Goyal addressed business leaders at a plenary session in London on Friday. He emphasized that "transformational growth" should be the prime focus of the bilateral partnership.

"Collaborations, cooperations, partnerships will be the right way to come in quickly for both British businesses and Indian businesses," Goyal said. He urged firms to use partnerships to accelerate market entry.

The CETA covers bilateral trade worth £48 billion annually. It is the UK's largest trade deal since Brexit, spanning 30 chapters including standalone provisions on gender, innovation, environment, and labour.

The agreement will liberalize 99% of UK tariffs and 90% of Indian tariffs. It could boost bilateral trade by £25.5 billion per year and add nearly £5 billion to each country's GDP in the long run.

Alongside CETA, the Double Contribution Convention (DCC) also takes effect on July 15. Indian temporary workers in the UK for up to five years can now save 25% of their salary into India's Provident Fund. This combines 12.5% employee contribution and 12.5% employer contribution. The amount earns 8.25% interest tax-free.

Goyal held "meaningful discussions" with UK Business and Trade Secretary Peter Kyle. They explored new opportunities to deepen economic and trade cooperation between the two countries.

The minister also launched a series of reports at the event. These included CII's 'Indian Roots British Soil,' UKIBC's CETA implementation manual, a CareEdge ratings report, and FICCI's bilateral partnership report.

Goyal criticized global rating agencies Fitch, Moody's, and S&P for being "unfair to India." He praised CareEdge for assessing India "objectively" compared to other agencies. He said weaker economies have received better ratings than India for "reasons best known to them."

Goyal exhorted smaller and medium enterprises (SMEs) to participate in trade delegations. He announced 500 such initiatives planned for Indian businesses worldwide.

The minister also offered government funds for a public-private partnership to boost tourism. He emphasized attracting "high-value tourism" and getting global company board meetings to visit India.

"Seeing is believing," Goyal said, underscoring the need to showcase India's evolution to global investors.
