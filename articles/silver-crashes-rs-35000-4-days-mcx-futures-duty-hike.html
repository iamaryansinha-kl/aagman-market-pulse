Silver Crashes Rs 35,000 in 4 Days: MCX Futures Hit by Duty Hike

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/MCX silver futures crashed Rs 35,300 over four sessions after Centre raised import duty to 15% from 9%
  • 02/Prices have corrected nearly 40% from the January record high of Rs 4.39 lakh to around Rs 2.65 lakh
  • 03/Near-term outlook remains cautious; Tata Mutual Fund recommends staggered buying for long-term investors

MCX silver futures crashed Rs 35,300 over four trading sessions. In Monday's session alone, prices slumped Rs 5,643 to Rs 2,65,949 per kg.

The selloff marks a dramatic reversal for the white metal. Silver had surged to a record high of Rs 4.39 lakh in January. In just four months, prices have corrected nearly 40%.

The Centre raised import duty on gold and silver to 15% from 9%. This is expected to weigh on domestic consumption. India remains the world's largest silver importer.

Silver derives substantial demand from industrial applications. It is used in solar panels, semiconductors, batteries, electronics, electric vehicles, and AI infrastructure. This makes it sensitive to global growth expectations.

"Markets began focusing on the potential impact of prolonged elevated oil prices on global growth. That concern affects industrial metals more heavily than pure defensive assets," said Ponmudi R.

MCX silver opened with a sharp gap-down and is holding above Rs 2,73,000. Immediate resistance is seen in the Rs 2,76,000-Rs 2,77,000 zone. A sustained move above could trigger recovery toward Rs 2,81,000-Rs 2,83,000.

On the downside, a breach below Rs 2,71,000 may drag prices back into the Rs 2,68,000-Rs 2,67,000 consolidation band. The near-term outlook remains cautious amid ongoing geopolitical uncertainty.

Tata Mutual Fund noted that slower solar installations and unwinding of long positions have eased supply tightness. However, the fund house maintained that silver remains a compelling long-term structural story.
