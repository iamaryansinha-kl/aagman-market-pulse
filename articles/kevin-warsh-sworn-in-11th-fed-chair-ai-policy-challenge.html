Kevin Warsh Sworn In as 11th Fed Chair Facing AI Policy Challenge

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Kevin Warsh sworn in as 11th Federal Reserve Chair on May 22, 2026, replacing Jerome Powell at the White House
  • 02/AI technology boom is reshaping the economy profoundly; inflation already more than 1 percentage point above the Fed's 2% target
  • 03/Fed Governor Waller calls for dropping easing bias; first FOMC meeting under Warsh scheduled for June 16-17

Kevin Warsh was sworn in as the 11th Federal Reserve Chair on May 22, 2026. President Trump administered the oath at the White House at 11 a.m. ET.

Warsh replaces Jerome Powell, whose term ended amid persistent inflation above the Fed's 2% target. The central bank has missed its inflation goal for more than five years.

An artificial intelligence boom is reshaping the economy in ways Fed officials say could be profound for workers, companies, and consumers. However, it will be hard for Warsh and his colleagues to assess these shifts in real time.

Inflation is already high and potentially heading higher. The economy faces oil prices driven over $100 per barrel by the U.S.-Israeli war with Iran, high import tariffs, and rising utility costs from the AI rollout.

Fed Governor Christopher Waller on Friday called for the central bank to drop its easing bias from the policy outlook. He said a rate cut should be no more likely than a rate increase, opening the door to a possible hike.

Warsh faces a difficult early choice. He must decide whether to raise interest rates to keep inflation from moving further beyond the 2% target, or risk his credibility as an inflation fighter from the outset.

Warsh previously served as Fed Governor from 2006 to 2011. He resigned in opposition to Fed bond-buying programs, and has long criticized the central bank's expanded role in financial markets.

The Fed's next monetary policy meeting is scheduled for June 16-17, 2026. Policymakers will vote on interest rates and submit new economic projections. Warsh will decide whether to reveal his own rate expectations.
