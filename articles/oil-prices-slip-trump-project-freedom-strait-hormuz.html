Oil Prices Slip as Trump Announces U.S. "Project Freedom" for Strait of Hormuz

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Brent crude drops 64 cents (0.59%) to $107.53/barrel on May 4 after Trump announces U.S. "Project Freedom" to guide ships through the Strait of Hormuz
  • 02/WTI crude falls 0.65% to $101.30; markets also weigh OPEC+ output increase of 188,000 barrels per day for June
  • 03/No U.S.-Iran peace deal yet; Barclays says $100/bbl not a new equilibrium and prices could spike to $110 if disruptions persist through May

Brent crude futures fell 64 cents, or 0.59%, to $107.53 a barrel by 2308 GMT on May 4, 2026. Prices had already settled down $2.23 on Friday, May 1.

U.S. President Donald Trump announced "Project Freedom" on Sunday. The operation will guide stranded vessels through the Strait of Hormuz starting Monday.

Trump described talks with Iran as "very positive" in a social media post. U.S. Central Command confirmed over 100 aircraft and 15,000 personnel will support the mission.

The Strait of Hormuz handles roughly 20 million barrels of oil daily. It is the world's most critical energy chokepoint, carrying about one-fifth of global petroleum consumption.

Iran effectively blocked the strait in late February 2026. Brent crude surged from around $60 to an intraday high of $126 on April 30, a 50% spike.

Barclays on May 1 raised its 2026 Brent forecast to $100 from $85. The bank warned that $100 a barrel is not a new equilibrium and prices could reprice toward $110 if disruptions persist.

OPEC+ confirmed a June output increase of 188,000 barrels per day. Seven members will raise targets, marking the third consecutive monthly hike.

Actual OPEC+ supply remains severely restricted. Hormuz disruption has curtailed exports from Saudi Arabia, Iraq, Kuwait and the UAE.
