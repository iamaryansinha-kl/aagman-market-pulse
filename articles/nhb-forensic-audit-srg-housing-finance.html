NHB Orders Forensic Audit of SRG Housing Finance Books

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/NHB issued a request for proposal on October 3 for a forensic audit of a Udaipur-based housing finance company that people familiar with the matter identified as SRG Housing Finance.
  • 02/An earlier inquiry flagged suspected fictitious loans of Rs 300-400 crore, roughly a third of SRG's reported loan book; its AUM stood at Rs 1,076 crore as of June, up 35% from a year earlier.
  • 03/Bids close on October 13; the selected firm gets 10 working days to audit in Udaipur and another 10 working days to submit its report.

The National Housing Bank has launched a forensic investigation into SRG Housing Finance. The audit will establish whether fictitious loans were created in the lender's books, repayment entries were manipulated, and the same loan assets were offered to more than one lender.

NHB issued a request for proposal, or RFP, on October 3. The tender seeks an audit firm for a non-deposit-taking housing finance company based in Udaipur.

The document does not name the company. People familiar with the matter identified the lender under scrutiny as SRG Housing Finance. NHB has yet to appoint an audit firm.

The scope is direct. NHB wants the auditor to "identify and investigate fictitious, dummy, non-genuine or unsupported loan accounts appearing in the books".

The auditor must establish whether the named borrowers exist at all. That requires checking customer identification records, sanctions, disbursements, bank accounts, loan documents, repayment trails and the use of funds.

The probe will also cover the company's systems. The shortlisted audit firm will investigate SRG's loan origination and accounting systems.

Auditors have been asked to inspect access controls and electronic logs. The focus is on the creation, deletion, alteration or backdating of loan accounts, customer records, EMI payments and non-performing asset classifications.

Unusual user activity and system overrides will be examined as well. These checks are designed to catch manual manipulation of entries after the fact.

Pledging practices form another pillar of the investigation. Auditors must establish whether the same borrower, security or receivable was pledged or otherwise represented to multiple lenders.

The audit will also identify any fictitious accounts offered as security for NHB refinance. It will check whether stressed loans were shown as standard assets to lenders.

Files will be verified for accounts where earlier observations had flagged false EMI collection postings. NHB has sought an independent, loan-by-loan assessment of non-performing assets.

That assessment covers two reference dates: March 31, 2025, and March 31, 2026. It includes a review of overdue amounts, reversals, restructuring and subsequent recoveries.

The auditor must quantify the financial impact of any irregularities identified. The findings will shape NHB's next steps on the matter.

The forensic audit follows an earlier inquiry that red-flagged the regulator's exposure to SRG. On September 10, ET reported that the inquiry had identified suspected fictitious loans worth Rs 300-400 crore.

The suspected accounts amounted to roughly a third of SRG's reported loan book. That is a heavy weight for any lender under regulatory review.

SRG had assets under management of Rs 1,076 crore as of June. That was up 35% from a year earlier, according to its investor disclosures.

The timeline is tight. Bids close on October 13, and the selected firm will have 10 working days to conduct the audit in Udaipur and another 10 working days to submit its report.

SRG has denied the allegations. In a September 10 stock exchange filing, the company said an NHB inspection was underway as part of the regulator's routine oversight.

"There has been no creation or maintenance of fictitious accounts by the company, nor has any amount been diverted for any purpose," SRG said in the filing.

NHB and SRG Housing Finance did not respond to ET's queries. The audit is an effort to establish facts; no fraud has been confirmed.
