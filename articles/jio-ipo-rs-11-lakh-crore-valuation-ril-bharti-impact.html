Jio IPO At Rs 11 Lakh Crore: What It Means For RIL And Bharti

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

Reliance Industries is preparing what could become India's largest-ever initial public offering. The planned Rs 30,000 crore Jio Platforms share sale would put a public market price on Mukesh Ambani's digital empire and reset the pecking order in the country's telecom industry.

The scale is historic. If completed as planned, the offering would surpass Hyundai Motor India's Rs 27,800 crore issue from 2024 and mark Reliance Industries' first public offering in three decades.

The valuation debate is where the story gets interesting. Motilal Oswal Financial Services estimates Jio Platforms could command a valuation of around Rs 11 lakh crore, broadly in line with its own assessed value of Rs 11.2 lakh crore.

At that level, Jio would trade at about 12 times its estimated FY28 enterprise value to EBITDA. That compares with roughly 10.3 times for Bharti Airtel's India business at its current market price, excluding Indus Towers.

Nomura's numbers are more conservative. The brokerage estimates Jio's IPO equity value at Rs 9.8 lakh crore to Rs 10.3 lakh crore at the proposed price band, with an enterprise value of Rs 10.9 lakh crore to Rs 11.4 lakh crore once net debt, spectrum liabilities and lease liabilities are added.

Nomura's implied FY28 EV/EBITDA multiple of 10.5 to 10.9 times sits below its own 12-times target multiple, but still above Bharti Airtel's implied valuation.

According to an earlier Bloomberg report, the company is set to price shares at Rs 1,065 to Rs 1,119 apiece, implying a market value of as much as Rs 10.3 lakh crore. At the upper end of the band, the issue could raise about Rs 30,200 crore.

The issue is scheduled to open for subscription on October 21 and close on October 23, with the anchor book opening on October 19 and listing planned for October 28. The details remain subject to change.

The offering is structured entirely as a fresh issue with no offer-for-sale component, meaning all proceeds go to the company. Existing marquee backers are seeking to increase their exposure rather than sell, and Akash and Isha Ambani personally led roadshows across the Americas, Europe and Southeast Asia.

For Reliance Industries, which owns around 66.4 per cent of Jio Platforms, the listing provides a transparent market value for one of its most important growth businesses. At Nomura's valuation range, RIL's Jio stake would be worth about Rs 481 to Rs 506 per RIL share before applying any holding-company discount. After a 20 per cent discount, that falls to roughly Rs 385 to Rs 405 per share.

Nomura estimates the combined impact could cut Jio's value in Reliance's sum-of-the-parts by around Rs 136 per share. But the brokerage argues that RIL's stock has already corrected sharply ahead of the listing and that the current price more than compensates for the potential haircut.

Motilal Oswal goes further. It estimates that RIL's current share price already factors in an 18 to 36 per cent holding-company discount on its Jio stake, depending on the valuation multiple assigned to Reliance Retail. On that reading, the IPO makes the discount transparent rather than destroying value, and could provide a route to unlocking it.

The conventional fear is that a mega IPO diverts telecom allocations from Bharti Airtel to Jio. Motilal Oswal believes those concerns are overstated, pointing out that Jio's initial free float will be only around 3 per cent, while Bharti has a much larger and stickier institutional shareholder base.

The brokerage also argues that Bharti does not deserve a significant discount to Jio. Bharti has stronger free cash flow generation and a higher return on capital employed, and its investments in Indus Towers, Airtel Africa and Nxtra provide additional value on top of the core telecom business.

Nomura also prefers Bharti Airtel, citing its higher proportion of premium subscribers, which helps limit subscriber downgrades and churn when tariffs rise. During the previous tariff increase, Bharti's average revenue per user rose by Rs 34 over three quarters against Jio's Rs 25, and Bharti added 2 per cent subscribers while Jio lost 0.3 per cent.

The tariff question is the real wildcard. Both brokerages expect the case for a hike to strengthen after the Jio IPO and Vodafone Idea's proposed fundraise, with Motilal Oswal building in a smartphone tariff increase of around 15 per cent in December 2026. That single assumption carries much of the sector's earnings math: Motilal expects Jio to deliver an EBITDA compound annual growth rate of around 15 per cent between FY26 and FY29.

The circular logic favours both stocks. A public listing forces Jio to focus on earnings growth, a tariff hike improves industry cash flows, and stronger earnings support a re-rating of both Jio and Bharti. For Reliance Industries, the test is whether public-market visibility unlocks the value of its digital business or exposes a larger-than-expected holding-company discount.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHEN WILL THE JIO PLATFORMS IPO OPEN AND LIST?
THE ISSUE IS SCHEDULED TO OPEN ON OCTOBER 21 AND CLOSE ON OCTOBER 23, 2026, WITH THE ANCHOR BOOK OPENING ON OCTOBER 19 AND LISTING PLANNED FOR OCTOBER 28. DETAILS REMAIN SUBJECT TO CHANGE.
WHAT VALUATION ARE BROKERAGES EXPECTING FOR JIO?
MOTILAL OSWAL ESTIMATES AROUND RS 11 LAKH CRORE, IMPLYING ABOUT 12 TIMES FY28 EV/EBITDA. NOMURA ESTIMATES AN EQUITY VALUE OF RS 9.8-10.3 LAKH CRORE, OR 10.5-10.9 TIMES FY28 EV/EBITDA.
IS THE IPO A FRESH ISSUE OR DOES IT INCLUDE AN OFFER FOR SALE?
IT IS EXPECTED TO BE AN ENTIRELY FRESH ISSUE OF AROUND RS 30,000 CRORE WITH NO OFFER-FOR-SALE COMPONENT, MEANING ALL PROCEEDS GO TO THE COMPANY.
WHAT DOES THE IPO MEAN FOR RELIANCE INDUSTRIES SHAREHOLDERS?
AT NOMURA'S VALUATION, RIL'S 66.4 PER CENT STAKE IS WORTH ABOUT RS 481-506 PER RIL SHARE BEFORE A HOLDING-COMPANY DISCOUNT, OR RS 385-405 AFTER 20 PER CENT. BOTH BROKERAGES ARGUE THE RIL SHARE PRICE ALREADY REFLECTS SUCH A DISCOUNT.
WHY DO BROKERAGES STILL PREFER BHARTI AIRTEL?
BHARTI HAS STRONGER FREE CASH FLOW, HIGHER RETURN ON CAPITAL EMPLOYED AND MORE PREMIUM SUBSCRIBERS. WITH JIO'S FREE FLOAT AT ONLY AROUND 3 PER CENT, BOTH MOTILAL OSWAL AND NOMURA SEE LIMITED THREAT TO BHARTI'S INSTITUTIONAL OWNERSHIP AND EXPECT A TARIFF HIKE TO LIFT BOTH STOCKS. GLOSSARY: EV/EBITDA: Enterprise value relative to earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation; a standard valuation multiple for telecom companies. Holding-company discount: The gap between the market value of a parent company and the value of its stakes in subsidiaries, reflecting complexity and tax friction. Free float: The portion of a listed company's shares actually available for public trading. Sum-of-the-parts: A valuation method that prices each of a conglomerate's businesses separately and adds them together. Anchor book: The portion of an IPO allocated to institutional investors a day or two before the public offering opens.
