Domestic Prop Trading Firms Face Higher Funding Costs Under RBI's New Rules

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

Proprietary trading firms are feeling the heat from new regulations.

Domestic prop trading firms are facing higher funding costs under new Reserve Bank of India rules. The regulatory changes tighten access to leverage and increase capital requirements for firms that trade with their own money rather than client funds.

The RBI's new framework requires prop trading firms to maintain higher capital buffers. The rules also limit the extent to which firms can borrow from banks and financial institutions to fund their trading positions. Higher capital requirements mean less leverage and lower returns on equity.

Funding costs are rising as a direct result. Banks are now required to apply stricter risk weights to lending to prop trading firms, which translates into higher interest rates. Some firms have reported a 100-200 basis point increase in borrowing costs.

The regulatory intent is to reduce systemic risk. Prop trading firms have grown rapidly in India, with many using significant leverage to amplify returns. The RBI is concerned that a market downturn could trigger cascading losses if firms are over-leveraged.

Smaller firms are more vulnerable. Large proprietary trading houses with deeper capital bases can absorb the higher costs. Smaller and mid-sized firms may be forced to scale back operations or shut down if they cannot access affordable funding.

The rules also affect market liquidity. Prop trading firms provide significant liquidity to Indian equity and derivatives markets. If higher costs force some firms to reduce activity, bid-ask spreads could widen and market depth could decline.
