NSE Exit Halves Activity in Unlisted Share Market

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Average daily client purchases of unlisted shares have nearly halved across platforms since NSE listed last month.
  • •- NSE accounted for an estimated 50-60% of trading volumes in the unlisted over-the-counter market.
  • •- Demand for NSE shares had intensified for years ahead of its September IPO, with millions of shares changing hands daily.
  • •- Metropolitan Stock Exchange of India is now the most actively bought unlisted stock, but volumes remain far below NSE levels.
  • •- Other names attracting interest include API Holdings, Oravel Stays, Hindustan Power Exchange and PPFAS.
  • •- Brokers say the shift is cyclical: new candidates eventually replace a listed giant.

When the National Stock Exchange of India listed on the bourse it owns last month, it took its shareholders' best trading opportunity with it. Trading activity in India's unlisted share market has dropped sharply since NSE's stock market debut, with the value of average daily client purchases nearly halving across various platforms, according to brokers who track the over-the-counter market. The arithmetic is simple and brutal: NSE had accounted for an estimated 50-60% of all unlisted trading volumes, so when its shares graduated to the regulated market, the unlisted market lost its gravitational centre in a single stroke.

The hole is a tribute to how large the phenomenon had become. For over a decade, NSE was among the most sought-after names in the unlisted market, and for the past two years it dominated the space outright, with demand picking up further ahead of its much-awaited IPO in the third week of September. The mechanics fed the frenzy: buying NSE shares unlisted once took months to complete, but the IPO run-up streamlined the process and millions of shares changed hands daily. Retail and HNI buyers who had treated pre-IPO NSE stock as a one-way bet — an exchange monopoly about to be repriced by public listing — generated turnover that brokers across the ecosystem lived on. That flow has now stopped, and the platforms that intermediated it are confronting a market roughly half its former size.

Umesh Paliwal, co-founder of Unlisted Zone, frames the arithmetic plainly: with NSE gone, activity was bound to slow sharply given its share of volumes. But he and other brokers argue the market is a cycle rather than a collapse. "Whenever a major unlisted company gets listed, some of the liquidity and investor attention naturally shifts, but another set of companies tends to emerge and take its place," said Manan Doshi, co-founder of Unlisted Arena. The emerging set is already visible: the Metropolitan Stock Exchange of India has become the most actively bought unlisted stock — an exchange, notably, rather than a technology or consumer name, suggesting that the buyer base NSE cultivated still wants exchange economics. Transline Technologies, Quality Enviro Engineers and Sun Drops Energia are also attracting interest, while Hitesh Dharawat of Dharawat Securities points to API Holdings (the Pharmeasy parent), Oravel Stays (OYO's parent) and Hindustan Power Exchange as the segment's other popular names. Newer interest is building in PPFAS, GFCL EV, MT&T Rental, Prisma AI, Goodluck Defence and Vervesemi.

The sales pitch, however, has changed. "NSE practically sold itself, since every investor understood an exchange's business model and its dominance," Paliwal noted. "With other companies, we'll need to explain the business, the growth story, and why it's worth owning before the IPO." That sentence captures the unlisted market's real economy: it is less a securities market than a pre-IPO research distribution channel, where liquidity exists because buyers believe they are front-running a listing. A stock that requires explanation trades at a discount to one that requires none, and the current crop of successors — MSEI excepted — all require explanation. The broker's job, Paliwal concedes, will take more effort, but he insists the opportunity remains.

There is also a structural question hanging over the space that no successor stock can answer. The unlisted market operates over the counter, beyond the regulatory perimeter that governs listed securities — no mandatory disclosure, no exchange surveillance, no settlement guarantee — and its volumes have long drawn quiet concern from regulators even as brokers argue they serve a legitimate price-discovery function for pre-IPO companies. A post-NSE contraction removes some of that attention pressure temporarily, but it also removes the market's most transparent, best-understood name, leaving a smaller ecosystem of harder-to-understand stocks. Investors who came in for NSE and stayed for everything else are, at the margin, less sophisticated than the clientele the market built its processes around.

Whether the cycle thesis or the gravity thesis prevails will be visible in platform volumes over the coming quarters. The optimists' case rests on a pipeline of plausible listees — the unlisted watchlist now reads like a directory of India's next tier of IPO candidates — and on retail investors' demonstrated appetite for pre-listing exposure after the NSE payoff. The sceptics' case is arithmetic: no name in the current crop carries NSE's brand recognition, franchise economics or near-certain listing probability, and half-sized volumes may be the new baseline rather than a trough. Doshi's formulation splits the difference with a broker's natural confidence: near-term moderation, yes — but the market, he says, is already searching for the next set of potential opportunities.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
HOW MUCH HAS UNLISTED MARKET ACTIVITY FALLEN?
The value of average daily client purchases has nearly halved across platforms since NSE's listing last month, brokers said.
WHY DID NSE MATTER SO MUCH TO THIS MARKET?
NSE accounted for an estimated 50-60% of unlisted trading volumes, and demand for its shares had built for over a decade before its September IPO.
WHICH STOCKS ARE IN DEMAND NOW?
Metropolitan Stock Exchange of India is the most actively bought, followed by names like API Holdings, Oravel Stays, Hindustan Power Exchange, Transline Technologies, PPFAS and GFCL EV.
IS THE MARKET EXPECTED TO RECOVER?
Brokers describe the shift as cyclical, arguing that new pre-IPO candidates will eventually replace NSE's volumes, though none currently matches its scale. GLOSSARY: - Unlisted share market: Over-the-counter trading in shares of companies not yet listed on a stock exchange. - Pre-IPO stock: Shares of a company bought before its initial public offering, typically at a discount. - Metropolitan Stock Exchange: India's other national stock exchange, still unlisted. - Liquidity: The ease with which an asset can be bought or sold without moving its price.
