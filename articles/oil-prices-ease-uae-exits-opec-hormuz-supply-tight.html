Oil Prices Ease to $111 as UAE Exits OPEC; Hormuz Keeps Supply Tight

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Brent crude futures for June dipped 1% to $111.25 a barrel on April 29, 2026, after climbing for seven straight sessions
  • 02/UAE announced exit from OPEC and OPEC+ effective May 1, but Strait of Hormuz blockade limits immediate supply relief
  • 03/Only 7 ships crossed Hormuz in the past day versus 125-140 normally; World Bank forecasts 24% energy price surge in 2026

Brent crude futures for June dipped 1% to $111.25 a barrel on April 29, 2026. The contract had climbed for the previous seven sessions.

The June Brent contract expires on April 30, 2026. The more active July contract traded at $104.12, down 28 cents.

The United Arab Emirates announced it will exit OPEC and OPEC+ effective May 1, 2026. Energy Minister Suhail Al Mazrouei called it a strategic choice based on national interest.

The UAE is OPEC's third-largest producer with capacity of about 4.8 million barrels per day. Its output has dropped 44% since Hormuz disruptions began, falling from 3.4 million to 1.9 million barrels daily.

"In normal times, this would have been very bearish news for the oil market," said John Kilduff, partner at Again Capital. "But with the Strait of Hormuz effectively closed, there's nowhere for that supply to go."

Between 125 and 140 ships usually cross the Strait of Hormuz daily. Only seven have done so in the past day, and none carried oil bound for global markets, per Kpler and SynMax data.

The World Bank forecasts energy prices will surge 24% in 2026 to their highest level since Russia's invasion of Ukraine. Brent has soared from below $60 at the start of the year to as high as $119.50 during the conflict.

Iran offered to reopen Hormuz if the U.S. lifts its blockade and ends the war, postponing nuclear talks. President Trump rejected the proposal, keeping the conflict deadlocked and supply risks elevated.
