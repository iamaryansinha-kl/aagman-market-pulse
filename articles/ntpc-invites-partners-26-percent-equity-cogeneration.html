NTPC Invites Partners for 26% Equity in Co-Generation Projects

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/NTPC Limited has invited Expressions of Interest from commercial and industrial entities willing to invest 26 per cent equity in co-generation projects, the state-run power company said on Sunday
  • 02/The plants will supply both steam and electricity to industrial users, operating alongside integrated power supply frameworks that combine coal, renewable energy and Battery Energy Storage Systems, marking a shift away from one-off supply contracts
  • 03/NTPC operates over 91 GW of installed capacity with more than 35 GW under active construction, and has set a corporate target of 149 GW of total generation by 2032 including 60 GW from renewables

NTPC Limited (NSE: NTPC) has invited Expressions of Interest from commercial and industrial entities. The state-run power company is seeking partners to hold 26 per cent equity in co-generation projects supplying steam and electricity to industrial users.

Co-generation plants produce both steam and electricity for an industrial user through a single integrated system. Generating the two together is more energy-efficient than operating separate facilities for each output.

"With industries increasingly focusing on energy security, cost optimisation and sustainability, co-generation offers an opportunity to enhance overall energy efficiency by producing steam and electricity through an integrated system," NTPC said on Sunday.

The projects will operate alongside integrated power supply frameworks that combine coal, renewable energy, and Battery Energy Storage Systems. "The proposed initiative seeks to address the diverse requirements of industries with reliable, economical and sustainable energy solutions through long-term power supply arrangements," the company added.

The initiative marks an effort by the power major to expand beyond traditional supply contracts into captive and cooperative generation with heavy industrial consumers. For industries, the draw is energy security and decarbonisation; for NTPC, it is long-term anchored demand.

"This initiative reflects NTPC's commitment to expanding its energy solutions portfolio and strengthening partnerships with industries," the company noted. "By leveraging its expertise in power generation and exploring innovative energy supply models, NTPC aims to support industrial growth while contributing to energy efficiency, sustainability and energy transition objectives."

NTPC operates over 91 GW of installed generation capacity, with a further 35 GW-plus under active construction. Its corporate target is 149 GW of total capacity by 2032, with 60 GW planned from renewable sources alone.

NTPC's operating asset base spans thermal, hydro, solar, and wind installations that maintain grid stability across the country. Beyond base power generation, the public sector utility has diversified into e-mobility, energy storage, waste-to-energy, nuclear power, and green hydrogen solutions.

So why would an industrial company want to own a quarter of a power plant? The logic runs both ways. For the industrial partner, co-generation offers a long-term, often cheaper source of both steam and electricity, built at or near its own facility, with a share of the economics rather than a pure utility bill. For NTPC, the equity stake anchors a long-term customer and extends its reach into the kind of behind-the-fence industrial energy supply that captive power plants have traditionally served, but under a cooperative structure where the utility brings its generation expertise and the industrial partner brings demand.

The model also sits naturally beside NTPC's broader push into integrated supply frameworks that combine coal, renewable energy and battery storage. A co-generation plant feeding a factory with steady process heat has a different load profile from a grid-scale solar park, and pairing the two within one supply arrangement lets NTPC match round-the-clock industrial demand with a cleaner mix of sources than a standalone thermal unit would allow.

Expressions of Interest are an early-stage tool: NTPC is sounding out which industries — chemicals, paper, textiles, food processing and refinery complexes are the classic co-generation users — see enough value in steam-plus-power economics to put equity on the table. The 26 per cent stake on offer signals a genuine partnership rather than a token arrangement, though the company has not said how many projects the initiative covers or on what timeline partners would be selected.

The move is also consistent with how NTPC has been broadening its identity. What began as a thermal generation utility now describes itself across a portfolio that spans e-mobility, energy storage, waste-to-energy, nuclear power and green hydrogen. Co-generation with industrial partners adds a customer-facing, contract-anchored dimension to that portfolio — less about building megawatts for the grid, more about locking in decades of supply relationships with India's heavy industry as it decarbonises.

WHAT HAS NTPC LIMITED ANNOUNCED?
NTPC LIMITED HAS INVITED EXPRESSIONS OF INTEREST FROM COMMERCIAL AND INDUSTRIAL ENTITIES WILLING TO INVEST 26 PER CENT EQUITY IN CO-GENERATION PROJECTS. THE STATE-RUN POWER COMPANY FLOATED THE INITIATIVE TO EXPLORE JOINT OPPORTUNITIES FOR SETTING UP PLANTS DESIGNED TO SUPPLY BOTH STEAM AND ELECTRICITY TO INDUSTRIAL USERS. THE MOVE MARKS A STRUCTURAL SHIFT FOR THE POWER MAJOR, FROM SELLING POWER UNDER TRADITIONAL SUPPLY CONTRACTS TO CO-OWNING GENERATION CAPACITY WITH LARGE INDUSTRIAL CONSUMERS.
WHAT IS CO-GENERATION AND WHY DOES NTPC BACK IT?
CO-GENERATION PRODUCES STEAM AND ELECTRICITY THROUGH A SINGLE INTEGRATED SYSTEM, WHICH IS MORE EFFICIENT THAN SEPARATE GENERATION. NTPC SAID THAT WITH INDUSTRIES INCREASINGLY FOCUSING ON ENERGY SECURITY, COST OPTIMISATION AND SUSTAINABILITY, CO-GENERATION OFFERS AN OPPORTUNITY TO ENHANCE OVERALL ENERGY EFFICIENCY.
HOW WILL THE PROJECTS BE STRUCTURED?
THE CO-GENERATION PLANTS WILL OPERATE ALONGSIDE INTEGRATED POWER SUPPLY FRAMEWORKS THAT COMBINE COAL, RENEWABLE ENERGY, AND BATTERY ENERGY STORAGE SYSTEMS. NTPC SAID THE PROPOSED INITIATIVE SEEKS TO ADDRESS THE DIVERSE REQUIREMENTS OF INDUSTRIES WITH RELIABLE, ECONOMICAL AND SUSTAINABLE ENERGY SOLUTIONS THROUGH LONG-TERM POWER SUPPLY ARRANGEMENTS. UNDER THE PARTNERSHIP MODEL, INDUSTRIAL USERS WOULD HOLD A DIRECT EQUITY STAKE IN THE GENERATING ASSETS THEMSELVES.
WHAT IS NTPC'S CAPACITY TODAY AND ITS 2032 TARGET?
NTPC OPERATES AN INSTALLED POWER GENERATION BASE OF OVER 91 GW, WITH AN ADDITIONAL PIPELINE EXCEEDING 35 GW UNDER ACTIVE CONSTRUCTION. THE UTILITY HAS SET A CORPORATE TARGET OF REACHING 149 GW OF TOTAL GENERATION CAPACITY BY 2032, WITH 60 GW PLANNED FROM RENEWABLE SOURCES ALONE. ITS OPERATING ASSET BASE COMPRISES THERMAL, HYDRO, SOLAR, AND WIND INSTALLATIONS TO MAINTAIN GRID STABILITY ACROSS THE COUNTRY.
WHAT DOES THIS MEAN FOR NTPC'S STRATEGY BEYOND POWER SUPPLY?
THE INITIATIVE MARKS AN EFFORT TO EXPAND BEYOND TRADITIONAL SUPPLY CONTRACTS INTO CAPTIVE AND COOPERATIVE GENERATION ARRANGEMENTS WITH HEAVY INDUSTRIAL CONSUMERS. NTPC SAID THE TIE-UPS REFLECT ITS COMMITMENT TO EXPANDING ITS ENERGY SOLUTIONS PORTFOLIO, STRENGTHENING PARTNERSHIPS WITH INDUSTRIES, AND SUPPORTING ENERGY TRANSITION OBJECTIVES. THE COMPANY HAS ALSO DIVERSIFIED INTO E-MOBILITY, ENERGY STORAGE, WASTE-TO-ENERGY, NUCLEAR POWER, AND GREEN HYDROGEN. THE ARRANGEMENTS ALSO AIM TO ALIGN DEDICATED STEAM AND ELECTRICITY INFRASTRUCTURE WITH BROADER INDUSTRIAL DECARBONISATION EFFORTS. GLOSSARY: Co-generation: A single plant producing both electricity and useful heat (typically steam) for an industrial process, capturing energy that a power-only plant would waste. Expression of Interest (EoI): A preliminary solicitation in which a company invites parties to indicate willingness to participate in a project before detailed terms are negotiated. Captive power: Electricity generated by a plant owned by, or dedicated to, a single industrial consumer rather than sold to the grid. Battery Energy Storage System (BESS): Large-scale batteries that store power and release it when needed, smoothing out variable renewable generation. Green hydrogen: Hydrogen produced using renewable electricity, seen as a low-carbon fuel for industry.
