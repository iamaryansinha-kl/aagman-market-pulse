Four Indian NBFCs to Raise ₹85.50 Billion via Floating-Rate Bonds

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/ICICI Home Finance, Tata Capital, Mahindra & Mahindra Financial Services and HDB Financial Services plan to raise ₹85.50 billion via floating-rate bonds this week
  • 02/AAA-rated floating-rate debt yields ~7.35%, roughly 30-40 bps lower than comparable fixed-rate bonds; coupons reset quarterly over 3-month T-bills
  • 03/One-year overnight index swap rates are pricing in at least 100 bps of RBI rate hikes over the next 12 months, possibly starting from August 2026

Four Indian non-banking finance companies are turning to floating-rate bonds to manage borrowing costs. ICICI Home Finance Company Ltd, Tata Capital Ltd, Mahindra & Mahindra Financial Services Ltd and HDB Financial Services Ltd plan to raise ₹85.50 billion ($887.74 million) this week. The bonds carry a three-year maturity.

These firms have traditionally relied on fixed-rate debt for their funding requirements. The shift comes as expectations of Reserve Bank of India rate hikes have pushed up fixed-rate yields. Several issuers have struggled to raise targeted amounts through conventional fixed-rate issuances.

Coupons for floating-rate bonds are priced at a spread over three-month Treasury bill yields. They reset quarterly, allowing returns to rise alongside benchmark rates. When rate hikes are expected, this structure benefits both issuers and investors.

Currently, spreads on AAA-rated floating-rate debt sit in the 193-210 basis points range over T-bills. This implies a yield of approximately 7.35%, or roughly 30-40 bps lower than comparable fixed-rate bonds.

Bets that the RBI will raise interest rates in 2026 have strengthened. India's April annual wholesale price index inflation jumped to its highest level in three and a half years. Overnight index swap rates have surged accordingly.

The one-year swap is now pricing in at least 100 basis points of hikes over the next 12 months. Market participants expect the first increase possibly from August 2026. This has made fixed-rate bonds less attractive to buyers.

"If an asset manager fully hedges the floating rate bond, the all-in yield is closer to 8.85%, compared with around 8.25% on a conventional fixed-rate bond. On a hedged basis, it makes a lot of sense for asset managers to add these papers," said Basant Bafna, head of fixed income at Mirae Asset Investment Managers (India).

Venkatakrishnan Srinivasan, founder and managing partner at Rockfort Fincap, cited the volatile interest-rate environment. He noted that several issuers have been struggling to raise targeted amounts through fixed-rate issuances.

EXPECTATIONS OF RESERVE BANK OF INDIA RATE HIKES IN 2026 HAVE PUSHED UP FIXED-RATE BOND YIELDS, MAKING BUYERS WARY OF LOCKING IN LOWER RATES. COMPANIES CAN BORROW AT A LOWER INITIAL COST VIA FLOATING-RATE BONDS, WHILE INVESTORS BENEFIT FROM RETURNS THAT RISE OVER TIME AS RATES INCREASE.
