Rupee Down 6% in 2026, REER at 2013 Taper Tantrum Levels: Axis Bank Sees More Weakness Ahead

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

The rupee has weakened nearly 6% in 2026, and the trade-weighted real effective exchange rate, or REER, has slid to 91.75, a level last seen during the 2013 taper tantrum, according to research by Axis Bank.

The REER has fallen from around 102 in April 2025 to 91.8 by July this year. A REER below 100 means the currency is cheaper in real, trade-weighted terms than its long-run average, and a reading at 2013 levels signals the rupee has already adjusted substantially.

Yet the adjustment may not be over. Tanay Dalal, economist at the private sector lender, said depreciation to a new fair value was likely completed as of March. But a sustained terms-of-trade shock combined with a roughly 5% loss in relative AI productivity would, in theory, translate into another 10% weakening of the REER over a year's time.

Part of the problem, Dalal said, could be a misalignment between the neutral interest rates needed for India's domestic growth and inflation balance and global trends.

The Reserve Bank of India has been the other big actor in this story. Dalal estimates the central bank has sold about $250 billion since mid-2023 to defend the rupee. That is nearly double the inflows seen through the dedicated dollar-inflows programme the RBI ran between June and August.

The assessment frames the central bank's dilemma. Persistent dollar selling defends the currency day to day but drains reserves, while letting the rupee adjust supports competitiveness at the cost of imported inflation.

For India, a weaker real exchange rate is not automatically bad news. Exporters gain pricing power, import substitution gets a push, and the REER slide partly reverses the overvaluation that built up over the previous two years. The cost shows up in fuel and commodity import bills and in the local-currency value of external debt.

The near-6% calendar-year decline in the rupee, against a REER already at 2013 levels, suggests the market is pricing in exactly the kind of further adjustment Axis Bank describes.
