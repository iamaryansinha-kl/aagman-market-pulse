Paramount-Warner Merger: $111B Deal Reshapes Hollywood Streaming

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Warner Bros Discovery shareholders approved an $81 billion sale to Paramount-Skydance, with total deal value near $111 billion including debt
  • 02/Combined HBO Max (12% market share) and Paramount+ (3%) would create streaming giant, though still trailing Netflix (19%) and Disney (27%)
  • 03/Deal faces regulatory review and concerns over CNN-CBS consolidation, Trump-Ellison ties, and potential layoffs from overlapping operations

Warner Bros Discovery shareholders approved the $81 billion sale of the company on Thursday. The total buyout is valued at nearly $111 billion including debt.

Paramount Skydance would own both Paramount+ and HBO Max. Executives plan to combine the streamers into a single platform.

HBO Max holds 12 per cent of US on-demand subscriptions. Paramount+ holds just 3 per cent, per JustWatch data from Q1 2026.

A combined service would still trail Prime Video at 17 per cent and Netflix at 19 per cent. Disney owns 27 per cent through Hulu and Disney+.

Paramount CEO David Ellison wants the merged company to release over 30 films annually. He pledged a 45-day exclusive theatrical window at CinemaCon last week.

Warner Bros captured 21 per cent of the 2025 domestic box office. Paramount managed only 6 per cent, driven largely by Mission: Impossible.

CNN would join CBS under the same ownership. Critics worry about editorial independence given the Ellison family's ties to President Donald Trump.

Regulatory filings indicate cost-cutting plans including layoffs. Paramount is taking on billions in debt to finance the megamerger.
