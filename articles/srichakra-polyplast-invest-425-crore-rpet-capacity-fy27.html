Srichakra Polyplast to Invest ₹425 Crore in rPET Capacity, Targets ₹1,000 Crore Revenue

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Srichakra Polyplast has lined up ₹425 crore in capex to expand food-grade rPET capacity from 90,000 tonnes to over 113,000 tonnes by 2026
  • 02/Revenue is projected to jump from ₹227 crore in FY25 to ₹400 crore in FY26 and ₹1,000 crore in FY27-28, a near fourfold expansion
  • 03/India's EPR mandate tightens to 40% recycled content from April 2026, but industry faces a supply shortfall of 3.3 lakh tonnes

Srichakra Polyplast Ltd has announced ₹425 crore in capital expenditure to expand its food-grade recycled PET capacity. The company will grow from around 90,000 tonnes to over 113,000 tonnes by 2026.

The Hyderabad-based firm reported revenue of ₹227 crore in FY25. It expects to hit ₹400 crore in FY26 and ₹1,000 crore in FY27-28.

The expansion follows a distributed model rather than a single large plant. The upcoming Krishnagiri facility in Tamil Nadu will anchor the bulk of new capacity. A Gujarat joint venture and the existing Telangana hub will support the network.

Srichakra operates EFSA- and US FDA-approved processes. It is among India's earliest commercial bottle-to-bottle recyclers and supplies global beverage and packaging brands.

The company currently ships around $1.5 million worth of certified recycled polymers per month. It is looking to convert improving demand visibility into long-term contracts.

India's Extended Producer Responsibility framework now requires 40% recycled content in PET bottles from April 2026, up from 30%. Industry data shows food-grade rPET availability at 3.54 lakh tonnes against a requirement of 6.84 lakh tonnes for FY27.

"The gap is not at the kerbside. It is at the converter," said Ravindra P V, co-founder and managing director of Srichakra Polyplast. He pointed to structural limits in turning collected plastic into certified, food-grade material.

Competition is heating up. Domestic recyclers Banyan Nation and Ganesha Ecosphere are scaling up. Global major Indorama Ventures has partnered with Varun Beverages to build a 100,000-tonne rPET facility in India.

"The biggest distortion in our industry today is not technology, capacity, or even feedstock availability. It is the GST leakage at the supplier end of the chain," Ravindra said. Compliant recyclers are competing against cost structures built on regulatory arbitrage.

"In 24 months, this industry will look bifurcated, with commodity recyclers on one side and compliance-grade players on the other," Ravindra added. The pace of Srichakra's scale-up will depend on securing high-quality feedstock and building traceable supply chains.
