Tata Stocks Tumble Up to 8% as Tata Trusts Calls Chandrasekaran's Tenure Extension Illegal

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

A full-blown governance war at Bombay House spilled into the stock market on Friday. Tata Chemicals slid as much as 8% to a day low of Rs 719, Tata Investment Corporation fell 5% to Rs 683, and TCS, India's largest IT services provider, dropped 3.5% to Rs 2,118. Tata Motors, Tata Elxsi and Tata Technologies slipped up to 3%.

The trigger was Tata Trusts' declaration that the reappointment of N Chandrasekaran as executive chairman of Tata Sons for a fresh five-year term is illegal.

The sequence of events is layered. Days earlier, the Reserve Bank of India rejected Tata Sons' request to remain a private, unlisted company, clarifying that its rules for shadow banks of a certain size, which require public shareholding, would apply to the holding company. Tata Sons' board then met and, by majority, resolved to reappoint Chandrasekaran for five years after his current term ends, alongside initiating work on the long-awaited holding company IPO.

The vote was 4-1. Noel Tata, chairman of Tata Trusts, was the lone dissenting director. Tata Trusts, which controls 66% of Tata Sons through the Sir Ratan Tata Trust and the Sir Dorabji Tata Trust, said publicly that the resolution was a legal nullity under the Articles of Association of Tata Sons.

The tie-breaking mechanics have become central to the dispute. Only two trust nominees, Noel Tata and Venu Srinivasan, sit on the Tata Sons board, and they voted in opposite directions. The board's view, supported by legal opinion, is that the session chairman can exercise a casting vote. Since Chandrasekaran could not vote on his own reappointment, Harish Manwani chaired the session and cast the deciding vote.

Tata Trusts' objections run deeper than the chairmanship. In July 2025, the trusts passed a resolution seeking to keep Tata Sons privately owned, while the Shapoorji Pallonji Group, which holds 18.37% and is the largest minority shareholder, sees a listing as the most practical route to unlocking value. Noel Tata has argued that structural alternatives could satisfy the RBI's directive without an explicit public listing, and has proposed a joint committee of trusts and Tata Sons to examine the options.

The looming Tata Sons AGM is now the critical event. Chandrasekaran needs to be reappointed as a director at the AGM to continue as chairman, and the trusts hold the decisive votes. But one of the two majority-holding trusts, the Sir Ratan Tata Trust, is barred by the Maharashtra charity commissioner from making decisions, so the meeting has been deferred for lack of quorum.

Chandrasekaran had resigned as Tata Sons chairman in August after the board deferred his reappointment decision, with the trusts raising reservations about the performance of new businesses and preferring a two-year executive term consistent with the group's retirement age of 65.

Listed companies with direct Tata Sons stakes are caught in the crossfire: Tata Chemicals holds 2.53% of Tata Sons, and Tata Investment Corporation holds a significant stake, which is why they led the declines.

For investors, the immediate issue is not operations but uncertainty: the timeline of the Tata Sons IPO, the chairmanship, and the balance of power between the 66% shareholder and the board all hang in the balance.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHY DID TATA STOCKS FALL ON FRIDAY?
Tata Trusts declared the Tata Sons board's reappointment of N Chandrasekaran as executive chairman for five years illegal. Tata Chemicals fell up to 8%, Tata Investment Corp 5% and TCS 3.5%.
WHAT IS THE RBI'S ROLE IN THE DISPUTE?
The RBI rejected Tata Sons' request to remain private and unlisted, clarifying that its shadow-banking rules requiring public shareholding apply to the holding company, which is pushing it toward a listing the trusts oppose.
WHAT HAPPENS NEXT?
The Tata Sons AGM is the next battleground. Chandrasekaran must be reappointed as a director to continue as chairman, but the AGM stands deferred because the Sir Ratan Tata Trust is barred by the charity commissioner from voting, leaving no quorum.
