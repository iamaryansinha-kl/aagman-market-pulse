TCS Q2 Results FY27: Profit Up 15% to ₹13,884 Crore, ₹12 Dividend

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/TCS Q2FY27 net profit rose 15% YoY to ₹13,884 crore and revenue rose 11% to ₹73,188 crore; the board declared a second interim dividend of ₹12 per share
  • 02/Deal wins totalled $9.6 billion in TCV and annualised AI revenue crossed $3.1 billion, above 10% of revenue, even as North America grew just 0.4% QoQ
  • 03/Watch whether the $9.6 billion deal pipeline converts to revenue growth in H2FY27, with Consumer Business negative and India revenue down 10.3% sequentially

Tata Consultancy Services (NSE: TCS) opened the September-quarter earnings season on October 8, 2026. Consolidated net profit rose 15% YoY to ₹13,884 crore, against ₹12,075 crore in Q2FY26.

Revenue from operations grew 11% YoY to ₹73,188 crore, versus ₹65,799 crore a year earlier. In constant currency terms, revenue rose 0.5% quarter-on-quarter.

The board declared a second interim dividend of ₹12 per share for FY27. Operating margin came in at 24% and net margin at 19%.

Three numbers frame this quarter. Deal wins totalled $9.6 billion in total contract value. Annualised AI revenue crossed $3.1 billion, above 10% of total revenue. And the operating margin held at 24%.

The $9.6 billion TCV is the headline in a weak demand environment. Indian IT stocks have been under pressure from soft discretionary spending, cautious client budgets and fears that AI will erode traditional services work.

CEO and MD K Krithivasan said the Porsche and Best Buy agreements represent a new category of transformation partnerships. "TCS is building repeatable platforms with clients to industrialise AI at scale," he said.

Strategic wins included a five-year partnership with Porsche AG and the proposed acquisition of MHP, Porsche's Germany-based management and IT consulting subsidiary. TCS will also transition Best Buy's India Global Capability Center into an AI Capability Center.

Aarthi Subramanian, Executive Director, President and COO, said AI momentum stayed strong across AI-native solutions, AI-led enterprise transformation, autonomous global business services and cybersecurity. AI is no longer just a threat to IT services revenue; it is now a revenue line.

Margins are the quiet story of the quarter. Holding a 24% operating margin while funding acquisitions, partnerships and niche talent suggests pricing discipline rather than discounting for growth.

Growth was not uniform across verticals. BFSI, the largest segment, grew 2.5% sequentially in constant currency. Manufacturing and Technology & Services each rose 3.1%.

Consumer Business declined 0.7% and Energy, Resources and Utilities fell 0.5%. Regional Markets and Others dropped a sharper 5.8%. YoY, Technology & Services grew 4.8%, Manufacturing 4.2% and BFSI 3.9%.

Geography told a similar split story. The UK was the strongest large market at 3.5% sequential growth. Latin America rose 4.3% and Asia Pacific 2%.

The soft spots were real. North America and Continental Europe grew only 0.4% each in constant currency QoQ. India revenue fell 10.3% sequentially, though it remained up 6% from a year earlier.

Other wins included Honeywell Technologies for SAP S/4HANA transformation, Aareal Bank for infrastructure and cloud, and Vodafone Business for technology modernisation. TCS launched an Industrial Autonomy and Engineering Lab in Pune and a NVIDIA-powered Autonomous Engineering Lab in Bengaluru.

The workforce stood at 598,056 employees. LTM attrition in IT services stayed stable at 13.3%, and learning hours rose 17% sequentially to 17.1 million, CHRO Sudeep Kunnumal said.

Cash conversion stayed strong. Net cash from operations was ₹14,190 crore, equal to 102.2% of net income. CFO Samir Seksaria pointed to strong cash conversion and industry-leading profitability while investing in acquisitions and niche talent.

The quarter was less about a sharp growth rebound and more about proof of durability. Large deals, AI-led work and international demand are holding up; the open question is when North America and discretionary spending follow.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT WERE TCS'S Q2FY27 NET PROFIT AND REVENUE?
CONSOLIDATED NET PROFIT ROSE 15% YOY TO ₹13,884 CRORE, FROM ₹12,075 CRORE A YEAR EARLIER. REVENUE FROM OPERATIONS GREW 11% YOY TO ₹73,188 CRORE, FROM ₹65,799 CRORE. IN CONSTANT CURRENCY, REVENUE ROSE 0.5% QUARTER-ON-QUARTER.
WHAT DIVIDEND DID TCS DECLARE FOR FY27?
THE BOARD DECLARED A SECOND INTERIM DIVIDEND OF ₹12 PER SHARE FOR THE FINANCIAL YEAR 2026-27.
HOW LARGE WERE TCS'S DEAL WINS IN Q2FY27?
TOTAL CONTRACT VALUE OF DEAL WINS STOOD AT $9.6 BILLION FOR THE QUARTER. STRATEGIC WINS INCLUDED A FIVE-YEAR PARTNERSHIP WITH PORSCHE AG, THE PROPOSED ACQUISITION OF MHP, AND THE TRANSITION OF BEST BUY'S INDIA GLOBAL CAPABILITY CENTER INTO AN AI CAPABILITY CENTER.
HOW BIG IS TCS'S AI REVENUE NOW?
ANNUALISED AI REVENUE REACHED $3.1 BILLION IN Q2FY27 AND CROSSED 10% OF TOTAL REVENUE. DEMAND SPANS AI-NATIVE SOLUTIONS, AI-LED ENTERPRISE TRANSFORMATION, AUTONOMOUS GLOBAL BUSINESS SERVICES AND CYBERSECURITY.
WHICH SEGMENTS WERE WEAK FOR TCS IN Q2FY27?
CONSUMER BUSINESS FELL 0.7% QOQ AND REGIONAL MARKETS AND OTHERS DECLINED 5.8% IN CONSTANT CURRENCY. NORTH AMERICA AND CONTINENTAL EUROPE GREW ONLY 0.4% EACH SEQUENTIALLY, WHILE INDIA REVENUE FELL 10.3% QOQ, THOUGH IT WAS UP 6% YOY. GLOSSARY: Constant currency: Growth measured without the effect of exchange-rate movements, so the underlying business trend is visible. Total contract value (TCV): The full dollar value of deals signed in a quarter. TCS reported $9.6 billion in Q2FY27. Operating margin: Operating profit as a percentage of revenue. TCS's 24% is among the highest in global IT services. Attrition (LTM): The share of employees who left over the last twelve months. TCS's IT services attrition held at 13.3%.
