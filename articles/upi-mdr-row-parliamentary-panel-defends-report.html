UPI MDR Row Escalates as Parliamentary Panel Chief Defends Report, Opposition Denies Backing

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

The controversy over charging merchants for UPI transactions has now spilled into Parliament. Bhartruhari Mahtab, chairman of the Parliamentary Standing Committee on Finance, on Saturday defended the committee's report on the Demands for Grants amid the row over Merchant Discount Rate, saying it was adopted unanimously after due procedure.

"Everything is on record," Mahtab said. He explained the process: questions were sent to the department, written answers were received, a draft report was prepared, circulated and adopted. His defence followed a sharp objection from Congress MP Manish Tewari, who rejected reports attributed to the chair that certain Opposition members supported the proposal to introduce MDR on digital payments.

Tewari's core argument rests on one word: explore. He said the committee's recommendation, made while examining the Demands for Grants of the Department of Financial Services for FY27 on March 12, 2026, was limited to asking the department to explore a self-reliant, tiered revenue model alongside the three-year incentive scheme for digital payments in Tier 3-6 cities.

He quoted the government's own response laid in the Lok Sabha on August 12: the department is currently exploring two options, restoring MDR for certain high-threshold transactions and merchants, or a tiered incentive structure to phase out government support. "The key words are currently exploring," Tewari said, adding that the proposal to levy a transaction fee on UPI never came before the committee after that stage.

What triggered the row is the NPCI framework announced on September 15. Under it, person-to-merchant UPI transactions above Rs 2,000 will attract an MDR of 0.4% capped at Rs 300 per transaction from October 15, 2026. Consumers continue to transact free of cost; the charge falls on select merchant transactions.

The backlash is broad. The All India Mobile Retailers Association has called the move outright robbery and demanded an immediate rollback, arguing that small merchants will either absorb the cost or shift away from UPI.

The government's position is that the zero-MDR regime, funded by exchequer incentives, is becoming unsustainable as UPI volumes scale. The committee's framing of a tiered, self-reliant revenue model is the middle path under discussion, but the political fight over who owns the decision has only begun.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT IS THE NEW UPI MDR RULE?
From October 15, 2026, person-to-merchant UPI transactions above Rs 2,000 attract a 0.4% MDR capped at Rs 300 per transaction. Consumers still pay nothing.
WHAT DID THE STANDING COMMITTEE ACTUALLY RECOMMEND?
It recommended exploring a self-reliant, tiered revenue model for digital payments, not imposing a specific charge, according to both the chair and the dissenting MP.
WHAT IS THE OPPOSITION'S OBJECTION?
Manish Tewari says the committee never endorsed an MDR, and that the current proposal never came before the panel after the exploration recommendation. KEY TAKEAWAYS: - Panel chief Mahtab defends unanimous report, says everything on record - Tewari denies Opposition backed MDR, stresses "explore" language - NPCI's 0.4% MDR on transactions above Rs 2,000 takes effect October 15 - Merchant bodies demand rollback, calling the charge robbery
