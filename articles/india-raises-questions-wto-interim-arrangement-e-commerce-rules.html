India Raises Questions at WTO Over Interim Arrangement to Implement E-Commerce Rules

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

India is pushing back at the WTO on e-commerce rules.

The country has raised formal questions about an interim arrangement being proposed to implement e-commerce trade rules at the World Trade Organization. The move signals India's cautious approach to global digital trade governance and its insistence on protecting policy space for developing countries.

The WTO has been struggling to reach consensus on e-commerce rules. Several advanced economies have pushed for binding commitments on digital trade, cross-border data flows, and prohibitions on data localisation requirements. India has resisted these proposals, arguing they favour technology-rich countries.

The interim arrangement is an attempt to move forward without full consensus. A group of countries is proposing to implement e-commerce rules among themselves while leaving the door open for others to join later. India wants clarity on how this framework will operate.

Key questions India has raised include whether the interim arrangement will eventually become binding on all WTO members, how developing countries will be accommodated, and what safeguards exist for national policy space on data governance and digital taxation.

India has been a leading voice for developing countries at the WTO. Along with South Africa and others, India has argued that binding e-commerce rules could prevent governments from regulating Big Tech, protecting domestic startups, and taxing digital transactions.

The outcome will shape global digital trade for decades. E-commerce has exploded in volume, but the rules governing it remain a patchwork of bilateral and regional agreements. A WTO framework would provide consistency but at the cost of reducing national flexibility.
