Samsung Electronics Shares Surge 6% as Union Suspends Planned 18-Day Strike

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Samsung Electronics and its union reached a tentative pay deal on May 20, suspending an 18-day strike by nearly 48,000 members
  • 02/Shares surged over 6% on the news, with the stock trading near 292,750 KRW after closing at 276,000 KRW prior day
  • 03/Union members will vote on the agreement from May 22 to May 27; outcome will determine if strike resumes

Samsung Electronics Co., Ltd. (KRX: 005930) reached a tentative wage deal with its labour union late on May 20, 2026. The agreement averts a planned 18-day strike set to begin May 21.

The union suspended the walkout by nearly 48,000 members while the deal goes to a vote. Voting will run from May 22 to May 27, union leader Choi Seung-ho announced.

Samsung shares surged over 6% following the news. The stock had closed at 276,000 KRW on May 20 and jumped to approximately 292,750 KRW in early trading.

The deal came after talks broke down multiple times earlier in the week. South Korean Labour Minister Kim Young-hoon stepped in to mediate, restarting negotiations on Wednesday.

The core dispute centred on how performance bonuses would be split. Workers wanted clarity on distribution between the profitable memory division and the loss-making logic chip unit.

Samsung is the world's largest memory chip maker. It accounts for almost a quarter of South Korea's total exports, making any production disruption a national economic risk.

A prolonged strike would have threatened chip supply chains during the ongoing AI boom. Global memory demand is already tight, and Samsung's fabs run at high utilisation.

The stock remains below its 52-week high of 299,500 KRW, reached on May 13, 2026. Investors are watching the union vote outcome closely.
