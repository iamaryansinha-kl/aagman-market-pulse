Chris Wood Cuts Indian Stocks, Bets Big on SK Hynix and Samsung for AI Boom

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Jefferies' Christopher Wood cuts select Indian equities to fund 4% positions in SK Hynix and Kioxia, while raising Samsung Electronics weighting by one percentage point
  • 02/DRAM stocks trade at 6.8x-9.2x forward earnings; Wood argues memory has become the core engine of the AI era and still looks cheap on PE metrics
  • 03/India portfolio hit: Ambuja Cements removed entirely, while GMR Airports, JSW Energy and Adani Energy Solutions face cuts of 2%, 1% and 1% respectively

Jefferies strategist Christopher Wood has cut select Indian equities to increase exposure to South Korean memory chipmakers. His flagship Greed & Fear portfolios now include SK Hynix and Kioxia at 4% weighting each, while Samsung Electronics gets a one percentage point boost.

Wood describes the ongoing AI build-out as "the most dramatic capex cycle Greed & Fear has ever seen." He argues memory chips are the "picks and shovels" winners of what he calls "the mother of all cycles."

The Asia ex-Japan portfolio re-initiated Hynix at 4% by removing PolicyBazaar entirely. Alibaba was trimmed by one percentage point to help finance the larger Samsung position.

In the India long-only portfolio, Ambuja Cements was removed completely. GMR Airports, JSW Energy and Adani Energy Solutions were reduced by two, one and one percentage points respectively.

Wood cites Micron CEO Sanjay Mehrotra's view that "memory has evolved from a peripheral component into the core engine driving productivity in the AI era." Micron has signed 16 strategic customer agreements covering roughly 20% of DRAM volumes.

On valuations, Wood notes SK Hynix trades at 7.8x, Samsung at 6.8x and Micron at 9.2x consensus 12-month forward earnings. He argues the DRAM industry should now be valued on price-to-earnings rather than price-to-book.

Wood also removed Alphabet and Alibaba from the global portfolio to make room. He concedes that memory stocks have been the most leveraged way to play the AI theme, better than his earlier Nvidia exit in October 2025.

The strategist warns that malinvestment concerns remain the biggest risk to the AI trade. A sudden realisation that hyperscalers cannot generate returns could trigger a sharp unwinding of capex funding.

WHAT NEW POSITIONS DID WOOD ADD TO HIS GREED & FEAR PORTFOLIOS?
WOOD ADDED SK HYNIX AND KIOXIA TO HIS GLOBAL LONG-ONLY PORTFOLIO WITH AN INITIAL 4% WEIGHTING EACH. HE ALSO INCREASED THE EXISTING SAMSUNG ELECTRONICS POSITION BY ONE PERCENTAGE POINT.
HOW ARE DRAM STOCKS VALUED CURRENTLY ACCORDING TO WOOD?
SK HYNIX TRADES AT 7.8X, SAMSUNG ELECTRONICS AT 6.8X, AND MICRON AT 9.2X CONSENSUS 12-MONTH FORWARD EARNINGS. WOOD ARGUES THESE SHOULD BE VALUED ON PRICE-TO-EARNINGS RATHER THAN PRICE-TO-BOOK.
WHAT IS WOOD'S VIEW ON THE AI CAPEX CYCLE?
WOOD DESCRIBES IT AS "THE MOST DRAMATIC CAPEX CYCLE GREED & FEAR HAS EVER SEEN." HE BELIEVES FALLING TOKEN COSTS WILL DRIVE EXPLOSIVE DEMAND FOR MEMORY VIA JEVONS PARADOX, KEEPING DRAM PRICES RISING.
WHAT RISK COULD END THE AI TRADE ACCORDING TO WOOD?
WOOD WARNS THAT CONCERNS ABOUT MALINVESTMENT ARE THE MOST LIKELY TRIGGER. A SUDDEN REALISATION THAT HYPERSCALERS AND COMPANIES LIKE OPENAI CANNOT GENERATE RETURNS ON THEIR MASSIVE INVESTMENTS COULD CURTAIL FUNDING.
