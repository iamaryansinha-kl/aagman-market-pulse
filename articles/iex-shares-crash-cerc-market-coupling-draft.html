IEX Shares Crash 8% After CERC Market Coupling Draft Threatens Monopoly

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/IEX shares crashed nearly 8% to ₹126.49 after CERC released a draft proposal introducing market coupling norms for power exchanges
  • 02/Grid India will become the Market Coupling Operator, setting uniform clearing prices and stripping IEX of its price discovery role
  • 03/IEX derives 78% revenue from transaction fees; its 84% market share dominance is now under threat from the regulatory overhaul

IEX (NSE: IEX) shares fell sharply on Monday, April 20, 2026. The stock dropped nearly 8% to ₹126.49 by 12:47 pm from its previous close of ₹135.81.

The decline followed a draft proposal by the Central Electricity Regulatory Commission (CERC). The proposal introduces market coupling norms for electricity price discovery.

Under the draft, Grid India will act as the Market Coupling Operator. It will aggregate bids from all exchanges and determine a uniform market clearing price.

Exchanges will continue to collect bids but will no longer set prices. IEX effectively becomes a bid collection front end rather than a price discovering exchange.

IEX currently commands around 84% market share in India's power exchange segment. Its discovered price has effectively served as the national benchmark until now.

The new norms could level the playing field for smaller rivals. Power Exchange India Limited and Hindustan Power Exchange may gain volumes without building their own liquidity pools.

IEX derives approximately 78% of its revenue from per-unit transaction fees. Any loss in market share would directly hit its top line.

The transition will begin with the Day Ahead Market (DAM). This is IEX's largest revenue segment before extending to Real-Time Market (RTM).

"IEX trades at a P/E of around 24, a premium built on its dominance. If that dominance fades, multiples could compress," said Balaji Rao Mudili, Research Analyst at Bonanza.

"The market is still in the middle of repricing what IEX is worth without that moat," he added.
