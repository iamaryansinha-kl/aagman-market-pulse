Retail Investors Turn Net Sellers in FY26 After Six-Year Buying Streak

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Retail investors turned net sellers in FY26 for the first time since FY19, with secondary market outflows of Rs 13,000 crore through November 2025
  • 02/FY25 saw retail inflows of Rs 1.25 lakh crore; FY24 saw Rs 47,241 crore — FY26 marks a sharp reversal
  • 03/Record SIP inflows of Rs 3.49 lakh crore and Rs 34,840 crore in IPOs show retail is still active, just rotating away from secondary markets

Retail investors turned net sellers in the secondary market during FY26, ending a six-year streak of consistent buying. NSE data shows retail traders were net sellers in four of the first seven months of the fiscal year.

The reversal marks the first time since FY19 that individual investors have pulled more money out of stocks than they put in. October 2025 recorded the steepest monthly outflow at Rs 12,061 crore.

Cumulative secondary market outflows reached approximately Rs 13,000 crore by November 2025. This compares sharply with FY25 inflows of Rs 1.25 lakh crore and FY24 inflows of Rs 47,241 crore.

Profit booking and global uncertainty drove the selling, according to exchange data. Muted returns in mid- and small-cap segments left investors dissatisfied with secondary market performance.

However, retail investors did not exit equities entirely. They redirected capital toward primary markets, investing a record Rs 34,840 crore in IPOs during FY26 through November.

SIP inflows remained remarkably resilient. Monthly contributions averaged over Rs 28,000 crore in FY26, hitting a record Rs 32,087 crore in March 2026. Total FY26 SIP inflows reached Rs 3.49 lakh crore.

The divergence reveals a tactical shift rather than a loss of confidence. Retail investors are chasing listing gains in IPOs while booking profits in existing holdings.

Foreign portfolio investor ownership in NSE-listed equities fell to a 13-year low of 16.7% in Q2 FY26. Domestic institutional investor ownership rose to a lifetime high of 18.3%.

Combined ownership by DIIs, retail investors, and HNIs reached a record 27.8% in Q2 FY26. This underscores a structural shift toward domestic capital in Indian markets.

Market performance was subdued in FY26. The Nifty 50 and Sensex rose only 10-11% in the first nine months, lagging peers like South Korea's Kospi which surged 104%.

235 lakh demat accounts were added in FY26 through December, taking the total beyond 21.6 crore. Unique investors crossed 12 crore in September 2025.

Analysts say retail investors are unlikely to return meaningfully to secondary markets unless price momentum improves. They typically follow performance trends rather than lead them.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHY DID RETAIL INVESTORS BECOME NET SELLERS IN FY26?
RETAIL INVESTORS TURNED NET SELLERS DUE TO PROFIT BOOKING AFTER SIX YEARS OF GAINS, MUTED RETURNS IN MID- AND SMALL-CAP SEGMENTS, AND GLOBAL UNCERTAINTY. NSE DATA SHOWS THEY WERE NET SELLERS IN FOUR OF THE FIRST SEVEN MONTHS OF FY26, WITH OCTOBER 2025 RECORDING THE STEEPEST MONTHLY OUTFLOW OF RS 12,061 CRORE.
HOW MUCH DID RETAIL INVESTORS SELL IN FY26?
RETAIL INVESTORS RECORDED SECONDARY MARKET OUTFLOWS OF APPROXIMATELY RS 13,000 CRORE IN FY26 THROUGH NOVEMBER 2025. THIS COMPARES WITH INFLOWS OF RS 1.25 LAKH CRORE IN FY25 AND RS 47,241 CRORE IN FY24, REPRESENTING A DRAMATIC REVERSAL.
ARE RETAIL INVESTORS COMPLETELY EXITING EQUITY MARKETS?
NO. WHILE SECONDARY MARKET PARTICIPATION DECLINED, RETAIL INVESTORS REMAINED ACTIVE IN PRIMARY MARKETS. THEY INVESTED A RECORD RS 34,840 CRORE IN IPOS DURING FY26 THROUGH NOVEMBER. SIP INFLOWS ALSO HIT A RECORD RS 3.49 LAKH CRORE FOR THE FULL FISCAL YEAR, WITH MARCH 2026 CONTRIBUTIONS AT RS 32,087 CRORE.
HOW DOES FY26 COMPARE TO PREVIOUS YEARS FOR RETAIL PARTICIPATION?
FY26 IS THE FIRST FISCAL YEAR SINCE FY19 WHERE RETAIL INVESTORS WERE NET SELLERS IN THE SECONDARY MARKET. THE FY26 SECONDARY MARKET OUTFLOW OF RS 13,000 CRORE STANDS IN STARK CONTRAST TO FY25 INFLOWS OF RS 1.25 LAKH CRORE. HOWEVER, INCLUDING PRIMARY MARKET ALLOCATIONS, TOTAL FY26 NET INFLOWS REACHED RS 40,685 CRORE VERSUS RS 1.59 LAKH CRORE IN FY25.
WHAT SHOULD INVESTORS WATCH GOING FORWARD?
INVESTORS SHOULD MONITOR WHETHER SECONDARY MARKET MOMENTUM IMPROVES, AS RETAIL PARTICIPANTS TYPICALLY FOLLOW PERFORMANCE TRENDS. KEY METRICS INCLUDE MONTHLY SIP CONTRIBUTIONS, WHICH HAVE HELD ABOVE RS 28,000 CRORE, AND THE PACE OF NEW DEMAT ACCOUNT ADDITIONS, WHICH REACHED 235 LAKH IN FY26 THROUGH DECEMBER. THE SHIFT IN MARKET OWNERSHIP TOWARD DOMESTIC CAPITAL ALSO WARRANTS ATTENTION, WITH DII OWNERSHIP AT A LIFETIME HIGH OF 18.3%.
