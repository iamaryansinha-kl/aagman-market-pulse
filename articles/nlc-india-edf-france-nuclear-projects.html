NLC India Partners EDF France for Nuclear, Drops ₹4,400 Cr Methanol

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/NLC India Limited (NSE: NLCINDIA) signs strategic agreement with EDF France to explore nuclear power projects using EPR and SMR technologies
  • 02/Company drops proposed ₹4,400-crore lignite-to-methanol project as sources say it was too expensive to be viable
  • 03/Follows similar NTPC-EDF MoU from April 2026, signalling coordinated public sector push into nuclear energy

NLC India Limited (NSE: NLCINDIA) has signed a strategic agreement with Électricité de France (EDF). The pact will explore nuclear power project development in India.

The agreement covers advanced technologies including European Pressurised Reactors (EPR) and Small Modular Reactors (SMR). EDF is the world's largest nuclear operator.

The move follows a similar MoU signed by NTPC and EDF in April 2026. It signals a coordinated push into nuclear energy by Indian public sector giants.

India aims to reach 100 GW of nuclear power capacity by 2047. This target supports the "Viksit Bharat" and "Net Zero Carbon by 2070" commitments.

NLC India has decided to drop its proposed ₹4,400-crore lignite-to-methanol project. Sources said the project was deemed too expensive to be viable.

The shelved project was designed to produce 4 lakh tons of methanol annually. It would have used 2.5 million tonnes of lignite as feedstock.

The cancellation marks a strategic pivot away from capital-intensive projects with thin margins. NLC is now prioritising cleaner, large-scale energy infrastructure.

The government's SHANTI Act 2025 opened nuclear power to private sector participation. Companies including Tata Power and Reliance are also exploring nuclear opportunities.

THE COMPANY DROPPED THE ₹4,400-CRORE LIGNITE-TO-METHANOL PROJECT BECAUSE SOURCES SAID IT WAS TOO EXPENSIVE AND NOT ECONOMICALLY VIABLE. THE PROJECT WAS DESIGNED TO PRODUCE 4 LAKH TONS OF METHANOL ANNUALLY USING 2.5 MILLION TONNES OF LIGNITE.

THE SUSTAINABLE HARNESSING AND ADVANCEMENT OF NUCLEAR ENERGY FOR TRANSFORMING INDIA (SHANTI) ACT 2025 OPENED INDIA'S NUCLEAR SECTOR TO PRIVATE SECTOR PARTICIPATION. THIS ALLOWS PRIVATE COMPANIES TO ENGAGE IN PLANT OPERATIONS, POWER GENERATION, AND EQUIPMENT MANUFACTURING.
