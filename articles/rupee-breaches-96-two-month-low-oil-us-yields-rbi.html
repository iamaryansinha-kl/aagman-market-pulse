Rupee Breaches 96 Per Dollar, Hits Two-Month Low; RBI Intervenes

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Rupee touched an intraday low of 96.1450 per dollar on September 29, its weakest level in about two months, before RBI intervention pulled it back
  • 02/US 10-year Treasury yield climbed 6 basis points to 5.24%, near a two-decade high; markets now price nearly 70% odds of an October Fed hike versus under 20% a month earlier
  • 03/Forex reserves fell $14.881 billion to $765.901 billion in the week to September 19; the October Fed decision and Brent near $107 are the next triggers

The Indian rupee breached the 96-per-dollar mark on Tuesday, September 29, 2026. The currency touched an intraday low of 96.1450, its weakest level in about two months.

The rupee opened at 95.88 per dollar against Monday's close of 95.9825. It weakened 16 paise to 96.13 in early trade as dollar demand surged.

The domestic unit pared most of the day's losses later in the session. Per Hindu BusinessLine, the rupee "ends nearly flat as traders remain glued to oil moves, RBI caps decline."

The trigger was a global bond sell-off. The US 10-year Treasury yield climbed 6 basis points to 5.24%, a level near a two-decade high.

Markets now price nearly a 70% probability of another US Federal Reserve rate increase in October. That is up sharply from less than 20% a month earlier.

The dollar index hovered near a two-month high through the session. Brent crude traded around $107 per barrel on concerns over prolonged Middle East supply disruptions, keeping importer demand for dollars elevated.

CONTEXT: The pressure on the rupee is a textbook rate-differential story. The US Federal Reserve hiked its policy rate by 25 basis points in September, its first hike in three years, narrowing the yield gap with India and making rupee assets relatively less attractive. Under its managed float, the RBI must balance currency stability against monetary independence, a tension economists describe through the Mundell-Fleming trilemma. The central bank has repeatedly stated it intervenes against volatility, not to defend any particular level. Even so, 96 has emerged as a defended psychological threshold since late July.

This is not the first time the 96 line has been tested this cycle. On September 17, the rupee slid to an intraday low of 96.10 before closing at 95.94 after suspected RBI intervention. On September 28, it settled at 95.97 after again breaching 96 intraday.

The record low for this cycle remains 96.96 per dollar, hit in May 2026. Tuesday's low of 96.1450 marks the weakest print since late July.

RBI intervention did the heavy lifting on Tuesday. TradingEconomics noted the breach of 96 could prompt the central bank to step up intervention, citing its greater capacity after the recent buildup in forex reserves from special forex measures.

Governor Sanjay Malhotra said India has attracted nearly $32 billion in foreign capital since the RBI announced special inflow measures on June 5. FCNR(B) deposits have exceeded the 2013 record.

Reserves, however, are being drawn down. Forex reserves fell $14.881 billion in the week to September 19 to $765.901 billion. That is still one of the world's largest stockpiles, but it is the visible cost of defending the currency.

IMPACT: For India's economy, a weaker rupee raises the landed cost of crude oil, LPG and electronics. That feeds imported-inflation risk just as the RBI prepares for its October monetary policy committee meeting, complicating the rate path.

External debt servicing becomes costlier in rupee terms for companies with unhedged dollar borrowings. Oil marketing companies face higher input costs, while airlines and consumer-electronics importers see margin pressure.

The flip side benefits exporters and remittance recipients. IT and pharma companies that bill in dollars and report in rupees, including TCS, Infosys and Sun Pharma, stand to gain currency translation tailwinds if the rupee stays weak.

Portfolio flows remain the swing factor. FII equity outflow pressure could intensify if US yields hold near two-decade highs, though the RBI's $765.901 billion reserve buffer and the $32 billion inflow response give it room to smooth disorderly moves.

What to watch next: the October Fed decision, Brent's reaction to Middle East headlines, and whether the RBI tolerates a sustained move past 96 or keeps absorbing dollar demand.
