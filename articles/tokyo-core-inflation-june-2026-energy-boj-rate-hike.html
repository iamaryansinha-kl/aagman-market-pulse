Tokyo Core Inflation 1.6% in June: Energy Broadens, BOJ to Review Hikes

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Tokyo core CPI rose 1.6% in June from a year earlier, accelerating from 1.3% in May as energy costs feed through
  • 02/The core-core index, which excludes fresh food and fuel, climbed to 1.9% in June from 1.6% in May, signaling broadening price pressures
  • 03/The Bank of Japan will review the data at next month's policy meeting, with the board conducting a quarterly review of growth and price forecasts

Tokyo's core consumer price index rose 1.6% in June from a year earlier. The data matched median market forecasts and accelerated from a 1.3% gain in May.

The index remained below the Bank of Japan's 2% target for the fifth consecutive month. Despite the acceleration, headline inflation continues to undershoot the central bank's goal.

A closely watched gauge that strips away both fresh food and fuel costs rose 1.9% in June. This core-core index climbed from 1.6% in May, indicating price pressures are spreading beyond energy.

"The impact of the Middle East situation is spreading primarily through energy," said Kanako Nakamura, economist at Daiwa Institute of Research. Crude oil prices have risen since around February, gradually feeding into electricity and gas bills.

Wholesale inflation spiked to a three-year high of 6.3% in May. This signals companies are already passing on higher costs from the energy shock to consumers.

The Bank of Japan raised interest rates to a 31-year high this month. The move marked a landmark step in policy normalisation as the central bank focuses on taming energy-driven price pressures.

Takeshi Minami, chief economist at Norinchukin Research Institute, cast doubt on the BOJ's inflation outlook. He said he doubts energy-driven pressures would push core inflation as high as 3% as the BOJ expects, given easing inflation concerns in the United States and Europe.

"The BOJ may be overly cautious about inflation risks," Minami said. The Middle East conflict has complicated the timing and pace of future rate hikes, as higher energy costs fuel inflation while squeezing an oil-import-dependent economy.
