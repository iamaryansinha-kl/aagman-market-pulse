Gold, Silver Seen Range-Bound for Second Week Amid US-Iran Talks: Analysts

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Gold gained ₹1,178 (~1%) to ₹1.52 lakh per 10g, silver surged ₹10,985 (4.4%) to ₹2.61 lakh per kg on MCX last week
  • 02/Analysts Pranav Mer and Jateen Trivedi see range-bound trade amid progress on US-Iran peace negotiations
  • 03/Watch China, US, Germany inflation, Eurozone and UK GDP, and Trump's China visit for directional cues

Gold and silver are expected to remain range-bound for a second consecutive week. Analysts say investors will track US-Iran peace talks and upcoming global macroeconomic data.

On the Multi Commodity Exchange (MCX), gold futures gained ₹1,178, or nearly 1 per cent, over the past week. The contract settled at ₹1.52 lakh per 10 grams.

Silver outperformed gold, climbing ₹10,985 or 4.4 per cent. It closed at ₹2.61 lakh per kg on MCX, marking strong gains for a second straight week.

In international markets, Comex gold advanced nearly 2 per cent to close at USD 4,730.7 per ounce. Silver jumped 5.8 per cent to finish at USD 80.86 per ounce.

"Gold prices momentum looks consolidative, while silver movement looks positive in the coming week," said Pranav Mer, Vice President at JM Financial Services. He added that markets will react to progress on US-Iran peace talks.

Jateen Trivedi, VP Research Analyst at LKP Securities, noted that lower US Treasury yields and a softer dollar index helped gold hold steady. Crude oil's correction after easing geopolitical risk premiums also lifted sentiment for bullion.

Silver was further supported by renewed copper buying, a weaker US dollar, and signs of supply tightness globally. The World Gold Council also cited continued central bank buying and renewed ETF inflows as supportive factors for gold.

However, gains were capped on Friday after US and Iranian forces clashed in the Persian Gulf. President Donald Trump later calmed markets by saying a ceasefire was still holding.

Traders will watch inflation data from China, Germany, and the United States this week. GDP numbers from the Eurozone and the UK, along with Trump's expected China visit, will also drive sentiment.
