Small Business Credit Outstanding Grows 13% in FY26, Asset Quality Improves

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

Small business lending is picking up.

Credit outstanding to micro, small, and medium enterprises grew 13% in FY26, with asset quality improving across the sector, according to a report from a credit information company. The numbers reflect a meaningful shift in the lending environment for small businesses, with banks and NBFCs becoming more willing to extend credit and borrowers demonstrating better repayment behaviour.

The 13% growth is a significant acceleration. Small business credit had been growing at a modest 5-7% in the preceding years, constrained by the risk aversion that followed the pandemic and the stress in the sector. The FY26 numbers suggest that lenders have regained confidence in the segment, supported by government guarantee schemes, improved credit information infrastructure, and the recovery of small business revenues as the economy normalises.

Asset quality improvement is the key positive. Non-performing assets in the small business segment have declined, with gross NPA ratios dropping below 6% for the first time since the pandemic. The improvement reflects both better credit selection by lenders and the genuine recovery of small businesses that had struggled with cash flow during the lockdowns and the subsequent demand slowdown. Collection efficiency has improved, and the restructuring of loans under the RBI's pandemic relief schemes has largely been completed without significant slippage.

Banks and NBFCs are both driving the growth. Public sector banks have been aggressive in lending to small businesses under government-mandated priority sector targets, while private banks and NBFCs have been targeting the higher-margin segments of the market. The co-lending model, where banks and NBFCs partner to share risk, has gained traction and is expanding access to credit for small businesses that previously struggled to get bank loans.

The government's role has been critical. The Emergency Credit Line Guarantee Scheme, which provided government-backed loans to small businesses during the pandemic, has been extended and modified to support continued lending. The Credit Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro and Small Enterprises has provided partial guarantee cover for loans, reducing the risk for lenders and encouraging them to extend credit to smaller and riskier borrowers.

Digital lending is transforming the market. Fintech lenders and digital platforms have made it easier for small businesses to access credit by using alternative data — transaction history, GST returns, bank statements — to assess creditworthiness. This has expanded the pool of creditworthy borrowers beyond those with traditional collateral or credit histories, and has reduced the time and cost of loan origination.

The sustainability of the growth depends on the economic cycle. Small businesses are highly sensitive to economic conditions, and a slowdown in demand or a rise in input costs could quickly reverse the asset quality improvement. The 13% growth is encouraging, but lenders need to maintain disciplined credit standards to avoid a repeat of the over-leveraging that led to the NPA crisis of the late 2010s.
