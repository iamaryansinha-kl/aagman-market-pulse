Rs 2,047 Crore Lenskart Block Deal: Platinum Jasmine Likely Sells 1.7% Stake

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

A big early investor is heading for the exit at Lenskart. Shares of the eyewear retailer fell more than 3% to Rs 683 on the NSE on Monday after over 3 crore shares changed hands in a block deal, snapping a two-session gaining streak.

According to ET Now, around 3.53 crore shares, or a 2% stake in Lenskart Solutions, traded in the block window. While buyers and sellers are yet to be officially confirmed, Platinum Jasmine was reportedly set to divest up to 3 crore shares, or around 1.7% of the company's equity. The deal was estimated at more than Rs 2,047 crore, with the shares likely placed at Rs 682.45 apiece, a discount of over 3% to the previous closing price.

Platinum Jasmine is a meaningful holder. The investor held nearly a 10% stake in Lenskart at the end of the April-June quarter of FY27, according to shareholding pattern data, meaning Monday's sale would still leave it among the larger non-promoter shareholders.

The optics of the exit clash with the stock's performance. Lenskart debuted in November 2025 after raising Rs 7,278 crore through a fresh issue and offer for sale, listing at Rs 395 on the NSE, a 1.75% discount to the Rs 402 issue price. The weak start did not last: the shares have gained around 58% in 2026 so far, about 5% in the last month, and the company now commands a market capitalisation of over Rs 1.2 lakh crore.

Brokerage sentiment remains bullish. Last month, Nomura initiated coverage with a Buy call and a target price of Rs 888, arguing that Lenskart has transformed from challenger to market leader with a long runway of over 10,000 planned stores, only 27% of that potential tapped so far. The brokerage noted the company holds a 5% market share, double that of the second-largest player, in India's highly fragmented prescription eyewear segment, and highlighted its end-to-end vertical integration and AI-enabled operations delivering a cost structure 35-40% below the industry average.

Block deals of this size from a near-10% holder often signal a staged exit rather than a loss of faith, and the market's ability to absorb Rs 2,047 crore of supply at just a 3% discount suggests demand for the stock remains deep.
