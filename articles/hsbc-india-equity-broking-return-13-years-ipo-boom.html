HSBC Plans Return to India Equity Broking After 13 Years Amid IPO Boom

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

HSBC is preparing to re-enter the Indian equity broking business after an absence of more than a decade, seeking to capitalise on a robust pipeline of share sales and rising demand from wealthy clients for equity market products.

The move marks a reversal of HSBC's decision to exit the domestic retail brokerage and depository business in 2013, and underscores the growing importance of India's capital markets and wealth management industry to global banks.

Rebuilding the Platform

The London-headquartered bank has begun the process of rebuilding its equities platform in India and is hiring senior executives for cash equities and institutional broking roles.

"Looking at the phone penetration and everybody having direct capital market access, the idea is to have digital capabilities, and getting to retail brokerage business is an important element there," one person with direct knowledge of the matter said.

The person added that interest among Indian customers for trading in international markets has also grown since the establishment of India's tax-neutral GIFT City hub.

HSBC is likely to launch retail broking services over the next few months. The bank already holds a broking licence through HSBC InvestDirect Securities (India) Private Limited, which it plans to use to revive the business.

IPO Boom Draws Global Banks

HSBC's re-entry comes as India's equity markets attract record retail participation and a strong pipeline of initial public offerings. Several large IPOs are expected over the coming quarters, prompting global and local financial firms to expand their capital markets and broking capabilities.

Barclays Plc is in advanced preparations to restart its equity capital market business in India, 10 years after shutting it down, ET reported in March.

HSBC is now India's largest foreign bank by balance sheet and one of the few foreign banks that still has a sizable consumer business in the country. Peers such as Citibank, Standard Chartered and Deutsche Bank have chosen to focus mainly on serving large institutions.

In a recent interview with CNBC-TV18, HSBC Group CEO Georges Elhedery said the bank is looking to increase investments in India with a growing focus on affluent customers. HSBC India reported a 4% year-on-year increase in net profit in the first half of 2026. In January, the bank received approval to open 20 branches across India, taking its total branch count to 46.
