Gold Heads for Fourth Weekly Loss on Fed Rate Hike Outlook

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Spot gold fell 0.5% to $4,007.95/oz and is set for a fourth straight weekly decline of nearly 4%
  • 02/Gold slipped below $4,000 for the first time since November 2025 as the US dollar surged on hawkish Fed repricing
  • 03/US May inflation broke above 4% for the first time in three years; traders price in at least three Fed rate hikes this year

Spot gold fell 0.5% to $4,007.95 per ounce on Friday morning. US gold futures for August delivery dropped 0.6% to $4,024.10.

The yellow metal is heading for a weekly loss of nearly 4%. It slipped below $4,000 for the first time since November 2025 on Wednesday.

"The rapid repricing of the hawkish Fed created a strong bullish momentum in the U.S. dollar, which eventually led to this significant downward drift in gold prices," said Kelvin Wong, senior market analyst at OANDA, in a Reuters report.

Wong believes gold's pullback from its late-January record high is likely to continue for several months. He sees prices eventually treading towards $3,400 per ounce over the long term.

Gold has fallen nearly 29% from its record high of $5,594.82 on January 29. The surge was initially fueled by US-Iran war inflation, followed by ramped-up rate-hike bets.

US inflation breached 4% in May, data released Thursday showed. This marks the first time inflation has crossed that threshold in three years.

Traders now price in at least three Fed rate hikes this year. The CME FedWatch Tool indicates about a 64% probability of a rate increase in September.

Higher rates reduce gold's appeal as a non-yielding asset. The USD index is set for a second consecutive weekly gain, making dollar-denominated gold costlier for overseas buyers.

Spot silver fell 2.5% to $56.42, platinum lost 1.5% to $1,577.15, and palladium slid 0.4% to $1,179.26. All precious metals were headed for weekly declines.
