IIT Ropar Launches India's First Fully Integrated Agricultural Intelligence Ecosystem

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/IIT Ropar's ANNAM.AI launches India's first fully integrated agricultural intelligence ecosystem with three-layer AI architecture
  • 02/The platform is backed by the Government's ₹990 crore national initiative to build AI Centres of Excellence across key sectors
  • 03/100 AI-powered weather stations are being deployed starting with Punjab, with a roadmap for pan-India expansion across multiple states

The Indian Institute of Technology (Ropar) has launched India's first fully integrated agricultural intelligence ecosystem. The system, developed by the Centre of Excellence (CoE) in AI for Agriculture called ANNAM.AI, is already operational in Punjab.

The ecosystem integrates three distinct layers into one platform. The infrastructure layer comprises SWAN micro-climate intelligence units and advanced weather stations. The intelligence layer, Krishi AI, uses computer vision to detect crops, pests, and damage. The engagement layer, Annam Chat Engine (ACE), delivers multilingual advisories directly to farmers.

The Centre of Excellence was established under the Government's ₹990 crore national initiative. This programme was launched by the Ministry of Education to create three AI Centres of Excellence across agriculture, healthcare, and sustainable cities.

"The intelligence ecosystem is designed to support the entire agricultural value chain," said Pushpendra P. Singh, Project Director of ANNAM.AI at IIT Ropar. "It offers AI-driven insights, precision advisories, hyperlocal climate intelligence, and crop diagnostics all in one platform."

By mid-2026, the platform has begun generating real-time insights for farmers in pilot regions across Punjab. Hundreds of farmers have already participated in the pilot phase and received real-time alerts and advisories through the system.

The gains for farmers are projected to be significant and measurable. With hyperlocal weather intelligence and AI-based advisories, farmers can reduce water usage by 20-30 per cent. The system can also help prevent 9-12 per cent of crop losses caused by sudden weather events.

The platform features offline AI capability, allowing farmers to access insights even without internet connectivity. This is critical for rural India where network coverage remains patchy in many agricultural regions.

ANNAM.AI has signed MoUs with Punjab Agri Export Corporation Ltd., Northern Farmers (a farmer-producers organisation), and the Indian Institute of Soil Science in Bhopal. The initiative will also roll out fully-funded AI training for 10,000 students, rural youth, and agri-professionals.

"ANNAM.AI's ecosystem is not just a technology platform; it is a nation-building initiative aimed at empowering farmers, strengthening food security, and preparing India for a climate-challenged future," said Singh. The platform aligns with Viksit Bharat @2047, Digital Agriculture Mission, and the IndiaAI Mission.
