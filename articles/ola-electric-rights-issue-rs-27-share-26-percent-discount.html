Ola Electric Rights Issue Priced at Rs 27 a Share, 26% Below Market

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

Ola Electric Mobility's board has fixed the price of its proposed rights issue at Rs 27 per equity share, roughly 26% below the current market price, the company said in a statement to stock exchanges on Wednesday.

The issue will raise up to Rs 1,000 crore. In its exchange statement, the company said the price "gives all existing shareholders an opportunity to add to their holding at a substantial discount."

The payment structure is the notable detail. Shareholders will pay 60% of the issue price on application, with the balance due on a final call on a date the board will set later.

A two-call structure gives shareholders more time to pay. It also reflects that the company does not need all the money immediately, spreading the cash inflow across two stages instead of demanding it upfront.

The rights issue was first approved by the board on September 28, when the company filed its draft letter of offer.

The Rs 1,000 crore issue sits within a broader Rs 1,500 crore fundraising enablement. Of that, Rs 780 crore was already raised through a qualified institutional placement in June 2026.

Chairman and Managing Director Bhavish Aggarwal will subscribe to his full entitlement, according to sources close to the transaction.

On Sunday, the company said Aggarwal had pledged a 4.32% stake solely to fund his subscription, with no shares being sold. The promoter group together holds around 32.97% of the company.

Proceeds from the issue could go towards reducing debt and putting the company on the path to cash breakeven without further funding in the near future, a banking source tracking the development told PTI.

Further details, including the record date that will determine which shareholders are eligible, will be disclosed as per applicable regulations.

For shareholders, the arithmetic is straightforward but worth spelling out. A rights issue offers new shares in proportion to existing holdings, so an investor who participates fully keeps the same percentage ownership and simply adds shares at Rs 27 apiece.

An investor who does nothing faces the opposite outcome. Their stake gets diluted as new shares are issued to those who subscribe, and the market price typically adjusts towards the blended value after the issue.

That is why promoter participation carries weight. Aggarwal taking his full entitlement — and pledging stake to fund it rather than selling any shares — signals confidence at the exact price the board has set.

The 26% discount also lowers the risk of the issue failing. Rights issues at deep discounts rarely struggle to find takers, since shareholders can sell their entitlement in the market if they do not want to invest more.

For Ola Electric, the structure as a whole suggests patience rather than distress: raise a buffer, let shareholders pay in stages, and point the company toward cash breakeven.
