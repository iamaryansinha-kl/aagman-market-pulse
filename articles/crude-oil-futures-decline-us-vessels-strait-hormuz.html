Crude Oil Futures Decline As US Vessels Transit Strait Of Hormuz Under Project Freedom

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/July Brent crude futures fell to $112.98, down 1.28%, and June WTI crude dropped to $104.12, down 2.16%, on May 5, 2026.
  • 02/Two US-flagged merchant vessels transited the Strait of Hormuz under US Central Command's Project Freedom, easing immediate supply fears.
  • 03/Analysts warn relief is temporary as Iran strikes UAE's Fujairah port and few inbound vessels are entering the Persian Gulf.

Crude oil futures declined sharply on Tuesday morning, May 5, 2026. July Brent crude futures traded at $112.98 per barrel, down 1.28%, while June WTI crude futures fell to $104.12, down 2.16%.

On the Multi Commodity Exchange (MCX), May crude oil futures dropped to ₹9,950, down 1.06% from the previous close of ₹10,057. June futures fell to ₹9,587 against ₹9,687, down 1.03%.

The decline followed confirmation from US Central Command that two US-flagged merchant vessels successfully transited the Strait of Hormuz. US Navy guided-missile destroyers are now operating in the Arabian Gulf under Project Freedom.

"American forces are actively assisting efforts to restore transit for commercial shipping," US Central Command said in a post on X on May 4, 2026. The operation marks the first escorted passage through the critical chokepoint since Iran effectively closed it.

However, analysts caution that any price relief will be temporary. Warren Patterson, Head of Commodities Strategy at ING Think, and Ewa Manthey, Commodities Strategist, said continuation of Project Freedom risks further escalation.

"Any relief from stranded vessels making their way through the Strait will be temporary, with very few inbound vessels moving into the Persian Gulf," they wrote in their Commodities Feed for Tuesday.

The analysts also noted that ICE Brent had rallied 5.8% on Monday to settle above $114 per barrel. This reflects the broader geopolitical tension that remains unresolved despite the limited vessel passage.

Iran has resumed attacks on neighbouring infrastructure. The UAE intercepted several Iranian missiles, and Fujairah port — which handles nearly 1.7 million barrels per day of crude exports — was hit by a drone.

"We are seeing the first signs of the ceasefire between the US and Iran breaking down amid a re-escalation in the Persian Gulf," Patterson and Manthey added. US President Donald Trump suggested the conflict could continue for another two to three weeks.
