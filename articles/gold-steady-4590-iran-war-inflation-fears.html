Gold Steady Near $4,590 After Drop as Iran War Fuels Inflation Fears

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Spot gold dipped 0.2% to $4,589.04 an ounce, stabilizing after a 2.4% drop over the previous two sessions
  • 02/The indefinite closure of the Strait of Hormuz continues to heighten inflation risks and keep central banks hawkish
  • 03/Ole Hansen at Saxo Bank says reopening the strait and a drop in oil would be the biggest short-term upside catalyst for gold

Spot gold steadied near $4,590 an ounce on April 29, 2026. The metal had fallen 2.4% over the previous two sessions to its lowest level in almost four weeks.

President Donald Trump said Iran has asked the US to lift a naval blockade of the Strait of Hormuz. The two-month war has upended global energy supplies and pushed crude oil prices sharply higher.

Inflation risks are escalating due to the ongoing energy supply shock. Central banks may keep interest rates steady for longer or even hike them, which weighs on non-yielding bullion.

The Federal Reserve is widely expected to hold rates at its meeting ending Wednesday. Investors are also watching whether Jerome Powell will remain at the board after his term ends.

Some technical selling emerged after gold broke below support around $4,650, said Ole Hansen. He is head of commodity strategy at Saxo Bank A/S.

Hansen added that the market's immediate focus remains on mediation efforts. He said a reopening of the strait and a subsequent drop in oil would be the biggest short-term upside catalyst for metals.

Gold has lost about 13% since the conflict began at the end of February. Rising bond yields have increased the opportunity cost of holding the metal.

Silver rose 0.1% to $73.13 an ounce in early trade. Platinum and palladium were little changed alongside a steady Bloomberg Dollar Spot Index.
