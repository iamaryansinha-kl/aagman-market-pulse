India Lays Groundwork for Big Business Boost Along the Coasts

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

India is looking to its coastlines for the next wave of growth.

The government is laying the groundwork for a major business boost along the country's coastlines, involving policy reforms, infrastructure development, and investment incentives to develop coastal economic zones and port-linked industrial corridors. The plan aims to leverage India's 7,500-kilometre coastline for manufacturing, trade, and logistics, replicating the success of coastal manufacturing hubs in China and Southeast Asia.

Coastal economic zones are the centrepiece of the strategy. These zones, planned along major ports like Jawaharlal Nehru Port Trust, Chennai, Visakhapatnam, Paradip, and Mundra, will offer tax incentives, simplified regulations, and world-class infrastructure to attract manufacturing and export-oriented industries. The goal is to create clusters that can compete globally on cost and efficiency.

Port-linked industrial corridors are being developed. The government is building rail and road connections between ports and industrial hinterlands to reduce logistics costs, which are currently among the highest in the world. The Sagarmala programme, which has been running for several years, is being expanded to include more ports and deeper hinterland connectivity.

The shipping and maritime sector will see major upgrades. India plans to increase its shipping capacity, develop shipbuilding yards, and attract international shipping lines to use Indian ports as transshipment hubs. Currently, a large portion of India's container traffic is transshipped through ports in Colombo, Singapore, and Dubai, adding cost and time.

Environmental concerns are part of the planning. Coastal development must balance economic growth with environmental protection, particularly for fragile ecosystems like mangroves, coral reefs, and fishing grounds. The government has stated that environmental clearances will be streamlined but not compromised, and that sustainable development practices will be mandated in coastal zones.

State governments are key partners. Coastal states like Gujarat, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, and Odisha are being encouraged to develop their own coastal investment policies and participate in the national programme. The success of the initiative will depend on coordination between the central government, state governments, and private investors.
