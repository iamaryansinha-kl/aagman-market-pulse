CBI Arrests DGCA Official, Reliance Exec in Drone Bribery Case

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/CBI arrests DGCA Deputy Director Mudavath Devula and Reliance consultant Bharat Mathur in ₹2.5 lakh drone bribery case on April 19, 2026
  • 02/Searches at four locations recovered ₹37 lakh cash, gold and silver coins, and multiple digital devices from the accused
  • 03/Case involves three drone import applications for Asteria Aerospace, a Reliance Jio Platforms subsidiary acquired for $2.45 million in 2019

The CBI arrested Mudavath Devula, Deputy Director General at DGCA's Airworthiness Directorate, and Bharat Mathur, a consultant linked to Reliance Industries and Asteria Aerospace, on April 19, 2026. The arrests relate to an alleged bribery of ₹2.5 lakh to secure approvals for drone imports.

The case was registered on April 18, 2026, following a tip-off that Devula demanded illegal gratification to process pending applications. Mathur allegedly contacted Devula on March 18 to enquire about three drone import files for Asteria Aerospace.

The CBI FIR states the bribe was fixed at ₹5 lakh per file, totaling ₹15 lakh for three applications. Devula allegedly asked Mathur to deliver the money at Essex Farms near IIT Delhi Flyover on the evening of April 18, 2026.

During the trap operation, investigators arrested both individuals and seized ₹2.5 lakh on the spot. Subsequent searches at four locations recovered additional cash worth ₹37 lakh, gold and silver coins, and multiple digital devices.

Asteria Aerospace is a Bengaluru-based drone technology company founded by Nihar Vartak and Neel Mehta in 2011. Reliance acquired it for $2.45 million in 2019, and Jio Platforms now holds a 74% stake as of March 2024.

Asteria provides aerial intelligence services to agriculture, construction, telecom and oil sectors. It has deployed over 400 drones. Revenue surged from ₹1.1 crore in FY2020 to ₹40 crore in FY2024.

Reliance Industries said it did not authorise the transactions and was not aware of Mathur's actions. "Mr Bharat Mathur was engaged as a consultant. We are not aware of any transaction involving him of the nature being referred to, nor have we approved any such unauthorised transaction," a company spokesperson stated.

The CBI has booked both accused under the Prevention of Corruption Act and the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023. India currently has approximately 39,000 permitted drones operating under the Drone Rules 2021.
