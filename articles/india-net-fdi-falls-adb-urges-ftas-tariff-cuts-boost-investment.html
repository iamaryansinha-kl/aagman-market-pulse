India's Net FDI Drops 74% in 3 Years; ADB Urges FTAs and Tariff Cuts to Reverse Flow

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •• India's net FDI fell from $38.6 billion in FY22 to just $10.2 billion in FY24, a drop of roughly 74%
  • •• ADB Chief Economist Albert Park recommends FTAs, lower import tariffs, and stronger manufacturing infrastructure to attract capital
  • •• Middle East crisis expected to shave 0.6 percentage points off India's GDP growth, bringing it to 6.3% for FY26

India's net Foreign Direct Investment has collapsed over the past three years, falling from $38.6 billion in 2021-22 to $28 billion in FY23 and crashing further to just $10.2 billion in FY24.

Net FDI, which is calculated as inflows minus outflows, dropped into single digits at roughly $1 billion for the full year FY25.

A modest recovery was visible during April-December of FY26, when net FDI improved to $3 billion, but the overall trajectory remains deeply concerning for policymakers.

Albert Park, Chief Economist at the Asian Development Bank, told PTI that India must aggressively pursue Free Trade Agreements, continue reducing import tariffs, and improve the overall business environment to make foreign investments competitive again.

Park emphasized that strengthening the manufacturing ecosystem through dedicated industrial zones with robust infrastructure and integrated facilities would make it easier for foreign firms to operate efficiently in India.

He also highlighted the importance of smart urban planning, noting that integrated planning addressing logistical, regulatory, and human capital needs is critical for sustained investment growth.

The Asian Development Bank projects India's GDP growth to remain robust at 6.9% for the current fiscal year, rising to 7.3% by FY28, driven primarily by strong domestic demand.

However, the ongoing Middle East crisis is expected to shave 0.6 percentage points off India's GDP growth, bringing it down to 6.3% for FY26, while also stoking inflation significantly.

On crude oil, Park warned that prices are likely to stay higher for longer due to disruption from the prolonged Middle East conflict, with the ADB's reference scenario projecting an average of $96 per barrel for 2026 and $80 per barrel for 2027.

Park appreciated India's reforms in labour and GST, saying the government should continue this momentum while remaining vigilant about global uncertainty that always triggers capital flight to safety.
