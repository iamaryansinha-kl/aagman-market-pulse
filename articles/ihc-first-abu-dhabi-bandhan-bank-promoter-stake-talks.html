IHC, First Abu Dhabi In Talks For Stake In Bandhan Bank Promoter BFSL

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Abu Dhabi's IHC is in early talks to buy into Bandhan Financial Services, the promoter of Bandhan Bank holding over a third of the lender.
  • 02/The deal would give early investors an exit: GIC and IFC jointly hold 30.3% of BFSL, a stake worth an estimated $500-600 million.
  • 03/BFSL has been working with Jefferies to find strategic investors for a 25-32% stake; First Abu Dhabi Bank and Qatar Investment Authority are also in the mix.

IHC, the investment company of Abu Dhabi's national security adviser and deputy ruler Sheikh Tahnoon bin Zayed al-Nahyan, is among parties in early-stage discussions to buy into Bandhan Financial Services Ltd. BFSL is the promoter of Bandhan Bank and owns a little more than a third of the private lender, people aware of the development said.

The move is aimed at giving early-stage financial investors an opportunity to sell their stakes. Economic Times was first to report in its March 13 edition that BFSL was looking to give its long-term investors an exit.

BFSL, led by Bandhan founder Chandra Shekhar Ghosh, has been working with advisers Jefferies to find strategic investors for a 25-32 per cent stake in the holding company.

The transaction would open up an exit for GIC of Singapore and the International Finance Corporation, backers of the firm for more than a decade. Together, the two held 30.3 per cent of BFSL at the end of March and have been seeking a full or partial exit. Their stake alone could be worth $500-600 million, the people cited said.

Other investors include Sidbi, which owns 8.1 per cent. The Financial Inclusion Trust and North East Financial Inclusion Trust together own 40.7 per cent, while the Bandhan Employees' Welfare Trust holds 14.6 per cent. If other shareholders also choose to exit, the transaction could become much larger.

BFSL has two subsidiaries. Bandhan Technologies is an IT services and AI-led technology company, while Bandhan Financial Holdings is the holding company of Bandhan Bank, Bandhan AMC, Bandhan Mutual Fund Trustee, Bandhan Investment Managers (Mauritius) and Bandhan Life Insurance.

As of March 31, BFSL owned 59.98 per cent of Bandhan AMC and 97.16 per cent of Bandhan Life Insurance. Singapore's GIC and Chrys Capital are also investors in the asset management firm.

Bandhan Financial Holdings held a 37.5 per cent stake in the bank at the end of June. Under the bank's licensing agreement with the RBI, it must reduce this to 26 per cent by 2030.

Banks and sovereign funds across the Gulf have been widely canvassed for a stake in BFSL. Those said to be in the mix include First Abu Dhabi Bank and the Qatar Investment Authority, along with private equity buyout funds such as Carlyle and EQT.

Bandhan Bank chairman Ghosh declined to comment, saying the company does not comment on market speculation. A First Abu Dhabi Bank spokesperson said it does not comment on individual client relationships, transactions or market speculation as a matter of policy.

The discussions remain at an early stage with no guarantee of a transaction. One investor examining the deal said discussions are picking up momentum but the contours are evolving, and that it is a complicated deal where a lot depends on the regulator.

Bandhan began as a microfinance lender in 2001 and became a full-fledged private sector bank in August 2015. It has one of India's largest microfinance loan books at Rs 35,000 crore as of June 30.

The bank's total loan book stands at Rs 1.56 lakh crore with deposits of Rs 1.65 lakh crore. Its capital adequacy ratio is 18.2 per cent with a common equity tier-1 ratio of 17.5 per cent, and it serves 31.8 million customers through 1,988 branches.

The stock has had a rough year on guidance cuts. The bank slashed its FY27 return-on-assets guidance while announcing June-quarter results in July, triggering a 15 per cent intraday plunge to Rs 177.50, its sharpest decline since the March 2020 Covid crash.

On Monday, the stock ended at Rs 157, up 13.32 per cent year to date, giving a market value of Rs 25,351.40 crore. The July guidance cut was driven by elevated technology costs and a moderation in net interest margin outlook, with FY27 loan growth cut to the lower end of the 14-15 per cent range.

JP Morgan analysts Anuj Singla and Harsh Wardhan Modi noted after the results that Bandhan called the growth cut pre-emptive, with on-ground asset-quality trends benign and improving delinquency across vintages, and credit cost guidance retained at 1.6-1.8 per cent.

The interest fits a broader Gulf corridor push into Indian financial services. Following Emirates NBD's acquisition of RBL Bank, West Asian institutions have been actively scouting Indian lending and fintech opportunities. Emirates NBD completed a $2.75 billion investment in RBL Bank in June, while IHC has committed $1 billion for a controlling stake in Sammaan Capital.

Commerce minister Piyush Goyal said last month that the UAE has already invested $25 billion in India as the seventh-largest source of FDI, with intent to invest another $25 billion, and aims to take total investments to $100 billion.

Bandhan Technologies, for its part, recently opened an office in Abu Dhabi and signed a strategic partnership in September 2026 with KMK Group, a subsidiary of the Khalid Al Hamed Group.

For Bandhan Bank investors, a Gulf sovereign entry at the promoter level would be a validation signal but also brings questions about control, regulatory approvals and the bank's microlending concentration in eastern India.

GLOSSARY: IHC: International Holding Company — an Abu Dhabi investment conglomerate led by Sheikh Tahnoon bin Zayed al-Nahyan. Promoter entity: The holding company that owns a controlling stake in a listed firm and is subject to regulatory stake norms. Sovereign wealth fund: A state-owned investment vehicle that invests in companies and assets globally. Return on assets (RoA): A profitability ratio showing how efficiently a bank generates profit from its balance sheet. NIM: Net interest margin — the difference between interest earned and interest paid, relative to interest-earning assets. Credit cost: The annual charge a bank expects to take on bad loans, expressed as a percentage of its loan book.
