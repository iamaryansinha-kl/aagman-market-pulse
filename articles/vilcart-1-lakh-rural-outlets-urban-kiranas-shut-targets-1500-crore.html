VilCart Reaches 1 Lakh Rural Outlets as 2 Lakh Urban Kiranas Shut; Targets ₹1,500 Crore by FY27

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/VilCart has built a network of over 1 lakh kirana stores across 30,000 villages, even as 2 lakh urban kiranas shut in the past year.
  • 02/The Bengaluru-based company reported ₹1,173 crore revenue in FY26 and is targeting ₹1,500 crore by FY27.
  • 03/VilCart is exploring a $25-30 million fundraise over the next two years to deepen rural distribution and expand into Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh, and Bihar.

VilCart has scaled to over 1 lakh kirana stores across 30,000 villages in rural India. The Bengaluru-based company is building density in southern India while urban kirana stores face mounting pressure.

About 2 lakh kirana stores have shut across India in the past year. Data from the All India Consumer Products Distributors Federation (AICPDF) and firms such as Redseer and Grant Thornton Bharat shows closures concentrated in urban markets.

"About 1.5% of the country's estimated 13 million outlets have closed, mainly in urban areas, as quick commerce takes over high-margin categories," said Prasanna Kumar C (CPK), Founder and CEO of VilCart.

VilCart reported ₹1,173 crore in revenue for FY26, up from ₹1,120 crore in FY25. The company is targeting ₹1,500 crore by FY27, with private labels expected to drive profitability.

Private label revenue has expanded from 5% in April 2025 to 18% by April 2026. The target is 25-30% by FY27, implying ₹375-450 crore in private label sales on a ₹1,500 crore topline.

"Private labels are central to how this business becomes profitable," said Prasanna Kumar. "Distribution gives us access, but owning the product is what drives margins."

VilCart services its store network every 24-48 hours with a reported 99% fulfilment rate. Repeat orders average 3.8-4.5 times a month at ₹4,500-₹5,000 per order.

The company reported a negative EBITDA margin of about 6.8% in FY25, implying losses exceeding ₹65 crore. That has narrowed to around 4.5% in FY26 as private labels scale.

Backed by Asia Impact, NABVENTURES, and Spark Capital, VilCart has raised about $26 million so far. It is now exploring a $25-30 million fundraise to expand into Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh, and Bihar.

The rural-to-rural model sources directly from farmer producer organisations and rural manufacturers. It cuts out three to four layers of intermediaries, improving farm-gate prices while keeping consumer costs competitive.

VilCart is also evaluating a shift to electric vehicles for last-mile delivery. The company operates around 300 diesel vehicles and sees fuel cost savings as a path to EV viability.

"The model is now working across markets, and the next phase is about scaling it faster," said Prasanna Kumar. "This is not just about selling products, it is about building distribution where it does not exist."
