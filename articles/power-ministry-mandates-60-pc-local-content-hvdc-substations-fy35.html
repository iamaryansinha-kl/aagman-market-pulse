Power Ministry mandates 60% local content in HVDC substations by FY35 in phased rollout

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/India's Power Ministry has mandated a phased Minimum Local Content (MLC) roadmap for HVDC substations, starting at 30% by FY28 and climbing to 60% by FY35.
  • 02/The 32.3 GW HVDC pipeline in the National Electricity Plan FY23-32 already has 14.5 GW tendered, with inter-regional capacity set to jump from 119 GW to 168 GW by FY32.
  • 03/Watch Hitachi Energy India, Siemens Energy India, GE Vernova T&D India, and BHEL as the limited pool of qualified converter transformer suppliers races to localise.

India's Ministry of Power has issued a revised roadmap under the Public Procurement (Preference to Make in India) framework for high-voltage direct current (HVDC) Line Commutated Converter (LCC) substations. The order, notified on May 2, 2026, mandates achieving 30% Minimum Local Content (MLC) by FY28, scaling to 40% by FY30, 50% by FY32, and 60% by FY35.

The phased approach replaces an earlier, less granular mandate of up to 60% domestic value addition. Under the revised order, EPC or turnkey projects for HVDC substations must now meet progressively stricter localisation thresholds tied to fiscal year deadlines.

India operates one of the world's largest transmission networks, spanning over 425,000 circuit km of lines and more than 900,000 MVA of transformation capacity at 220 kV and above. As renewable capacity surges, the grid is adding HVDC, GIS, STATCOMs, synchronous condensers, and advanced substation systems.

The National Electricity Plan (NEP) FY23-32 outlines a 32.3 GW HVDC pipeline. Of this, approximately 14.5 GW has already been tendered and awarded. The plan also calls for 191,000 circuit km of new transmission lines and 1,270 GVA of additional transformation capacity over the decade.

Inter-regional transmission capacity is targeted to rise from the current 119 GW to 143 GW by 2027 and further to 168 GW by 2032. In addition, 33 GW of HVDC bi-pole links are planned to support long-distance bulk power transfer from renewable-rich states to load centres.

According to Motilal Oswal Financial Services (MOFSL), transformer manufacturers in India are experiencing incremental opportunities from rising HVDC project share. These projects require specialised, high-value converter transformers and reactors supplied by a limited pool: Hitachi Energy India, Siemens Energy India, GE Vernova T&D India, and Bharat Heavy Electricals Ltd (BHEL: NSE).

The move extends the PPP-MII framework beyond high-volume items like smart meters into complex, capital-intensive transmission infrastructure. The ministry's 2021 PPP-MII order and subsequent amendments first brought energy meters and smart meters under local content rules; HVDC represents the most advanced expansion yet.

The localisation push originates from the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT) Make in India order, first issued in 2017 and revised in 2020. It aims to reduce import dependence in strategic sectors, encourage domestic manufacturing, and foster technology partnerships for critical HVDC components such as converter transformers, valves, and control systems.

The notification may reshape bidding dynamics for large transmission projects, where global suppliers have historically dominated. With only a handful of qualified domestic players capable of manufacturing converter-grade equipment, the 9-year runway to 60% MLC gives both incumbents and new entrants time to scale up.
