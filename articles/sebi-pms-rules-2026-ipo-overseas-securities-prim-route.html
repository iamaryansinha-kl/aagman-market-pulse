Sebi Notifies New PMS Rules Allowing IPO, Overseas Investments

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Sebi has notified the Portfolio Managers Regulations, 2026, replacing the 2020 framework effective April 1, 2027.
  • •- Portfolio managers can now invest in IPOs, primary market debt issuances, listed securities, money market instruments and mutual funds on behalf of clients.
  • •- The rules also permit investment in foreign securities, unlisted securities and exchange-traded derivatives.
  • •- A new PRIM route lets portfolio managers invest client funds in direct plans of Indian mutual funds, ETFs, index funds and SIFs with a Rs 25 lakh minimum.
  • •- The framework introduces Independent Fund Managers and relaxes principal officer eligibility to graduates.

Markets regulator Sebi has notified a new portfolio management services framework that broadens where portfolio managers can put client money, adds a dedicated route for investing in mutual funds, and relaxes entry requirements for key personnel — the most comprehensive reset of the PMS industry since the existing regulations came into force.

The Securities and Exchange Board of India (Portfolio Managers) Regulations, 2026, which replace the 2020 framework, will become effective from April 1, 2027, according to the notification dated October 7.

Under the new framework, portfolio managers will be permitted to invest in IPOs, primary market issuance of debt securities, securities listed or traded on a stock exchange, money market instruments, and mutual funds on behalf of their clients. The permission list extends further: portfolio managers can also invest in foreign securities, unlisted securities and exchange-traded derivative instruments.

For large-value accredited investors, the rules open an even wider door. A portfolio manager may offer discretionary, non-discretionary or advisory services for investment of up to one hundred per cent of the assets under management of large value accredited investors in unlisted securities, subject to appropriate disclosures in the disclosure document and terms agreed between client and manager.

The most closely watched addition is PRIM — the Portfolio Managers Route for Investing in Mutual Fund units. The new route enables portfolio managers to invest clients' funds in direct plans of mutual funds, including ETFs, index funds and Specialised Investment Funds of Indian asset management companies, with a minimum investment threshold of Rs 25 lakh. Entities exclusively offering PRIM will be required to maintain a minimum net worth of Rs 2 crore.

PRIM formalises something the industry has long wanted: a regulated wrapper for managers whose strategies are built on mutual fund portfolios. Until now, such offerings sat in a grey zone between PMS and advisory, and the new route gives them a defined regulatory home with a clear minimum ticket and capital requirement.

Sebi also introduced the concept of Independent Fund Managers, who can manage and operate client portfolios in association with registered portfolio managers, while the registered portfolio manager retains responsibility and liability for their activities — a structure that could expand the pool of professional talent available to PMS firms without diluting accountability.

On the compliance side, the regulator relaxed the educational qualification requirement for principal officers. Under the new rules, even graduates will be eligible to function as principal officer, lowering a barrier that had previously restricted who could lead a portfolio management operation.

The notification follows the Sebi board's clearance to the proposal in September, and gives the industry roughly six months to restructure offerings, update disclosure documents and re-paper client agreements before the April 2027 effective date.

For clients, the practical effect is a wider menu. PMS investors — typically high-net-worth individuals with tickets of Rs 50 lakh and above — will be able to access primary market opportunities and overseas securities through their managed portfolios in a way the 2020 framework did not cleanly permit. The unlisted securities window for large-value accredited investors, in particular, pulls PMS products closer to the alternative investment fund format, potentially blurring the line between the two categories over time.

For the industry, the rules arrive as PMS competes with both the surging mutual fund complex and the rapidly growing AIF segment. A broader permitted-investment universe, a formal MF route and easier principal officer norms all point in one direction: Sebi wants the portfolio management industry to scale, and is giving it the regulatory room to do so.

The Independent Fund Manager provision deserves a closer look, because it changes who can run money inside a PMS structure. Until now, the principal officer and the registered portfolio manager carried the full weight of portfolio decisions. Under the new framework, professional fund managers can operate client portfolios in association with a registered firm, creating a path for star managers to launch strategies under an existing licence rather than build a full registration from scratch. Sebi was careful to keep accountability intact: the registered portfolio manager retains responsibility and liability for the independent fund manager's activities, so firms cannot outsource their regulatory obligations along with their investment decisions.

The unlisted securities window works the same way — opportunity paired with a guardrail. Permitting up to one hundred per cent of a large value accredited investor's assets in unlisted securities makes PMS a credible vehicle for pre-IPO and private-market exposure, a space that has until now been the near-exclusive territory of alternative investment funds. The guardrail is the client category: the full-allocation window applies only to large value accredited investors, the regulatory classification for the most sophisticated, largest-ticket clients, and only with explicit disclosures and agreed terms.

For existing portfolio managers, the next six months are an operational sprint. Disclosure documents must be revised to reflect the new investment powers, client agreements re-papered where strategies will expand into IPOs or overseas securities, and principal officer plans reassessed under the relaxed graduation norm. Firms that treat April 1, 2027 as a deadline rather than a launch date will find the transition considerably cheaper.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT DOES THE NEW SEBI PMS FRAMEWORK ALLOW?
Portfolio managers can invest in IPOs, primary market debt issuance, listed securities, money market instruments, mutual funds, foreign securities, unlisted securities and exchange-traded derivatives on behalf of clients.
WHEN DO THE NEW RULES TAKE EFFECT?
The Securities and Exchange Board of India (Portfolio Managers) Regulations, 2026 replace the 2020 framework and become effective from April 1, 2027.
WHAT IS THE PRIM ROUTE?
PRIM is the Portfolio Managers Route for Investing in Mutual Fund units, letting portfolio managers invest client funds in direct plans of Indian mutual funds including ETFs, index funds and SIFs, with a Rs 25 lakh minimum investment threshold.
WHAT CHANGED FOR PRINCIPAL OFFICERS?
Sebi relaxed the educational qualification requirement, making even graduates eligible to function as principal officer of a portfolio management firm. GLOSSARY: - PMS (Portfolio Management Services): Regulated discretionary or advisory investment services for high-net-worth clients, with a minimum ticket size set by Sebi. - PRIM: Portfolio Managers Route for Investing in Mutual Fund units, a new Sebi-defined channel for PMS investments into MF direct plans. - Discretionary service: A mandate where the portfolio manager makes investment decisions without seeking client approval for each trade. - Unlisted securities: Shares or instruments not traded on a recognised stock exchange, typically less liquid than listed ones. - SIF (Specialised Investment Fund): A Sebi-regulated mutual fund category designed for sophisticated investors with differentiated strategies.
