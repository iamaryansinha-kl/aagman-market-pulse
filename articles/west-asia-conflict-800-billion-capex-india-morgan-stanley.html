West Asia Conflict May Trigger $800 Billion Capex Boost For India

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Morgan Stanley projects $800 billion in additional cumulative investments for India over the next five years due to West Asia geopolitical tensions
  • 02/Defence spending could rise from 2% to 2.5% of GDP by FY2031, while oil import dependence at 85% remains a key vulnerability
  • 03/60% of new capex expected in energy transition, defence manufacturing and data centres; GDP growth seen at 6.5-7%

Morgan Stanley has projected a sharp rise in India's domestic investments due to ongoing West Asia conflict. The global brokerage sees $800 billion in additional cumulative capex over the next five years.

"We raise our investment rate forecast to 37.5 per cent of GDP in FY2030," the report stated. The previous estimate stood at 36.5 per cent.

Nearly 60 per cent of this new capital expenditure is expected to flow into energy transition, defence manufacturing and data centres. These sectors could strengthen India's medium-term growth trajectory significantly.

Defence spending is likely to climb from 2 per cent of GDP to 2.5 per cent by FY2031. This aligns with the government's indigenisation push under the Make in India framework.

India's data centre sector may emerge as a major beneficiary. Global firms are actively seeking alternatives amid rising geopolitical uncertainty in the region.

However, India's 85 per cent crude oil import dependence and 50 per cent natural gas reliance remain critical vulnerabilities. The report flags these as the biggest economic risks if tensions persist.

"The central policy challenge is not to eliminate India's external dependence overnight, but to reduce concentration risk," the report noted. It added that strengthening domestic buffers is the priority.

The fertiliser sector faces similar pressure, with heavy import dependence for phosphatic and potassic variants. This leaves agriculture exposed to supply disruptions and price spikes.

Morgan Stanley maintained India's real GDP growth forecast at 6.5-7 per cent. Capex-led expansion is expected to support this trajectory despite near-term headwinds.
