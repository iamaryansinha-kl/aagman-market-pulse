Moody's Raises India FY27 GDP Growth Forecast to 7% From 6%, Citing Resilience to Middle East Shock

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

Moody's has joined the list of forecasters betting that India can absorb the oil shock and keep growing. The ratings agency raised its real GDP growth forecast for the current fiscal year to 7% from 6%, explicitly citing India's resilience amid the Middle East conflict, and expects India to grow faster than all other G-20 economies and similarly rated emerging markets.

The upgrade leans heavily on the first-quarter data. India's GDP expanded 7.8% in April-June of FY27, comfortably above the RBI's 7% forecast and the 7.1% median estimate in a Reuters poll. The composition was broad: private consumption grew 7.1%, gross fixed capital formation rose 11.9% to 34.3% of GDP from 31.4% a year earlier, manufacturing expanded 9.2%, and financial, real estate and professional services grew about 12%. Private-sector capital investment increased by more than Rs 5 trillion from a year earlier, and corporate capital expenditure rose 11%.

The shock the economy is absorbing is real. Brent crude crossed $100 a barrel this month, spiked towards $110 and is hovering around $102, with Hormuz disruption far from normal and Houthi attacks threatening another chokepoint. For an oil importer, that means a higher import bill, a pressured rupee near 96 to the dollar, and the risk of higher fuel costs feeding into inflation.

Moody's itself has warned that a prolonged conflict could push inflation above its 4.8% FY27 forecast, with higher subsidy spending, weaker Middle East remittances and softer external demand as transmission channels. El Nino-related food price pressures add another risk.

Then there is the tariff front. The US House has passed legislation that would give President Donald Trump the power to impose tariffs of up to 100% on countries that continue buying Russian energy. India has said it will protect its economic interests and energy security, and will not simply abandon Russian crude. The complication is that replacing those barrels is hard right now: Gulf supplies are disrupted, inventories are depleted and tanker costs are elevated. Russian crude imports had risen to about 1.8 million barrels per day in November 2025 before declining.

Fiscal data shows the government keeping up the investment cushion. India's April-July fiscal deficit was Rs 4.55 lakh crore, or 26.8% of the FY27 target, while capital expenditure rose to Rs 4.5 lakh crore from Rs 3.5 lakh crore a year earlier. Moody's cautioned that infrastructure and defence spending, combined with possible energy subsidies, could slow fiscal consolidation.

The agency is careful to note that resilience is not immunity. A 100% tariff would have very different consequences across sectors depending on their exposure to the American market, and the rupee's slide near 96 keeps pressure on the RBI, where traders have increased expectations of rate hikes.

Even so, the upgrade captures a shift in how forecasters view the economy. Before the April-June data, expensive oil and trade disruption were expected to weaken consumption and investment. Instead, investment accelerated, manufacturing held strong and services kept expanding, suggesting the economy has developed a better capacity to absorb external shocks than before.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT DID MOODY'S CHANGE IN ITS INDIA FORECAST?
It raised FY27 real GDP growth to 7% from 6%, citing resilience to the Middle East conflict, and expects India to outgrow all G-20 economies.
WHAT RISKS DOES MOODY'S STILL FLAG?
Inflation above its 4.8% forecast if oil stays high, El Nino food-price pressures, a wider current account deficit, weaker remittances, and the US House-passed bill threatening up to 100% tariffs on buyers of Russian energy.
WHAT DRIVEN Q1 GROWTH OF 7.8%?
Broad-based strength: consumption up 7.1%, gross fixed capital formation up 11.9% to 34.3% of GDP, manufacturing up 9.2%, and private capital investment up more than Rs 5 trillion year-on-year.
