CEA Nageswaran Flags Challenging H2 FY27 As Oil, Rate Pressures Bite

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/CEA V Anantha Nageswaran said the second half of FY27 will be challenging as oil, rates and global growth risks bite
  • 02/India's landed cost of fuel hit $117 a barrel in September versus $80-85 in June-August; July-August data point to around 7.3% GDP growth
  • 03/He defended the 7.8% Q1 FY27 print as credible, while admitting methodology changes may have added 30-40 basis points

India's Chief Economic Adviser V Anantha Nageswaran has warned that the second half of FY27 will be a difficult stretch. He flagged higher oil prices, rising interest rates and growing downside risks to global growth.

"The second half of the year is going to be a challenging one," Nageswaran said on September 30, 2026. He was speaking in a conversation with ReNew chairman and CEO Sumant Sinha.

September has already been difficult because of rising oil prices. India's landed cost of fuel reached $117 a barrel for the Indian basket, compared with $80-85 during June-August.

The jump from the $80-85 range to $117 a barrel marks a sharp squeeze in the space of weeks. A higher landed cost feeds directly into the import bill and adds to domestic price pressures.

The CEA also pointed to rising interest rates, both in the world and in India. Increased downside risks to global growth add another layer of pressure on the domestic outlook.

Despite the caution, July-August data point to GDP growth of around 7.3%. That offers an early read on the second quarter of FY27.

Nageswaran defended the 7.8% GDP growth print for Q1 FY27, the April-June quarter. He said the number did not come "out of thin air."

He pointed to GST collections, credit growth and exports in July and August as supporting evidence. The 7.8% reading also exceeded the Reserve Bank of India's 7% forecast for the quarter.

The CEA was also transparent about the methodology. Changes including the double-deflation method could have contributed around 30-40 basis points to the headline rate, because import prices had risen sharply.

On inflation, Nageswaran noted retail prices were running at 4.3%. That is within the RBI's 2-6% tolerance band.

The central bank will have to assess whether the rise in prices warrants a monetary policy response. The key question is whether pressures stay confined to food and energy, or spread through second-round effects. Confined shocks are easier to look through; broad-based ones are not.

The CEA said the boost from GST rate cuts was still being felt in the economy. He credited reforms implemented since 2014 for growth resilience, noting their impact took time to emerge. In his telling, the current resilience is a delayed dividend of that long reform grind.

The warning comes even as global institutions have raised India's FY27 growth forecasts. S&P Global Ratings and the Asian Development Bank lifted theirs to 7%, Fitch Ratings to 6.9%, and the OECD to 7.1%.

Moody's had earlier raised its projection to 7% from 6%. All of these sit above the RBI's 6.7% FY27 projection. India's economy grew 7.8% in 2025-26.

The contrast is striking: global agencies are upgrading India's full-year outlook even as the CEA braces for a harder six months. His message is that near-term headwinds, not the growth story itself, have worsened.

The near-term watchpoints are the oil price trajectory, the RBI's policy response, and the actual H2 GDP prints. The Q2 FY27 data will test the CEA's roughly 7.3% read. Confirmation of the challenging-H2 thesis will come from the full second-quarter and third-quarter prints due later this year.
