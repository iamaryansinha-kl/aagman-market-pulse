Airtel Money Debuts on LSE: Pound 529 Million OFS, Biggest in 5 Years

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Airtel Money raised pound 529 million selling 270 million existing shares at pound 1.96 apiece, the biggest listing on the London Stock Exchange in five years
  • 02/The offer valued the Africa fintech arm of Airtel Africa at around pound 5.3 billion, and was multiple times oversubscribed according to an Airtel spokesperson
  • 03/Shares briefly touched pound 2 in early conditional trading before slipping to pound 1.94; full admission to the LSE is expected on October 14

Airtel Money, the financial services arm of Airtel Africa and formally known as Airtel Mobile Commerce NV, debuted on the London Stock Exchange on Friday. The company raised roughly pound 529 million through an offer for sale of 270 million existing shares, making it the largest listing on the bourse in the last five years.

The listing valued Airtel Money at around pound 5.3 billion, with shares priced at pound 1.96 apiece. An Airtel spokesperson said the offer was multiple times oversubscribed, attracting strong demand from a broad mix of investors.

The sellers were minority shareholders. Qatar Investment Authority and TPG Inc sold approximately 270 million shares. The structure puts big institutional names on both sides of the trade.

Mastercard Asia Pacific has an additional 27 million shares available through an over-allotment option. That option opens once the stock is formally listed on October 14.

The anchor investor is the International Finance Corporation. It received an allotment of 34,285,714 shares worth around pound 67.2 million. Around 8 million shares were allocated to retail investors in the UK.

Early trading was two-sided. Shares briefly touched pound 2 apiece before falling to pound 1.94, slightly below the issue price of pound 1.96. Conditional trading began on Friday, and full trading with formal admission to the LSE is expected on October 14.

Airtel Money is a scaled fintech business operating across 13 sub-Saharan African markets. It serves approximately 53 million monthly active users, with a digital-first model focused on enabling mass financial inclusion. Payments at population scale, not a niche play, are the heart of the investment case.

For Indian readers, the listing carries a specific connection. Airtel Africa, the parent company, is the Africa telecom business promoted by Bharti Enterprises, the group founded by Sunil Mittal. Sunil Mittal, chairman of Bharti Enterprises, welcomed the listing: "I am delighted to welcome the successful listing of Airtel Money on the London Stock Exchange. This listing is a vote of confidence in the UK as an attractive global destination for investment, with a stable business and policy environment attractive for long-term investors."

The London listing gives Airtel Money access to a deep and institutional investor base, the spokesperson added. It is supported by a well-established governance and disclosure framework. The soft debut, with the stock slipping below its issue price in early trade, shows the market is pricing Airtel Money carefully even after an oversubscribed book.

The listing structure tells a second story beneath the debut. Every share sold came from existing holders — Qatar Investment Authority, TPG and, through an over-allotment option, Mastercard Asia Pacific — not from the company itself.

That makes the event a liquidity event for early backers rather than a capital raise. The sellers chose the London market's depth to exit at scale; the company gains a listed currency without diluting anyone.

The anchor allocation shows where the conviction sat. The International Finance Corporation, the World Bank Group's private-sector arm, took 34,285,714 shares worth around 67.2 million pounds — a long-horizon institution validating the franchise.

Retail investors in the UK received around 8 million shares, a deliberate inclusion that broadens the register beyond institutions and signals the offer was priced for a public market, not just book-builders.

The trading pattern on day one was honest but soft. Shares touched 2 pounds briefly before settling at 1.94 — a hair below the 1.96 issue price that multiple-times-oversubscribed books could not hold through the first session of conditional trading.

Conditional trading is not the full event. Formal admission to the exchange arrives on October 14, when the over-allotment shares from Mastercard Asia Pacific become available and the over-allotment greenshoe can stabilise the price.

The scale of what listed is easy to miss. Fifty-three million monthly active users across 13 sub-Saharan markets is a payments and fintech footprint larger than the customer base of most European banks.

For the Bharti group, the listing extends a pattern of treating telecom as infrastructure and everything layered on it as the business. Sunil Mittal's statement framed the debut as much about London's credibility as a venue as about Airtel Money itself.

The five-year superlative matters for context. No larger listing has priced on the London Stock Exchange since 2021 — a drought broken not by a British company but by an Africa-focused fintech promoted from India.

AIRTEL MONEY OPERATES ACROSS 13 SUB-SAHARAN AFRICAN MARKETS AND SERVES APPROXIMATELY 53 MILLION MONTHLY ACTIVE USERS. ITS DIGITAL-FIRST MODEL IS FOCUSED ON ENABLING MASS FINANCIAL INCLUSION. GLOSSARY: Offer for sale: A listing route where existing shareholders sell their stakes; the company raises no money itself. Conditional trading: Early dealing in a new listing before formal admission to the exchange. Over-allotment option (greenshoe): An option allowing extra shares to be sold after listing, typically used to stabilise the share price. Anchor investor: A large institutional investor allotted shares before an IPO opens, signalling confidence to the market. Monthly active user: A customer who used a service at least once in a given month — the standard fintech usage metric. Formal admission: The date a company's shares are officially listed on an exchange and full trading begins.
