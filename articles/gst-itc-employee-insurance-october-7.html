GST ITC on Employee Insurance: Council to Weigh 18% Credit

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/The GST Council is likely to consider on October 7 a proposal to let employers claim input tax credit on GST paid for employee insurance cover
  • 02/Group life and health policies bought by businesses for employees currently attract 18% GST, with no credit available to offset it
  • 03/No decision has been taken yet; the item is only on the agenda for consideration at the October 7 meeting

The GST Council is expected to take up a proposal at its October 7 meeting. The proposal would allow employers to claim input tax credit, or ITC, on GST paid for insurance cover provided to employees.

The change, if approved, would alter how one common business expense is taxed. It would touch nearly every organised employer that provides life or health cover to its workforce.

Under the Goods and Services Tax law, individuals who buy life or health policies for themselves or their families pay no GST. The premium carries a full exemption.

The treatment changes when a business buys the same kind of cover for its employees. Tax is charged on that cover, and until now the business could not claim any credit on it.

Currently, an 18% GST is levied on all such group insurance policies. That 18% sits on top of the premium and cannot be recovered.

Input tax credit is the mechanism that normally prevents tax from piling up in a supply chain. A business offsets the GST it pays on inputs against the GST it collects on sales.

Most business purchases qualify for this offset. Employee insurance premiums have been an exception, leaving the 18% as a real cost for the employer rather than a pass-through.

That makes group cover one of the few taxed inputs for which no relief exists. The effective cost of providing insurance stays higher than the headline premium.

The proposal before the Council would close that gap. Employers paying GST on employee policies would be able to claim the credit, bringing insurance in line with other business inputs.

Sources familiar with the proposal see a direct benefit for companies. "This will make insurance cheaper for every employer to provide," a source said.

The logic runs through the organised sector. Almost every organised employer provides some cover for its workforce, so the cost of providing that coverage comes down across the board.

A lower effective cost matters beyond corporate budgets. It should assist the spread of cover, extending insurance to more employees and their families.

It would also support the insurance industry itself. Cheaper group cover for employers means a wider base of policies and premiums over time.

For employees, the effect would be indirect but real. Companies facing a lower cost to insure their staff have less reason to trim coverage levels or shift premiums onto workers.

The proposal also removes an asymmetry built into the current structure. An individual buying a policy pays no GST, while a company buying the same protection pays 18% it cannot recover.

The October 7 item is a consideration point, not a decision. The Council could approve the proposal, modify it, or defer it to a later meeting.

Employers and insurers will track the agenda closely. The outcome on October 7 will determine whether the 18% GST on group cover becomes a recoverable input tax.

Until the Council speaks, the current rule stands. Group insurance for employees attracts 18% GST, and the credit remains out of reach.

WHAT IS THE CURRENT GST RATE ON GROUP INSURANCE POLICIES FOR EMPLOYEES?
AN 18% GST IS CURRENTLY LEVIED ON ALL GROUP INSURANCE POLICIES BOUGHT BY BUSINESSES FOR THEIR EMPLOYEES. THIS APPLIES TO BOTH LIFE AND HEALTH COVER PURCHASED BY AN EMPLOYER FOR ITS WORKFORCE.
CAN EMPLOYERS CLAIM INPUT TAX CREDIT ON THIS GST TODAY?
NO. UNTIL NOW, A BUSINESS BUYING LIFE OR HEALTH COVER FOR ITS EMPLOYEES COULD NOT CLAIM INPUT TAX CREDIT ON THE GST CHARGED. THIS IS WHY THE 18% TAX BECOMES A REAL COST FOR THE EMPLOYER RATHER THAN A RECOVERABLE INPUT TAX.
HOW ARE LIFE AND HEALTH POLICIES BOUGHT BY INDIVIDUALS TAXED?
UNDER THE GOODS AND SERVICES TAX LAW, GST IS EXEMPT WHEN INDIVIDUALS BUY LIFE OR HEALTH POLICIES FOR THEMSELVES OR THEIR FAMILY. THE SAME KIND OF COVER ATTRACTS 18% GST WHEN A BUSINESS BUYS IT FOR ITS EMPLOYEES.

SOURCES SAY THE PROPOSAL "WILL MAKE INSURANCE CHEAPER FOR EVERY EMPLOYER TO PROVIDE." SINCE ALMOST EVERY ORGANISED EMPLOYER PROVIDES COVER FOR ITS WORKFORCE, A LOWER COST SHOULD ASSIST THE SPREAD OF COVER AND SUPPORT THE INSURANCE INDUSTRY ITSELF.
