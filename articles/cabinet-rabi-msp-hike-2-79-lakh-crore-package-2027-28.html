Cabinet Clears Rs 2.79 Lakh Crore Package, Raises Rabi MSP for Six Crops; Wheat Up Rs 25 to Rs 2,610 Per Quintal

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- The Union Cabinet on September 30 approved higher MSPs for six rabi crops for the 2027-28 marketing year, inside a Rs 2.79 lakh crore package that also includes a Rs 54,000 crore Green Energy Corridor Phase III
  • •- Wheat MSP rose Rs 25 per quintal to Rs 2,610, a modest 1% increase versus last year's Rs 160 hike; masur, mustard and safflower saw the largest increases
  • •- Total payout to farmers is estimated at Rs 90,962 crore for 324 lakh tonnes of procurement

The Union Cabinet on Wednesday approved an increase in the minimum support prices for all six mandated rabi crops for the 2027-28 marketing year, Information and Broadcasting Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw said. The decision came as part of a larger Rs 2.79 lakh crore package cleared by the Cabinet, which also included a Rs 54,000 crore Green Energy Corridor Phase III and an extension of the Ujjwala LPG subsidy.

CONTEXT: MSP is the assured price at which government agencies buy crops from farmers if market rates fall below it. The rabi MSP announcement, typically made in late September or early October, sets the price floor ahead of winter sowing and signals the government's crop-preference and food-security priorities. The increase is recommended by the Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices (CACP), which factors in the cost of production, weather risks including El Nino concerns, and global and domestic price trends.

The government's wheat MSP decision was notably restrained this year. The support price for wheat, the main rabi crop, was raised by Rs 25 per quintal to Rs 2,610 for 2027-28, from Rs 2,585 in 2026-27. Vaishnaw said the production cost of wheat is Rs 1,264 per quintal, so the MSP remains at more than double the cost of production.

Among pulses and oilseeds, the increases were steeper. Masur (lentil) MSP was hiked by Rs 390 per quintal to Rs 7,390, from Rs 7,000 in the previous season. Rapeseed and mustard rose Rs 413 to Rs 6,613 per quintal, from Rs 6,200. Safflower saw the largest absolute increase, up Rs 675 to Rs 7,215 per quintal from Rs 6,540.

Barley MSP was increased to Rs 2,286 per quintal from Rs 2,150, a rise of Rs 136. Gram (chana) MSP went up by Rs 83 to Rs 5,958 per quintal, from Rs 5,875.

For context, last year's increases were larger across the board. For the 2026-27 marketing year, announced on October 1, 2025, wheat MSP had been raised by Rs 160 per quintal to Rs 2,585, while safflower saw a Rs 600 increase and masur Rs 300. The relatively modest wheat hike this year comes with central pool wheat stocks at comfortable levels and foodgrain production targets being trimmed amid El Nino concerns.

The government expects the new MSPs to result in a total payout of about Rs 90,962 crore to farmers for an estimated 324 lakh tonnes of procurement in the 2027-28 marketing year. That compares with an estimated Rs 84,263 crore payout for 297 lakh tonnes in the previous season.

The agriculture ministry has set a wheat production target of 121 million tonnes for the 2026-27 crop year (July-June). Rabi sowing usually begins in October, with harvesting starting in April, so the MSP announcement lands just ahead of planting decisions.

IMPACT: The payout bill matters for fiscal math and, indirectly, for food inflation, which the RBI is watching closely in its current hawkish phase. Higher MSPs for pulses and oilseeds are designed to incentivise crop diversification away from cereals, a long-standing policy goal. Comfortable wheat stocks at 33.3 million tonnes as of September 1, well above the buffer norm of 27.58 million tonnes, gave the government room to keep the wheat increase modest while still assuring farmers a price more than double their production cost.

For markets, the announcement is a routine but watched seasonal event. The package's non-MSP components, particularly the Rs 54,000 crore Green Energy Corridor Phase III, carry separate implications for power infrastructure and renewable energy transmission.

WHAT IS THE NEW WHEAT MSP FOR 2027-28?
The Cabinet approved a wheat MSP of Rs 2,610 per quintal for the 2027-28 rabi marketing year, an increase of Rs 25 from Rs 2,585 in 2026-27. The government said the production cost of wheat is Rs 1,264 per quintal, keeping the MSP at more than double the cost.
WHICH RABI CROP SAW THE LARGEST MSP INCREASE?
Safflower saw the largest absolute increase at Rs 675 per quintal to Rs 7,215. Among widely grown crops, masur rose Rs 390 to Rs 7,390 per quintal and rapeseed-mustard rose Rs 413 to Rs 6,613 per quintal.
HOW MUCH WILL THE MSP HIKES COST THE GOVERNMENT?
The payout to farmers is estimated at Rs 90,962 crore for an estimated 324 lakh tonnes of procurement in 2027-28, up from roughly Rs 84,263 crore for 297 lakh tonnes in the previous season. The MSP decision was part of a broader Rs 2.79 lakh crore package.
WHEN DOES RABI SOWING BEGIN?
Rabi sowing usually begins in October and harvesting starts in April. The MSP announcement sets the assured price floor just ahead of planting decisions, and also steers crop choice toward pulses and oilseeds.
WHY WAS THE WHEAT HIKE SMALLER THAN LAST YEAR'S?
The Rs 25 per quintal wheat increase is much smaller than the Rs 160 hike for 2026-27. Central pool wheat stocks stand at 33.3 million tonnes, well above the buffer norm, and the CACP recommendation factored in production costs, El Nino concerns and domestic and global price trends.
