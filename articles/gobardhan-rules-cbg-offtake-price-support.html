Govt Rolls Out GOBARdhan Rules: Assured CBG Offtake, Administered Price for 10 Years

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

India's compressed biogas industry is getting the one thing that has held it back: a buyer and a price. The Petroleum and Natural Gas Ministry has operationalised the Rs 23,731-crore GOBARdhan scheme with guidelines dated September 15, offering producers assured offtake of up to 100% of output, a government-administered price for at least 10 years, and capital assistance per project.

The scheme, which runs from 2026-27 to 2035-36, has six components: assured CBG offtake, a pricing framework, capital assistance, pipeline infrastructure, credit guarantee support and a CBG ecosystem challenge fund.

The demand side is now mandatory. City Gas Distribution entities will be required to procure and sell CBG within their overall gas supplies for CNG transport and domestic PNG segments, under a phased obligation of 3% in FY2026-27, 4% in FY2027-28 and 5% from FY2028-29 onwards. Eligible producers can opt for assured offtake of up to 100% of CBG available for sale, with gas mapped to CGD entities or to CBG clusters for injection into trunk pipelines.

The framework carries teeth. Firm offtake through the CGD route will be governed by tripartite agreements between the producer, the CGD entity and GAIL, which has been designated the synchro operator responsible for procurement, pooling and synchronisation. If a CGD entity fails to execute a firm offtake agreement within the stipulated period, the ministry may consider rationalising its APM or non-APM gas allocation to that extent.

On price, the government has set an administered CBG price of Rs 2,110 per million British thermal unit, equivalent to about Rs 98 per kg at 95% methane content, excluding taxes and compression charges. The price holds for a minimum of 10 years, until March 31, 2036, and can be revised prospectively by the Project Approval Board based on production economics and inflation. The government will also provide affordability support to GAIL for 10 years, capped at Rs 10 per kg of CBG procured, to bridge the gap between the administered procurement price and downstream affordability.

Capital assistance goes up to Rs 30 crore per project. Greenfield plants get Rs 1.25 crore per tonne per day of eligible CBG capacity, brownfield expansions get 50% of the greenfield rate for additional capacity, and existing biogas plants upgraded to CBG get Rs 0.60 crore per TPD subject to a Rs 5-crore ceiling. A separate component reimburses up to 50% of eligible expenditure on feedstock aggregation and organic-manure machinery, capped at Rs 0.75 crore per TPD. The assistance is milestone-linked, with the final tranche tied to verified plant performance through SCADA data; failure to meet milestones can trigger encashment of bank guarantees.

The scheme formally subsumes the earlier SATAT initiative from September 1, 2026, with existing SATAT liabilities honoured and retail outlets encouraged to migrate into the GOBARdhan framework.

For producers, the combination of a defined price and assured demand finally gives revenue visibility when seeking project finance. The government is betting the package can move CBG from a niche waste-to-energy segment into a larger component of India's gas system, while monetising agricultural residue and cattle dung into renewable gas and organic manure.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT IS THE ADMINISTERED CBG PRICE UNDER GOBARdhan?
Rs 2,110 per mmBtu, about Rs 98 per kg at 95% methane content, fixed for at least 10 years until March 31, 2036.
WHAT ARE THE CGD PROCUREMENT OBLIGATIONS?
City gas distributors must procure CBG at 3% of supplies for CNG/PNG in FY27, 4% in FY28 and 5% from FY29 onwards.
HOW MUCH CAPITAL ASSISTANCE IS AVAILABLE?
Up to Rs 30 crore per project, milestone-linked, with the final tranche tied to verified plant performance.
