HEG Bags Rs 217.56 Crore Lithium-Ion Battery Order From Indus Towers

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

HEG is finding a second act in batteries. Shares of the graphite electrode maker rallied up to 5% on Monday to a day's high of Rs 248.90 on the NSE after it said subsidiary Replus Engitech received orders from Indus Towers, formerly Bharti Infratel, for the supply of lithium-ion battery banks worth Rs 217.56 crore.

According to the exchange filing, the order size is inclusive of GST and the contracts are to be executed on or before March 31, 2027, or such extended date as may be mutually agreed. The company clarified the transaction does not involve related parties and no promoter or group company has any interest in the entity awarding the order.

Replus Engitech, a subsidiary of unlisted Bhilwara Energy, is a technology-driven manufacturer focused on India's battery energy storage system and electric vehicle markets. It specialises in advanced lithium-ion technology with in-house expertise in battery management systems and energy management systems, offering AI-enabled battery pack manufacturing for stationary storage across residential, commercial, industrial and transmission and distribution applications, as well as telecom, hybrid systems and e-mobility across 2W, 3W, light commercial vehicles and automated guided vehicles.

The order lands amid a corporate restructuring. Earlier this month, HEG announced a demerger into two separately listed entities: the graphite electrodes business moves to HEG Graphite, to be renamed HEG and run as a pure-play graphite electrodes company, while the existing listed company retains the advanced materials, battery energy solutions and green power businesses as HEG Advanced Materials. Shareholders receive one share in the spun-off company for every share held, a 1:1 ratio. Under the same scheme, Bhilwara Energy will be amalgamated into HEG, with HEG issuing eight equity shares for every seven Bhilwara Energy shares held.

The market backdrop is bruised. HEG's share price has declined 65% over the past month and 60.27% so far this calendar year, a slide largely tied to the demerger math, while the stock is down 52.43% over the past year. A Rs 217.56 crore telecom-infrastructure order will not rewrite that story on its own, but it signals where the advanced-materials half of the new HEG wants to compete.

Indus Towers, one of the world's largest telecom tower companies, has been steadily converting diesel backup at tower sites to battery and hybrid power, and contracts of this kind typically repeat as tower portfolios get upgraded.
