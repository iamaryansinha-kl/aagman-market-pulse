Sebi Eyes Position-Limit Review For Non-Agri Contracts

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Sebi is examining position limits for non-agricultural commodity derivative contracts to improve liquidity and depth, chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey said on Saturday, October 3.
  • 02/Consultations on the review are complete and guidelines will follow; Pandey gave no specific timeline or limit numbers.
  • 03/Last month, the Sebi board approved allowing foreign portfolio investors into physically settled non-agricultural commodity derivatives, subject to safeguards.

The Securities and Exchange Board of India (Sebi) is examining position limits for non-agricultural commodity derivative contracts, chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey said on Saturday. The review aims to improve liquidity and market depth without weakening risk controls, he said.

Pandey was speaking at an event organised by the Commodity and Capital Market Participants Association of India (CPAI), where the focus was reforms in the commodity derivatives market. Market design, he said, should allow contracts to gain scale.

Position limits cap the size of positions that market participants can build in a contract. The Sebi review will assess whether a recalibration of these limits can help non-agricultural contracts draw more trading interest, and Pandey stressed that risk controls will not be diluted in the process.

Consultations on the matter have been completed, the chairman said. Guidelines will follow, although Pandey did not specify a timeline or indicate what the revised limits could look like.

On agricultural commodities, Pandey flagged a related design issue. In some contracts, he said, physical settlement from the outset can impede market development. A phased approach could allow contracts to mature before physical settlement becomes mandatory.

Beyond contract design, Sebi is working to reduce structural friction in commodity markets. This includes engaging with stakeholders on GST-related issues that affect participants who give or receive commodities through exchange platforms.

On technology, Pandey said it should serve the specific needs of commodity markets, which include producers, commercial users, farmers, processors and physical hedgers. "Technology can improve access and efficiency, but its design must reflect their needs while preserving fair access and market integrity," he said.

On investor awareness, Sebi will strengthen efforts under Project Jagrook to spread awareness of commodity derivatives among farmers, farmer producer organisations (FPOs), MSMEs, hedgers and other market users. "Access without understanding is not inclusion," Pandey said.

The regulator is also working towards deeper and more liquid cash markets. Wider participation, stronger securities borrowing and lending, and efficient hedging and arbitrage are expected to improve price discovery and strengthen the interaction between cash and derivatives markets.

Pandey cautioned that simpler regulation should not mean weaker compliance. Robust controls over client funds, margins, reporting and supervision remain fundamental, and "trust and market integrity" must not be compromised.

Last month, the Sebi board approved a proposal to allow foreign portfolio investors (FPIs) to participate in physically settled, non-agricultural commodity derivative contracts. Such participation will be subject to safeguards.

Taken together, Pandey's comments point to a calibrated effort to deepen India's commodity derivatives ecosystem. Market participants will now await the detailed guidelines that Sebi has said will follow the completed consultations.
