Ambani, Mittal Join Global AI Commission With 40 World Leaders

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Reliance's Mukesh Ambani and Bharti's Sunil Bharti Mittal join AI for Good Global Commission as founding members
  • 02/Commission launched by Rwanda President Paul Kagame, Salesforce CEO Marc Benioff, and ITU Secretary-General Doreen Bogdan-Martin
  • 03/Over 40 founding members include Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, Microsoft President Brad Smith, Amazon CEO Andy Jassy

Reliance Industries Chairman Mukesh Ambani and Bharti Enterprises Chairman Sunil Bharti Mittal have joined the AI for Good Global Commission. The new international body brings together over 40 global leaders to promote responsible artificial intelligence development.

The commission was launched by Rwanda's President Paul Kagame, Salesforce Chair and CEO Marc Benioff, and International Telecommunication Union (ITU) Secretary-General Doreen Bogdan-Martin on Thursday. It aims to steer the responsible development and adoption of rapidly evolving AI technology.

"The Commission brings together more than 40 Founding Members — including Heads of State and Government, industry CEOs and top executives, and Heads of UN agencies — to help define practical pathways to strengthen trust, expand access and unlock AI's potential," the ITU said in a release.

The commission aims to reduce the gap among governments, industry and international institutions. Economies worldwide are working to strengthen AI adoption while addressing safety, governance, and equitable access issues.

Ambani and Mittal are among the commission's founding members alongside several prominent global technology leaders. The list includes Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, Microsoft Vice Chair and President Brad Smith, Amazon CEO Andy Jassy, Google's President for Research James Manyika, Qualcomm CEO Cristiano Amon, Anthropic co-founder Jack Clark, Accenture CEO Julie Sweet, Vodafone Group CEO Margherita Della Valle and Cohere CEO Aidan Gomez.

Roughly 2.2 billion people worldwide still lack internet access. One of the commission's key priorities will be to boost digital connectivity and enable broader participation in the AI economy. It will ensure developing countries have a voice in shaping AI's future.

Rwanda's President Paul Kagame, co-chair of the global commission, said: "One thing is certain: technology is supposed to be a force for good, and we have a responsibility to use it accordingly. Let us work together to reduce inequality, and allow more and more of our citizens to benefit from the good AI can deliver."

The commission will convene its inaugural meeting at the AI for Good Global Summit 2026. The summit will be held in Geneva from July 7 to July 10. The meeting will focus on defining practical pathways for responsible AI deployment across sectors and borders.
