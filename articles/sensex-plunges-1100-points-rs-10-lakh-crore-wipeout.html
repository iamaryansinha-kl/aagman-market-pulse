Sensex Plunges 1,100 Points From High, Rs 10 Lakh Crore Wiped Out

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- The Sensex fell about 630 points to near 72,600, more than 1,100 points below the day's high; the Nifty broke below 22,450.
  • •- Nearly Rs 10 lakh crore in market value was wiped out, with smallcaps and midcaps leading the collapse.
  • •- Four drivers: oil at $114, resumed FII selling, a broader-market bleed, and rising US rate-hike expectations.
  • •- A counter-case exists: the US 10-year yield has fallen to 4.96% and IPO refund money could lift the market next week.

Friday's gap-up open did not last an hour. The Sensex sank around 630 points to trade near 72,600 — more than 1,100 points below the day's high — while the Nifty 50 plunged roughly 260 points to break below 22,450, as soaring crude prices and the resumption of foreign investor selling collided on a day when the global backdrop offered no shelter. The damage tallied nearly Rs 10 lakh crore in erased market value, and the internal picture was uglier than the headline: the Nifty Smallcap 100 crashed around 4%, the Midcap 100 tumbled around 3%, and market breadth on the NSE ran ten-to-one in favour of decliners, with 3,081 stocks falling against just 297 advances. Only the Nifty IT index managed to stay green, and that with marginal gains. The Sensex leadership reflected the day's anxieties: Tata Steel, Adani Ports, BEL, Titan, NTPC, IndiGo, Bajaj Finserv, Trent, HUL, SBI, Maruti Suzuki and M&M fell between 2% and 4% each, with UltraTech Cement, Power Grid, Tech Mahindra and Kotak Mahindra Bank close behind — a list that spans rate-sensitives, commodity producers, consumer names and lenders, which is what a macro-driven selloff looks like when it is not really about any of them.

The four factors behind the rout form a single chain of causation, and oil is the first link. Brent's jump to $114 a barrel — on Houthi attacks against Saudi Arabia, the evacuation of Aramco's Dhahran headquarters, and reports of drawn-up US-Israeli strike plans on Iran — converts directly into inflation, import-bill and rate-hike arithmetic for an economy that imports the overwhelming majority of its crude. The second link is foreign money: FIIs were net sellers of nearly Rs 3,459 crore of Indian equities on Thursday, more than double the Rs 1,407 crore they had bought on Wednesday, and have now been net sellers in 11 of 14 sessions this month. The October selling follows a brief reversal in September and has the feel of a sustained de-risking rather than a one-day trade. It was visible at the single-stock level too: Swiggy crashed 13.8% to a 52-week low after its quarterly loss widened, eMudhra extended a three-session rout to 39% after its certificate-business exit, and the day's biggest casualties clustered in exactly the high-multiple pockets that foreign and momentum money had crowded into.

The third factor is the market's own composition. The broader indices' underperformance was the day's defining feature, and it has a valuation story attached. "The sharp correction in the broader market, particularly in small-caps, might continue since their valuations, driven up by liquidity rather than fundamentals, are hard to justify," said VK Vijayakumar, Chief Investment Strategist at Geojit Investments, adding that fairly-valued large-caps growing earnings well present the value opportunity for patient investors. The sector map confirmed the rate-sensitivity logic: Nifty Metal, Realty and Consumer Durables each dropped over 3%, and the Nifty PSU Bank index fell more than 2%, while defensives and exporters held relatively firm.

The fourth factor is Washington. Metals and realty led losses amid rising expectations that the US Federal Reserve will keep rates elevated for longer, with the October 27-28 policy meeting and next week's PCE inflation data in focus; traders largely expect the Fed to hold next week, while the probability of a December hike stands at 80%. That is a remarkable setup — the market is simultaneously pricing a US hike, an oil shock, and record domestic IPO absorption — and it explains why every bounce attempt on Friday was sold into within minutes of the open. For a market that had climbed for weeks on liquidity and earnings upgrades, the speed of the reversal was itself the story: intraday, the gap-up at the open was fully surrendered before the first hour closed. The fourth factor is Washington.

The contrarian case was on record before the closing bell. Vijayakumar argued that Brent at $114 is a major negative but that the US 10-year yield's sharp decline to 4.96% and the rupee's appreciation to 94.70 against the dollar are positives that can restrain FII selling, and that more than Rs 3 lakh crore of locked-up IPO money returns to the system once allotments are completed by Monday — a potential liquidity surge that could lift the market next week, led by large-caps. Technically, the picture is more fragile: Axis Direct's Hemang Gor flagged 22,500 as critical support for the Nifty, a level the index had already broken through at the time of writing, with a sustained move back above 22,800 needed to ignite any short-covering rally toward 23,000. Friday afternoon's trade would tell whether the Rs 10 lakh crore wipeout was a shakeout or the start of something deeper. For now, the market heads into the weekend carrying three known unknowns — the Fed's meeting, the US midterms, and the next headline out of the Gulf — and a positioning setup where the bears have won every argument that mattered on Friday morning.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHY DID THE MARKET FALL ON FRIDAY?
Four factors: Brent crude surging to $114 on Middle East escalation, FII selling resuming (Rs 3,459 crore on Thursday), a sharper correction in smallcap and midcap stocks, and rising expectations of a US Fed rate hike in December.
HOW BAD WAS THE DAMAGE?
The Sensex fell about 630 points to near 72,600, over 1,100 points off the day's high, and nearly Rs 10 lakh crore in market value was wiped out. Smallcaps fell around 4% and decliners outnumbered advancers ten-to-one.
WHAT COULD STABILISE THE MARKET?
Per Geojit's VK Vijayakumar: a lower US 10-year yield at 4.96%, a firmer rupee at 94.70, and over Rs 3 lakh crore of IPO refund money returning after Monday's allotment could support a large-cap-led rebound. GLOSSARY: MARKET BREADTH — The ratio of advancing to declining stocks; ten decliners per advancer signals a broad-based selloff rather than index-heavy profit booking. PCE (PERSONAL CONSUMPTION EXPENDITURES) — The Fed's preferred inflation gauge; next week's reading shapes expectations for the December rate decision. FII/FPI SELLING — Net sales of Indian equities by foreign institutional investors; persistent outflows pressure both the currency and equity valuations.
