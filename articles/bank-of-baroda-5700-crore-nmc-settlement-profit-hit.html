Bank of Baroda Agrees ₹5,700 Crore NMC Settlement, Profit to Take Hit

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Bank of Baroda agrees to pay $600 million (~₹5,700 crore) in out-of-court settlement for NMC Health litigation
  • 02/Settlement amount equals nearly 28% of FY26 net profit of ₹20,021 crore; no provision made, treated as contingent liability
  • 03/Analysts say one-off hit on FY27 profitability but long-term growth and capital position remain intact

State-owned Bank of Baroda has agreed to pay $600 million, or approximately ₹5,700 crore, in an out-of-court settlement. The settlement resolves years-long litigation linked to the collapse of UAE-based healthcare provider NMC Health.

The one-time payout is equivalent to nearly 28% of the lender's FY26 net profit of ₹20,021 crore. It is expected to dent earnings in the coming quarters. However, analysts say it is unlikely to affect the bank's long-term growth prospects or capital position.

"The bank has not made a provision for the settlement and treated it as a contingent liability, which means it's a fresh hit on the company's profit and loss account," said Akshay Tiwari, associate vice president at Asit C Mehta Investment Intermediates.

Sunny Agrawal, head of fundamental research at SBI Securities, said the settlement amount will likely impact first or second quarter earnings. It will also weigh on FY27 profitability. However, since it is a one-off event, the bank is likely to continue growing in double digits.

"Bank of Baroda continues to have a healthy capital position, and the settlement is unlikely to affect its long term financial stability or growth prospects," said Abhinav Tiwari, research analyst at Bonanza.

The litigation stemmed from insolvency proceedings initiated before the Abu Dhabi Global Market Court and the UK High Court. The administrators alleged that Bank of Baroda's Abu Dhabi branch helped process financing transactions that enabled NMC Health to conceal its true financial position.

Under the settlement, Bank of Baroda's Abu Dhabi branch agreed to pay about ₹5,700 crore to the joint administrators. Following the settlement, the Abu Dhabi proceedings have been discontinued. The UK proceedings are in the process of being withdrawn.

The settlement marks a shift from the bank's position in its FY26 annual report. The bank had maintained that claims could not be crystallised because of its "robust defence in facts and law."

Shares of Bank of Baroda traded 3.3% lower at ₹251.50 as of 2:40 pm on Friday. The stock had declined 4.2% on Thursday after the settlement was disclosed. Bank of Baroda carries the second-highest weight, about 13%, in the Nifty PSU Bank index.

Separately, the bank reported Q1FY27 business growth of 15.46% year-on-year to ₹30.51 trillion. Global advances rose 17.42% to ₹14.17 trillion and deposits grew 13.81% to ₹16.34 trillion.
