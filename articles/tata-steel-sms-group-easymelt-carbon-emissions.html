Tata Steel, SMS Group Deploy World-First EASyMelt Tech to Cut Emissions 50%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Tata Steel signs definitive agreements with SMS Group to deploy the world's first EASyMelt technology at its Jamshedpur blast furnace
  • 02/The project aims to cut carbon emissions by over 50% from the blast furnace's baseline operation in a phased rollout
  • 03/The collaboration advances Tata Steel's net zero target of 2045, one of the most ambitious in global steelmaking

Tata Steel Ltd (NSE: TATASTEEL) has signed definitive agreements with Paul Wurth SA, part of Germany's SMS Group GmbH. The partnership will implement the world's first EASyMelt (electrically-assisted syngas smelter) technology.

The industrial demonstration will proceed at the 'E' Blast Furnace in Tata Steel's Jamshedpur Works. The implementation will be carried out in a phased manner.

The project targets cutting carbon emissions by over 50% compared to the blast furnace's baseline operation. This represents a major leap in brownfield decarbonisation for existing steel plants.

Tata Steel and SMS Group first signed an MoU in June 2023 to collaborate on decarbonising iron making. Following a successful front-end loading study, both parties have now moved to definitive agreements.

T.V. Narendran, CEO and Managing Director of Tata Steel, said the transition to low-carbon steelmaking will be shaped by the company's ability to reimagine existing production ecosystems. He added that the collaboration marks a significant milestone in Tata Steel's journey to net zero.

Jochen Burg, CEO of SMS Group, said the commitment from an industry leader will enable the company to bring EASyMelt technology to life. He noted that building the first industrial-scale unit paves the way for future brownfield decarbonisation projects worldwide.

Tata Steel has set a net zero carbon emissions target by 2045. This ranks among the most ambitious climate targets adopted by any major steel producer globally. India, as the world's second-largest steel producer, has set a national net zero target of 2070.

Tata Steel shares closed at Rs 212.65 on April 21, 2026, up 0.47% for the session. The stock has gained 41% from its 52-week low of Rs 77.75 touched in March 2026.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT IS THE EASYMELT TECHNOLOGY BEING IMPLEMENTED BY TATA STEEL AND SMS GROUP?
EASYMELT (ELECTRICALLY-ASSISTED SYNGAS SMELTER) IS A CUTTING-EDGE METALLURGICAL TECHNOLOGY DEVELOPED BY SMS GROUP'S PAUL WURTH. IT UTILIZES BLAST FURNACE TOP GAS RECYCLING FOR SYNGAS PRODUCTION THROUGH REFORMING OF COKE OVEN GAS. THE RESULTING SYNGAS IS INJECTED AT BOTH SHAFT AND TUYERE LEVEL, WITH PLASMA TORCHES HEATING THE GAS AT TUYERE LEVEL TO TEMPERATURES BETWEEN 1,700°C AND 2,200°C.
WHERE WILL THE EASYMELT TECHNOLOGY BE DEMONSTRATED?
THE WORLD'S FIRST INDUSTRIAL-SCALE DEMONSTRATION OF EASYMELT WILL BE AT THE 'E' BLAST FURNACE IN TATA STEEL'S JAMSHEDPUR WORKS IN JHARKHAND, INDIA. THE IMPLEMENTATION WILL PROCEED IN A PHASED MANNER FOLLOWING A SUCCESSFUL FRONT-END LOADING STUDY.
HOW MUCH WILL CARBON EMISSIONS BE REDUCED?
THE PROJECT AIMS TO CUT CARBON EMISSIONS BY OVER 50% COMPARED TO THE BLAST FURNACE'S BASELINE OPERATION. DEPENDING ON ENERGY INPUT CONFIGURATION, THE TECHNOLOGY CAN POTENTIALLY ACHIEVE EMISSION SAVINGS OF ABOVE 60% COMPARED TO THE TRADITIONAL BLAST FURNACE-BASIC OXYGEN FURNACE ROUTE.
WHAT IS TATA STEEL'S NET ZERO TARGET?
TATA STEEL HAS SET A TARGET TO ACHIEVE NET ZERO CARBON EMISSIONS BY 2045. THIS IS ONE OF THE MOST AMBITIOUS CLIMATE TARGETS ADOPTED BY ANY MAJOR STEEL PRODUCER GLOBALLY. THE EASYMELT COLLABORATION REPRESENTS A KEY MILESTONE IN THIS JOURNEY.
WHAT IS THE HISTORY OF THE TATA STEEL-SMS GROUP PARTNERSHIP?
TATA STEEL AND SMS GROUP SIGNED AN INITIAL MOU IN NOVEMBER 2022 TO EXPLORE DECARBONISATION OPPORTUNITIES. THEY FOLLOWED WITH A SECOND MOU IN JUNE 2023 AT THE METEC TRADE FAIR TO COLLABORATE ON DECARBONISING THE IRON MAKING PROCESS. AFTER A SUCCESSFUL FRONT-END LOADING STUDY, THE PARTIES HAVE NOW ENTERED DEFINITIVE AGREEMENTS TO PROCEED WITH THE EASYMELT DEMONSTRATION PROJECT.
