VilCart Hits 1 Lakh Rural Stores as 2 Lakh Urban Kiranas Shut; Eyes ₹1,500 Cr by FY27

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/VilCart now services over 1 lakh kirana stores across 30,000 villages even as 200,000 urban kirana stores shut in the past year.
  • 02/The company targets ₹1,500 crore revenue by FY27, up from ₹1,173 crore in FY26, and is exploring a $25-30 million fundraise.
  • 03/Private labels have grown from 5% of revenue in April 2025 to 18% in April 2026, with a target of 25-30% by FY27.

VilCart, a Bengaluru-based rural commerce startup, now services over 1 lakh kirana stores across 30,000 villages. This expansion comes as 200,000 kirana stores have shut across India over the past year.

The closures represent about 1.5% of the country's estimated 13 million retail outlets. Data from AICPDF, Redseer, and Grant Thornton Bharat shows these shutdowns are concentrated in urban markets.

Quick commerce platforms are capturing high-margin categories in cities. This has squeezed traditional kirana stores in urban India while rural markets continue to grow.

"Private labels are central to how this business becomes profitable," said Prasanna Kumar C (CPK), Founder and CEO of VilCart. "Distribution gives us access, but owning the product is what drives margins."

Private labels now contribute 18% of VilCart's revenue, up from 5% in April 2025. The company targets 25-30% by FY27, implying ₹375-450 crore in private label revenue.

VilCart reported ₹1,120 crore in revenue for FY25 with a negative EBITDA margin of about 6.8%. Losses exceeded ₹65 crore that year.

The EBITDA margin has narrowed to around 4.5% in FY26. Revenue reached ₹1,173 crore, and private labels are expected to drive a move toward breakeven.

The company services stores every 24-48 hours with a reported 99% fulfilment rate. Repeat orders run 3.8-4.5 times a month at ₹4,500-₹5,000 per order.

VilCart uses kirana stores as aggregation and last-mile nodes rather than running its own retail outlets. This compresses delivery costs that historically made rural commerce unviable at scale.

The company is also evaluating a shift to electric vehicles for last-mile delivery. It plans to gradually convert its fleet of around 300 diesel vehicles as utilisation improves.

Backed by Asia Impact, NABVENTURES, and Spark Capital, VilCart has raised about $26 million so far. This is a fraction of rival Jumbotail's $120 million-plus in funding.

VilCart is exploring a $25-30 million fundraise over the next two years. The capital will support deeper penetration in existing markets and selective expansion into Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh, and Bihar.

"The model is now working across markets, and the next phase is about scaling it faster," Kumar said. "This is not just about selling products, it is about building distribution where it does not exist."
