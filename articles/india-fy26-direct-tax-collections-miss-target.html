India FY26 Direct Tax Collections Miss Revised Target by Over ₹10,000 Crore

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Net direct tax collections for FY26 reached ₹23.40 lakh crore, growing 5.12% year-on-year but falling short of the revised estimate of ₹24.21 lakh crore by over ₹10,000 crore.
  • 02/Corporate tax collections grew 12.75% and met revised targets at ₹10.91 lakh crore, while personal income tax collections grew only 2.7% against a 6.2% growth expectation, reaching ₹11.32 lakh crore versus the ₹13.12 lakh crore target.
  • 03/Indirect tax collections exceeded targets at 101.2% of the revised estimate, while the fiscal deficit target of 4.4% of GDP is expected to be met through expenditure savings despite the direct tax shortfall.

India's net direct tax collections for fiscal year 2026 reached ₹23.40 lakh crore, registering a 5.12% increase compared to the previous year. Despite the growth, collections fell short of the government's revised estimate of ₹24.21 lakh crore by over ₹10,000 crore, marking a notable miss in revenue expectations.

The shortfall was driven primarily by underperformance in personal income tax collections. Personal income tax grew only 2.7% during the year, significantly below the 6.2% growth that the government had projected in its revised estimates.

Personal income tax collections stood at approximately ₹11.32 lakh crore against a revised target of ₹13.12 lakh crore. This gap of nearly ₹1.8 lakh crore in personal income tax was the single largest contributor to the overall direct tax miss.

In contrast, corporate tax collections performed strongly and largely met expectations. Corporate tax grew by 12.75% year-on-year and reached approximately ₹10.91 lakh crore, close to the revised target of ₹11 lakh crore.

The divergence between corporate and personal income tax performance highlights a shift in the tax base. While corporate profits and compliance improved, personal income tax growth remained sluggish, suggesting weaker salary growth or reduced compliance in the individual taxpayer segment.

Indirect tax collections provided some relief, exceeding the revised estimate at 101.2% of the target of ₹15.53 lakh crore. Customs duty reached 102% of its revised estimate at ₹2.58 lakh crore, excise duty hit 101% at ₹3.38 lakh crore, and central GST collections stood at 100.8% of the ₹9.58 lakh crore target.

Despite the direct tax shortfall, the government maintained a cash surplus of around ₹3.7 lakh crore as of late March. Expenditure savings are expected to help the government meet its fiscal deficit target of 4.4% of GDP for the year.

The FY26 budget had initially assumed gross tax collections of ₹42.70 lakh crore, representing a 10.8% growth over FY25. However, the health and national security cess severely underperformed, collecting only 63% of its revised estimate, adding another layer to the revenue picture for the year.
