BofA Sees RBI Hiking Repo Rate 100 bps by H1 2027 on Inflation Risks

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/BofA Securities expects the RBI to hike the repo rate by a cumulative 100 bps through H1 2027, starting with 25 bps to 6.25% in October 2026
  • 02/The hawkish call — 50 bps in Q4 2026 and 50 bps in Q1 2027 — is driven by Brent near $107 and the rupee past 96 per dollar
  • 03/Most brokerages still expect an extended pause, making BofA's forecast a clear outlier; the October MPC meeting is the first test

Bank of America Securities expects the Reserve Bank of India to raise the repo rate by a cumulative 100 basis points through the first half of 2027. The brokerage's first increase of 25 bps is pencilled in for October 2026, taking the policy rate to 6.25% from 6.00% currently.

BofA splits the tightening into two equal halves. It expects 50 bps of hikes across the October and December meetings of 2026, and another 50 bps in the first quarter of 2027.

If the full path is delivered, the repo rate would reach 7.00% by March 2027. That would unwind much of the easing cycle the RBI has engineered over the past two years.

This is a brokerage forecast, not an RBI decision. The central bank has committed to no such path, and the MPC under Governor Sanjay Malhotra has so far balanced growth support against currency and imported-inflation pressure.

CONTEXT: The call is anchored in imported-inflation risk. Brent crude has climbed to around $107 per barrel on Middle East supply-disruption concerns, and India imports over 85% of its crude.

A weak rupee compounds the problem. The currency breached 96 per dollar in late September, hitting two-month lows and raising the landed cost of oil, gold and other imports.

Crude spikes transmit into Indian CPI with a lag. Fuel and freight costs move first, followed by broader prices over a typical 3-6 month window.

The domestic inflation backdrop is already firm. Headline CPI ran at 4.38% in June and 4.45% in July, above the RBI's 4% target for a second straight month, and the central bank's own projections see FY27 inflation near 5% with a Q3 peak close to 5.9%.

The global backdrop leaves little room for comfort either. The US Federal Reserve hiked its policy rate by 25 bps in September 2026, its first increase in three years, and markets price nearly a 70% probability of another US hike in October.

The US 10-year Treasury yield sits near 5.24%, close to a two-decade high. That narrows the rate differential with India, pressuring the rupee and encouraging foreign portfolio outflows — India has already seen heavy FII selling through 2026.

CONTEXT: The RBI's inflation framework targets 4% CPI with a 2-6% tolerance band. With its own forecast pointing to a Q3 print near the top of that band, the growth-versus-inflation trade-off has shifted decisively toward price stability.

BofA's view is a clear outlier against consensus. Brokerage surveys as recent as August — Barclays, Motilal Oswal and Elara among them — showed most economists expecting the RBI to hold through 2026, with at most a single 25 bps hike in early 2027.

The direction of travel, however, has support beyond BofA. "While immediate policy tightening is unlikely, elevated inflation projections suggest that the next move on rates is going to be a hike," ICRA chief economist Aditi Nayar said, adding that she expects CPI of 4.7% in August and a reading above 5% in September.

IMPACT: For bond markets, a hiking cycle would steepen an already elevated yield curve. Government and corporate borrowing costs would rise, and the 10-year yield's push higher would likely extend.

Banks could see near-term support to net interest margins as lending rates reprice faster than deposits. That trade, however, typically reverses once deposit costs catch up.

Rate-sensitive equities face the sharper risk. Real estate and autos are the most leveraged to credit conditions, and a rising repo rate historically cools both home loans and vehicle finance demand.

A wider India-US rate differential would ease pressure on emerging-market fund flows. For the rupee, aggressive RBI action would be the first credible line of defence after weeks of selling.

The risks to BofA's forecast cut both ways. Crude retreating sharply or a dovish Fed pivot could keep the RBI on hold through 2027, while oil holding above $100 and the rupee slipping further could force the MPC's hand even earlier than the brokerage expects.
