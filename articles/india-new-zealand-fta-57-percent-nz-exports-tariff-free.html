India-New Zealand FTA to Make 57% of NZ Exports Tariff-Free From Day 1

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

India and New Zealand have struck a trade deal.

The free trade agreement between the two countries will make 57% of New Zealand exports tariff-free from day one of implementation. The comprehensive pact covers trade in goods, services, and investment, expanding a relationship that has been relatively modest in scale.

New Zealand stands to gain significantly. The country is a major exporter of dairy products, meat, wool, and wine. India currently maintains high tariffs on many of these products, and the FTA will provide New Zealand producers with preferential access to India's 1.4 billion consumers.

For India, the agreement opens opportunities in services and investment. Indian information technology companies, consultants, and professionals will get improved access to the New Zealand market. Indian investors will also benefit from clearer rules for cross-border investment.

The 57% figure covers the share of New Zealand's export value that will face zero tariffs immediately. Additional products will see tariffs phased down over time, with the goal of covering nearly all bilateral trade eventually.

The FTA is part of India's broader trade diplomacy. New Delhi has been aggressively pursuing bilateral and regional trade agreements to offset the impact of global protectionism and diversify its export markets beyond traditional partners.

For New Zealand, the deal reduces dependence on China. Like Australia, New Zealand has been seeking to diversify its trade relationships, and India represents a large, growing market with demographic tailwinds.
