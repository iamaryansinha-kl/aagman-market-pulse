World Bank IFC Targets $10 Billion Annual India Investment by 2030

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/IFC aims to double annual India investments to $10 billion by 2030, up from $5.4 billion in FY2024/25
  • 02/India is IFC's largest investment destination globally with a $10.3 billion portfolio across equity and debt
  • 03/Key sectors include renewable energy, urban infrastructure, financial services, and municipal bonds

The International Finance Corporation (IFC) plans to scale its annual investments in India to $10 billion by 2030. The World Bank Group's private-sector arm currently invests about $5.4 billion annually, up sharply from $1.3 billion in FY2021/22.

Imad Fakhoury, IFC's South Asia regional director, told Reuters the DFI remains committed to India despite global uncertainties. "We're very focused on staying the course because India is also staying the course," he said on Thursday.

India is IFC's largest investment destination globally. Its portfolio stands at approximately $10.3 billion as of June 2025, spanning equity and debt instruments across multiple sectors.

Equity investments account for more than a third of IFC's India portfolio. Recent bets include Manappuram Finance, Federal Bank, PNB Housing Finance, TVS Emerald, and Leap Agri Silos.

IFC is in talks with Indian states and urban local bodies to invest in municipal bonds. The DFI could act as an anchor investor to mobilise private funding for infrastructure projects.

In September 2025, IFC committed $60 million for water and wastewater projects in Visakhapatnam. This marks its first direct lending to an Indian city and could expand the underdeveloped municipal bond market.

The $10 billion target represents an 85% increase from current annual investment levels. Sectors targeted include renewable energy, urban infrastructure, and financial services.
