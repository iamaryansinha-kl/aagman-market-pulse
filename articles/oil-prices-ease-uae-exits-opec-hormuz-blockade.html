Oil Prices Ease to $111 as UAE Exits OPEC; Hormuz Blockade Keeps Supply Tight

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Brent crude June futures fell 1% to $111.25 a barrel on April 29 after a seven-session winning streak
  • 02/UAE announced its exit from OPEC and OPEC+ on April 28, effective May 1, ending 59 years of membership
  • 03/The Strait of Hormuz is moving just 2 million barrels per day versus normal 20 mb/d, keeping supply fears elevated

Brent crude futures for June dipped 1% to $111.25 a barrel on April 29, 2026. The contract had climbed for the previous seven sessions straight.

The June Brent contract expires on April 30, 2026. The more active July contract traded down 28 cents at $104.12.

The United Arab Emirates announced on April 28 that it will exit OPEC and the wider OPEC+ alliance. The withdrawal takes effect on May 1, 2026, ending 59 years of membership.

Energy Minister Suhail Al Mazrouei said the decision serves the UAE's "national interests." He cited frustration with OPEC quota limits that did not reflect the country's expanded production capacity.

The UAE produced approximately 3.6 million barrels per day in March. Its installed capacity stands at 4.8 million barrels per day, well above its OPEC-assigned quota.

The exit lands amid the worst Middle East supply crisis in decades. The Strait of Hormuz is currently moving only 2 million barrels per day, down from roughly 20 million normally.

Goldman Sachs has warned that Brent could average above $100 through 2026 if Hormuz remains restricted. ANZ expects Brent to settle at $88 by year-end if shipping lanes reopen.

The International Energy Agency called the Hormuz closure the "greatest global energy security challenge in history." The U.S. EIA estimates OPEC+ nations shut in 7.5 million barrels per day in March.
