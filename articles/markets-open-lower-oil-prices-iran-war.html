Markets to Open Lower as Oil Prices Spike on Iran War Tensions

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/GIFT Nifty futures signal weak opening at 24,002, below Monday's close of 24,092.70
  • 02/Brent crude hovers near $109 per barrel as Iran war tensions disrupt Strait of Hormuz
  • 03/FPIs offload ₹1,151 crore for sixth straight session; DIIs buy ₹4,124 crore

Indian markets are poised to open lower on Tuesday. GIFT Nifty futures traded at 24,002 points as of 7:58 a.m. IST, signaling a drop below Monday's Nifty close of 24,092.70.

The weakness stems from surging oil prices driven by the ongoing Iran war. Brent crude hovered around $109 per barrel as diplomatic efforts to end the conflict appeared stalled.

The Strait of Hormuz handles about one-fifth of global oil flows. Disruption risks from the geopolitical crisis are amplifying supply concerns and pushing prices higher.

Elevated oil prices pose risks for India, the world's third-largest crude importer. Higher crude inflates the import bill, stokes inflation, and pressures corporate earnings.

Foreign portfolio investors sold domestic stocks worth ₹1,151 crore on Monday. This marks the sixth consecutive session of FPI outflows from Indian equities.

Domestic institutional investors remained buyers for a third straight session. They purchased shares worth ₹4,124 crore, providing some counterbalance to FPI selling.

On Monday, both Nifty and Sensex snapped a three-session losing streak. The rebound was led by information technology stocks after last week's sharp selloff.

UltraTech Cement beat quarterly profit estimates on improved demand. Coal India also posted larger-than-expected profits, while SBI Cards reported 14% year-on-year profit growth.
