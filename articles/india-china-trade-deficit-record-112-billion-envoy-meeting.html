India-China Trade Deficit Hits Record $112.16B as Envoys Meet to Boost Ties

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Indian Ambassador Vikram Doraiswami met Chinese Commerce Ministry official Wang Liping on June 29 to discuss trade cooperation
  • 02/China remains India's largest trading partner with bilateral trade at $151.1 billion, but deficit swelled to record $112.16 billion
  • 03/India's exports to China surged 36.66% to $19.47 billion while imports rose 16% to $131.63 billion in FY2025-26

Indian Ambassador to China Vikram Doraiswami met Wang Liping on June 29. Wang serves as Director General of the Department of Asian Affairs at China's Ministry of Commerce. The meeting focused on trade and commercial cooperation.

The Indian Embassy posted details on X. Both sides exchanged views on bilateral dialogue mechanisms. They discussed ways to strengthen cooperation in areas of mutual interest.

China overtook the US to become India's largest trading partner in 2025-26. Bilateral trade reached $151.1 billion during the fiscal year. The figure highlights deep economic ties despite political tensions.

The trade deficit widened to an all-time high of $112.16 billion. This marks a sharp jump from $99.2 billion recorded in 2024-25. India's imports from China stood at $131.63 billion.

India's exports to China rose 36.66% to $19.47 billion. The growth rate outpaced import expansion of 16%. However, the export base remains roughly one-seventh of import volumes.

Both nations are normalizing relations after the 2020 Eastern Ladakh conflict. They have resumed the Kailash-Manasarovar Yatra, visas, flights and high-level dialogue. Trade remains a key pillar of the bilateral relationship.

India has long demanded China open its IT, pharma and agricultural sectors. These are India's areas of competitive strength. Ambassador Doraiswami's meeting is expected to step up dialogue on these issues.
