India Reservoir Storage Drops to 38.7%: Over 50% Dams Below 40% Capacity

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Storage in India's 166 major reservoirs fell to 38.72% of total 183.565 BCM capacity at 71.082 BCM, per Central Water Commission data.
  • 02/The southern region is worst hit at 28% capacity, with Telangana at just 20.87% and Karnataka at 24%.
  • 03/Levels are 14 percentage points below last year and 26.5 points below the 10-year average as summer heatwaves intensify.

Storage in 166 major Indian reservoirs has dropped to 38.72% of total capacity. The live storage stands at 71.082 billion cubic metres against 183.565 BCM capacity, Central Water Commission data showed.

Over half of these reservoirs now hold less than 40% of their designed capacity. The shortfall is concentrated in the southern and eastern regions.

The southern region is worst affected with storage at just 28% of 55.288 BCM capacity. Telangana dams are at 20.87%, Karnataka at 24%, and Kerala at 28%.

The eastern region holds 34% of capacity at 7.481 BCM. West Bengal is critically low at 12%, while Assam stands at 18.5%.

Current levels are 14 percentage points lower than a year ago. They are also 26.5 percentage points below the 10-year normal average.

Over a third of India received deficient or no rainfall between March 1 and April 29, the India Meteorological Department reported. Pre-monsoon showers have started in some southern areas.

The northern region is relatively better at 43% capacity, led by Punjab at 68%. Rajasthan and Himachal Pradesh are at 48.3% and 36.6% respectively.

With peak summer heatwaves spreading across the country, storage levels are expected to decline further before monsoon arrives.

HOW DOES THE CURRENT STORAGE COMPARE TO LAST YEAR AND THE 10-YEAR AVERAGE?

WHAT IS DRIVING THE DECLINE IN RESERVOIR STORAGE?
OVER A THIRD OF INDIA RECEIVED DEFICIENT OR NO RAINFALL FROM MARCH 1 TO APRIL 29, PER THE INDIA METEOROLOGICAL DEPARTMENT. PEAK SUMMER HEATWAVES ACROSS THE COUNTRY ARE ACCELERATING EVAPORATION AND WATER DEMAND.
WHAT ARE THE IMPLICATIONS OF LOW RESERVOIR STORAGE FOR INDIA?
LOW STORAGE THREATENS SUMMER IRRIGATION FOR KHARIF CROP SOWING, DRINKING WATER SUPPLY IN URBAN AREAS, AND HYDROPOWER GENERATION. LEVELS ARE EXPECTED TO DECLINE FURTHER BEFORE THE MONSOON ARRIVES.
