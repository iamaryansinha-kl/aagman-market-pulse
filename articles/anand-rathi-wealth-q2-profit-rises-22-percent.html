Anand Rathi Wealth Q2 FY27: PAT Up 22% to Rs 122 Crore, Rs 4 Dividend

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Anand Rathi Wealth Q2 FY27 consolidated business PAT rises 22% YoY to Rs 122 crore; revenue grows 16% to Rs 357 crore
  • 02/Record quarterly net inflows of Rs 4,186 crore, up 39% YoY, even as foreign investors sold amid the US-Israel-Iran conflict
  • 03/Board declares Rs 4 per share interim dividend; company has already achieved 52% of its full-year PAT guidance of Rs 460 crore

Anand Rathi Wealth Ltd (NSE: ANANDRATHI) reported a 22% year-on-year rise in consolidated business PAT for Q2 FY27. Profit reached Rs 122 crore and revenue grew 16% to Rs 357 crore. The board declared a Rs 4 per share interim dividend.

The quarter was tough for equity markets. Management cited the US-Israel-Iran conflict, higher US bond yields, a strong dollar, elevated crude prices and continued foreign portfolio investor outflows as drags on sentiment. Even so, the profit growth was helped by strong client inflows and higher AUM despite weak equity markets.

The standout number is client inflows. Net inflows hit a record Rs 4,186 crore in Q2 FY27, up 39% from a year earlier. Equity mutual fund net inflows also set a quarterly record at Rs 2,867 crore, up 39% year on year.

Mutual fund distribution revenue rose 18% year on year to Rs 145 crore. Assets under management grew 18% to Rs 1.08 lakh crore as of September 2026.

Management described the result as consistent, market-agnostic performance in a challenging quarter. CEO Rakesh Rawal and Joint CEO Feroze Azeez said domestic investors kept conviction while foreign investors stayed cautious, with existing clients deepening relationships. Corrections create opportunities for disciplined investors, they added.

For the first half of FY27, standalone profit rose 25% to Rs 237 crore. The company has banked 52% of its full-year PAT guidance of Rs 460 crore and 49% of its revenue guidance of Rs 1,415 crore. Long-term growth guidance stays at 20-25%.

On a reported consolidated basis, including fair value gains, ESOP expenses and related tax effects, Q2 PAT was Rs 89.3 crore against Rs 99.8 crore a year earlier. H1 reported PAT stood at Rs 252.3 crore versus Rs 193.6 crore.

Active client families grew 12% to 14,309, with over 1,500 added net in a year. Relationship managers rose by 45 to 431. The firm serves HNI clients across 19 Indian cities plus Dubai and London, with GIFT City approvals in hand.

The record inflows carry a macro message. Foreign portfolio investors have been selling Indian equities through the quarter, and domestic money filled the gap — a trend the company explicitly flagged.

That rotation is the engine of the wealth-management industry. As household financial savings move from property and gold into markets, distributors with established HNI franchises capture the flow first.

The composition of the inflows matters too. Equity mutual fund inflows of Rs 2,867 crore made up the bulk of the record quarter, and that is the segment where distribution revenue — up 18% to Rs 145 crore — is earned.

Headcount tells the same story from the cost side. Relationship managers rose by 45 over the past year to 431, a deliberate bet that high-touch service scales better than portals for the HNI segment it serves.

The client base is compounding steadily. More than 1,500 client families were added on a net basis over the last twelve months, taking active families to 14,309.

The dividend signals confidence in that trajectory. A Rs 4 payout on a Rs 5 face value is substantial, and it accompanies guidance progress rather than distracting from it.

On guidance arithmetic, the first half delivered 52% of the full-year profit target. Wealth management is a second-half-weighted business, so the Rs 460 crore figure looks comfortably in reach — the company said as much through its growth framework.

Two profit numbers appear in the results, and the gap needs decoding. Business profit of Rs 122 crore strips out fair-value swings and ESOP charges; reported consolidated profit of Rs 89.3 crore includes them and compares with Rs 99.8 crore a year earlier.

The company prefers the business measure for a reason. Fair-value gains and ESOP costs are accounting artefacts rather than operating performance, and quarter-to-quarter noise in them says little about the franchise.

Geography is the next leg. Beyond 19 Indian cities, the firm operates in Dubai and London, and regulatory approvals for GIFT City are in hand — a route to overseas structures for Indian wealth.

The scalable-model claim underpins the 20-25% long-term growth guidance. This quarter's numbers — record inflows, steady client additions, and operating profit ahead of plan — are the evidence management offered for it.

GLOSSARY: Business PAT: A company's operating profit measure, excluding one-off accounting items such as fair-value changes and ESOP charges. Assets under management (AUM): The total market value of client assets a wealth manager administers. Net inflows: New client money brought in during a period, net of withdrawals — a measure of franchise pull. Interim dividend: A dividend declared and paid during a financial year, ahead of the final dividend. Face value: The nominal value assigned to a share at issue; dividends are often quoted relative to it. GIFT City: The Gujarat International Finance Tec-City, India's international financial services centre with a separate regulatory regime. ESOP expenses: The accounting cost of employee stock option grants, charged against reported profit. Relationship manager: A dedicated adviser who manages a portfolio of client relationships at a wealth firm.
