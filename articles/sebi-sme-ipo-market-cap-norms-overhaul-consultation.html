Sebi Proposes Market Cap-Based SME IPO Framework Overhaul

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Sebi's consultation paper proposes replacing paid-up capital criteria with market capitalisation for SME IPO eligibility.
  • •- Issuers with post-issue market cap up to Rs 1,000 crore must list on the SME platform; Rs 1,000-4,000 crore can opt in with stricter reporting.
  • •- Minimum operating profit requirement rises from Rs 1 crore in two of three years to an average Rs 3 crore per year.
  • •- Minimum allottees increase from 200 to 500; trading lot cut to one share; compulsory market making scrapped.
  • •- SME platforms have seen 1,496 listings since inception, 365 migrations, and Rs 40,222 crore raised.
  • •- Public comments are invited until November 4, 2026.

The Securities and Exchange Board of India on Wednesday proposed a sweeping overhaul of the regulatory framework governing small and medium enterprise IPOs, centred on a single conceptual shift: judging companies by what the market thinks they are worth, rather than by the nominal size of their paid-up capital.

Under the proposed framework, issuers with post-issue market capitalisation of up to Rs 1,000 crore would be required to list on the SME platform, while those with market capitalisation above Rs 1,000 crore and up to Rs 4,000 crore could opt for the SME route subject to additional financial reporting requirements, including restated consolidated statements under Indian Accounting Standards covering the last three financial years. Public comments on the proposals are open until November 4.

The change replaces rules written for a different era. Currently, issuers with post-issue paid-up capital of up to Rs 10 crore can access the SME platform, and those above Rs 10 crore and up to Rs 25 crore may opt in. Sebi's working group argued that paid-up capital no longer reflects a company's economic size, valuation or growth potential — and the regulator's analysis of 735 main board and SME IPOs across the last three financial years confirmed a significant disconnect between the two measures. A company can be economically large with thin paid-up capital, or small in value terms with inflated share count.

The proposals build in flexibility for borderline cases. An issuer that files for a main board IPO would have to refile under the SME framework if its estimated post-issue market capitalisation falls to Rs 1,000 crore or below by the red herring prospectus stage; conversely, an SME filer whose valuation rises above Rs 1,000 crore but stays at or below Rs 4,000 crore can continue on the SME route, while one that crosses Rs 4,000 crore must refile for the main board.

Quality thresholds rise alongside. The minimum operating profitability requirement for SME IPOs would increase from the current Rs 1 crore of operating profit in at least two of the three preceding financial years to average operating profit of at least Rs 3 crore per year over the three preceding years, computed on restated consolidated financials. Issuers that miss the profitability bar could still use an alternative book-building route — but only if at least 75 per cent of the net offer is allotted to qualified institutional buyers. If that allocation is not achieved, the entire subscription money must be refunded.

The retail experience changes materially. The minimum application value is proposed at Rs 1 lakh, structured as four lots of at least Rs 25,000 each; book-built SME issues would gain anchor investor allocation of up to 60 per cent of the QIB portion; the minimum number of allottees rises sharply from 200 to 500; and each retail investor would receive at least one minimum lot, with the remainder allotted proportionately rather than in rigid multiples. The trading lot for SME securities would drop to one share, replacing today's price-band-based framework — a move aimed at deepening liquidity in stocks that often trade in thin, volatile bands.

Underwriting and market-making rules are liberalised. The requirement for 100 per cent underwriting of SME IPOs, and the additional obligation that lead managers underwrite at least 15 per cent of the issue on their own account, would be removed, with main board underwriting provisions applying instead. Compulsory market making — currently mandated for at least three years from listing, with the market maker required to hold at least 5 per cent of listed securities — would be scrapped entirely, though existing market-making requirements continue until Sebi issues a separate circular rescinding them.

Lock-ins are recalibrated: the minimum promoters' contribution stays locked for three years, promoters' excess holdings for one year, and pre-issue capital held by non-promoters would see its lock-in cut from one year to six months — a meaningful liquidity improvement for early investors. Migration rules move to the same market-cap logic, with voluntary migration available between Rs 1,000 crore and Rs 4,000 crore and mandatory migration above Rs 4,000 crore; companies already listed keep their existing frameworks, and draft filings already made continue under current rules unless issuers elect the new regime.

The stakes are visible in the numbers Sebi cited. A total of 1,496 entities have listed on SME platforms since inception as of July 31, 2026 — including 365 that subsequently migrated to the main board — raising Rs 40,222 crore, with a combined market capitalisation of Rs 4,70,984 crore. The review, the regulator said, seeks to make the framework proportionate to the size and nature of SME issuers, cut avoidable compliance costs, widen capital market participation and preserve investor-protection safeguards.

If adopted, the overhaul would redraw the boundary between India's two listing tiers — and with the Jio IPO opening next week on a main board preparing for its largest-ever raise, the timing underlines how central the primary market has become to India's capital-formation story, at every scale.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT IS SEbi PROPOSING FOR SME IPOS?
Replacing paid-up capital criteria with market capitalisation: up to Rs 1,000 crore post-issue market cap must list on the SME platform; Rs 1,000-4,000 crore can opt in with Ind AS restated financials.
HOW DO PROFITABILITY RULES CHANGE?
Operating profit requirement rises from Rs 1 crore in two of three years to an average Rs 3 crore per year over three years; a book-building route with 75% QIB allotment exists for issuers that miss it.
WHAT CHANGES FOR RETAIL INVESTORS?
Minimum application of Rs 1 lakh across four lots, anchor investors allowed, minimum allottees raised from 200 to 500, and trading lot cut to one share.
WHEN ARE COMMENTS DUE?
Public comments on the consultation paper close on November 4, 2026. GLOSSARY: - SME platform: NSE Emerge and BSE SME, listing venues for smaller companies with lighter compliance. - Paid-up capital: The nominal value of shares actually issued by a company, regardless of market price. - Market capitalisation: Share price multiplied by shares outstanding — what the market values a company at. - Anchor investor: A qualified institutional investor allotted shares ahead of an IPO book opening. - QIB: Qualified Institutional Buyer — banks, mutual funds and institutions meeting regulatory thresholds.
