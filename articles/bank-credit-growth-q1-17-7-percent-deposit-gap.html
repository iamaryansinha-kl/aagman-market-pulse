Bank Credit Growth Hits 17.7% in Q1, Widens Gap Over Deposit Growth at 12%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Bank lending stood at ₹215.5 trillion as of 15 June, up 17.7% YoY; deposits at ₹258.4 trillion, up 12%, widening the funding gap
  • 02/Large and mid-sized banks posted credit growth of 12.9-21.3%; smaller banks such as Jammu and Kashmir Bank and Dhanlaxmi Bank saw 25.5-26.5% growth
  • 03/FCNR(B) deposit relaxation could add 1.5-2 percentage points to deposit growth; credit growth expected to moderate to 13% for full FY27

Bank lending continued to outpace deposit growth in the June quarter. Credit expansion remained resilient despite headwinds from the US-Iran war, increasing pressure on banks to mobilise deposits faster.

Bank lending stood at ₹215.5 trillion as of 15 June, up 17.7% from a year ago. Deposits stood at ₹258.4 trillion, up 12% year-on-year, according to the latest RBI data.

Most large and mid-sized banks posted year-on-year credit growth between 12.9% and 21.3%. Smaller banks such as Jammu and Kashmir Bank and Dhanlaxmi Bank reported growth as high as 25.5-26.5%.

Deposit growth for mid-sized and large banks was in a range of 7.0-14.9%. Some smaller banks saw deposit growth of 16.8-17.1%. Union Bank was the outlier with loans growing 12.5% but deposits up only 3.5%.

"Strong credit growth has been supported by demand from corporates, which have shifted to bank borrowings amid rising corporate bond yields and elevated cost of external commercial borrowings," said Anil Gupta, senior vice president at Icra Ltd.

Growth in retail gold loans, driven by surging gold prices following rupee depreciation and higher import duties, also supported loan growth momentum. Higher working capital requirements from supply-side disruptions due to the US-Iran war added to credit demand, said Nirav Shah, managing director of Equirus Capital.

The RBI's recent window incentivising FCNR(B) deposits should help ease some pressure. Karan Gupta, director at India Ratings and Research, estimates this could add 1.5-2 percentage points to the base case deposit growth forecast of 11-11.5%.

"That would bring deposit growth much closer to credit growth and provide significant relief over the next quarter," Karan Gupta said. However, Icra's Anil Gupta believes the gap may persist as FCNR funds get deployed and credit growth continues to outpace deposits.

"With FCNR deposits, we expect the share of CASA deposits to further decline, which will push up the overall cost of funds," Anil Gupta added.

Credit demand is expected to moderate as supply-side pressures ease and bond market borrowings normalise. Yields on five-year AAA-rated corporate bonds have softened to 7.40-7.43% from around 8.00% in late May.

"Banking system credit growth for FY27 should settle at around 13%, even though Q1 provisional numbers have come in stronger," said Karan Gupta. Retail credit and MSME demand supported by credit guarantee schemes will continue to drive growth.

WHAT IS THE CURRENT BANK CREDIT AND DEPOSIT GROWTH RATE?
AS OF 15 JUNE, BANK LENDING STOOD AT ₹215.5 TRILLION, UP 17.7% YEAR-ON-YEAR. DEPOSITS STOOD AT ₹258.4 TRILLION, UP 12% YEAR-ON-YEAR.
WHICH BANKS ARE SEEING THE FASTEST CREDIT GROWTH?
SMALLER BANKS SUCH AS JAMMU AND KASHMIR BANK AND DHANLAXMI BANK POSTED CREDIT GROWTH OF 25.5-26.5%. LARGE AND MID-SIZED BANKS REPORTED GROWTH BETWEEN 12.9% AND 21.3%.
WHY IS CREDIT GROWTH OUTPACING DEPOSIT GROWTH?
CORPORATES HAVE SHIFTED TO BANK BORROWINGS AMID RISING CORPORATE BOND YIELDS AND HIGHER EXTERNAL COMMERCIAL BORROWING COSTS. RETAIL GOLD LOANS HAVE ALSO SURGED. SUPPLY-SIDE DISRUPTIONS FROM THE US-IRAN WAR HAVE INCREASED WORKING CAPITAL NEEDS.
HOW WILL FCNR(B) DEPOSITS HELP?
THE RBI'S FCNR(B) DEPOSIT RELAXATION COULD ADD 1.5-2 PERCENTAGE POINTS TO DEPOSIT GROWTH, WHICH IS FORECAST AT 11-11.5% BASE CASE. THIS WOULD NARROW THE CREDIT-DEPOSIT GAP.
WHAT IS THE OUTLOOK FOR FULL-YEAR FY27 CREDIT GROWTH?
BANKING SYSTEM CREDIT GROWTH FOR FY27 IS EXPECTED TO SETTLE AT AROUND 13%, ACCORDING TO INDIA RATINGS. Q1 PROVISIONAL NUMBERS ARE STRONGER BUT SHOULD MODERATE AS SUPPLY-SIDE PRESSURES EASE AND BOND MARKET BORROWINGS NORMALISE.
