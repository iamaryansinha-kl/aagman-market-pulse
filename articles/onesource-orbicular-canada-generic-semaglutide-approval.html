OneSource Orbicular Secures Canada Approval for Generic Semaglutide

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Orbicular, OneSource partner, secures Health Canada approval for generic semaglutide, the second such approval in Canada
  • 02/CDMO expanding cartridge filling capacity from 40M to 200M units with $100M capex investment over 18-24 months
  • 03/Revenue doubled from $85M to $170M in 2 years; targeting $400M in 3 years as semaglutide patents expire in 100+ countries

OneSource Specialty Pharma announced on May 4, 2026 that its partner Orbicular has received approval from Health Canada for generic semaglutide, the active ingredient in Novo Nordisk's blockbuster drug Ozempic. This marks only the second generic semaglutide approval granted in Canada.

The approval positions OneSource and its partners among the first generic entrants in what is the world's second-largest semaglutide market by volume. Canada represents a critical foothold as the global wave of patent expirations begins to reshape the GLP-1 landscape.

Despite the regulatory milestone, OneSource shares traded flat at Rs 1,731 on the NSE, suggesting investors had largely priced in the approval or are waiting for commercial traction before repricing the stock.

OneSource operates as a contract development and manufacturing organization focused on biologics, giving it a specialized position in a sector where manufacturing complexity often blocks new competitors. The CDMO model allows partners like Orbicular to bring products to market without building their own facilities.

The company is aggressively scaling capacity to meet anticipated demand. Cartridge filling capacity is being expanded from 40 million units to 200 million units over the next 18 to 24 months. This fivefold expansion signals management's confidence in the order pipeline ahead.

Supporting this scale-up is a $100 million capital expenditure program that includes a new injectable manufacturing facility in Visakhapatnam. The facility will anchor OneSource's ability to serve multiple markets as patent cliffs open across geographies.

Semaglutide patents are set to expire in more than 100 countries, including major markets like India, Canada, and Brazil. This creates a multi-year tailwind for generic and biosimilar manufacturers with the technical capability to produce complex peptides.

The global GLP-1 drug market is projected to exceed $100 billion by the end of this decade, up from roughly $30 billion today. OneSource's revenue has already doubled from $85 million to $170 million in the past two years, and management has set a target of $400 million within three years.
