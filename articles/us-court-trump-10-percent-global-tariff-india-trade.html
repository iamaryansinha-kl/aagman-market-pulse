US Court Blocks Trump 10% Global Tariff, Strengthens India's Trade Hand

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/US Court of International Trade delivers 2-1 ruling on May 7, 2026, declaring Trump's 10% global tariff under Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 unlawful and unauthorized
  • 02/Court orders refunds with interest on all Section 122 duties paid, reducing India's tariff exposure and boosting its position in ongoing bilateral trade agreement negotiations
  • 03/USTR's Section 301 investigations launched in March 2026 against India on structural excess capacity and forced labour continue to pose significant trade penalty risks

The US Court of International Trade has struck down President Donald Trump's 10% global tariff. The three-judge panel delivered a 2-1 ruling on May 7, 2026, finding the tariff unauthorized by law.

The tariff was imposed under Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 on February 20, 2026. It was introduced within hours after the Supreme Court invalidated Trump's IEEPA-based reciprocal tariffs in the Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump ruling.

The court found the US has a trade deficit, not a balance-of-payments deficit. Section 122 requires "large and serious" balance-of-payments problems to justify tariffs, which the administration failed to demonstrate.

The court ordered refunds with interest for all Section 122 duties already paid by plaintiffs. The surcharge applied to all US trading partners, including India, for up to 150 days.

The ruling strengthens India's hand in ongoing negotiations for a bilateral trade agreement with the US. New Delhi had been delaying finalising tariff cut commitments as the US tariff regime kept shifting after multiple court setbacks.

India's goods exports to the US reached $103.85 billion in 2025, marking an 18.9% increase over the previous year. Its imports from the US rose 9.8% to $45.6 billion during the same period.

Per the preliminary India-US interim bilateral trade deal framework announced on February 2, 2026, India had agreed to eliminate or lower tariffs on most industrial and agricultural goods. The US had agreed to lower reciprocal tariffs to 18% from 25%.

"No trade deal was signed between India and the US so the framework has no legal tenability. India is well within its rights for a re-think based on new tariff realities," a source tracking the negotiations told businessline.

Both the reciprocal tariffs under IEEPA and the later 10% Section 122 tariffs were on legally weak footing, pointed out Ajay Srivastava from the Global Trade and Research Initiative.

However, the ongoing Section 301 investigations launched by the USTR in March 2026 remain worrying for New Delhi. These probes into structural excess capacity in manufacturing and forced labour import bans could trigger significant trade penalties.

The US administration has been "playing a cat-and-mouse game, using one legal provision to impose broad tariffs, and when courts block it, shifting to another questionable legal tool," Srivastava noted.

At present, the US is not prepared to reduce its standard MFN tariffs while expecting India to lower or eliminate duties across most sectors. Under such conditions, any trade deal risks becoming one-sided, Srivastava added.
