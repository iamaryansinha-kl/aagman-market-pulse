Adani Energy Q2 Profit Triples to Rs 1,323 Crore, Revenue Up 53%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Adani Energy Solutions reported consolidated net profit of Rs 1,323 crore for Q2 FY27, up 148% from Rs 534 crore a year earlier.
  • •- Revenue from operations rose 53% year-on-year to Rs 10,076 crore, while total income climbed 51% to Rs 10,239 crore.
  • •- Finance costs rose 35% to Rs 1,180 crore, and the cost of power purchased more than doubled to Rs 3,244 crore.
  • •- Total outstanding borrowings stood at Rs 51,520 crore at the end of September, up from Rs 48,898 crore at the end of March.
  • •- For the first half of FY27, consolidated profit more than doubled to Rs 2,472 crore on revenue of Rs 19,787 crore, up 47%.

Adani Energy Solutions, the transmission and distribution arm of the Adani portfolio, delivered one of the sharpest earnings expansions in the group's quarterly reporting season on Thursday. Consolidated net profit for the September quarter of FY27 came in at Rs 1,323 crore, up 148% from the Rs 534 crore the company earned in the same period last year — a near-tripling that reflects both the rapid commissioning of new transmission assets and a favourable base, since the year-ago quarter was still absorbing the costs of the group's post-2023 balance-sheet rebuild.

Revenue grew in step with the bottom line, rising 53% year-on-year to Rs 10,076 crore from Rs 6,596 crore, with total income up 51% at Rs 10,239 crore. Sequentially, the growth is more measured but still positive: profit rose 15% from the June quarter's Rs 1,149 crore, while revenue climbed nearly 4% from Rs 9,711 crore — a pattern consistent with a company where incremental projects commission through the year rather than in one burst. Profit before tax more than doubled to Rs 1,767 crore from Rs 746 crore a year earlier.

The cost structure shows where the growth is being bought. Total expenses rose 54% to Rs 8,756 crore, marginally outpacing revenue growth. The cost of power purchased more than doubled to Rs 3,244 crore from Rs 1,601 crore — a line that swells as the company's distribution business in Mumbai and Mundra scales up — while construction expenses related to service concession arrangements rose to Rs 2,256 crore from Rs 1,625 crore, reflecting the ongoing build-out of transmission infrastructure. The mix matters: power-purchase costs are largely pass-through in nature, recovered through tariffs, whereas construction expenses on concession arrangements are capital in character, building the asset base from which future regulated revenue will flow. Depreciation and amortisation increased to Rs 601 crore from Rs 509 crore as new assets entered service.

The number that investors in an infrastructure compounder watch most carefully is the finance-cost line, and here the trend is worth noting. Finance costs rose 35% year-on-year to Rs 1,180 crore from Rs 872 crore, tracking the increase in total outstanding borrowings, which stood at Rs 51,520 crore at the end of September 2026 compared with Rs 48,898 crore at the end of March — a rise of about Rs 2,622 crore in six months. In a rising-rate environment that has already punished leveraged lenders and NBFCs this quarter, a 35% increase in the quarterly interest bill is the kind of line item that determines whether earnings growth translates into equity value; on Thursday's numbers, the 148% profit expansion more than absorbed it, but the trajectory of the borrowing base is the metric that will decide future quarters.

For the first half of FY27, the picture is consolidated strength: net profit rose 136% to Rs 2,472 crore from Rs 1,046 crore, revenue climbed 47% to Rs 19,787 crore from Rs 13,415 crore, and profit before tax more than doubled to Rs 3,208 crore. The half-yearly run-rate puts the company on a trajectory that would make it one of the faster-growing listed power utilities in India, though the comparisons flatter to some extent because the year-ago base was depressed by the group's financing crunch and by project delays that have since normalised. The cleaner signal is the sequential momentum — revenue up 4% and profit up 15% over the June quarter — which suggests the commissioning cycle is still accelerating rather than plateauing. For shareholders, the shape of the next few quarters will be determined less by demand, which is contract-backed, than by execution: how many awarded projects reach commercial operation, and at what cost the enlarged balance sheet gets refinanced. Both variables are, in principle, knowable well before the numbers land — project commissioning schedules are public, and debt maturity profiles are disclosed — which makes this one of the more trackable earnings stories in the Indian infrastructure complex.

Adani Energy's print fits a pattern visible across the group's results this season: strong operational delivery, expanding profit, and a balance sheet that is growing again after the consolidation of the past two years. The strategic logic is the national power-transmission build-out, where long-term contracts and regulated returns give the business unusual earnings visibility for an infrastructure company. The open questions are the familiar ones for the group — refinancing costs on a Rs 51,520 crore debt base, the pace of new project awards, and how quickly commissioned assets can lift return on equity from its current levels. Thursday's numbers answered the growth question decisively; the returns question remains the one the stock will trade on from here. The transmission sector itself is in a sweet spot — rising power demand, a national grid being rebuilt for renewables, and a pipeline of interstate projects being auctioned — and Adani Energy, among the largest private players in the space, is positioned to capture a meaningful share of that pipeline, provided it can keep funding costs from eating the spread between regulated returns and its rising interest bill.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT WERE ADANI ENERGY SOLUTIONS' Q2 FY27 NUMBERS?
Consolidated net profit rose 148% year-on-year to Rs 1,323 crore, while revenue from operations increased 53% to Rs 10,076 crore.
HOW DID FINANCE COSTS TREND?
Finance costs rose 35% to Rs 1,180 crore, while total borrowings increased to Rs 51,520 crore at end-September from Rs 48,898 crore at end-March.
WHAT DROVE THE STRONG YEAR-ON-YEAR GROWTH?
A combination of new transmission and distribution assets commissioning, a depressed year-ago base from the group's 2023 financing crunch, and doubling power-purchase costs that scale with the distribution business.
HOW DID THE FIRST HALF OF FY27 LOOK?
Net profit rose 136% to Rs 2,472 crore and revenue climbed 47% to Rs 19,787 crore, with profit before tax more than doubling to Rs 3,208 crore. GLOSSARY: - Service concession arrangements: Contracts under which a company builds or operates infrastructure in exchange for the right to charge users or receive government payments. - Consolidated results: Financials that include a parent company and all its subsidiaries as a single entity. - Finance costs: Interest and related expenses on borrowings.
