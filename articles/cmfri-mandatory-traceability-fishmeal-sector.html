CMFRI Demands Mandatory Traceability in India's Fishmeal Sector

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/CMFRI-led stakeholder consultation on April 29 calls for mandatory end-to-end traceability in India's fishmeal sector to curb IUU fishing
  • 02/A first-of-its-kind CMFRI pilot study finds mesopelagic deep-sea resources economically viable for sustainable fishmeal production
  • 03/Deputy Commissioner Sanjay Pandey urges implementation of National Traceability Framework; industry-research linkages flagged as critical

CMFRI hosted a stakeholder consultation on April 29, 2026. Marine experts demanded mandatory traceability across India's fishmeal and fish oil sector.

The call aims to curb Illegal, Unreported and Unregulated (IUU) fishing. Experts said end-to-end transparency from harvest to consumer is essential.

Industry representatives, scientists and policymakers attended the ICAR-CMFRI meet. They stressed traceability ensures sustainability, compliance and market credibility.

A first-of-its-kind CMFRI pilot study was presented. It indicated mesopelagic deep-sea resources could be economically viable for fishmeal production.

CMFRI Director Grinson George said the institute will conduct fish stock assessments. He added the focus is on fleet upgradation for deep-sea operations.

"Fishmeal needs sustainable practices to conserve resources and fetch better prices," said George Ninan, Director, ICAR-CIFT.

Deputy Commissioner Sanjay Pandey urged implementation of the National Traceability Framework. Shubdeep Ghosh, Assistant Director, ICAR, called for stronger industry-research linkages.

Participants said diversifying raw material sources is critical. This would reduce pressure on traditional fish stocks while supporting livelihoods.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT IS THE KEY DEMAND RAISED BY MARINE EXPERTS AT THE CMFRI MEET?
MARINE EXPERTS HAVE DEMANDED MANDATORY END-TO-END TRACEABILITY IN INDIA'S FISHMEAL AND FISH OIL SECTOR TO CURB ILLEGAL, UNREPORTED AND UNREGULATED (IUU) FISHING.
WHO ORGANISED THE STAKEHOLDER CONSULTATION ON FISHMEAL TRACEABILITY?
THE CONSULTATION WAS ORGANISED BY ICAR-CENTRAL MARINE FISHERIES RESEARCH INSTITUTE (CMFRI) ON APRIL 29, 2026, AND ATTENDED BY MARINE SCIENTISTS, INDUSTRY REPRESENTATIVES AND POLICYMAKERS.
WHAT DID THE CMFRI PILOT STUDY FIND?
THE FIRST-OF-ITS-KIND CMFRI PILOT STUDY IN INDIA INDICATED THAT MESOPELAGIC DEEP-SEA RESOURCES COULD BE ECONOMICALLY VIABLE FOR FISHMEAL PRODUCTION, THOUGH EXPERTS CALLED FOR FURTHER RESEARCH.
WHAT IS THE NATIONAL TRACEABILITY FRAMEWORK?
THE NATIONAL TRACEABILITY FRAMEWORK IS A REGULATORY MECHANISM URGED BY DEPUTY COMMISSIONER SANJAY PANDEY FOR IMPLEMENTATION IN THE FISHMEAL SECTOR TO ENSURE TRANSPARENCY AND ACCOUNTABILITY.
WHY DOES TRACEABILITY MATTER FOR THE FISHMEAL SECTOR?
END-TO-END TRACEABILITY FROM HARVEST TO CONSUMER ENSURES SUSTAINABILITY, REGULATORY COMPLIANCE AND MARKET CREDIBILITY. IT PREVENTS UNSUSTAINABLE PRACTICES AND SUPPORTS DOMESTIC AND INTERNATIONAL MARKET ACCESS.
