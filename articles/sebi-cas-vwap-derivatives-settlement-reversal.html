Sebi Brings Back 30-Minute VWAP for Expiry-Day Settlement

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Expiry-day settlement for stock and index derivatives will use the VWAP of underlying stocks in the last 30 minutes of normal trading.
  • •- The change reverses August's CAS framework, under which closing auction prices also settled derivatives.
  • •- CAS will continue to set official closing prices for about 200 stocks.
  • •- Normal trading in CAS stocks now runs to 3:30 pm; the auction runs 3:30-3:40 pm.
  • •- Stock and index derivatives trade until 3:45 pm; expiring index derivatives close at 3:30 pm.
  • •- The cash market reopens from 3:50 pm to 4 pm for trades at the closing price.

The Securities and Exchange Board of India admitted, in the way regulators do, that it had got one wrong. In a circular issued late on Thursday, the market regulator reverted to the volume-weighted average price method for calculating derivatives settlement prices on expiry days — unwinding the most contested element of the closing auction session framework it introduced barely two months ago.

Under the revised rules, expiring index derivatives will be settled using an index value calculated from the VWAPs of constituent stocks during the last 30 minutes of normal trading, and expiring stock derivatives will settle at the underlying stock's VWAP over the same window. That is a direct departure from the August framework, in which the closing prices discovered through the auction were also used to settle expiring derivatives — a coupling that traders blamed for chaotic, unpredictable settlement values and sharp losses on expiry days.

The closing auction session itself survives, but with a narrower job. CAS will continue to determine the official closing prices of the roughly 200 stocks it covers, separating closing-price discovery for the cash market from derivatives settlement — a distinction market participants had argued was essential. "Sebi has demonstrated its responsiveness to market needs by recognising the importance of separating derivatives settlement from the closing auction session, allowing each to serve its distinct purpose," said Ravi Varanasi, partner at SPRV Consultants. "Retaining the 30-minute VWAP methodology for derivatives settlement while preserving CAS for cash market price discovery is a pragmatic decision."

The episode is a compact case study in how market microstructure reforms can go wrong. CAS, introduced in August, collected buy and sell orders into a closing auction instead of executing them immediately, with the goal of making closing-price discovery more efficient and reducing the impact of orders placed in the final seconds of the session. What it produced instead was low participation, uncertainty over indicative Nifty and Sensex values during the auction window, and — derivatives traders complained — settlement prices that bore little relation to where the market had actually traded all afternoon. After sustained protests, Sebi sought public comments on the auction's functioning, market timings and settlement methodology; Thursday's circular is the answer.

The accompanying schedule changes are equally concrete. Normal trading in CAS-covered stocks will now continue until 3:30 pm instead of ending at 3:15 pm. The auction then runs from 3:30 pm to 3:40 pm: the first minute calculates a reference price and shifts eligible orders into the auction, limit and market orders are both accepted between 3:31 pm and 3:36 pm, after which only limit orders can be added and existing market orders cannot be modified or cancelled, and order entry closes at a random moment between 3:38 pm and 3:40 pm before matching determines the closing price. Index derivatives trade until 3:45 pm — except those expiring that day, which close at 3:30 pm — and stock derivatives trade until 3:45 pm even on expiry days. A fifteen-minute cash-market reopening from 3:50 pm to 4 pm allows trades at the closing price. Sebi also clarified that unexecuted limit orders generally carry forward into the auction, except stop-loss orders and orders priced outside permitted bands.

The market's reaction on Friday morning was immediate: shares of capital-market participants — the NSE, the BSE and brokerages such as Groww — rose as much as 3 per cent, the market pricing in smoother expiry mechanics and, arguably, a regulator that corrects course quickly. The deeper lesson of the CAS affair is about sequencing: closing-price reform and derivatives settlement were always separable problems, and bundling them created two weeks of expiry-day anxiety that the unbundling has now resolved. India's derivatives market is among the world's largest by volume; the plumbing that settles it at expiry deserves the same engineering care as the products themselves.

For the hundreds of thousands of retail traders who took the other side of those August expiry settlements, the circular is vindication — and a reminder of how much of the index-options economy runs on the details of settlement methodology. The 30-minute VWAP window is broad enough to resist manipulation, familiar from the pre-August regime, and now explicitly firewalled from the closing auction's price-discovery function. Sebi's consultation process, for all its tardiness, produced a market-endorsed answer: the auction can keep the closing price, and the derivatives market can keep its settlement. Each mechanism now does one job, which is generally the point at which complex market infrastructure stops misbehaving.

Whether the closing auction itself survives in the long run is a question Thursday's circular pointedly did not answer. With settlement no longer riding on its output, CAS must now justify itself purely on the quality of cash-market closing prices — and the next few months of participation data will determine whether it earns that justification or follows the settlement coupling into the bin.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT DID SEBI CHANGE?
Expiry-day derivatives settlement reverts to the 30-minute VWAP of underlying stocks, decoupled from the closing auction session that had been setting settlement prices since August.
WHAT HAPPENS TO THE CLOSING AUCTION SESSION?
CAS survives but only sets official closing prices for about 200 stocks; it no longer determines derivatives settlement values.
WHAT ARE THE NEW TIMINGS?
Normal trading in CAS stocks runs to 3:30 pm, CAS runs 3:30-3:40 pm, derivatives trade to 3:45 pm (expiring index derivatives close at 3:30 pm), and the cash market reopens 3:50-4 pm for closing-price trades.
WHY DID SEBI REVERSE COURSE?
Trader complaints about low auction participation, uncertain indicative index values and sharp expiry-day losses prompted public consultations, leading to the revised framework. GLOSSARY: - VWAP (volume-weighted average price): The average price of a security weighted by traded volume over a period — the benchmark used for settlement calculations. - CAS (closing auction session): A closing window where buy and sell orders are collected and matched to determine a stock's official closing price. - Expiry-day settlement: The final cash settlement of derivatives contracts on their expiration date, based on the official settlement price. - Derivatives: Financial contracts (futures and options) whose value derives from an underlying stock or index.
