Rupee Crashes to 96.31 vs Dollar, Biggest Fall in 2 Months

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/The rupee fell half a per cent to 96.3150 against the US dollar on Thursday, logging its steepest single-day decline in more than two months.
  • 02/Rising US bond yields lifted the dollar index sharply to 101.9, denting emerging-market currencies across the board.
  • 03/The rupee has now breached the psychological 96 level twice in three days; its lowest close on record remains 96.96 from May 20.

The Indian rupee crashed half a percentage point on Thursday, October 1, 2026. It fell to 96.3150 against the US dollar, marking its biggest single-day decline in more than two months.

The slide came on a day when India's prime equity gauges were already under heavy stress. Benchmark indices posted their longest losing run in a quarter century, or 25 years.

The trigger for the rupee's fall was a rise in US bond yields. Higher yields strengthen the dollar and put emerging-market currencies under simultaneous pressure.

That pressure was clearly visible in the dollar index, which tracks the greenback against a basket of major currencies. The index climbed sharply to 101.9 during the session.

A half-percentage-point move is large by currency-market standards. Rupee pairs typically trade in fractions of a per cent per day, so a 0.5% fall signals strong and broad-based selling rather than routine volatility.

The rupee had closed the previous session at 95.83 per dollar. Thursday's drop of half a per cent from that level was steep by the currency's recent standards.

More significantly, the rupee breached the psychological 96 level for the second time in three days. Repeated breaks of a round-number threshold indicate that depreciation pressure is building rather than fading.

To put the move in context, the rupee's lowest close so far remains 96.96 per dollar, recorded on May 20, 2026. Thursday's level of 96.3150 is still roughly 65 paise above that record low, but the gap has narrowed quickly.

The weakness was not unique to India. When US bond yields rise, global investors tend to rotate out of riskier emerging-market assets and toward dollar-denominated debt, hitting local currencies in tandem.

On Thursday, that dynamic played out in full in domestic markets. Stocks and the currency fell together, with the equity benchmarks extending their worst losing streak in a generation.

The simultaneous pressure on the rupee and the equity gauges underlines how exposed emerging markets remain to US interest-rate moves. A stronger dollar raises the cost of dollar debt and discourages fresh foreign inflows.

Psychological levels such as 96 matter in currency markets because they shape trader positioning, corporate hedging, and importer behaviour. Each breach tends to accelerate demand for dollars from businesses rushing to cover exposure.

For now, the rupee remains above its record low close of 96.96 hit in May. But the speed of the recent decline, with two breaches of the 96 mark in three sessions, shows the currency is on the back foot.
