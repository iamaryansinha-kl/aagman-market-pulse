India CPI Inflation Hits 5.54% in September, Highest Since Oct 2024

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

India's retail inflation climbed to 5.54% in September 2026, data released on Monday showed, marking the sharpest price escalation since October 2024 and the highest reading of the current year under the existing CPI series. The print accelerated from 4.83% in August and came in above the 5.4% forecast in a Reuters poll of economists, extending a run of upside surprises that has now lasted four consecutive months above the Reserve Bank of India's 4% medium-term target.

The September number takes headline inflation within striking distance of the upper end of the RBI's 2-6% tolerance band, dramatically narrowing the central bank's room to look through supply shocks. With the Iran conflict keeping crude elevated and a below-normal monsoon hitting farm output, price pressures are broadening beyond food into fuel-linked categories, setting up a difficult policy trade-off between supporting growth and defending price stability.

The food basket was the single biggest driver of the acceleration. Onion prices surged, while sugar, milk and cereals also turned costlier, according to HDFC Bank principal economist Sakshi Gupta. Food inflation had already been creeping up through August, but September's 70-basis-point jump in the headline rate points to a wider set of sub-segments contributing to the rise, DBS Bank senior economist Radhika Rao said. Below-normal rainfall, imported price pressures and the knock-on effect of costlier fuel have all fed into the food complex.

Core inflation, which strips out volatile food and fuel items, is also flashing warning signs. The RBI expects core inflation at 4.4%, and Rao said the core gauge now aligns with the central bank's assessment that price risks are "no longer benign". Non-food components are expected to feel the impact of an upcoming pass-through of higher fuel costs, suggesting the September print may not be the peak even if global crude stabilises.

The policy response is now the main question for markets. Alexandra Hermann Prasad, lead economist at Oxford Economics, said the print reinforces the case for more rate hikes and expects two further 25-basis-point increases, in December and February. "The RBI last week clearly demonstrated a willingness to change tune quickly as inflation, and inflation upside risks, continue to rise," Prasad said, noting that underlying demand pressures remain soft but are being overwhelmed by supply-side impulses.

Elara Securities economist Garima Kapoor said she sees another two hikes from the RBI in the current financial year as sticky commodity prices and rising global yields keep the central bank on a firmly hawkish path. Kotak Mahindra Bank chief economist Upasna Bhardwaj went further, projecting the inflation trajectory to trend above 6% over the next few months as adverse base effects and food price shocks play out. She expects an additional 50 basis points of repo rate hikes, with upside risks if geopolitical tensions do not subside.

IDFC FIRST Bank chief economist Gaura Sengupta struck a relatively measured tone, noting that core inflation remains moderate and that much of the pressure is supply-driven. She sees full-year FY27 CPI inflation tracking 5.2%, with a terminal repo rate of 6.0% to 6.25%. EMKAY Global chief economist Madhavi Arora expects the third quarter of FY27 to average close to 6.1%, which would leave the real policy rate negative even after further hikes, and retains a call for 75 basis points of cumulative tightening, delivered in 25-basis-point steps in December and February.

HDFC Bank's Gupta expects the next release to print closer to 6% and full-year inflation to settle at 5.4% as second-round effects of elevated oil prices and uneven monsoons feed through. Vikram Chhabra of 360 ONE Asset said he expects inflation to inch close to 6% by end-2026, with deficient monsoon hurting kharif yields and low reservoir levels posing a risk to rabi output. He is pencilling in another 50 basis points of rate hikes in the current cycle.

For households, the squeeze is already visible. The RBI's own survey showed household inflation expectations at 9.1% for September and rising, well above the actual print. For bond markets, the inflation trajectory has been the dominant force behind a relentless sell-off that has taken the benchmark 10-year government bond yield to a three-year high above 7.37%, with the RBI itself adding to the pressure through open-market sales of gilts and a higher cash-reserve maintenance requirement.

The rupee, meanwhile, has been under periodic pressure from elevated oil import bills, and a tighter policy stance would help defend the currency while anchoring expectations. Equity markets have taken the shift in stride so far, but rate-sensitive sectors such as autos, real estate and consumer durables face a margin and demand squeeze if borrowing costs ratchet higher into the festive season.

The RBI's own projections, revised at the recent policy review, now see third-quarter FY27 inflation at 6%, up from 5.9% earlier, even as it upgraded the growth forecast to 7.1%. Monday's print suggests those inflation risks are tilted to the upside. The central bank's next policy decision lands in early December, and after September's surprise, very few economists are willing to bet against another rate increase.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT WAS INDIA'S CPI INFLATION IN SEPTEMBER 2026?
Retail inflation, measured by the Consumer Price Index, rose to 5.54% year-on-year in September 2026, up from 4.83% in August and above the 5.4% economists' forecast.
WHY IS THE SEPTEMBER INFLATION PRINT SIGNIFICANT?
It is the highest since October 2024, the fourth straight month above the RBI's 4% medium-term target, and it moves headline inflation close to the 6% upper limit of the central bank's tolerance band.
WHAT IS DRIVING INFLATION HIGHER?
Food inflation is the main driver, with onions, sugar, milk and cereals rising, alongside imported price pressures from the West Asia energy shock, higher fuel costs and a deficient monsoon hurting farm output.
WHAT DO ECONOMISTS EXPECT THE RBI TO DO?
Most expect at least one more repo rate hike in December 2026, with several forecasting two to three hikes in total and a terminal repo rate between 6% and 7% this cycle.
HOW DOES THIS AFFECT BOND MARKETS AND HOUSEHOLDS?
Rising inflation expectations have driven the 10-year bond yield to a three-year high above 7.37%, while household inflation expectations at 9.1% signal consumers are already feeling the squeeze. GLOSSARY: Consumer Price Index (CPI): The main measure of retail inflation in India, tracking price changes in a basket of goods and services urban and rural households consume. Repo rate: The interest rate at which the RBI lends to commercial banks, and the primary tool of monetary policy. Tolerance band: The RBI's mandated inflation range of 2% to 6% around the 4% target; sustained breaches trigger a formal explanation to the government. Basis points: One hundredth of a percentage point; used to describe changes in interest rates and yields. Core inflation: CPI excluding volatile food and fuel items, used to gauge underlying price pressures. Base effect: The impact of the year-ago price level on the current inflation reading; an unusually low or high prior-year base distorts the comparison. Rabi and kharif: India's two crop seasons; kharif crops are sown with the monsoon and harvested in autumn, while rabi crops are sown in winter and harvested in spring. Real policy rate: The repo rate adjusted for inflation; a negative reading means money is effectively cheap even after nominal hikes.
