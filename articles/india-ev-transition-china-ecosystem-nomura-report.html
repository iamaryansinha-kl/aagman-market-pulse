India's EV Transition Needs China-Style Ecosystem Push: Nomura Report

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Nomura report says charging infrastructure availability is the biggest enabler for India's EV mass adoption and transition away from ICE vehicles
  • 02/China's EV penetration surged from 5.7% in 2020 to 53.3% in 2025, backed by ~20 million public charging points and sustained policy support
  • 03/Nomura recommends India gradually shift from FAME subsidies to ecosystem-driven approach with tax benefits, scrappage policies, and credit mechanisms

India's electric vehicle transition needs a coordinated ecosystem push similar to China's model. A Nomura report released on May 1, 2026 highlights charging infrastructure as the critical trigger for mass EV adoption.

The report states that charging availability remains the biggest enabler in shifting consumers away from internal combustion engine vehicles. "Charging infra as a key enabler — infra-readiness can unlock mass adoption," the Nomura report said.

China has scaled to approximately 20 million public charging points by 2025. The network covers tier 1 and tier 2 cities plus highways, though lower-tier regions still face gaps.

This infrastructure push accelerated China's EV penetration sharply. It rose from 5.7 per cent in 2020 to 53.3 per cent in 2025, reflecting structural market transformation.

"ICE vehicles are in a structural decline and steadily losing market share as EVs have achieved cost parity, technological superiority and mainstream consumer acceptance," the report stated.

China's success came from sustained policy support and coordinated investments across the value chain. State-owned enterprises, private companies, and energy players all contributed to the build-out.

While China phased out direct subsidies in 2022, it continued supporting EVs through purchase tax exemptions. It also used scrappage incentives and the New Energy Vehicle credit system to push traditional automakers toward electrification.

For India, Nomura suggests shifting gradually from upfront subsidies like FAME and PM E-Drive. The focus should move to tax benefits, scrappage policies, and credit mechanisms instead.

Nomura identified three key success factors for Chinese EV manufacturers. These are sustained R&D investments, value chain control, and rapid scale-building to reduce costs.

"Those who build cost advantages with scale will be better positioned to gain market share," the report stated. Indian manufacturers need to build core competencies in battery technology, software, and supply chain localisation.
