Indian Oil PSUs Bleed ₹30,000 Crore as Fuel Prices Held Steady Amid Global Shock

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/India's state-run oil marketing companies have bled an estimated ₹30,000 crore in under-recoveries since mid-March 2026 by keeping retail fuel prices frozen
  • 02/Brent crude surged over 50% from $72 to nearly $144 per barrel after the US and Israel struck Iran on February 28, disrupting the Strait of Hormuz
  • 03/Government cut excise duty by ₹10 per litre to absorb part of the burden; prolonged high crude could force capex recalibration

India's state-run oil marketing companies have bled an estimated ₹30,000 crore in under-recoveries since mid-March 2026. Indian Oil Corporation Ltd (IOC), Bharat Petroleum Corporation Ltd (BPCL) and Hindustan Petroleum Corporation Ltd (HPCL) kept retail fuel prices frozen despite surging crude costs.

Retail prices for petrol, diesel, LPG and aviation turbine fuel have not changed since February 28, 2026. Brent crude was around $72 per barrel before the US and Israel struck Iran that day.

Brent crude surged over 50% as the conflict widened and Iran closed the Strait of Hormuz. Prices briefly touched nearly $144 per barrel at the peak of the escalation.

Daily under-recoveries for the oil marketing companies were estimated at about ₹18 per litre on petrol and ₹25 per litre on diesel during April. This translated into average losses of ₹600-700 crore per day.

The government cut excise duty on petrol by ₹10 per litre and on diesel by ₹10 per litre to soften the blow. Without this intervention, the total loss would have swelled to nearly ₹62,500 crore, sources said.

India's petrol price stayed at ₹94.77 per litre and diesel at ₹87.67 per litre. In contrast, Spain saw a 34% hike, Japan 30%, Germany 27% and the United Kingdom 22%.

The companies also faced higher freight charges, elevated marine insurance and emergency crude sourcing costs. Supply networks were stretched by panic buying after the Strait of Hormuz disruption.

Sources warned that prolonged high crude prices could raise working capital borrowings and force some capital expenditure recalibration. Investments in refining expansion and energy security infrastructure will continue with government backing.

WHY DID GLOBAL CRUDE OIL PRICES SURGE SO SHARPLY?
BRENT CRUDE WAS AROUND $72 PER BARREL BEFORE THE US AND ISRAEL LAUNCHED STRIKES ON IRAN ON FEBRUARY 28, 2026. THE CONFLICT WIDENED, IRAN CLOSED THE STRAIT OF HORMUZ, AND PRICES BRIEFLY JUMPED TO NEARLY $144 PER BARREL.
WHAT UNDER-RECOVERIES ARE THE OIL COMPANIES FACING PER LITRE?
DAILY UNDER-RECOVERIES WERE ESTIMATED AT ABOUT ₹18 PER LITRE ON PETROL AND ₹25 PER LITRE ON DIESEL DURING APRIL. AT PEAK CRUDE PRICES, THE CENTRE'S EFFECTIVE ABSORPTION WAS ESTIMATED AT AROUND ₹24 PER LITRE FOR PETROL AND ₹30 PER LITRE FOR DIESEL.
HOW DOES INDIA'S FUEL PRICING COMPARE TO OTHER COUNTRIES?
PETROL PRICES ROSE ABOUT 34% IN SPAIN, 30% IN JAPAN, ITALY AND ISRAEL, 27% IN GERMANY AND 22% IN THE UNITED KINGDOM. IN INDIA, PETROL REMAINED AT ₹94.77 AND DIESEL AT ₹87.67 WITH NO RATIONING OR SUPPLY DISRUPTIONS.
WHAT ARE THE RISKS AHEAD FOR THE OIL MARKETING COMPANIES?
PROLONGED ELEVATED CRUDE PRICES COULD LEAD TO HIGHER WORKING CAPITAL BORROWINGS AND SOME RECALIBRATION OF CAPITAL EXPENDITURE PLANS. INVESTMENTS IN REFINING EXPANSION, ENERGY SECURITY, ETHANOL BLENDING AND BIOFUELS WILL CONTINUE WITH GOVERNMENT BACKING.
