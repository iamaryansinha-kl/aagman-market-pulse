Markets 'Factored In' 6.6% GDP Growth, 5.1% Inflation Amid Oil Shock: SEBI Analyst

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/SEBI-registered analyst Kunal Saraogi says Indian markets have already priced in lower GDP growth at 6.6% and higher inflation at 5.1%
  • 02/The oil shock from West Asia tensions has driven Brent crude near $96 per barrel, widening India's current account deficit to an estimated 2.2%
  • 03/Saraogi expects Indian equities to recover over the next year, advising investors to stay invested in quality stocks

Indian markets have already "factored in" concerns around lower GDP growth and higher inflation projections for 2026. SEBI-registered research analyst Kunal Saraogi told ANI that the ongoing oil shock and geopolitical tensions are largely priced in.

GDP growth for the current fiscal year is projected at 6.6 per cent, down from 7.1 per cent last year. Inflation is forecast at 5.1 per cent, above the RBI's comfortable target band of 4 per cent.

"Markets have already factored that in; that is because of this oil shock that we are going through, and we have seen much worse shocks in 2020," Saraogi said. He was speaking at the PHD Chamber of Commerce and Industry's 8th Annual Convention on Capital Market & Commodity Market.

The S&P Global and Crisil 'India Forward' report projects Brent crude averaging close to $96 per barrel this fiscal. India's current account deficit is expected to widen from 0.8 per cent to roughly 2.2 per cent as energy imports surge.

India imports 85-90 per cent of its crude oil needs, with 45-50 per cent coming from West Asia. This makes the economy particularly sensitive to disruptions in the region, including the Strait of Hormuz.

The rupee is projected to weaken toward 93 per dollar amid capital outflows. Benchmark 10-year government bond yields are expected to settle around 7 per cent by year-end.

Saraogi acknowledged India's underperformance relative to other markets. "India is a big importer of energy. So because of that we have been hurt more than other countries and that has caused this underperformance," he explained.

However, Saraogi expressed optimism for Indian equities over the next year. "The worst of this is behind us and next one year I think will be good," he said. He advised investors to remain invested despite volatility.

On the shift from fixed deposits to mutual funds, Saraogi noted that India's retail participation is growing. "We now have about 24 crore demat accounts. So stock markets are no longer the preserve of the rich or the very well-educated," he added.

The Sensex closed at 77,328.19 on May 9, down 516.33 points. The Nifty ended at 24,176.15, falling 150.50 points. Crude oil traded at ₹9,020, down ₹45.
