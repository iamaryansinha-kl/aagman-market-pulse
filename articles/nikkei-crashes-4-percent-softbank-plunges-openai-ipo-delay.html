Nikkei Crashes 4.15% as SoftBank Plunges 12.53% on OpenAI IPO Delay Report

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Japan's Nikkei 225 plunged 4.15% to close at 69,360.88 on June 26, erasing most gains from the previous session's record high
  • 02/SoftBank Group crashed 12.53% after the New York Times reported OpenAI is considering delaying its IPO until next year
  • 03/AI-related stocks Advantest and Kioxia fell 9.64% and 11.24% respectively, while strategists still see long-term rally potential in chip sectors

Japan's Nikkei 225 share average closed 4.15% lower on Friday, June 26, 2026. The benchmark fell to 69,360.88, erasing most of the gains from the previous session.

The index had surged 4.6% to a record high in the previous session. Friday's drop left the Nikkei down 2.65% for the week.

SoftBank Group, the tech investment giant led by CEO Masayoshi Son, plummeted 12.53%. The sell-off was triggered by a New York Times report that OpenAI is considering delaying its initial public offering until next year.

"The news was negative for SoftBank Group as well as overall investors as AI is the centre of the market," said Shuutarou Yasuda, market analyst at Tokai Tokyo Intelligence Laboratory. "The market wondered if there was anything negative in the industry outlook."

Other AI-related heavyweights tumbled alongside SoftBank. Advantest fell 9.64% and Tokyo Electron dropped 3.21%. Memory chipmaker Kioxia crashed 11.24%.

The broader Topix index closed 1.32% lower at 3,963.36, slipping 2% for the week. Sentiment was further hurt by a sharp decline in South Korea's KOSPI, which triggered circuit breakers for the second time this week.

"The sharp decline in the Korean benchmark also hurt investor sentiment," said Naoki Fujiwara, senior fund manager at Shinkin Asset Management.

Despite the rout, strategists expect the Nikkei's rally to continue. "AI and chip-related shares have been volatile, but in the long term, their stock prices will be firm, supported by solid earnings," said Chizuru Morishita, researcher at NLI Research Institute. "The AI-boom has brought an industry revolution, not just a temporary trend."

Bucking the broader decline, Toyota Motor gained 0.9% and bank shares rose. Mizuho Financial Group and Sumitomo Mitsui Financial Group each climbed more than 0.8%.

Of more than 1,500 stocks on the Tokyo Stock Exchange's prime market, 58% rose, 39% fell and 2% traded flat.
