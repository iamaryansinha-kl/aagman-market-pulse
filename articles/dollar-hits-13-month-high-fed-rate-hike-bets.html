Dollar Index Hits 13-Month High at 101.8 as Fed Rate Hike Bets Surge

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Dollar index touched a 13-month peak of 101.8 on Wednesday, driven by shifting expectations from Fed rate cuts to hikes
  • 02/Traders now price in one rate hike as soon as October and a 50/50 chance of a second by year-end, reversing pre-war bets
  • 03/May core PCE inflation data due Thursday; economists expect 3.4%, well above the Fed's 2% target

The dollar index touched a 13-month high of 101.8 on Wednesday. It was trading around 101.5 on Thursday, heading for its biggest monthly gain in nearly a year.

The greenback hit a 13-month peak against the euro, pushing the single currency below $1.14. Sterling sank to $1.316, having hit a seven-month low of $1.314 on Wednesday.

The Japanese yen remained near its weakest in 40 years at around 161.9 per dollar. Further gains could trigger Japanese intervention, which traders expect around the 162 level.

Gold briefly fell below $4,000 an ounce for the first time in over seven months. Bitcoin also slipped under $60,000 for the first time since 2024, both hurt by dollar strength.

This month, 2-year U.S. Treasuries have surged 14 basis points to 4.16%. That compares with just a 2-bp rise in German 2-year yields to 2.56%.

Traders who expected Fed rate cuts before the U.S.-Israeli war on Iran now see hikes ahead. The market is pricing one hike as soon as October and a 50/50 chance of a second by year-end.

"If the Fed is serious about restoring price stability, a significant tightening of monetary policy will be required," said MUFG currency strategist Lee Hardman. He noted this "makes sense that more hikes have been priced in, recently encouraging a stronger U.S. dollar."

The Fed's preferred inflation gauge, core PCE for May, is due Thursday. Economists polled by Reuters expect a 3.4% reading, well above the central bank's 2% target.

"My view is that this is creating a USD-positive feedback loop where speculators are adding and technicals are breaking," said Brent Donnelly, president at Spectra Markets. "That feedback loop will probably burn itself out soon."

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
HOW HIGH DID THE DOLLAR INDEX CLIMB?
THE DOLLAR INDEX, WHICH MEASURES THE U.S. CURRENCY AGAINST A BASKET OF SIX OTHERS, TOUCHED A 13-MONTH PEAK OF 101.8 ON WEDNESDAY. IT WAS TRADING AROUND 101.5 ON THURSDAY.
WHAT IS DRIVING THE DOLLAR'S SURGE?
INVESTORS ARE INCREASINGLY BETTING THAT THE FEDERAL RESERVE WILL RAISE INTEREST RATES AT LEAST ONCE THIS YEAR, POSSIBLY AS SOON AS OCTOBER, WITH A 50/50 CHANCE OF A SECOND HIKE BY YEAR-END. THIS IS A SHARP REVERSAL FROM EARLIER EXPECTATIONS OF RATE CUTS.
HOW ARE OTHER CURRENCIES AND ASSETS AFFECTED?
THE EURO FELL BELOW $1.14, STERLING HIT A SEVEN-MONTH LOW OF $1.314, AND THE YEN REMAINED NEAR 161.9 PER DOLLAR — ITS WEAKEST IN 40 YEARS. GOLD BRIEFLY DROPPED BELOW $4,000 AN OUNCE, AND BITCOIN SLIPPED UNDER $60,000 FOR THE FIRST TIME SINCE 2024.
WHAT INFLATION DATA IS DUE AND WHY DOES IT MATTER?
THE FED'S PREFERRED INFLATION GAUGE, CORE PERSONAL CONSUMPTION EXPENDITURES (PCE) FOR MAY, IS DUE THURSDAY. ECONOMISTS EXPECT A 3.4% RISE, WELL ABOVE THE FED'S 2% TARGET, WHICH COULD REINFORCE RATE HIKE EXPECTATIONS.
WHAT IS THE OUTLOOK FOR THE DOLLAR?
ANALYSTS ARE DIVIDED. MUFG STRATEGIST LEE HARDMAN SAYS MORE TIGHTENING IS NEEDED TO RESTORE PRICE STABILITY. BUT BRENT DONNELLY OF SPECTRA MARKETS WARNS THE USD-POSITIVE FEEDBACK LOOP WILL "PROBABLY BURN ITSELF OUT SOON." JAPAN MAY ALSO INTERVENE IF THE YEN HITS 162 PER DOLLAR.
