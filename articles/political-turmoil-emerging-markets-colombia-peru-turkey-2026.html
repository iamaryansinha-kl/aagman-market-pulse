Political Turmoil Hits Emerging Markets: Colombia Bonds Plunge 4.4% as Leftists Gain

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Colombia local bonds crashed 4.4% in the last month, the worst performer in the developing world, as left-wing Senator Ivan Cepeda gains traction ahead of presidential elections
  • 02/Peru's currency weakened 1.5% after leftist Roberto Sanchez secured a runoff spot, while Turkey's opposition party was hollowed out by a court ruling that triggered a stock market circuit breaker
  • 03/Major asset managers including JPMorgan, Allianz, and PPM America are cutting exposure to Colombia, Peru, and Brazil as political uncertainty collides with rising inflation and interest rates

Emerging markets are getting slammed by a fresh wave of political turmoil stretching from Latin America to Eastern Europe. Investors are reducing exposure as elections and court rulings derail months of strong returns.

Colombia is the hardest hit. Local bonds posted a 4.4% loss in the last month, the worst performance across the developing world. Left-wing Senator Ivan Cepeda is gaining ground in presidential polls, leaving traders fretting over a divided opposition.

Peru is rattled too. The yield on 30-year local notes is hovering near the highest since August. The currency has weakened 1.5% since leftist Roberto Sanchez unexpectedly secured a runoff spot.

Turkey offered the starkest reminder of how fast political risk can re-emerge. An Ankara appeals court annulled the 2023 congress of the Republican People's Party, hollowing out the political opposition. The decision sparked a rout that triggered a circuit breaker in stocks and sent dollar bonds plunging.

Bolivian bonds tumbled as street protests against the government threatened supplies of food and medicine to the nation's capital. In Malaysia, markets briefly roiled after Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim raised the prospect of a snap general election.

The episodes are a fresh reminder of underlying risks that still plague the asset class, even after delivering strong returns over the past year. "Political risk manifests itself when the macro is under pressure," said Francesc Balcells, chief investment officer at FIM Partners, whose firm oversees $5 billion.

"I wouldn't be surprised if we see more of that going forward," Balcells added. The flareups come as developing economies grapple with higher inflation amid the war in Iran and a surge in US Treasury yields.

Major fund houses are heading into election season with trimmed positions. Matthew Graves at PPM America said he's carrying "pretty limited exposure." Jeff Grills, head of EM debt at Aegon Asset Management, is underweight Brazilian debt as elections add to fiscal doubts.

WHY ARE INVESTORS PULLING MONEY OUT OF EMERGING MARKETS NOW?
INVESTORS ARE REDUCING EXPOSURE DUE TO UPCOMING PRESIDENTIAL ELECTIONS IN COLOMBIA AND PERU WHERE LEFT-WING CANDIDATES ARE GAINING TRACTION. THIS POLITICAL INSTABILITY IS COUPLED WITH RISING INFLATION, SURGING US TREASURY YIELDS, AND HIGHER INTEREST RATES ACROSS DEVELOPING ECONOMIES.

AN ANKARA APPEALS COURT ANNULLED THE RESULTS OF THE 2023 CONGRESS OF THE REPUBLICAN PEOPLE'S PARTY, THE MAIN OPPOSITION. THIS DECISION SPARKED A ROUT THAT TRIGGERED A CIRCUIT BREAKER IN STOCKS, SENT DOLLAR BONDS PLUNGING, AND PROMPTED STATE LENDERS TO HEAVILY INTERVENE IN CURRENCY MARKETS.
