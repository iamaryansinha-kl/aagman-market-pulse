India to Maintain ₹12.22 Lakh Cr Capex Despite West Asia Stress

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Expenditure Secretary V Vualnam confirms India will maintain the budgeted ₹12.22 lakh crore capital expenditure for FY27 despite global fiscal stress.
  • 02/The West Asia war since February 28 has sent crude oil soaring from $73 to $126 per barrel, creating severe fiscal uncertainty for India.
  • 03/FY27 fiscal deficit is now seen at 4.5% of GDP vs the budgeted 4.3%, following a downward revision in nominal GDP under the new series.

Expenditure Secretary V Vualnam has affirmed that India will stick to its budgeted ₹12.22 lakh crore capital expenditure for FY27. The commitment comes despite mounting fiscal stress triggered by the prolonged West Asia crisis.

Speaking at the ICPP Growth Conference organised by Ashoka University, Vualnam said capex is a priority item. He stated the government will preserve it at budgeted levels regardless of emerging stress points.

The West Asia war that began on February 28 has pushed crude oil prices to a four-year high of $126 per barrel. This is a sharp jump from the roughly $73 level seen before the conflict.

India imports 60 per cent of its LPG usage. Of that, 90 per cent flows through the now-closed Strait of Hormuz. Vualnam called this a "very challenging situation" for the domestic energy supply chain.

The FY27 Budget had pegged the fiscal deficit at 4.3 per cent of GDP. Following a downward revision in India's nominal GDP under the new series, the deficit is now seen at 4.5 per cent of GDP.

To contain retail petrol and diesel prices, the Centre cut excise duties. This move is estimated to cost the exchequer ₹7,000 crore for a 15-day period alone.

The government has also imposed an export duty of ₹23 per litre on diesel. Aviation turbine fuel faces a steeper levy of ₹33 per litre to ensure adequate domestic availability.

Tax buoyancy is another concern, Vualnam noted. Revenue pressures could further squeeze the already tight fiscal space in the coming quarters.

Focus areas for government capex this fiscal include highways, railways, shipping, ports and urban development. Vualnam said these sectors are deemed critical for sustaining growth momentum.

"So, the fiscal stress is indeed very much a reality, but at the same time the priority sectors... the capex would really be a priority item which we would like to preserve," Vualnam said.

Despite the headwinds, Vualnam struck a note of cautious optimism. He said India's fiscal prudence over the years has placed the country on solid footing during these unpredictable times.
