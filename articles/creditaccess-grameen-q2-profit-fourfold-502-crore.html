CreditAccess Grameen Profit Jumps Fourfold to Rs 502 Crore

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- CreditAccess Grameen's Q2 net profit surged fourfold to Rs 502 crore from Rs 126 crore a year earlier.
  • •- Total income rose 26% to Rs 1,905 crore while expenses fell to Rs 1,234 crore from Rs 1,340 crore.
  • •- Gross NPA improved to 1.79% from 4.06%; credit cost provisions dropped to Rs 299 crore from Rs 526 crore.
  • •- The lender raised FY27 return guidance sharply — ROA to 5.2-5.8% from 4.0-4.8%, ROE to 21-24% from 16-20%.

Microfinance's clean-up is showing up in the numbers now. CreditAccess Grameen, India's largest NBFC-MFI, reported a fourfold surge in second-quarter net profit to Rs 502 crore from Rs 126 crore a year earlier, as lower credit costs and improved asset quality compounded into sharply higher profitability. The print was strong enough for the lender to tear up its own return guidance: return on assets for the year is now guided at 5.2-5.8%, up from an earlier 4.0-4.8%, and return on equity at 21-24%, up from 16-20%. For a sector that spent two years absorbing the pain of over-rapid growth and a regulatory crackdown, a guidance reset of that size is a statement, not a footnote.

The P&L mechanics tell the story cleanly. Total income rose 26% year-on-year to Rs 1,905 crore from Rs 1,509 crore, while total expenses actually fell to Rs 1,234 crore from Rs 1,340 crore — revenue compounding and costs declining at the same time. The swing factor was credit cost: the lender provided Rs 299 crore to cover credit risk in the quarter, against Rs 526 crore in the year-ago period, a 43% decline that flowed almost directly to the bottom line. Credit cost is the single most important line in any microfinance P&L, and this quarter it behaved.

The balance sheet confirms the recovery is real, not cosmetic. Assets under management grew 19.5% year-on-year to Rs 30,946 crore, with management guiding annual AUM growth of 20-25% and credit cost at 3-4%. The asset-quality transformation is the headline within the headline: gross non-performing assets fell to 1.79% at the end of September from 4.06% a year back — less than half the year-ago ratio, and a level the sector has not seen since before the 2022 stress. Provisions are not just lower because the lender got lucky with recoveries; the underlying book is performing.

Management is pairing the confidence with selectivity and price cuts. "We will remain cautious in districts showing drought-related challenges, while meeting the growing credit needs of our seasoned customers, and stepping up new customer acquisition," managing director Ganesh Narayanan said — an explicit acknowledgement that rural stress is not evenly distributed, and that the book will grow by choosing geographies rather than blanket expansion. The company also cut loan pricing by 50 basis points from October, a move that signals both lower funding costs and enough confidence in asset quality to compete for share rather than defend margin through rate.

The market context makes the quarter more notable. Microfinance lenders spent 2023-24 under the shadow of self-regulatory organisation tightening, state-level lending disruptions, and questions about over-indebtedness in borrower households. CreditAccess's numbers suggest the sector has come out the other side: growth is back near 20%, credit costs are guided at manageable mid-single digits, and the largest player is guiding ROE above 20%. The stock market has been re-rating the sector through the year, and a fourfold profit print with doubled guidance gives that re-rating a fresh anchor.

The stock market has been re-rating the sector through the year, and a fourfold profit print with doubled guidance gives that re-rating a fresh anchor. It also sets a benchmark rivals will now be measured against: if the biggest book can halve its gross NPA ratio in twelve months while compounding AUM near 20%, the laggards' excuses get thinner with every results season.

The guidance reset deserves its own emphasis, because it is rare for a lender to move its return targets this aggressively mid-year. Raising ROA guidance by more than a full percentage point implies management believes the credit-cost improvement is structural rather than cyclical — that the book it has rebuilt post-2022 is simply better underwritten than the one that created the stress. If the 3-4% guided credit cost holds through the second half, the ROE range of 21-24% would place CreditAccess among the most profitable lenders in Indian financial services, small-ticket lending included.

For investors, the watch-items are the ones management itself named. Drought-related districts could re-emerge as credit-cost pressure if the rural economy softens into the second half; the 50-basis-point pricing cut will test whether yields can stay above funding costs if competition intensifies; and a 20-25% AUM growth target is ambitious in a category where regulators remain watchful about over-leveraging borrowers. But those are the problems of a lender growing into strength, not defending against stress. The fourfold profit jump is the proof point; the doubled guidance is the claim; the next two quarters will show whether rural India cooperates with both.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT DROVE THE FOURFOLD PROFIT JUMP?
Lower credit costs (provisions of Rs 299 crore vs Rs 526 crore a year earlier), 26% revenue growth, and a gross NPA ratio that halved to 1.79% from 4.06%.
WHAT GUIDANCE DID THE COMPANY RAISE?
FY27 ROA guidance to 5.2-5.8% from 4.0-4.8%, and ROE guidance to 21-24% from 16-20%. AUM growth is guided at 20-25% with credit cost at 3-4%.
WHAT ELSE DID MANAGEMENT ANNOUNCE?
A 50-basis-point cut in loan pricing from October, and caution on lending in districts facing drought-related challenges. GLOSSARY: CREDIT COST — The expense of provisioning for loan losses, expressed in rupees or as a percentage of average assets; the key swing line in a lender's P&L. NBFC-MFI — A non-banking financial company licensed as a microfinance institution, lending to low-income households, typically in rural areas. ROA / ROE — Return on assets and return on equity; the standard profitability measures lenders guide against.
