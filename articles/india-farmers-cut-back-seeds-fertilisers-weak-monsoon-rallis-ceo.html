India's Farmers Cut Back on Seeds, Fertilisers as Weak Monsoon Dents Crop Confidence, Rallis India CEO Says

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

India's weak monsoon is prompting farmers to cut back on spending on pesticides, fertilisers and seeds this year, the chief executive of Tata Chemicals unit Rallis India said.

The June-September monsoon, which accounts for nearly 70% of the country's annual rainfall, has been erratic this year and remains well below normal. This raises concerns about crop yields, rural incomes and food inflation in Asia's third-largest economy.

Monsoon rainfall was 15% below the average as of September 21, Barclays said this week. Rainfall was 18% below normal so far in September and 16% below normal in August.

Farmers Tighten Spending

"When rainfall certainty is very low, farmers tend to save on resources," Rallis India Managing Director and CEO Gyanendra Shukla said in an interview. "Farmers are not very certain of full harvest as they would have anticipated."

Indian farmers typically sow summer crops such as rice, soybeans and cotton from June as monsoon rains arrive. Winter crops including wheat and rapeseed are planted from October after the monsoon retreats.

"There will be compression in the overall use of crop protection products, fertilisers and seed," Shukla said. He highlighted that demand for herbicides and fungicides had been particularly weak.

Peers Also Report Weak Demand

Rallis peers Dhanuka Agritech and Godrej Agrovet have also blamed erratic monsoon rains for weak demand for crop-protection products.

Consolidation Ahead

India's farm input sector is ripe for consolidation, Shukla said. He argued that low barriers to entry had led to an overcrowded market.

"There are too many players," he said. He added that companies focused on innovation and strong partnerships would be best positioned to withstand intensifying competition.
