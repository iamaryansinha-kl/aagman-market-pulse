India to Finalise Critical Minerals Stockpiling Policy in One Month

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Mines Secretary Keshav Chandra said the critical minerals stockpiling policy will be announced in about a month
  • 02/Four dedicated critical minerals processing parks are planned across Gujarat, Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh and Odisha
  • 03/Over 30 critical minerals have been identified covering demand from defence, energy, pharma and telecom

India will announce its critical minerals stockpiling policy in about a month. Mines Secretary Keshav Chandra confirmed the timeline on Friday.

"We are on the verge of finalising the policy on stockpiling of critical minerals which will be announced in about a month's time" - Keshav Chandra, Secretary, Ministry of Mines.

He spoke on the sidelines of the India Mines and Minerals Conclave 2026. The event was organised by industry body Assocham in New Delhi.

The aim is to maintain a strategic stock of critical minerals. The goal is to spur growth in sectors such as defence, advanced electronics and aerospace.

The urgency is not abstract. Global supply chains for these minerals have faced repeated shocks in recent years, including export curbs imposed by China on key materials.

Stockpiling gives India a buffer against such disruptions. It ensures strategic sectors keep running even if imports are cut off or global prices spike.

The logic mirrors strategic petroleum reserves. Just as oil stockpiles guard against supply shocks, mineral stockpiles would guard the industries that depend on them.

The government has already released a list of over 30 critical minerals. The list accounts for requirements of defence, agriculture, energy, pharmaceutical and telecom sectors.

The list itself was built bottom-up from sector demand rather than geological availability. That makes the stockpile a demand-security tool, not just a mining policy.

Copper, lithium, nickel, cobalt and rare earth elements feature among the essentials. These are raw materials for clean energy technologies including wind turbines, electricity networks, electric vehicles and battery manufacturing.

That list maps directly onto India's energy transition. Electric vehicles, grid storage and renewable power all depend on minerals that India largely imports today.

Stockpiling alone, however, does not build self-reliance. It secures supply but does nothing to capture the value of processing and refining.

That is where the four processing parks come in. The government is working on dedicated critical minerals processing parks in Gujarat, Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh and Odisha.

The parks are designed to fast-track value addition for key battery materials. Processing at home reduces dependence on imported refined minerals and keeps more of the value chain onshore.

The four coastal and industrial states offer ports, power and existing industrial ecosystems. That lowers the cost of setting up refining and processing capacity.

Together, the two moves form a single playbook. Stockpiling secures supply against shocks, while the processing parks move India up the battery-materials value chain.

India's EV and battery makers currently rely on imported processed minerals. Domestic processing parks would shorten that supply chain and cut exposure to overseas refining bottlenecks.

The policy will also test coordination between the centre and states. Land, power and clearances for the four parks will need to move in step with the stockpiling framework.

A complete strategy combines three layers: stockpiling for security, processing for value addition, and domestic exploration for supply. Friday's announcement covers the first two.

The policy announcement, expected by early November 2026, will spell out the structure of the stockpile. Details on the minerals covered, stockpile size and procurement mechanisms will be closely watched by industry.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHEN WILL INDIA ANNOUNCE ITS CRITICAL MINERALS STOCKPILING POLICY?
MINES SECRETARY KESHAV CHANDRA SAID ON FRIDAY THAT THE POLICY ON STOCKPILING OF CRITICAL MINERALS WILL BE ANNOUNCED IN ABOUT A MONTH'S TIME, PUTTING THE EXPECTED TIMELINE AT EARLY NOVEMBER 2026.
WHY DOES INDIA WANT TO STOCKPILE CRITICAL MINERALS?
THE AIM IS TO MAINTAIN A STRATEGIC STOCK OF CRITICAL MINERALS TO SPUR THE GROWTH OF SECTORS SUCH AS DEFENCE, ADVANCED ELECTRONICS AND AEROSPACE, AND TO BUFFER AGAINST GLOBAL SUPPLY DISRUPTIONS AND EXPORT CURBS.
WHICH FOUR STATES WILL HOST THE CRITICAL MINERALS PROCESSING PARKS?
THE GOVERNMENT IS WORKING ON FOUR DEDICATED CRITICAL MINERALS PROCESSING PARKS ACROSS GUJARAT, MAHARASHTRA, ANDHRA PRADESH AND ODISHA TO FAST-TRACK VALUE ADDITION FOR KEY BATTERY MATERIALS.
HOW MANY CRITICAL MINERALS ARE ON INDIA'S LIST AND WHY DO THEY MATTER?
THE GOVERNMENT HAS RELEASED A LIST OF OVER 30 CRITICAL MINERALS COVERING REQUIREMENTS OF DEFENCE, AGRICULTURE, ENERGY, PHARMACEUTICALS AND TELECOM. COPPER, LITHIUM, NICKEL, COBALT AND RARE EARTH ELEMENTS AMONG THEM ARE ESSENTIAL FOR CLEAN ENERGY TECHNOLOGIES.
WHICH CRITICAL MINERALS ARE ESSENTIAL FOR CLEAN ENERGY AND EVS?
COPPER, LITHIUM, NICKEL, COBALT AND RARE EARTH ELEMENTS ARE THE ESSENTIAL RAW MATERIALS REQUIRED FOR CLEAN ENERGY TECHNOLOGIES, INCLUDING WIND TURBINES, ELECTRICITY NETWORKS, ELECTRIC VEHICLES AND BATTERY MANUFACTURING. GLOSSARY: Rare earth elements: A group of 17 metallic elements used in high-tech applications — motors, defence systems, electronics. Processing them is complex and currently dominated by China. Stockpiling: Government-maintained strategic reserves of essential raw materials, meant to cushion supply shocks and price spikes — the same playbook used for crude oil and foodgrains. Value addition: Processing raw minerals into refined or semi-finished forms (such as battery-grade materials) inside the country, rather than exporting ore and importing the finished product.
