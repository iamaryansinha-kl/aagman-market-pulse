Top-10 Firms Gain Rs 3.65 Lakh Crore in Market Value; TCS Shines

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Nine of the top-10 most valued firms gained a combined Rs 3.65 lakh crore last week.
  • •- TCS added Rs 1.09 lakh crore, the most among the top 10, to Rs 8.91 lakh crore.
  • •- Bharti Airtel rose Rs 61,407 crore; Infosys Rs 55,893 crore; Reliance Rs 53,048 crore.
  • •- ICICI Bank crossed Rs 10 lakh crore in market value, joining the top tier.
  • •- Larsen & Toubro was the only laggard, down Rs 9,232 crore.
  • •- The Sensex rose 2,256.94 points (3.11%) and the Nifty 625.7 points (2.77%).

Last week's rally left a receipt. The combined market valuation of nine of India's ten most valuable companies surged by Rs 3.65 lakh crore in the five sessions ended October 16, as the Sensex climbed 2,256.94 points or 3.11 per cent and the Nifty advanced 625.7 points or 2.77 per cent — the strongest weekly showing for the benchmarks in roughly six months. The ninth name on the list did not participate at all: Larsen & Toubro, the sole laggard among the top tier, shed Rs 9,232.26 crore of value.

The leaderboard tells the week's story in order. Tata Consultancy Services added the most, Rs 1,08,542.63 crore, taking its valuation to Rs 8,91,134.96 crore — a single-week gain larger than most companies' entire market capitalisation, earned on the strength of September-quarter results that re-ignited the AI-demand trade. Bharti Airtel followed with a Rs 61,406.53 crore addition to Rs 11,88,815.45 crore, and Infosys jumped Rs 55,893.05 crore to Rs 4,71,409.41 crore. The technology complex's two heaviest weights thus contributed nearly Rs 1.64 lakh crore of the week's total between them, the arithmetic behind the Nifty IT index's 11.7 per cent surge.

Reliance Industries, the country's most valuable firm at Rs 16,37,437.19 crore after adding Rs 53,047.56 crore, enters this week with more than a rally behind it — the Rs 30,213 crore Jio Platforms public issue opens Wednesday, and the parent stock's weekly strength is partly the market pre-positioning around the anchor book that opens Monday. HDFC Bank gained Rs 32,379.05 crore to Rs 11,22,628.07 crore, and ICICI Bank soared Rs 30,769.95 crore to Rs 10,02,871.44 crore — a quietly historic close, the first time ICICI has finished a week above the Rs 10 lakh crore mark, exactly the kind of milestone its 20 per cent profit-growth quarter was built to deliver.

The rest of the top ten filled out modestly. Bajaj Finance edged up Rs 14,786.65 crore to Rs 6,11,326.72 crore, LIC added Rs 4,933.5 crore to Rs 5,02,204.82 crore, and State Bank of India gained Rs 3,323.07 crore to Rs 8,87,722.84 crore. Only L&T declined, to Rs 4,99,725.06 crore — a slip that, against the week's tape, reads less as weakness than as rotation, with the capital-goods weight giving up ground to the technology and financial names the rally rewarded.

The ordering beneath the numbers is worth a moment. Reliance leads, followed by Bharti Airtel, HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, TCS, SBI, Bajaj Finance, LIC, L&T and Infosys — a top ten in which four of the names bank or finance the economy, two run its digital plumbing, and one insures its households. That concentration is the index's strength and its vulnerability in a single frame: when the technology and financial trades are working, as they were last week, the headline index compounds; when both reverse together, the same concentration amplifies the fall. The Rs 3.65 lakh crore weekly gain is therefore best read not as wealth created but as risk repriced — the market paying up for the sectors it now believes carry the earnings.

Whether that belief survives the fortnight is the live question. The Jio subscription window will test the retail bid from Wednesday, the October 28 Fed decision will settle the rate question one way or the other, and the Q2 earnings that powered TCS and Infosys now face the harder comparator of expectations raised. Last week's Rs 3.65 lakh crore was the market's vote that Indian corporate earnings can carry the index even while foreign investors withdraw — a vote that, unlike most market votes, comes with a date by which it must be cast again.

The weekly gain also re-orders the market's own internal map. With ICICI Bank across Rs 10 lakh crore and TCS within striking distance of its own previous peaks, the top of the index now prices in a world where private-bank profitability and IT's AI narrative are both simultaneously true — the two assumptions that have rarely held together for long stretches and that, last week, did. L&T's lone decline punctures the consensus usefully: capital goods, the trade built on the government-spending cycle, is the one corner of the top ten where the earnings calendar offers less immediate proof. If next week's results season confirms that divergence — banks and IT delivering, industrials lagging — the Rs 3.65 lakh crore will harden into a rotation with a destination, not just a rally with a number. The market has already voted its direction. The earnings season now decides whether the ballot counts.

Against all of this sits the arithmetic that makes the week remarkable in the first place: an index that gained more in five sessions than most quarters deliver, with nine of its ten largest constituents participating, in the same week that foreign portfolio investors sold more than they had in any month this year. Two markets occupied the same trading floor — one global, leaving; one domestic, arriving — and for five days at least, the domestic one was bigger. That is the single most important fact about Indian equities in 2026, and last week it acquired a number: Rs 3.65 lakh crore, printed while the sellers were still selling.
