RBI Tightens NDF Rules: Hedging Only, 20% Cash Reserve Widened

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/RBI restricted rupee non-deliverable derivative contracts at GIFT City to hedging purposes only, ending any-purpose access for overseas clients.
  • 02/A 20% cash reserve requirement now extends to banks' own rupee currency hedges, deposited daily with the central bank.
  • 03/The rupee jumped 1% on Monday after falling over 6% this year on oil costs and capital outflows.

The Reserve Bank of India on Monday, October 12, 2026, tightened rules on rupee non-deliverable derivative contracts, limiting overseas clients to using them only for hedging purposes. The central bank also widened a 20% cash reserve requirement to cover banks' own rupee currency hedges for the first time.

The move is the latest in a series of measures announced by the RBI since Saturday, all aimed at supporting the rupee. The currency has been under sustained pressure from steep oil prices and steady capital outflows, and policymakers have run out of patience with one-way bets.

Banks operating units in India's International Financial Services Centre at GIFT City, along with certain primary dealers, previously enjoyed wide latitude under the offshore framework. They could offer non-deliverable rupee derivatives to non-resident users for any purpose whatsoever, including pure speculation on the currency's direction.

That freedom is now gone. Under the revised framework, these entities can offer non-deliverable rupee derivatives only for hedging, stripping away a channel that speculators had used to bet against the currency without ever touching the onshore market.

The second prong of Monday's action targets authorised dealers directly. They will now be required to maintain a foreign exchange risk reserve for rupee derivative contracts used to hedge currency risk on their own assets and liabilities, a category previously untouched by the cash deposit rule.

The reserve applies to all rupee currency contracts in which the authorised dealer buys foreign currency against the rupee. It equals 20% of the rupee equivalent of the notional amount of each such contract, a significant capital lock-up given the size of bank derivative books.

Crucially, the reserve must be deposited in cash with the RBI on a daily basis. It must be maintained until the underlying contract expires, locking up bank funds and raising the cost of running large derivative books during volatile markets.

Non-deliverable derivative contracts are settled purely in cash, usually in dollars, with no rupees changing hands. Because settlement happens offshore, beyond the reach of domestic regulations, the NDF market had become a favoured venue for expressing bearish views on the Indian currency.

Reuters reported earlier on Monday that banks had asked the RBI to scrap the 20% cash deposit entirely for FX derivatives hedging current-account exposures. Instead, the industry proposed charging a flat fee, arguing the reserve ties up costly balance-sheet capacity that lenders need elsewhere.

The central bank clearly declined that request. Rather than removing the reserve for current-account hedges, it extended the 20% requirement to an entirely new category: banks' own hedges on their asset-liability positions, deepening the collateral burden industry had hoped to escape.

The measures landed on a market already rattled by a dramatic weekend package of regulatory changes. The RBI raised the minimum daily maintenance of the cash reserve ratio to 99% from 90%, effective for the fortnight beginning October 16, squeezing liquidity far harder than the earlier norm allowed.

It also barred the rebooking of cancelled INR-linked forex derivatives contracts, closing a loophole used to rebuild positions quietly. At the same time, it slashed the threshold for forex derivatives without underlying exposure from $100 million down to just $5 million, bringing far more trades under regulatory scrutiny.

A special dollar window for three public-sector oil companies opened on October 12 as part of the package. The facility lets refiners source dollars directly from the central bank at a stable rate, easing a key source of demand in the spot market and damping panic buying.

Brent crude has been trading above $100 per barrel amid the ongoing West Asia conflict. India's oil import bill has surged as a result, draining dollars from the economy and pressuring the rupee from the trade side even as exporters' dollar inflows fail to keep pace.

The RBI has been selling dollars from its foreign exchange reserves to stem rupee depreciation. Those reserves remain among the largest in the world, giving the central bank firepower, though intervention drains liquidity and complicates its own inflation and growth calculus.

The scale of the defence is visible in bond markets. The 10-year government bond yield touched a three-year high of 7.37% on Monday, as the liquidity squeeze from the CRR change and reserve requirements fed through to funding conditions across the banking system.

For traders, the message from the central bank is unmistakable. Every avenue for speculating against the rupee, whether onshore or offshore, is being closed, taxed, or collateralised, and the currency responded immediately to the cumulative weight of the announcements.

The rupee, which had fallen more than 6% this year, jumped 1% on Monday following the weekend announcements. Whether the rebound holds will depend on oil prices, capital flows, and how long banks can absorb the new reserve burden without passing on higher hedging costs to corporate clients in the months ahead.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT DID THE RBI CHANGE ABOUT RUPEE NDF CONTRACTS ON OCTOBER 12, 2026?
BANKS WITH UNITS AT GIFT CITY IFSC AND SOME PRIMARY DEALERS CAN NOW OFFER NON-DELIVERABLE RUPEE DERIVATIVES TO NON-RESIDENT USERS ONLY FOR HEDGING, ENDING THE EARLIER ANY-PURPOSE PERMISSION.
WHAT IS THE NEW 20% FOREIGN EXCHANGE RISK RESERVE REQUIREMENT?
AUTHORISED DEALERS MUST DEPOSIT CASH EQUAL TO 20% OF THE RUPEE EQUIVALENT OF THE NOTIONAL AMOUNT FOR RUPEE CONTRACTS WHERE THEY BUY FOREIGN CURRENCY AGAINST THE RUPEE TO HEDGE THEIR OWN ASSETS AND LIABILITIES, PAID DAILY TO THE RBI UNTIL THE CONTRACT ENDS.
HOW DID THE RUPEE REACT TO THE RBI'S WEEKEND AND MONDAY MEASURES?
THE RUPEE JUMPED 1% ON MONDAY, RECOVERING PART OF A DECLINE THAT HAD EXCEEDED 6% SINCE THE START OF THE YEAR.
WHAT OTHER MEASURES DID THE RBI ANNOUNCE OVER THE WEEKEND?
THE CRR MINIMUM DAILY MAINTENANCE WAS RAISED TO 99% FROM 90% FROM OCTOBER 16, REBOOKING OF CANCELLED INR-LINKED DERIVATIVES WAS BARRED, THE NO-EXPOSURE DERIVATIVES THRESHOLD WAS CUT FROM $100 MILLION TO $5 MILLION, AND A SPECIAL DOLLAR WINDOW OPENED FOR THREE PSU OIL COMPANIES.
WHAT HAPPENED TO THE 10-YEAR GOVERNMENT BOND YIELD ON MONDAY?
THE 10-YEAR YIELD HIT A THREE-YEAR HIGH OF 7.37% ON MONDAY AS THE RBI'S LIQUIDITY-TIGHTENING MEASURES PUSHED UP FUNDING COSTS. GLOSSARY: NON-DELIVERABLE FORWARD (NDF): A CASH-SETLED FORWARD CONTRACT ON A CURRENCY, TYPICALLY SETTLED IN US DOLLARS OFFSHORE, WITH NO ACTUAL EXCHANGE OF THE UNDERLYING CURRENCY. CASH RESERVE REQUIREMENT: THE PORTION OF A BANK'S DEPOSITS THAT MUST BE HELD AS CASH WITH THE CENTRAL BANK, HERE EXTENDED AS A 20% RISK RESERVE ON CERTAIN RUPEE DERIVATIVE POSITIONS. AUTHORISED DEALER: A BANK OR FINANCIAL INSTITUTION LICENSED BY THE RBI TO DEAL IN FOREIGN EXCHANGE AND EXECUTE CURRENCY TRANSACTIONS ON BEHALF OF CLIENTS. IFSC/GIFT CITY: INDIA'S INTERNATIONAL FINANCIAL SERVICES CENTRE IN GUJARAT, AN OFFSHORE-STYLE JURISDICTION WHERE BANKS CAN OFFER DERIVATIVES TO NON-RESIDENT CLIENTS UNDER RELAXED RULES. NOTIONAL AMOUNT: THE FACE VALUE OF A DERIVATIVE CONTRACT USED TO CALCULATE PAYMENTS AND, IN THIS CASE, THE BASE FOR THE 20% RUPEE-EQUIVALENT CASH RESERVE. HEDGING: TAKING A FINANCIAL POSITION TO OFFSET AN EXISTING CURRENCY OR MARKET RISK, AS OPPOSED TO BETTING ON PRICE MOVEMENTS FOR PROFIT.
