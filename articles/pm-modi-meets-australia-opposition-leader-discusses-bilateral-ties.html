PM Modi Meets Australia's Opposition Leader, Discusses Bilateral Ties

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

PM Modi is meeting both sides of Australia's political divide.

During his visit to Melbourne, Prime Minister Narendra Modi met with Australia's Opposition Leader to discuss bilateral ties, trade cooperation, and regional security issues. The meeting follows Modi's earlier engagement with Prime Minister Anthony Albanese and reflects India's strategy of building relationships across the political spectrum.

Australia's political system means the Opposition could form government in the future. By meeting with the Opposition Leader, Modi ensures that India's bilateral relationship has continuity regardless of which party is in power. This is standard diplomatic practice but carries extra weight given the strategic importance of the India-Australia partnership.

The discussion covered trade and economic cooperation. Both sides reviewed progress on the India-Australia Economic Cooperation and Trade Agreement and discussed next steps toward a comprehensive free trade agreement. Australia is keen to expand exports of critical minerals, uranium, and education services to India.

Regional security was also on the agenda. Both India and Australia are concerned about China's growing influence in the Indo-Pacific. The Quad partnership — which includes India, Australia, the United States, and Japan — was discussed as a framework for regional stability.

The meeting comes amid Modi's broader tour of Australia. The Prime Minister has already held formal talks with Albanese, addressed business forums, and met with the Indian diaspora. The Opposition Leader meeting rounds out the diplomatic engagement.

India's bipartisan approach is paying dividends. The India-Australia relationship has grown significantly under both Labor and Coalition governments, with consistent support for deeper trade, defence, and people-to-people links.
