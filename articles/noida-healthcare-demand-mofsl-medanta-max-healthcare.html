Noida Healthcare Boom: MOFSL Picks Medanta, Max Healthcare, Sees 28% CAGR

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Noida-Greater Noida has only 4,500 super-specialty beds for a 1.3 million population, signaling a supply shortage
  • 02/MOFSL recommends Buy on Medanta with target Rs 1,490 and Max Healthcare with target Rs 1,260
  • 03/Medanta's Noida facility is expected to achieve EBITDA breakeven in the second half of FY2027

Noida-Greater Noida has only around 4,500 super-specialty beds serving a population of 1.3 million. The region's population has grown at a 5.4% compound annual rate over the past five years.

Demand is being driven by IT services, manufacturing, electronics, and financial services sectors. Affordable housing and improved connectivity have transformed Noida from a satellite city into a self-sustaining urban center.

Hospital operators are reporting rising occupancy levels and robust revenue growth. Mature assets are operating at high utilization rates while newly commissioned facilities are scaling up faster than expected.

Patient demand is expanding beyond Noida into East Delhi, Ghaziabad, Bulandshahr, and Aligarh. The region remains significantly underpenetrated in advanced healthcare services like organ transplants and robotic-assisted procedures.

Siddhartha Khemka, Head of Research at Motilal Oswal Financial Services, issued Buy recommendations on two key players. He sees strong structural tailwinds supporting the sector over the medium term.

MOFSL recommends Buy on Global Health (NSE: MEDANTA), operating the Medanta brand, with a target price of Rs 1,490. Medanta is projected to deliver a 28% earnings CAGR over FY26-28.

The brokerage also recommends Buy on Max Healthcare Institute (NSE: MAXHEALTH) with a target of Rs 1,260. Max is expected to post revenue, EBITDA, and PAT CAGRs of 14%, 15%, and 20% respectively over FY26-28.

However, competition for specialist doctors has intensified as multiple operators expand simultaneously. This pressure on medical talent costs remains a key risk to profitability in the region.
