India IPOs Raise Record Rs 94,205 Crore in H1 FY27

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Indian mainboard IPOs raised a record ₹94,205 crore in H1 FY27 (April-September), 35% above the previous first-half record of ₹69,533 crore.
  • 02/The average listing gain for H1 FY27 IPOs doubled to 19% from 7% a year earlier; 46 of 64 listed IPOs were trading above issue price as of September 29.
  • 03/The H2 pipeline is heavy: 145 companies with Sebi approval aim to raise ₹2.78 lakh crore, while 102 more companies await approval for another ₹1.87 lakh crore.

Indian companies raised a record ₹94,205 crore through mainboard IPOs in the first half of 2026-27. The figure covers April to September and comes from PRIME Database.

A total of 78 companies hit the market during the period. That is 35% higher than the previous first-half record of ₹69,533 crore, raised by 65 IPOs in the same period last year.

The record was not obvious at the start of the year. Fundraising in the first three months, April to June, stood at just ₹3,794 crore. Secondary markets remained volatile through that phase.

Activity picked up sharply from August. IPO fundraising crossed ₹20,000 crore in that month. It then accelerated further in September, when mobilisation topped ₹40,000 crore.

The IPO surge fed into a broader boom in public equity fundraising. Overall mobilisation hit a record ₹2.43 lakh crore in H1 FY27. That is up 75% from ₹1.39 lakh crore a year earlier, with IPOs, offers for sale and QIPs all contributing.

The biggest issue of the period set the tone. The National Stock Exchange raised ₹22,563 crore, the largest IPO of the half. SBI Funds Management followed with ₹9,795 crore, and Manipal Health Enterprises raised ₹9,275 crore.

Deal size also trended upward. The average mainboard IPO size rose to ₹1,208 crore, compared with ₹1,070 crore in the same period last year.

Demand for new issues was intense through the half. Of the 64 IPOs for which subscription data was available, 42 were subscribed more than 10 times. Within that group, 25 received bids of more than 50 times.

The remaining issues also found buyers. Nine IPOs were subscribed more than 3 times, while 13 were subscribed between 1 and 3 times.

Retail investors came out in much larger numbers than before. The average number of retail applications per IPO rose to 17.71 lakh in H1 FY27, from 12.69 lakh a year earlier.

Retail money followed the footfall. Retail investors applied for shares worth ₹2.47 lakh crore across these issues. The final allocation to them stood at ₹25,944 crore.

Listing-day performance kept the cycle going. The average listing gain for the 64 IPOs that had listed stood at 19%. That compares with just 7% in the first half of last year.

The gains have held up despite a shaky broader market. As of September 29, 46 of the 64 IPOs were trading above their issue price. PRIME Database said the average return for these IPOs was 32%, even as geopolitical concerns triggered a broader market correction.

Foreign investors made a clear distinction between primary and secondary markets. Foreign portfolio investors put ₹44,960 crore into the primary market in H1 FY27. Over the same period, they sold ₹1.64 lakh crore in the secondary market.

A little over two-fifths of the IPO money was fresh capital. Fresh issues accounted for ₹38,510 crore, or 41% of the mainboard total. Of that fresh-issue money, 42% was aimed at repayment of debt.

Existing shareholders used the window too. Offers for sale by private promoters stood at ₹26,048 crore. Private equity and venture capital investors sold shares worth ₹9,313 crore through IPOs.

Other public-market routes also saw heavy traffic. Offers for sale through stock exchanges rose more than five times to ₹55,337 crore, mainly driven by government divestment. Separately, 34 companies raised ₹61,553 crore through qualified institutional placements, up 36% from last year.

The pipeline for the second half is already loaded. Around 145 companies planning to raise roughly ₹2.78 lakh crore have Sebi approval and are waiting to launch. Another 102 companies looking to raise around ₹1.87 lakh crore are awaiting approval.
