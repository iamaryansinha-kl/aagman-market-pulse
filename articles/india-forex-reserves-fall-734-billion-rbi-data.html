India Forex Reserves Fall $12.95 Billion to $734.60 Billion: RBI Data

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/India's foreign exchange reserves fell $12.95 billion to $734.60 billion in the week ended October 2, RBI data released on Friday showed — the second straight weekly decline
  • 02/The drop was driven by foreign currency assets, down $10.66 billion, and gold, down $2.28 billion; the two-week cumulative fall stands at about $31.3 billion
  • 03/Reserves peaked at a record $785.70 billion on September 4 on swap-driven flows; watch the pace of depletion and further repo rate action in next week's data

India's foreign exchange reserves fell $12.95 billion to $734.60 billion in the week ended October 2, the Reserve Bank of India said in its weekly data released on Friday. It was the second straight weekly decline.

Reserves had fallen $18.34 billion to $747.56 billion in the week ended September 25. Taken together, the two-week cumulative drawdown now stands at roughly $31.3 billion — a sharp reversal from the record levels seen just weeks earlier.

The slide follows a record. Reserves touched an all-time high of $785.70 billion in the week ended September 4, a peak inflated by exceptional flows rather than organic balance-of-payments strength, and therefore prone to give back gains.

The September surge was powered by a special concessional swap window for diaspora deposits. Through that facility, banks raised a record sum of nearly $133 billion, and those one-off flows swelled the headline reserve figure well above trend.

That swap-driven peak is now unwinding fast. The pattern of the past two weeks points to the RBI likely selling dollars to defend the rupee through the oil shock. The drawdown comes against a backdrop of repo rate hikes.

Foreign currency assets bore the brunt of the decline, falling $10.66 billion to $604.74 billion in the October 2 week. FCA in dollars captures appreciation or depreciation of non-US units such as the euro, pound and yen.

Gold reserves fell $2.28 billion to $106.413 billion. Special Drawing Rights rose $15 million to $18.65 billion, while India's reserve position with the IMF slipped $15 million to $4.78 billion, leaving those line items effectively flat.

Even after the drawdown, reserves remain substantial by historical standards. The direction is clear. Watch next week's RBI data for the pace of depletion, and any further repo rate action.

THE DECLINE WAS DRIVEN BY A FALL IN FOREIGN CURRENCY ASSETS, WHICH DROPPED $10.66 BILLION TO $604.74 BILLION, AND GOLD RESERVES, WHICH FELL $2.28 BILLION TO $106.413 BILLION. RESERVES HAVE FALLEN ABOUT $31.3 BILLION OVER TWO WEEKS. THEY DROPPED $18.34 BILLION TO $747.56 BILLION IN THE WEEK ENDED SEPTEMBER 25, AND A FURTHER $12.95 BILLION TO $734.60 BILLION IN THE WEEK ENDED OCTOBER 2, AS THE SWAP-INFLATED PEAK FROM EARLY SEPTEMBER UNWINDS.
WHY DID INDIA'S FOREX RESERVES HIT A RECORD HIGH IN SEPTEMBER?
RESERVES TOUCHED AN ALL-TIME HIGH OF $785.70 BILLION IN THE WEEK ENDED SEPTEMBER 4. THAT PEAK WAS BOOSTED BY HEAVY FLOWS FROM A SPECIAL CONCESSIONAL SWAP FOR DIASPORA DEPOSITS, THROUGH WHICH BANKS RAISED A RECORD SUM OF NEARLY $133 BILLION. WITH THOSE FLOWS NOW ROLLING OFF, THE HEADLINE NUMBER IS NORMALISING.

INVESTORS SHOULD TRACK NEXT WEEK'S RBI RESERVES DATA FOR THE PACE OF DEPLETION. THE PATTERN OF THE PAST TWO WEEKS POINTS TO THE RBI SELLING DOLLARS TO DEFEND THE RUPEE THROUGH THE OIL SHOCK, AGAINST A BACKDROP OF REPO RATE HIKES. ANY FURTHER RATE ACTION WILL SIGNAL HOW THE CENTRAL BANK IS BALANCING CURRENCY DEFENSE AGAINST INFLATION PRESSURES. AN ACCELERATION IN THE DRAWDOWN WOULD POINT TO DEEPER INTERVENTION, WHILE A SLOWER PACE WOULD SIGNAL THE RBI IS COMFORTABLE WITH THE RUPEE'S CURRENT LEVEL.
