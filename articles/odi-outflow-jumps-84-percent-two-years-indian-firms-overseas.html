Actual ODI Outflow Jumps 84% In Two Years As Indian Firms Expand Overseas

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Actual ODI outflow from India has surged 84% over the past two years, reflecting aggressive overseas expansion by Indian companies
  • 02/Indian firms are investing in manufacturing, technology, and resource assets across multiple geographies
  • 03/The trend signals deepening globalisation of Indian corporate sector and diversification beyond domestic markets

Overseas direct investment from India has jumped sharply. Actual ODI outflow rose 84% over the past two years.

Indian companies are aggressively expanding abroad. They are investing in manufacturing facilities, technology assets, and natural resources across multiple continents.

The surge reflects a maturing corporate sector seeking growth beyond domestic boundaries. Companies are chasing new markets, supply chain resilience, and strategic resources.

Manufacturing investments feature prominently in the ODI surge. Indian firms are setting up production facilities in Southeast Asia, Africa, and Latin America to serve local and export markets.

Technology and digital services investments have also accelerated. Indian IT and SaaS companies are acquiring firms and building development centres in North America and Europe.

Resource-seeking investments in mining and energy assets continue to grow. Companies are securing raw material supplies critical to India's industrial expansion.

The 84% jump marks a significant acceleration from historical trends. ODI outflows had remained relatively modest for years before this recent surge.

Policy liberalisation has supported the outflow growth. The government has eased overseas investment norms for Indian companies in recent years.

The trend aligns with India's broader economic ambitions. As the domestic market matures, companies naturally seek growth opportunities in faster-growing regions.

Geographic diversification is a key theme. Indian firms are spreading investments across ASEAN, Africa, the Middle East, and developed markets rather than concentrating in one region.

The ODI surge also reflects rupee depreciation. A weaker rupee makes foreign assets relatively cheaper for Indian acquirers.

Analysts expect the trend to continue. India's corporate sector is building global footprints that will shape the next decade of growth.
