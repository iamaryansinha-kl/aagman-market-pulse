Dallas Fed, IEA, Baker Hughes: Strait of Hormuz Traffic to Recover in H2 2026

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Dallas Fed Q1 2026 Energy Survey: 39% of 120 oil firms expect normal Hormuz traffic by August, 26% by November, 14% even later
  • 02/Global oil supply crashed 10.1 mb/d to 97 mb/d in March; Strait carried 20% of world oil flows before Feb 28 shutdown
  • 03/IEA base case sees shipments resume from May; Baker Hughes assumes Strait fully operational in H2 2026 if conflict ends in Q2

Baker Hughes and the Federal Reserve Bank of Dallas expect traffic in the Strait of Hormuz to normalise by the second half of 2026. The 34 km-long chokepoint has been virtually shut since February 28, 2026.

The escalation between the US-Israel coalition and Iran on that date triggered the biggest disruption in the history of global oil and gas markets. Roughly 20% of global oil supply flowed through the Strait before the war.

In the Dallas Fed's Q1 2026 Energy Survey, 39 per cent of participating oil and gas companies said they expect normal traffic by August 2026. Another 26 per cent expect normalisation by November 2026.

The survey collected responses between April 15 and 20 from 120 firms. Of these, 78 were exploration and production companies and 42 were oilfield services firms.

Baker Hughes, in its Q2 2026 and FY2026 guidance, said it assumes "Middle East disruptions continue through the end of June, without further escalation. Conflict resolved at the end of Q2 (2026), with Strait of Hormuz fully operational during all H2 2026."

The International Energy Agency's base case assumes oil shipments gradually resume from May 2026. IEA balances show oil market deficits returning to a surplus averaging 2.5 million barrels per day in H2 2026.

The cumulative supply deficit peaks in June before correcting almost linearly by year end. This assumes recovery in oil production and refinery activity through Q3 2026.

In the IEA's "protracted case", disruptions to Middle East energy production remain high. Energy flows to international markets stay largely restricted, causing deficits to persist.

This scenario would push oil demand into a large year-on-year contraction. Demand would fall by 5 million barrels per day on average from Q2 through Q4 2026.

The remaining shortfall in supply lifts the global call on stocks to an untenable 6 million barrels per day. Aggregate losses would reach almost 2 billion barrels by year end.

The IEA's April oil market report showed global oil supply plummeted 10.1 million barrels per day to 97 million barrels per day in March. Continued attacks on Middle East energy infrastructure drove the collapse.

Baker Hughes CEO Lorenzo Simonelli said the shutdown removed about 10% of global oil supply and disrupted roughly 20% of global LNG output. He called it the biggest oil supply disruption ever recorded.

The Dallas Fed survey also captured rising concern about future risk. 48 per cent of respondents said it is "very likely" that geopolitical events will disrupt traffic again within the next five years.

Another 38 per cent view repeat disruption as "somewhat likely". 79 per cent of executives expect higher insurance, freight costs and tolls to add at least $2 per barrel to shipping costs.
