El Niño 2026 Could Rival 2015 Event; IMD Sees 92% Monsoon Deficit

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Sub-surface Pacific heat is building comparable to the formidable 2015 El Niño and could eclipse the 2023 event
  • 02/IMD forecasts 2026 monsoon at 92% of LPA (87cm), implying ~80cm rainfall with a ±5% model error margin
  • 03/El Niño likely to strengthen in Aug-Sep; 35% probability of deficient rains threatens Kharif crops and food inflation

Latest assessments indicate a powerful El Niño is taking shape beneath the equatorial eastern Pacific. Sub-surface heat accumulation rivals the formidable 2015 episode and could eclipse the 2023 event.

In 2023, India recorded a below-normal monsoon at 6% deficit. 2015 was the 10th driest year in a century with a 14% rainfall shortfall. However, 1997 saw a normal monsoon despite a strong El Niño.

"Evolving conditions have led sections of the media to invoke the spectre of a so-called 'Godzilla El Niño,'" says Swadhin Behera, Director at Japan Agency for Marine-Earth Science and Technology.

The India Meteorological Department issued its first long-range forecast on April 13, 2026. It predicts the southwest monsoon at 92% of the Long Period Average of 87 cm, implying around 80 cm of rainfall.

The forecast carries a model error of ±5%. There is a 35% probability of deficient rainfall, 31% chance of below-normal rains, and only 34% combined probability of normal to excess rainfall. "This is the lowest first long-range forecast in at least 26 years," says Aditi Nayar, Chief Economist at ICRA.

Weak La Niña-like conditions are transitioning to neutral. El Niño is expected to peak in August and September when standing Kharif crops need water most. About 60% of Indian farmers depend on monsoon rainfall, and central and western India face the highest deficit risk.

A mitigating factor is the possible development of a positive Indian Ocean Dipole toward the end of the season. IMD Director-General Mrutyunjay Mohapatra notes this could partially offset the El Niño impact.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT IS THE IMD'S 2026 MONSOON FORECAST?
THE INDIA METEOROLOGICAL DEPARTMENT FORECASTS THE 2026 SOUTHWEST MONSOON AT 92% OF THE LONG PERIOD AVERAGE (LPA) OF 87 CM, IMPLYING APPROXIMATELY 80 CM OF RAINFALL WITH A MODEL ERROR OF ±5%. THIS IS THE LOWEST FIRST LONG-RANGE FORECAST IN AT LEAST 26 YEARS.
HOW DOES THE EVOLVING EL NIÑO COMPARE TO PREVIOUS EVENTS?
SUB-SURFACE HEAT ACCUMULATION IN THE EQUATORIAL PACIFIC IS COMPARABLE TO THE 2015 EPISODE, WHICH CAUSED A 14% RAINFALL DEFICIT IN INDIA. THE 2023 EL NIÑO, WHICH CAUSED A 6% MONSOON DEFICIT, HAD NOTABLY LOWER SUB-SURFACE HEAT AT THIS STAGE.
WHAT IS THE PROBABILITY OF DEFICIENT OR BELOW-NORMAL RAINFALL?
THE IMD ASSIGNS A 35% PROBABILITY TO DEFICIENT RAINFALL (BELOW 90% OF LPA), A 31% CHANCE OF BELOW-NORMAL RAINFALL (90-95% OF LPA), AND ONLY A 34% COMBINED PROBABILITY OF NORMAL, ABOVE-NORMAL, OR EXCESS RAINFALL.
WHICH REGIONS AND CROPS ARE MOST AT RISK?
CENTRAL AND WESTERN INDIA FACE THE HIGHEST DEFICIT RISK, INCLUDING PUNJAB, HARYANA, AND RAJASTHAN. KHARIF CROPS SUCH AS RICE, PULSES, OILSEEDS, COTTON, MAIZE, AND SOYBEAN ARE MOST VULNERABLE, ESPECIALLY IN AUGUST AND SEPTEMBER WHEN WATER DEMAND PEAKS.
ARE THERE ANY MITIGATING FACTORS THAT COULD HELP THE MONSOON?
YES. A POSITIVE INDIAN OCEAN DIPOLE (IOD) IS EXPECTED TO DEVELOP TOWARD THE END OF THE MONSOON SEASON, WHICH HISTORICALLY ENHANCES RAINFALL OVER INDIA. ADDITIONALLY, BELOW-NORMAL NORTHERN HEMISPHERE SNOW COVER IN JANUARY-MARCH 2026 COULD PARTIALLY SUPPORT MONSOON RAINS.
