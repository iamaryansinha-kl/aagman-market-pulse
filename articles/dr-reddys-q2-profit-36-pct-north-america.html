Dr Reddy Profit Falls 36% as North America Slumps

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Net profit fell 36% year-on-year to Rs 913.4 crore, from Rs 1,426.8 crore a year earlier.
  • •- Revenue declined 1.5% to Rs 8,671 crore; EBITDA fell 24.2% to Rs 1,783 crore, with margin contracting to 20.6% from 26.7%.
  • •- North America revenue plunged 23% to Rs 2,489 crore — the single biggest drag on the quarter.
  • •- Emerging Markets rose 24% and India grew 17%, and a pipeline of biosimilars, vaccines and CDMO deals provides the counter-narrative.

For Indian pharma's largest exporters, the US market has been the gift that kept on giving for two decades — and the bill is now arriving in instalments. Dr Reddy's Laboratories on Friday reported a 36% year-on-year decline in net profit to Rs 913.4 crore for the September quarter, from Rs 1,426.8 crore a year earlier, on revenue that fell 1.5% to Rs 8,671 crore from Rs 8,805.1 crore. The damage was concentrated in one geography: North America, the company's largest export market, saw revenue plunge 23% to Rs 2,489 crore. When a fifth of your business shrinks by nearly a quarter, the rest of the P&L barely matters — and in this case, it compounded: EBITDA fell 24.2% to Rs 1,783 crore from Rs 2,351.1 crore, and the EBITDA margin contracted to 20.6%, from 26.7% a year earlier. That is a 620 basis-point margin give-up in twelve months, and it cannot be explained by volumes alone — it is the signature of a business mix shifting away from its most profitable products.

The segment detail reveals how uneven the global pharma map has become. Global Generics revenue declined 1% to Rs 7,792.7 crore, but within that, the contrasts were stark: North America -23%, Emerging Markets +24% to Rs 2,059.5 crore, India +17% to Rs 1,850.8 crore, and Europe roughly flat at Rs 1,393.5 crore. The pharmaceutical services and active ingredients (PSAI) business, which supplies drug ingredients, declined 12% to Rs 831 crore from Rs 945 crore. In effect, the company is being rebalanced in real time away from the US generics battlefield — where price erosion, competition and channel consolidation have squeezed Indian exporters for years — and toward domestic and emerging-market demand. That pivot is visible in the numbers, but not yet fast enough to offset the American hole, and the margin contraction shows the new mix is, for now, a lower-margin mix.

The half-year picture makes the trend line unmistakable. In the first half of FY27, total revenue declined 4% to Rs 16,741.3 crore; North America revenue fell 29% to Rs 4,693.8 crore, while Emerging Markets rose 27% to Rs 3,892.3 crore, India grew 17% to Rs 3,568.4 crore and Europe climbed 7%. A market that was once the anchor of Dr Reddy's growth story has, in eighteen months, become its largest headwind — a pattern visible across the Indian pharma complex as US generics pricing remains brutal and new competition absorbs every niche the incumbents once owned.

Management's counter-narrative rests on what it built while the US generics engine was stalling. During the quarter, Dr Reddy's partnered with Takeda on QDENGA, a dengue vaccine approved by India's drug regulator; expanded its collaboration with Gilead Sciences for lenacapavir, an investigational once-yearly HIV prevention drug, across 120 countries; and, with the GARDP Foundation, secured Thai regulatory approval for zoliflodacin, an antibiotic for drug-resistant gonorrhoea, within six months of US clearance — a sequence of wins in precisely the high-scrutiny, high-barrier categories where Indian pharma has historically lacked a seat at the table. It won USFDA approval for a rituximab biosimilar for the American market, launched a nivolumab biosimilar in India, and received India's first biosimilar approval for intravenous abatacept. Sugammadex launched in the US as a hospital drug; generic semaglutide was approved in ten countries across Latin America, Africa and the Middle East, plus Canada and India — a portfolio of moves that reads less like a quarter's work and more like a five-year rebuild of what the company sells and where it sells it.

There is also a quietly material second engine. The company's contract development and manufacturing arm, Aurigene Pharmaceutical Services (APSL), generated more than $100 million in revenue over the twelve months ended September 2026 and signed a strategic long-term manufacturing and supply agreement with a leading global pharmaceutical company covering the US, Europe, Canada and emerging markets. CDMO income is steadier, stickier and less price-erosion-prone than US generics — precisely the kind of revenue base the company needs while its legacy model resets.

"We continued to improve the overall health of our business, delivering double-digit base business growth and EBITDA margins of over 20% during the quarter," co-chairman and managing director G V Prasad said, framing the print as a transition quarter rather than a setback. The market was unmoved in either direction: the stock ended Friday at Rs 1,229.50, down 0.77%, in a session where the broader market was falling far harder. Sometimes a shrug is its own verdict — investors have seen enough of the US generics story to know the recovery, if it comes, will be measured in years, not quarters, and they are pricing the company on what comes after. The pipeline gives Dr Reddy's something most of its peers lack: options. Whether those options convert before the US drag compounds is the question the next four quarters must answer, and it is a genuinely open one.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHY DID DR REDDY'S PROFIT FALL 36%?
North America revenue dropped 23% to Rs 2,489 crore while EBITDA margin contracted to 20.6% from 26.7%, dragging net profit down to Rs 913.4 crore from Rs 1,426.8 crore a year earlier.
WHAT GREW IN THE QUARTER?
Emerging Markets revenue rose 24% to Rs 2,059.5 crore and India revenue grew 17% to Rs 1,850.8 crore, partially offsetting the US decline.
WHAT IS THE COMPANY'S NEW GROWTH STORY?
Biosimilars (rituximab US approval, nivolumab launch in India, first Indian abatacept biosimilar), vaccines (Takeda's QDENGA), global partnerships with Gilead and GARDP, and a CDMO arm crossing $100 million in annual revenue. GLOSSARY: GENERIC DRUG — A medicine sold without brand protection after the original patent expires; competition among generics producers drives prices down steeply, especially in the US. BIOSIMILAR — A near-copy of a biologic drug, made from living cells; harder to replicate than a generic and a higher-margin growth segment for Indian pharma. CDMO — Contract development and manufacturing organisation; a company paid to make drugs for other pharmaceutical firms, providing stable, contract-based revenue. PSAI — Pharmaceutical services and active ingredients; the business of supplying the chemical building blocks of drugs to other manufacturers.
