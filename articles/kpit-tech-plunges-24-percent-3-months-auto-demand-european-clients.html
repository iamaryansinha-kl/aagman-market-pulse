KPIT Tech Plunges 24% in 3 Months as BMW, Volkswagen Cut Spending

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/KPIT Technologies shares have fallen about 24-25% in three months, sliding from Rs 730-740 in early July to Rs 555-560 by late September 2026
  • 02/The rout began on July 1 with a 17% single-day crash after the company warned of a sudden slowdown in revenue from European auto clients, including BMW and Volkswagen
  • 03/Q2 FY27 results are due in October; brokerages warn of further estimate cuts if European OEM spending does not stabilise

KPIT Technologies Ltd (NSE: KPITTECH) shares have fallen roughly 24-25% over the past three months. As of late September 2026, the stock traded around Rs 555-560, well below the Rs 730-740 level seen in early July.

The slide began on July 1, 2026, when the stock crashed about 17% in a single session. The trigger was an unexpected warning of a slowdown in revenue from European automotive clients, which blindsided investors and brokerages alike.

CONTEXT: KPIT is an engineering R&D (ER&D) outsourcing specialist focused on the auto industry, particularly software-defined vehicles. Unlike mainstream IT services, ER&D revenue is tightly linked to OEM program spending, making it far more cyclical. That exposure became a liability in 2026 as weak European EV demand, Chinese competition, and tariff uncertainty forced carmakers to slash budgets.

The numbers confirm the pressure. In Q1 FY27 (quarter ended June 2026), revenue came in at $177 million, down 3.6% quarter-on-quarter in constant currency. EBIT margin fell to 12.3%, down 360 basis points sequentially, while adjusted PAT stood at Rs 116.4 crore — down 28.5% QoQ and 32.3% YoY.

The weakness was not evenly spread. Passenger car revenue fell 2.4% QoQ, but commercial vehicle revenue dropped 12.2% QoQ. Coverage has flagged BMW and Volkswagen, which cut production and technology spending amid soft European EV demand and broader auto weakness.

Management has acknowledged a tough near term. Co-founder and CEO Kishor Patil guided that Q2 FY27 revenue will likely be flattish, with improvement in the second half and a return to growth by Q4 FY27. "Margins will improve, but it will improve incrementally, not significantly till our revenue go back," Patil said on the post-earnings call.

The company is leaning on new products and geographic diversification to rebuild. It is scaling platforms such as Beacon, N-Dream and Technica, targeting off-highway and truck OEMs, and pushing into the Americas and SEIMA to reduce a European revenue base that still exceeds 50%. Management reiterated a 22-24% EBITDA margin goal by FY29.

Brokerages have turned cautious. JPMorgan downgraded the stock after the Q1 warning, and other brokerages trimmed target prices, citing weaker-than-expected margins and muted FY27 guidance. The concerns centre on whether FY27 becomes a second consecutive year of organic revenue decline.

IMPACT: The read-through extends to other auto-ER&D names such as Tata Elxsi, LTTS and Tata Technologies, which face the same European client budget squeeze. Lower utilisation pressures margins across the sector. Conversely, a European auto recovery would restore revenue visibility quickly — but a weak Q2 could trigger further estimate cuts and prolong the selling.

The valuation reset has been severe. KPIT's 52-week high stands at Rs 1,328, meaning the stock has nearly halved from its peak. Market capitalisation is roughly Rs 15,379 crore, promoter holding is about 39.42%, and the year-to-date decline is around 51.77%.
