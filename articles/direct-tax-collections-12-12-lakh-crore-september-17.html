India's Net Direct Tax Collections Rise 13% to Rs 12.12 Lakh Crore Till September 17 of FY27

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

India's direct tax collections are running well ahead of last year. Net direct tax collections rose 12.96% year-on-year to Rs 12.12 lakh crore as of September 17 of financial year 2026-27, according to data released by the Income Tax department on Friday.

Gross direct tax collections increased 15.19% to Rs 14.32 lakh crore during the period, up from Rs 12.44 lakh crore a year earlier. Refunds issued stood at Rs 2.20 lakh crore, up 29.19% from the year-ago period, which accounts for the gap between gross and net growth rates.

Corporate taxes are doing the heavy lifting. Net corporate tax collections rose to Rs 5.56 lakh crore from Rs 4.65 lakh crore a year earlier, a growth of nearly 20%. Net non-corporate tax collections, which include personal income tax, increased to Rs 6.16 lakh crore from Rs 5.81 lakh crore.

On a gross basis, corporate tax collections rose to Rs 6.95 lakh crore from Rs 5.96 lakh crore, while gross non-corporate collections rose to Rs 6.98 lakh crore from Rs 6.21 lakh crore.

The standout number is the Securities Transaction Tax. Net STT collections climbed to Rs 40,214.36 crore, compared with Rs 26,305.72 crore in the corresponding period last year, a rise of over 52%. STT is charged on every securities transaction on recognised exchanges, and the surge tracks the elevated trading volumes that have defined Indian markets this year, including the massive NSE IPO that opened this week.

Collections under other minor heads were marginally negative at Rs -0.20 crore, against Rs 280.51 crore a year earlier.

The figures cover taxes paid by companies as well as individuals, Hindu Undivided Families, firms, associations of persons, bodies of individuals, local authorities and artificial juridical persons.

Strong direct tax collections matter for the fiscal math. The government has budgeted a fiscal deficit of 4.3% of GDP for FY27, and tax buoyancy well into double digits gives the exchequer headroom on both the deficit and capex fronts even as it refunds money faster.
