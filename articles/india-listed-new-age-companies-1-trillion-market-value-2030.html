India's Listed New-Age Companies May Hit $1 Trillion Market Value by 2030

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

India's new-age companies are on a trajectory toward massive scale.

Listed new-age companies could reach a combined market value of $1 trillion by 2030, according to market projections. The figure represents a dramatic expansion from current levels and reflects the rapid digitalisation of Indian commerce and services.

The new-age sector includes companies that built their businesses primarily through digital platforms. E-commerce giants like Flipkart and Amazon India, food delivery platforms like Swiggy and Zomato, fintech companies like Paytm and PhonePe, and online travel aggregators like MakeMyTrip are all part of this ecosystem.

Zomato's successful IPO in 2021 opened the floodgates. Several new-age companies have since gone public, and more are in the pipeline. The public markets have provided these companies with capital to fund growth and visibility to attract talent and partners.

The $1 trillion projection assumes continued growth in digital adoption. India's internet user base has crossed 80 crore, and smartphone penetration continues to rise. The addressable market for digital services is expanding as more consumers move online for shopping, payments, entertainment, and work.

Profitability remains a challenge. Many new-age companies have prioritised growth over margins, and investors are increasingly demanding a path to sustainable profits. Companies that can demonstrate unit economics and operating leverage will command premium valuations.

The sector is also attracting global capital. Private equity firms, venture capital funds, and sovereign wealth funds from the Middle East and Asia are investing heavily in Indian digital businesses, seeing the country as the next major growth market after China.
