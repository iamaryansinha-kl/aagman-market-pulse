RBI Net Debt Sale Hits ₹1 Trillion, Biggest in Over a Decade

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/RBI has net sold bonds worth ₹1 trillion in FY2026-27, the biggest annual net sale in more than a decade
  • 02/The previous record was ₹900 billion in FY2018, when the central bank sold bonds to absorb demonetisation-era liquidity
  • 03/Treasury officials expect another ₹1-1.5 trillion of OMO sales by December; a majority of respondents in a Reuters poll expect a policy rate hike next week

The Reserve Bank of India has net sold bonds worth ₹1 trillion this financial year. Treasury officials confirmed the figure on Monday, calling it the central bank's biggest annual net bond sale in more than a decade.

Market participants expect the total to double by December. The RBI is withdrawing liquidity from a banking system that is flush with cash.

The surplus stems from a special window that allowed lenders to raise dollars. While the inflow helped protect India's forex reserves and supported the rupee as oil prices rose, it pushed overnight rates below the RBI policy rate.

According to RBI data available since FY2015, the previous record was in FY2018. The central bank had net sold ₹900 billion that year to deal with the fallout of demonetisation of ₹500 and ₹1,000 notes.

Alok Sharma, head of treasury at ICBC, said he expects OMO sales worth another ₹1 trillion. He said a hike in banks' cash reserve ratio would have the same effect.

Harsimran Sahni, head of treasury at Anand Rathi Global Finance, expects the RBI to sell an additional ₹1-1.5 trillion worth of bonds during October-November. The firm manages around ₹150 billion in government debt.

Either step would be consistent with tighter monetary policy. A majority of respondents to a Reuters poll expect the RBI to hike its policy rate next week.

The government also reduced the supply of three- and five-year debt for the rest of the fiscal year. Market participants said this signals more open-market operations from the RBI.
