FII Outflows Hit $60 Billion; AMFI CEO Says SIPs Will Lure Them Back

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Foreign investors have pulled over $60 billion from Indian equities since October 2024, testing domestic market resilience
  • 02/Monthly SIP inflows hold firm near ₹31,000 crore with 6.3 crore retail investors absorbing FII selling pressure
  • 03/AMFI targets 10 crore investors by 2030 and AUM of ₹150 lakh crore; 55% of SIP accounts now from B-30 cities

Foreign investors have pulled over $60 billion from Indian equities since October 2024. The sustained selling has sparked debate about whether domestic investors are funding FII exits.

Monthly mutual fund inflows through SIPs remain robust at nearly ₹31,000 crore. India's 6.3 crore retail SIP investors have largely absorbed the shock.

Venkat N. Chalasani, CEO of the Association of Mutual Funds in India (AMFI), rejects the argument that retail investors are bankrolling foreign fund exits. He says domestic liquidity demonstrates market maturity.

"Some people say we are providing an easy exit for FIIs but that's not the case," Chalasani said. "This proves the maturity of the market, and it will be one of the biggest positive factors attracting FIIs back in a big way."

India's mutual fund AUM-to-GDP ratio stands at 20-21%. This compares to a global average of 65% and over 100% in developed economies.

More than 55% of SIP accounts are now from B-30 cities outside India's top 30 urban centres. Around 40% of monthly contributions originate there.

A SEBI survey found 53% of Indian households are aware of mutual funds but only 6% have invested. AMFI targets 10 crore investors by 2030 and AUM of ₹150 lakh crore.

"It is not the timing of the market that matters. It is the time you spend staying in the market," Chalasani said to long-term investors.
