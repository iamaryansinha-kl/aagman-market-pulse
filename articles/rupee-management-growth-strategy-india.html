Rupee Management Without Rate Hikes: A Growth-First Strategy For India

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Sunil Sanghai argues India must prioritise growth over aggressive interest rate hikes to manage rupee, inflation and fiscal concerns
  • 02/India could attract $100 billion additional annual investment by simplifying approvals, improving tax certainty and cutting transaction timelines
  • 03/Supply-side interventions and gold monetisation offer alternatives to monetary tightening that risks slowing private investment

India should not raise interest rates aggressively to manage rupee depreciation, inflation and fiscal deficit concerns. Growth must remain the central priority at this stage of the economic journey.

Sustained growth will move millions into the middle class and help India become a developed nation. A sharp rate increase risks slowing private investment and reducing economic momentum.

The most effective way to stabilise the rupee is improving foreign exchange inflows. India needs larger and more consistent foreign portfolio investment and foreign direct investment.

Foreign investors seek a clearer, consistent and globally acceptable taxation framework. Tax uncertainty and prolonged litigation discourage long-term capital flows into the country.

Large transactions in India still often take over twelve months to close. Sanghai estimates India could attract $100 billion of additional investment annually by simplifying implementation and improving approval timelines.

India holds one of the world's largest privately held gold stocks. Through financial structures, idle household gold can be channelled into productive use and reduce current account deficit pressure.

Current inflation appears supply-side driven rather than demand-driven. Supply-side interventions like improving logistics and accelerating infrastructure execution offer more sustainable outcomes than rate hikes.

Protecting the fiscal deficit does not require sacrificing growth. Accelerating privatisation and asset monetisation can unlock substantial value from public sector assets.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHY DOES THE AUTHOR OPPOSE INTEREST RATE HIKES TO MANAGE THE RUPEE?
THE AUTHOR ARGUES THAT INDIA STILL REQUIRES INVESTMENT, CAPACITY CREATION AND CONSUMPTION EXPANSION. A SHARP INCREASE IN INTEREST RATES RISKS SLOWING PRIVATE INVESTMENT AND REDUCING ECONOMIC MOMENTUM AT A TIME WHEN GROWTH IS CRITICAL FOR MOVING MILLIONS INTO THE MIDDLE CLASS.
HOW CAN INDIA STABILISE THE RUPEE WITHOUT RAISING RATES?
THE MOST EFFECTIVE WAY IS TO IMPROVE THE FLOW OF FOREIGN EXCHANGE INTO THE COUNTRY. INDIA NEEDS LARGER AND MORE CONSISTENT FOREIGN PORTFOLIO INVESTMENT AND FOREIGN DIRECT INVESTMENT, ALONG WITH A CLEARER, MORE CONSISTENT AND GLOBALLY ACCEPTABLE TAXATION FRAMEWORK.
HOW MUCH ADDITIONAL FDI COULD INDIA ATTRACT WITH REFORMS?
ACCORDING TO THE AUTHOR, INDIA HAS THE POTENTIAL TO ATTRACT AT LEAST USD 100 BILLION OF ADDITIONAL INVESTMENT CAPITAL EVERY YEAR IF IMPLEMENTATION IS SIMPLIFIED, APPROVAL TIMELINES IMPROVED AND A PREDICTABLE REGULATORY ENVIRONMENT IS CREATED.
WHAT ROLE CAN GOLD PLAY IN SUPPORTING THE RUPEE?
INDIA POSSESSES ONE OF THE WORLD'S LARGEST PRIVATELY HELD STOCKS OF GOLD. THROUGH WELL-DESIGNED FINANCIAL STRUCTURES AND MARKET-LINKED SCHEMES, IDLE HOUSEHOLD GOLD CAN BE CHANNELLED INTO PRODUCTIVE ECONOMIC USE, REDUCING DEPENDENCE ON IMPORTED GOLD AND CUTTING PRESSURE ON THE CURRENT ACCOUNT DEFICIT.
WHAT IS THE AUTHOR'S VIEW ON CURRENT INFLATIONARY PRESSURES?
THE AUTHOR BELIEVES CURRENT INFLATION IS DRIVEN MORE BY SUPPLY-SIDE CONSTRAINTS THAN BY EXCESSIVE DEMAND. IN SUCH A SCENARIO, MONETARY TIGHTENING MAY NOT BE THE MOST EFFECTIVE SOLUTION, AND SUPPLY-SIDE INTERVENTIONS LIKE IMPROVING LOGISTICS AND ACCELERATING INFRASTRUCTURE EXECUTION WOULD PRODUCE MORE SUSTAINABLE OUTCOMES.
