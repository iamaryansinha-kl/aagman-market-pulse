eMudhra to Exit Global Certificate Business After Key Compromise

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- eMudhra will voluntarily exit its global SSL/TLS certificate business after a security incident involving compromised private keys.
  • •- The stock fell another 4% to Rs 331 on Friday; it is now down 39% in three sessions.
  • •- The breach: a flawed key generation ceremony published weak, reverse-engineerable keys for eight intermediate certificate authorities.
  • •- The company says the affected segment contributes only about 1% of global revenue, and its India-regulated DSC and eSign businesses are unaffected.

eMudhra is abandoning the business at the centre of its worst-ever week. The digital trust company said on Friday it will voluntarily exit the global SSL/TLS certificate business, days after disclosing that private keys linked to eight of its intermediate Certificate Authority certificates had been compromised. The stock slid another 4% to Rs 331 on the BSE, extending a rout that has now erased 39% of the company's value in three trading sessions — a collapse triggered not by earnings or guidance, but by a security incident that struck at the core of what a certificate authority sells: trust.

The technical root cause is the kind of failure that keeps security engineers awake. In a regulatory filing, eMudhra explained that a flaw in its Key Generation Ceremony caused a wrap/unwrap step to produce a second set of weak keys alongside the original strong keys — and it was the weak set that was published to browser root stores. The roots and issuing CAs had passed independent audits two years running and had been on the trust lists of Apple, Google, Microsoft and Mozilla since mid-2025. Last weekend, an independent researcher discovered that the weak keys could be reverse-engineered — possibly with AI-assisted techniques — and reported the finding to the major root programs. Once a private key for a certificate authority can be reconstructed, every certificate that authority might issue is untrustworthy by definition, which is why the fallout moved so fast and so far.

The company's response was swift, and its defence is that the damage is contained. Chairman V. Srinivasan said eMudhra revoked the affected certificate authorities within 14 hours of discovery, made a prompt disclosure, and confirmed that no end-entity certificates had been issued by the affected CAs — meaning no actual website or application certificates were forged. Its infrastructure was not compromised, no data was breached, and all physical and logical security controls remained intact, the company said. The math that management wants investors to focus on: the global SSL/TLS business contributes only about 1% of eMudhra's global revenue, and rising data-sovereignty requirements plus free certificates from large providers had already squeezed the segment's economics.

The chairman's framing is that the board chose to amputate a marginal business rather than defend it. Rising data-sovereignty and compliance costs had increased the complexity and cost of operating the segment globally, Srinivasan said, while free SSL/TLS certificates from some large entities had reduced its business potential and return on investment relative to the company's revenue base. In that telling, the incident was the final push out of a business eMudhra was already struggling to justify. The counterargument is the one the market spent three sessions pricing: a certificate authority sells trust, and a key-generation flaw discovered by an outside researcher — not by the company's own audits, which the roots had passed two years running — is precisely the kind of failure that makes enterprise customers audit everything else you operate.

The government has publicly drawn the boundary of the incident. The Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In), the Centre's nodal cybersecurity agency, issued an urgent advisory after the compromise came to light, and the Ministry of Electronics and IT said it had taken serious note and was working with stakeholders. Crucially for eMudhra's domestic franchise, MeitY clarified that the company's Digital Signature Certificate and eSign services — regulated by the Controller of Certifying Authorities — remain unaffected, and that the impact on Indian websites and applications was "very limited and for a very short interval." MeitY also noted that eMudhra operates two distinct businesses: the CCA-regulated domestic trust services, and the global PKI business that secures websites and software for browsers worldwide. It is the second of these that is being shut. For the domestic franchise, the stakes are lower but real: the DSC and eSign operations that make up the regulated core are unaffected by the incident, and MeitY's public reassurance was aimed squarely at the government and enterprise customers who rely on them for statutory filings and digital signing. Whether they draw that distinction — or begin quietly evaluating alternatives — will determine whether 39% proves an overreaction or the start of a longer repricing.

The open question is whether 1% of revenue can justify 39% of market value. Management argues the social-media attention was disproportionate to the segment's contribution, and the board concluded after detailed deliberations that exiting was the right decision for shareholders. eMudhra is working directly with potentially affected customers to issue replacement certificates so critical services are not disrupted, and continues its root-cause investigation with the root programs. But the company itself conceded the incident could pose reputational risks "the extent of which cannot be quantified" — and for a company whose entire product is the guarantee that you are talking to who you think you are, reputational risk is not a footnote. It is the business model.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENED AT EMUDHRA?
A flawed key generation ceremony produced weak private keys for eight intermediate certificate authorities, which were published to browser root stores. A researcher showed they could be reverse-engineered, prompting revocations and the eventual decision to exit the segment.
HOW MUCH OF EMUDHRA'S BUSINESS IS AFFECTED?
The global SSL/TLS certificate segment contributes about 1% of global revenue. The company says its India-regulated DSC and eSign services are unaffected.
WHY DID THE STOCK FALL 39% IN THREE SESSIONS?
Investors are pricing reputational damage beyond the segment's direct revenue contribution — certificate authorities sell trust, and a key-compromise incident undermines the core product even when containment is fast. GLOSSARY: SSL/TLS CERTIFICATE — A digital credential that encrypts the connection between a browser and a website and verifies the site's identity; issued by certificate authorities like eMudhra's global PKI unit. CERTIFICATE AUTHORITY (CA) — A trusted entity that issues digital certificates; browsers and operating systems maintain "root stores" listing which CAs they trust by default. KEY GENERATION CEREMONY — A highly controlled cryptographic process for creating the public-private key pairs on which a CA's trust depends; a flaw in this ceremony was eMudhra's root cause.
