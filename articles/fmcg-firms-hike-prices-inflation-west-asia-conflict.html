FMCG Firms Hike Prices 4-7% on Inflation From West Asia Conflict

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Dabur India (NSE: DABUR) has raised prices by 4% across its portfolio while also reducing grammage in small packs to maintain popular price points
  • 02/Godrej Consumer Products (NSE: GODREJCP) hiked soaps 5%, detergents 6-7%, insecticides ~7%; Marico raised hair oils 6-7% and Saffola oils
  • 03/West Asia conflict-driven crude volatility has severely hit packaging costs; companies warn of possible second round of hikes if geopolitical tensions persist

FMCG companies have begun calibrated price hikes to tackle inflation triggered by the West Asia conflict. The crude price volatility has severely hit packaging costs among other raw materials.

Dabur India (NSE: DABUR) has already raised prices by 4 per cent across different parts of its business. Global CEO Mohit Malhotra said the company also reduced grammage in small packs to maintain popular price points.

"We may also need to take a second round of price increase, depending on the geopolitical situation," Malhotra said during the company's fourth quarter earnings call on Thursday.

Godrej Consumer Products (NSE: GODREJCP) raised prices by 5 per cent in soaps, 6-7 per cent in detergents and about 7 per cent on household insecticides. The hikes come on the back of cost inflation in palm oil and crude-oil derivatives.

An analyst report by Nuvama Institutional Equities noted that Marico (NSE: MARICO) hiked prices by 6-7 per cent in value-added hair oils. It also took price increases in Saffola edible oils.

Rakshit Hargave, MD & CEO of Britannia Industries (NSE: BRITANNIA), said the company will "selectively have to take price increases, and this includes both grammage adjustment."

Several companies have begun focusing on alternative sourcing of packaging inputs facing disruptions from the West Asia crisis. Players in packaged drinking water and paints have already taken more than one round of price hikes.

Industry experts said depending on geopolitical tensions, FMCG players may need further price hikes in coming months if inflationary pressures persist. The measures were detailed in company earnings calls and management statements published on May 10, 2026.
