Investment Banks Earn Rs 2,274 Crore From 81 Mainboard IPOs Jan-Sep

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Investment banks earned a cumulative Rs 2,274 crore in fees from managing 81 mainboard IPOs between January and September 2026.
  • 02/Those 81 issues collectively raised about Rs 1.05 lakh crore, including a record September that saw 30 mainboard IPOs raise Rs 38,785 crore.
  • 03/Fee economics are diverging: mega-deals like the Rs 22,561 crore NSE IPO pay roughly 0.82%, well below the ~1.86% average for smaller issues.

Investment banks in India have earned a cumulative Rs 2,274 crore in fees so far in 2026. The money came from managing 81 mainboard IPOs between January and September.

Those 81 mainboard issues collectively raised about Rs 1.05 lakh crore. That works out to a blended fee of roughly 2.15% of the amount raised across the mainboard pipeline.

The broader numbers are even larger. A total of 228 IPOs, combining mainboard and SME issues, raised Rs 112,941 crore through September 21, 2026.

The mainboard portion of that haul was Rs 105,768 crore. SME issues accounted for the remaining roughly Rs 7,173 crore, underscoring how dominant large listings remain.

September 2026 was the standout month. Thirty mainboard IPOs raised Rs 38,785 crore, making it the busiest September for listings since 1996.

The single biggest driver was the National Stock Exchange IPO. The issue raised Rs 22,561 crore in September, the second-largest public offering in India's history.

The NSE IPO generated about Rs 186 crore in fees for its bankers. That amount was shared among roughly 20 investment banks, an effective fee of approximately 0.82% of issue size.

CONTEXT: IPO underwriting fees in India typically run at 2-2.5% for mid-sized deals, but percentage fees get negotiated sharply lower on jumbo issues. The average IPO fee last year was about 1.86%, and the NSE deal shows how blockbuster listings compress that further.

The NSE syndicate was a who's who of the street. Book-running lead managers included Kotak Mahindra Capital, JM Financial, Morgan Stanley, Citigroup, HSBC, JPMorgan, SBI Capital Markets, Axis Capital, ICICI Securities, HDFC Bank and DAM Capital, among others.

The NSE deal completed its journey quickly. The issue closed on September 21, and the shares listed on BSE on September 24 after a fully subscribed book.

Syndicates are getting bigger, not smaller. Hyundai's IPO used 10 bankers, the NSE issue used about 20, and Jio Platforms filed its DRHP in June with 19 book-running lead managers.

The fee pool at stake is meaningful in global terms too. Indian investment banks earned about $1.3 billion in investment-banking fees in 2025, with JP Morgan topping the ECM league at $66.9 million, a 10.3% share of the $649 million ECM fee pool.

The busy calendar reflects a strong primary-market run. High-profile listings spanned exchanges, infrastructure, financial services and consumer sectors, with institutional book-building now the norm.

IMPACT: ECM fees are flowing directly into brokerages' Q2 and Q3 FY27 earnings, and the equity capital markets desk has become a rare bright spot in an otherwise fee-pressured investment-banking year marked by an M&A slowdown.

The incentive to supply more paper is building. September 2026 saw record DRHP filings, suggesting the Q3 and Q4 pipeline could stay heavy if market conditions hold.

The SME segment, by contrast, is smaller in rupee terms. SME issues contributed only a fraction of the Rs 112,941 crore raised, which is why mainboard mandates remain the prize for investment banks.

The risks are equally clear. Fee compression on blockbuster deals means banks must chase volume, and a market correction could freeze the issuance window altogether.
