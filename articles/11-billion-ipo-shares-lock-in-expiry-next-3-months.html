$11 Billion Worth of IPO Shares Face Lock-In Expiry Over Next 3 Months

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

A major supply overhang is coming to Indian equity markets.

Over $11 billion worth of shares from recent initial public offerings will see their lock-in periods expire over the next three months. These shares are currently held by pre-IPO investors, promoters, and anchor investors who are barred from selling.

Lock-in periods typically range from six months to one year from the date of listing. Once the lock-in expires, these large shareholders are free to offload their holdings in the open market.

The expiry affects multiple large IPOs from 2025 and early 2026. Companies that went public with substantial offer-for-sale components and significant pre-IPO private equity backing are particularly vulnerable.

The supply overhang could pressure stock prices. When large blocks of shares become available for sale, the increased supply can depress prices if demand does not match the additional volume.

Promoter lock-in expiries are watched closely. If promoters choose to sell, it can signal a lack of confidence and trigger broader selling by retail investors. Conversely, if promoters hold or buy more shares, it can be viewed as a positive signal.

Foreign institutional investors and mutual funds will be key buyers if the selling pressure materialises. Their willingness to absorb the additional supply will determine the extent of price impact.
