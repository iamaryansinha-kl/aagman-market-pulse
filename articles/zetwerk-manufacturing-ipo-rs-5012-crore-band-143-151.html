Zetwerk IPO: Price Band Rs 143-151, Rs 5,012 Crore Issue Opens Oct 15

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Zetwerk Manufacturing Businesses has fixed its IPO price band at Rs 143-151 per share; the Rs 5,012.33 crore issue opens October 15, 2026
  • 02/The book-built issue combines a Rs 2,830 crore fresh issue with an offer for sale of 14.45 crore shares worth about Rs 2,182 crore
  • 03/The company reported a loss in FY2026 with negative EPS, making the P/E unascertainable; three-year weighted average RoNW stands at negative 15.64%

Zetwerk Manufacturing Businesses Private Limited has fixed the price band for its initial public offering at Rs 143 to Rs 151 per share. The Rs 5,012.33 crore IPO opens for subscription on Wednesday, October 15, 2026.

The issue will remain open until October 19, 2026, giving investors three days to place bids. Shares are planned to list on both the BSE and the NSE, with the tentative listing date set for October 23, 2026.

The price band of Rs 143 at the floor to Rs 151 at the cap is set against a face value of Rs 1 per share. Allotment of shares is expected to be finalised on October 21, 2026.

The book-built issue is a mix of fresh capital and an exit for existing shareholders. It includes a fresh issue of Rs 2,830 crore alongside an offer for sale of 14.45 crore shares aggregating approximately Rs 2,182 crore.

The minimum bid is 99 equity shares, with bids thereafter in multiples of 99. Employees bidding in the reserved portion receive a discount of Rs 14 per share on the final offer price.

Zetwerk Manufacturing Businesses, incorporated in 2017, is a technology-led global manufacturing platform. It connects industrial and consumer enterprises with manufacturing partners for customised and large-scale production.

Its platform spans electronics, renewable energy, aerospace and defence, oil and gas, AI infrastructure, and industrial automation. The company runs an asset-light model, offering end-to-end services from design and supplier matching to quality control and logistics through its proprietary Zetwerk OS platform.

Scale is the centrepiece of the story. Zetwerk operates in over 25 countries with more than 10,000 suppliers, over 20 manufacturing facilities across India, the US and Mexico, and 1,100 customers including Fortune 500 companies.

In FY2026, the company served 1,802 customers in total, of which 1,613 were in India and 189 were overseas. That breadth gives it a diversified demand base across geographies and end sectors.

There is a significant financial caveat investors must weigh. The company reported a loss in FY2026 on restated consolidated financials, and both basic and diluted earnings per share were negative.

With negative EPS, the price-to-earnings ratio is unascertainable at the offer price. The weighted average return on net worth for the last three financial years stands at negative 15.64%.

In effect, this is a loss-making but scaled manufacturing-platform story. The pricing leans on revenue scale and platform economics rather than current earnings.

The use of proceeds explains the dual logic of the issue. Funds raised will primarily go toward repaying and prepaying certain outstanding borrowings, funding inorganic growth through unidentified strategic acquisitions, and general corporate purposes.

Of the total estimated proceeds of Rs 2,160 crore earmarked under specific borrowing objectives, Rs 1,300 crore is allocated to one repayment or prepayment objective and Rs 860 crore to another. The balance supports acquisitions and corporate needs.

The issue seeks a valuation of Rs 25,583 crore. At that level, the market will be paying for the platform's growth runway rather than present profitability.

The book-running lead managers for the issue are Kotak Mahindra Capital Company, Morgan Stanley India, Goldman Sachs (India) Securities, Avendus Capital, JM Financial, HSBC Securities & Capital Markets (India), and Pantomath Capital Advisors. KFin Technologies is the registrar to the issue.

The pitch to investors rests on the platform model rather than factories. Zetwerk does not own most of the capacity it sells; it orchestrates it — matching industrial and consumer enterprises with manufacturing partners across more than 25 countries, and layering design, quality control and logistics on top through its proprietary Zetwerk OS system. That asset-light structure is what lets a company with just over 20 manufacturing facilities claim more than 10,000 suppliers and 1,100 customers, including Fortune 500 names.

The order book spans some of the economy's fastest lanes: electronics, renewable energy, aerospace and defence, and AI infrastructure sit alongside oil and gas and industrial automation. In FY2026 the platform served 1,802 customers — 1,613 in India and 189 overseas — a mix that gives the story both a domestic-manufacturing angle and an export one.

The counterweight is the financial record. A loss in FY2026, negative earnings per share and a three-year weighted average return on net worth of negative 15.64 per cent mean the Rs 25,583 crore valuation cannot be defended on trailing earnings — there are none. Investors are effectively pricing the network, the customer relationships and the acquisition pipeline, not current profitability. That makes this a proposition for those willing to underwrite execution risk rather than a traditional earnings story.

It also makes the use of proceeds worth watching. More than half of the identified deployment — Rs 1,300 crore of the Rs 2,160 crore earmarked objectives — goes to repaying or prepaying borrowings, with another Rs 860 crore for a second repayment objective, before a rupee is spent on the unidentified acquisitions the company also intends to pursue. The OFS component, roughly Rs 2,182 crore of shares from existing holders, adds a further note: insiders are realising value at the same entry point as the public.

GLOSSARY: Asset-light model: A business structure where the company owns little physical capacity and instead coordinates partner facilities and suppliers. Book-built issue: An IPO where investors bid within a price band and the final price is discovered from demand. Offer for sale (OFS): The part of an IPO where existing shareholders sell their shares rather than the company issuing new ones. Return on net worth (RoNW): Profit measured against shareholders' equity; a negative value means the company is loss-making against its capital base. Fresh issue: The portion of an IPO where the company issues new shares and receives the proceeds directly.
