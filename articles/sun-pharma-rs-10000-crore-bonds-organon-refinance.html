Sun Pharma Raises Rs 10,000 Cr Bonds to Fund Organon Deal

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Sun Pharma is raising Rs 10,000 crore through two tranches of rupee-denominated bonds.
  • •- The Rs 7,000 crore tranche carries an 8.20% coupon; the Rs 3,000 crore tranche carries 8.05% with a 2028 put option.
  • •- Proceeds will partly refinance bridge loans taken for the $11.75 billion Organon acquisition agreed in April.
  • •- Anchor investors likely include SBI Pension Fund, SBI Life, ICICI Prudential Life and ICICI Prudential Mutual Fund.
  • •- Crisil has assigned AAA/Stable to the proposed NCDs; maturities are in 2031.
  • •- Organon posted $6.2 billion revenue and a 31% adjusted operating margin in calendar 2025.

India's largest drugmaker by market value is tapping the country's deepest pool of money to pay for its biggest ever deal. Sun Pharmaceutical Industries is raising Rs 10,000 crore through two tranches of rupee-denominated bonds, people aware of the development told ET, with the funds earmarked partly to refinance bridge loans the company took to acquire US healthcare firm Organon in an all-cash transaction valued at $11.75 billion. The structure tells the story of India's corporate bond market in a single trade: two tranches, coupons of 8.20% and 8.05%, maturities in 2031, and a put option in 2028 on the smaller tranche allowing investors early redemption under the issue terms.

The pricing follows the company's board approval last week to issue non-convertible debentures worth up to Rs 15,000 crore, a borrowing programme the company disclosed without final tranche-wise terms; this week's issue fills in those numbers. The anchor book reads like a directory of Indian institutional money: SBI Pension Fund, SBI Life Insurance, ICICI Prudential Life Insurance, Aditya Birla Sun Life Insurance, ICICI Prudential Mutual Fund, Edelweiss Mutual Fund and SBI Mutual Fund are among the likely participants — pension, insurance and mutual fund capital that constitutes the backbone of demand for long-dated, highly rated Indian corporate debt. Crisil Ratings has assigned AAA/Stable to the proposed NCDs, and SBI Capital Markets, Standard Chartered Bank and MUFG Securities India are arranging the sale.

The transaction is the domestic mirror of the Organon acquisition agreed in April, when Sun Pharma committed to buy the Merck spin-off in an all-cash deal at a $11.75 billion enterprise value, marking a major expansion of its global business and women's health portfolio. Organon's standalone numbers make the strategic logic legible: the company reported revenue of $6.2 billion and adjusted operating profit of around $2 billion in calendar year 2025, a healthy operating margin of roughly 31%, according to a Crisil report. Sun Pharma is expected to deploy $2.0-2.5 billion of its own cash surplus and internal accruals toward the acquisition, with the balance — roughly $9.5-10 billion — funded through debt; the bridge loans now being refinanced were the first leg of that debt, priced for speed rather than duration.

Refinancing them into ten-year rupee paper at sub-8.5% coupons is where the financial engineering shows. Bridge debt sits on a balance sheet like a fire under management; converting it into long-dated, put-optioned AAA-rated bonds converts urgency into structure, locking in fixed costs while the acquisition's earnings — Organon's $2 billion of adjusted operating profit — begin to flow through the combined entity. The 2028 put option on the Rs 3,000 crore tranche gives investors a five-year exit ahead of the 2031 maturity, a feature increasingly standard in Indian institutional debt that prices flexibility into the coupon.

The choice of rupee funding for a dollar-denominated acquisition is its own signal. Sun Pharma could have tapped the overseas bond market or syndicated loans abroad; instead it is funding the refinance from domestic pension, insurance and mutual fund money, keeping the currency mismatch between its revenues and its liabilities deliberate and visible. India's pharma majors earn heavily in dollars and euros, giving Sun a natural hedge that most borrowers cannot claim — Organon's US women's health and biosimilars franchises will generate the very currency needed to service this debt. That logic, more than any credit argument, explains why the book is expected to clear comfortably at these coupons.

For the corporate bond market, the deal is another data point in a record year. India's private-placement market has already crossed Rs 8.5 trillion through September, with September itself the highest month in more than six years, as companies front-load issuance ahead of expected rate moves. A marquee AAA borrower printing Rs 10,000 crore across two tranches in a single week — with pension and insurance money anchoring the book — demonstrates both the depth of demand for quality credit and the extent to which India's biggest acquisitions are now financed substantially at home, in local currency, at investment-grade spreads. It also extends a pattern: from Reliance Industries' benchmark prints to HDFC Bank's perennial programme, the largest Indian corporates increasingly treat the domestic bond market as their first call for big-ticket funding, with offshore issuance reserved for specific currency needs. The arrangers' final allocations will show how much of the book the named anchors absorbed; the coupons, in any case, are set.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
HOW MUCH IS SUN PHARMA RAISING AND AT WHAT PRICE?
Rs 10,000 crore across two tranches: Rs 7,000 crore at an 8.20% coupon and Rs 3,000 crore at 8.05% with a put option in 2028; maturities are in 2031.
WHAT WILL THE MONEY BE USED FOR?
The proceeds partly refinance bridge loans taken for Sun Pharma's $11.75 billion acquisition of Organon agreed in April.
WHO ARE THE LIKELY INVESTORS?
Institutional anchors include SBI Pension Fund, SBI Life, ICICI Prudential Life, Aditya Birla Sun Life, ICICI Prudential MF, Edelweiss MF and SBI MF; Crisil rates the NCDs AAA/Stable.
WHO IS ARRANGING THE ISSUE?
SBI Capital Markets, Standard Chartered Bank and MUFG Securities India. GLOSSARY: - Non-convertible debenture: A bond that cannot be converted into equity, repaid at maturity. - Bridge loan: Short-term financing used until permanent funding is arranged. - Put option: The investor's right to seek early redemption of a bond on preset terms. - Enterprise value: A company's total value including debt, used as the price basis for acquisitions.
