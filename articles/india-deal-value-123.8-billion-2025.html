India Deal Value Jumps 18% to $123.8 Billion in 2025 Despite Lower Volume

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/India's total deal value reached $123.8 billion in 2025, up 18% from $106.3 billion in 2024, per an EY report released April 20
  • 02/Deal volumes fell 3% as investors shifted toward fewer, larger transactions in infrastructure and cross-border M&A
  • 03/2026 outlook remains resilient with dealmakers focusing on mature, high-value deals amid geopolitical uncertainty

India's overall deal activity jumped 18% by value to $123.8 billion in 2025, according to an EY report. This marks a sharp rise from $106.3 billion recorded in 2024.

Deal volumes actually declined 3% during the same period. The market shifted toward fewer but larger transactions, reflecting a more selective approach by investors.

Infrastructure emerged as the top sector by deal value, clocking $24.6 billion. This represents a 35% jump from $18.2 billion in 2024, despite an 18% drop in deal count.

Cross-border M&A saw volumes decline 20%, but combined value surged 155% to $33.2 billion from $13 billion in 2024. Inbound deals from the Middle East and Japan drove much of this growth.

Private equity investments rose 8% to $60.7 billion across 1,475 deals in 2025, up from $56.2 billion in 2024. PE exit value also climbed to $32.9 billion from $28.2 billion, though exit volumes dropped to 257 from 285.

The industrials and automotive sector reported the highest deal value growth, rising 105% year-on-year alongside a 4% increase in volumes. Government capex of INR 11.21 lakh crore in FY26 supported large strategic deals.

"Investors are not stepping away from India; they are becoming more selective. Instead of pursuing multiple smaller opportunities, they focused on deals that offered long-term relevance at valuations that made sense," said Amit Khandelwal, Managing Partner, Strategy and Transactions, EY India and Africa region.

Deal activity in 2026 is expected to stay resilient. However, geopolitical uncertainty and energy price volatility may lead to longer timelines and heightened due diligence.
