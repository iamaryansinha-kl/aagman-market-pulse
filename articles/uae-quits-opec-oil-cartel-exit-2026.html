UAE Quits OPEC: 3.64 Million Barrels/Day Exit Threatens Cartel's Oil Grip

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/UAE announces shock exit from OPEC, removing 3.64 million barrels/day of production from the cartel within days.
  • 02/Strait of Hormuz closure has already shut in 10 million barrels/day, or 10% of global supplies, making the exit timing less immediately disruptive.
  • 03/Analysts warn the departure could trigger a domino effect of exits and undermine OPEC's long-term market power and credibility.

The United Arab Emirates announced it will quit OPEC in a matter of days. The decision blindsided partner nations after six decades of membership. Energy Minister Suhail Al Mazrouei cited the war in Iran as the tipping point.

The UAE is OPEC's third-largest producer. It pumped 3.64 million barrels per day in February, according to the International Energy Agency. That output far exceeded its official OPEC quota.

The Strait of Hormuz closure has forced Gulf producers to shut in at least 10 million barrels per day. That equals roughly 10% of world supplies. The shutdown means the UAE exit will be less disruptive in the immediate term.

"OPEC's market power will diminish," said Greg Brew, an analyst at Eurasia Group. "The UAE's departure will undermine the group's credibility." The emirates represented a significant portion of overall OPEC capacity.

Sultan Al Jaber, chief executive of Abu Dhabi National Oil Co., long chafed against OPEC+ quotas. The UAE had invested billions in new capacity and often pumped above its limit. It earned a rare public rebuke from Saudi Arabia.

"If oil producing capacity is leaving the cartel's influence, that's bearish over a three-to-five year timeframe," said Clayton Seigle of CSIS. He warned of a potential domino effect if other coalition members follow Abu Dhabi out the door.

Saudi Arabia has struggled to rein in overproducing members. The kingdom recently shifted strategy to boost supply rather than support prices. It shoulders the burden of future adjustments as OPEC+ reliability weakens.

"What's less clear is when we will have oversupply again," said Bob McNally of Rapidan Energy Group. The real test of OPEC's potency will come when it next tries to intervene in the market. That could be years away.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHY IS THE UAE LEAVING OPEC?
ENERGY MINISTER SUHAIL AL MAZROUEI SAID THE TIPPING POINT WAS THE WAR IN IRAN. THE CLOSURE OF THE STRAIT OF HORMUZ FORCED PRODUCTION SHUTDOWNS, MAKING THE EXIT LESS DISRUPTIVE IN THE SHORT TERM AND ALLOWING THE UAE TO RAMP UP AFTER THE WAR WITHOUT QUOTA CONSTRAINTS.
HOW MUCH OIL DOES THE UAE PRODUCE?
THE UAE PRODUCED 3.64 MILLION BARRELS PER DAY IN FEBRUARY, ACCORDING TO THE INTERNATIONAL ENERGY AGENCY. THIS MAKES IT OPEC'S THIRD-LARGEST PRODUCER AND REPRESENTS A SIGNIFICANT SHARE OF THE CARTEL'S TOTAL CAPACITY.
WHAT IS THE IMMEDIATE IMPACT OF THE UAE EXIT ON GLOBAL OIL SUPPLIES?
THE IMMEDIATE IMPACT IS LIMITED BECAUSE THE STRAIT OF HORMUZ CLOSURE HAS ALREADY SHUT IN AT LEAST 10 MILLION BARRELS PER DAY, OR 10% OF WORLD SUPPLIES. ONCE THE WATERWAY REOPENS, THE UAE COULD BOOST PRODUCTION UNFETTERED BY OPEC QUOTAS.
WHAT ARE ANALYSTS SAYING ABOUT OPEC'S FUTURE?
GREG BREW OF EURASIA GROUP SAID OPEC'S MARKET POWER WILL DIMINISH AND CREDIBILITY WILL BE UNDERMINED. CLAYTON SEIGLE OF CSIS WARNS THE DEPARTURE IS BEARISH OVER A THREE-TO-FIVE YEAR TIMEFRAME AND RAISES FEARS OF A DOMINO EFFECT.
WHO IS THE KEY FIGURE BEHIND THE UAE'S OPEC EXIT?
SULTAN AL JABER, CHIEF EXECUTIVE OFFICER OF ABU DHABI NATIONAL OIL CO., HAS LONG CHAFED AGAINST OPEC+ QUOTAS. HE DROVE BILLIONS IN CAPACITY INVESTMENTS AND PUSHED OUTPUT ABOVE THE UAE'S STIPULATED LIMIT, SETTING THE STAGE FOR THE DEPARTURE.
