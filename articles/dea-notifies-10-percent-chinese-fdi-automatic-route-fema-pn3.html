DEA Notifies 10% Chinese Ownership Cap for Automatic FDI Route Under FEMA, Easing Six-Year Curbs

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/The Department of Economic Affairs (DEA) on May 2, 2026, formally notified FEMA amendments allowing overseas companies with up to 10% Chinese or Hong Kong shareholding to invest in India under the automatic route.
  • 02/This follows the Union Cabinet's approval on March 10, 2026, and DPIIT's Press Note 2 (2026 series) dated March 15, 2026, amending the restrictive Press Note 3 framework introduced on April 17, 2020.
  • 03/Watch whether the 60-day fast-track approval mechanism for five manufacturing sectors draws meaningful capital, and if global funds with minor Chinese limited partners start deploying under the clarified beneficial ownership threshold.

The Department of Economic Affairs (DEA) on May 2, 2026, notified amendments to the Foreign Exchange Management (Non-debt Instruments) Rules, 2019, formalising a calibrated relaxation of India's six-year-old foreign direct investment restrictions on China-linked capital.

The notification allows foreign companies with Chinese or Hong Kong shareholding of up to 10 per cent to invest in India under the automatic route in sectors where FDI is permitted, subject to sectoral caps. This marks a structural shift from the blanket prohibition imposed under Press Note 3 of 2020.

The policy change traces back to March 10, 2026, when the Union Cabinet chaired by Prime Minister Narendra Modi approved amendments to the press note (PN) 3 of 2020 issued by the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT). DPIIT subsequently operationalised the change through Press Note 2 (2026 series) on March 15, 2026.

Under the original Press Note 3 notified on April 17, 2020, foreign firms with even a single share held by investors from land-border countries—including China, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal, Bhutan, Myanmar, and Afghanistan—were required to seek mandatory government approval. The rule was introduced to curb opportunistic takeovers of Indian companies during the COVID-19 pandemic.

The DEA notification now introduces a threshold-based test. "Beneficial owner" will carry the same meaning as assigned in clause (fa) of sub-section (1) of section 2 of the Prevention of Money-laundering Act, 2002. Under the Prevention of Money-laundering (Maintenance of Records) Rules, 2005, controlling ownership interest means ownership of or entitlement to more than 10 per cent of shares, capital, or profits.

Importantly, the relaxed rules do not apply to entities registered in China, Hong Kong, or other land-border nations. Direct investments from these jurisdictions still require government approval. The 10% exemption applies only to overseas entities with minority, non-controlling beneficial ownership from these countries.

The notification also clarifies that multilateral banks or funds of which India is a member will not be treated as an entity of a particular country, nor shall any country be treated as the beneficial owner of such investments. However, investments from entities with direct or indirect ownership by citizens or entities of land-border countries that do not require prior approval will still be subject to reporting requirements specified by the Reserve Bank of India.

Despite the restrictive regime since 2020, China's direct investment footprint in India has remained marginal. According to DPIIT data, China ranks 23rd among FDI source countries, accounting for only 0.32 per cent of total FDI equity inflows into India—equivalent to approximately USD 2.51 billion between April 2000 and December 2025. India's cumulative FDI equity inflows during the same period stood at Rs 51,85,639 crore (USD 776.75 billion).

The broader trade imbalance remains stark. India's exports to China declined 14.5 per cent to USD 14.25 billion in FY 2024-25, while imports rose 11.52 per cent to USD 113.45 billion, pushing the bilateral trade deficit to USD 99.21 billion.

For investments exceeding the 10% threshold or involving controlling interest, the government has introduced a 60-day time-bound approval mechanism for five manufacturing sectors: capital goods, electronic capital goods, electronic components, polysilicon, and ingot-wafer. This is down from the previous 8-12 week average processing time.

The amendment primarily addresses a practical pain point for global investment funds and multinational investors with minor exposure to Chinese limited partners or shareholders, who were previously caught in the sweeping approval net of Press Note 3.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT IS THE EXACT THRESHOLD FOR AUTOMATIC ROUTE FDI UNDER THE NEW DEA NOTIFICATION?
OVERSEAS COMPANIES WITH CHINESE OR HONG KONG SHAREHOLDING OF UP TO 10 PER CENT CAN NOW INVEST IN INDIA UNDER THE AUTOMATIC ROUTE, PROVIDED THE INVESTMENT REMAINS NON-CONTROLLING. THIS IS BASED ON THE PREVENTION OF MONEY-LAUNDERING (MAINTENANCE OF RECORDS) RULES, 2005, WHICH DEFINES CONTROLLING OWNERSHIP INTEREST AS MORE THAN 10 PER CENT OF SHARES, CAPITAL, OR PROFITS.
DOES THIS MEAN CHINESE COMPANIES CAN NOW INVEST DIRECTLY IN INDIA WITHOUT GOVERNMENT APPROVAL?
NO. ENTITIES REGISTERED IN CHINA, HONG KONG, OR OTHER LAND-BORDER COUNTRIES STILL REQUIRE MANDATORY GOVERNMENT APPROVAL. THE 10 PER CENT RELAXATION APPLIES ONLY TO OVERSEAS COMPANIES—SUCH AS GLOBAL FUNDS OR MULTINATIONALS—THAT HAVE MINORITY, NON-CONTROLLING CHINESE SHAREHOLDING.
WHAT IS THE TOTAL FDI FROM CHINA INTO INDIA SINCE 2000?
ACCORDING TO DPIIT DATA, CHINA RANKS 23RD AMONG FDI SOURCE COUNTRIES WITH ONLY 0.32 PER CENT OF TOTAL EQUITY INFLOWS—APPROXIMATELY USD 2.51 BILLION BETWEEN APRIL 2000 AND DECEMBER 2025. INDIA'S TOTAL CUMULATIVE FDI EQUITY INFLOWS DURING THIS PERIOD WERE RS 51,85,639 CRORE (USD 776.75 BILLION).
WHICH SECTORS QUALIFY FOR THE 60-DAY FAST-TRACK APPROVAL?
THE GOVERNMENT HAS INTRODUCED A 60-DAY TIME-BOUND APPROVAL MECHANISM FOR FIVE MANUFACTURING SECTORS: CAPITAL GOODS, ELECTRONIC CAPITAL GOODS, ELECTRONIC COMPONENTS, POLYSILICON, AND INGOT-WAFER. THIS IS A REDUCTION FROM THE PREVIOUS 8-12 WEEK AVERAGE PROCESSING TIME.
WHAT HAPPENED TO PRESS NOTE 3 OF 2020?
PRESS NOTE 3, ISSUED ON APRIL 17, 2020, MANDATED GOVERNMENT APPROVAL FOR ALL FDI FROM LAND-BORDER COUNTRIES. IT WAS INTRODUCED TO PREVENT OPPORTUNISTIC TAKEOVERS DURING THE COVID-19 PANDEMIC. THE MARCH 2026 AMENDMENTS DO NOT REPEAL IT ENTIRELY BUT NARROW ITS SCOPE TO CONTROLLING OR MAJORITY INVESTMENTS, ALLOWING UP TO 10 PER CENT MINORITY STAKE UNDER THE AUTOMATIC ROUTE.
