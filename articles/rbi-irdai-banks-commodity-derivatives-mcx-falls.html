RBI, IRDAI Block Banks From Commodity Derivatives, MCX Drops 3.4%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/SEBI Chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey says RBI and IRDAI are not inclined to allow banks and insurers to invest in commodity derivatives
  • 02/MCX shares fall 3.4% after the announcement; the stock had rallied on hopes of broader institutional participation
  • 03/SEBI had in September 2025 sought to enable banks and pension funds to trade commodities; the initiative is now stalled

SEBI Chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey said on May 4 that RBI and IRDAI oppose allowing banks and insurers to trade commodity derivatives. The statement sent MCX shares down 3.4%.

Both regulators cited high volatility and risk management concerns. Commodity derivatives were also deemed a mismatch for the long-term investment needs of insurance firms.

Pandey said SEBI will halt further efforts to expand market access. "They have their rationale... we will leave it here," he told reporters on Monday.

The reversal ends a push SEBI began in September 2025. At that time, the regulator had committed to engaging the government to enable banks and pension funds in commodities.

MCX (NSE: MCX) closed at ₹2,968.30 on May 3. The stock hit a 52-week high of ₹2,985.10 after rallying on reform hopes in prior months.

The exchange had surged 62% in 2025 and 95% in 2024. Institutional participation was seen as key to deepening liquidity in India's commodity markets.

Pandey also said PFRDA is reviewing pension fund access but has not disclosed a decision. SEBI separately plans an advisory on AI risks for market intermediaries.
