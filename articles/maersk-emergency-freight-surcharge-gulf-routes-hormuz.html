Maersk Imposes $1,800-$3,800 Emergency Freight Surcharge on Gulf Routes

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Maersk introduces emergency freight surcharge on Gulf routes amid persistent security risks around the Strait of Hormuz
  • 02/Shippers will pay $1,800 per 20-ft container, $3,000 per 40-ft container, and $3,800 for reefers and special cargo
  • 03/Surcharge applies with immediate effect from April 27 and includes 14 days of in-transit storage

Maersk has introduced an emergency freight surcharge on cargo to and from the Gulf region. The move significantly raises logistics costs as security risks persist around the Strait of Hormuz.

The surcharge was announced in a trade notice issued on April 27, 2026. Industry sources confirmed it is effective immediately.

Under the new structure, shippers will pay $1,800 per 20-ft container. For 40-ft containers, the rate is $3,000. Reefers, special cargo, and dangerous goods will attract $3,800 per unit.

The fee covers shipments linked to the UAE, Saudi Arabia (Dammam and Jubail), Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain, Iraq, and parts of Oman. It applies to cargo originating from, destined for, or transiting through affected Gulf ports.

Maersk stated the surcharge addresses the need for alternative logistics arrangements. This includes rerouting cargo, temporary storage at intermediate ports, and deploying additional capacity.

The surcharge includes 14 days of storage in transit. Additional storage and reefer plug-in charges will apply beyond that period.

Singapore-based Ocean Network Express had earlier announced an Emergency Surcharge on March 4. It cited exceptional operational and security challenges in the Middle East, including the effective closure of the Hormuz corridor.

Industry sources said the surcharges are expected to raise landed costs. They will also disrupt pricing contracts and compress margins for Indian exporters and importers. Key affected sectors include petrochemicals, food products, and engineering goods.

With major shipping lines continuing to avoid the Hormuz corridor, elevated freight rates and longer transit times are expected to persist until stability returns to the critical maritime trade route.
