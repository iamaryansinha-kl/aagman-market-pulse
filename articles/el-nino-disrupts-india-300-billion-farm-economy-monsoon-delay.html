El Niño Disrupts India's $300 Billion Farm Economy as Monsoon Delays

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/India's monsoon rains are delayed by nearly two weeks, disrupting the $300 billion farm economy during peak kharif planting season in June
  • 02/A super El Niño developing over the Pacific Ocean is expected to drive the weakest monsoon in 11 years, intensifying dry conditions
  • 03/Inflation is running at its highest levels since early 2025; the Reserve Bank of India has flagged abnormal weather as a risk to economic forecasts

India's crucial monsoon rains are delayed by nearly two weeks. The late arrival is disrupting the nation's $300 billion farm economy just as the kharif planting season begins.

June is the key planting window for rainy-season crops such as rice, cotton, and millet. Farmers typically wait for the first showers before seeding their fields.

The worst dry spell has hit central India and the Deccan region. This belt accounts for about 90% of India's soybean and sugarcane, 80% of cotton, and 70% of peanuts and pulses.

Rainfall in Nashik — India's onion heartland — has been running at just 16% of the long-term average. Onion price spikes in this region have historically triggered inflation and political fallout.

A super El Niño developing over the Pacific Ocean is expected to worsen conditions. It is forecast to drive the weakest monsoon India has seen in 11 years.

The Reserve Bank of India has already flagged abnormal weather as a risk to its economic forecasts. Inflation is currently running at its highest levels since early 2025.

India banned rice exports after the 2023 monsoon delays to steady domestic markets. Similar export restrictions are now widely expected for sugar in 2026.

Rice stockpiles in government warehouses have grown by nearly half since the 2023 export ban. Wheat reserves are also ample, providing some buffer against the dry spell.

INDIA'S FARM ECONOMY IS VALUED AT APPROXIMATELY $300 BILLION. IT IS HEAVILY DEPENDENT ON THE SOUTHWESTERN MONSOON RAINS THAT ARRIVE EACH JUNE.
WHICH CROPS ARE MOST AT RISK FROM THE DELAYED MONSOON?
KHARIF CROPS INCLUDING RICE, COTTON, MILLET, SOYBEAN, SUGARCANE, PEANUTS, AND PULSES ARE AT RISK. THE CENTRAL AND DECCAN REGIONS ACCOUNT FOR 90% OF SOYBEAN AND SUGARCANE, AND 80% OF COTTON.
HOW SEVERE IS THE RAINFALL SHORTFALL IN KEY FARMING REGIONS?
RAINFALL IN NASHIK — INDIA'S ONION HEARTLAND — HAS BEEN AT JUST 16% OF THE LONG-TERM AVERAGE. THE BROADER CENTRAL AND DECCAN REGION IS FACING THE WORST DRY SPELL.
WHAT IMPACT WILL EL NIÑO HAVE ON INDIA'S MONSOON?
THE DEVELOPING SUPER EL NIÑO IS EXPECTED TO DRIVE THE WEAKEST MONSOON IN 11 YEARS. IT WILL WORSEN ALREADY PARCHED GROWING CONDITIONS ACROSS LARGE PARTS OF THE COUNTRY.
WHAT HAS THE RESERVE BANK OF INDIA SAID ABOUT THE WEATHER RISKS?
THE RBI HAS FLAGGED ABNORMAL WEATHER CONDITIONS AS A RISK TO ITS ECONOMIC FORECASTS. INFLATION IS ALREADY AT ITS HIGHEST LEVELS SINCE EARLY 2025, AND FOOD PRICE PRESSURES ARE EXPECTED TO INTENSIFY.
