US 100% Pharma Tariff September 29: What Indian Drugmakers Face

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/The US 100% ad valorem tariff on patented pharmaceuticals and their APIs now applies to all importers outside the 17 Annex III companies, effective 12:01 am EDT on September 29, 2026
  • 02/India faces the default 100% rate on covered patented products, while generics and biosimilars stay exempt under Proclamation 11020 of April 2, 2026
  • 03/Watch the Commerce generics review due by April 2027 and Trump's phased generics threat (100% in August 2028, 200% in August 2029) — no implementing proclamation exists yet

The 100% era for patented pharmaceuticals begins in the United States today. From 12:01 am EDT on September 29, 2026, every importer of patented drugs and their ingredients outside a small club of exempt companies pays a 100% ad valorem duty.

This is the second wave of Proclamation 11020, signed by President Donald Trump on April 2, 2026. The measure rests on Section 232 of the Trade Expansion Act of 1962, after a Commerce Department investigation found import reliance in patented medicines a national security threat.

The proclamation cites stark FDA figures for 2025. About 53% of patented medicines distributed in the US were made abroad, and only 15% of patented active ingredients by volume were produced domestically.

Coverage is precise. The tariff attaches to products tied to a valid, unexpired US patent — effectively drugs listed in the FDA Orange Book or Purple Book — plus their APIs and key starting materials, spanning Chapters 29 and 30 of the US tariff schedule.

The first wave hit on July 31, 2026, covering 17 large manufacturers named in Annex III, including Pfizer, Johnson & Johnson, Merck, Eli Lilly, AbbVie, Amgen, AstraZeneca, Novartis and Sanofi. Today's date captures everyone else: mid-sized makers, distributors, contract packagers and hospital purchasing groups.

Rates are tiered by route. Companies with a Commerce-approved onshoring plan plus a most-favoured-nation (MFN) pricing agreement pay 0%; the UK pays 0%; the EU, Japan, South Korea, Switzerland and Liechtenstein pay 15%; onshoring-plan-only companies pay 20%, rising to 100% on April 2, 2030. Everyone else pays 100%.

India has no company-specific agreement and no treaty rate. Indian exporters of US-patented products and covered ingredients therefore face the full 100% default from today.

That matters because India is the world's largest producer of generics by volume and a primary global supplier of APIs. The pain is concentrated in a narrow slice: Indian companies shipping patented, Orange Book-listed molecules or covered key starting materials into the US.

Timing mechanics are unforgiving. There is no in-transit exception — the entry date into the US decides, not the ship date — and CBP implementing guidance CSMS #69395344, issued July 30, 2026, sets the reporting rules. Importers outside Annex III have filed Chapter 99 provisions since July 31; from today, duty falls due on the same entries.

Landed cost on an unlisted patented product can double overnight. For a branded medicine not made in the US and not originating in the five treaty economies, the duty jumps from the ordinary rate to 100% of customs value.

The relief is structural: generics and biosimilars are excluded from the regime for now. The proclamation directs Commerce to report within one year — by roughly April 2027 — on whether generics should be brought in.

That exclusion is the shield for India's generics machine, which dominates Indian pharma exports to the US. Companies such as Dr Reddy's Laboratories, Cipla and Lupin, whose US revenue is overwhelmingly generics and biosimilars, sit outside today's tariff.

Sun Pharmaceutical Industries (NSE: SUNPHARMA) and Aurobindo Pharma (NSE: AUROPHARMA) are the names in focus. Economic Times reporting indicates select Indian specialty drugs and ingredients have been exempted — relief that depends on a formal Commerce determination.

Several zero-rate specialty categories exist under the proclamation: orphan-designated drugs, nuclear medicines, plasma-derived therapies, fertility treatments, cell and gene therapies, and antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs). Qualifying requires a Commerce determination published in the Federal Register.

Indian companies with exposure span two groups. Those with US-patented products or covered API exports to the US face the 100% rate directly from today, unless a specialty determination or company agreement applies.

The second group is indirectly exposed through customer economics. US buyers of Indian-made patented ingredients now carry doubled landed costs, which will pressure volumes, renegotiations and sourcing shifts toward treaty-origin supply chains.

The legal footing is durable. The Supreme Court's February 20, 2026 ruling in Learning Resources v. Trump struck down IEEPA-based reciprocal tariffs but did not touch Section 232. These duties are permanent until affirmatively removed, not contingent on emergency powers.

There is also a separate levy in the background. Section 301 forced-labour duties took effect July 24, 2026 and can reach generics finished from Chinese or Indian ingredients — a distinct channel from today's Section 232 change.

The bigger risk sits ahead. On July 21, 2026, Trump said imported generics would stay duty-free for two years from August 1, then face 100% in August 2028 and 200% in August 2029.

No implementing proclamation, executive order or Commerce rule has yet formalised that generics phased plan. Today's exclusion could narrow fast if the April 2027 review turns hostile.

The company-specific track shows where policy is heading. By August 31, 2026, 26 manufacturers had entered MFN pricing agreements covering about 89% of the US branded market, with nine August deals committing close to $20 billion in US manufacturing.

For Indian pharma investors, the near-term read is two-sided. Today's 100% wave is largely avoidable for generics-heavy Indian exporters, but any patented-product exposure, specialty-category qualification, or long-dated generics dependence carries a live policy clock.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT TARIFF TAKES EFFECT ON SEPTEMBER 29, 2026?
THE 100% AD VALOREM SECTION 232 DUTY UNDER PROCLAMATION 11020 OF APRIL 2, 2026 NOW APPLIES TO ALL IMPORTERS OF US-PATENTED PHARMACEUTICALS AND THEIR APIS OUTSIDE THE 17 ANNEX III COMPANIES, EFFECTIVE 12:01 AM EDT ON SEPTEMBER 29, 2026.
ARE INDIAN GENERIC DRUGS SUBJECT TO THE 100% TARIFF?
NO. GENERICS AND BIOSIMILARS ARE EXCLUDED FROM PROCLAMATION 11020 FOR NOW. COMMERCE MUST REPORT BY ROUGHLY APRIL 2027 ON WHETHER GENERICS SHOULD BE BROUGHT IN, AND A PHASED PLAN (100% IN AUGUST 2028, 200% IN AUGUST 2029) HAS BEEN ANNOUNCED BUT NOT YET IMPLEMENTED.
WHICH INDIAN COMPANIES ARE MOST EXPOSED?
INDIAN COMPANIES SHIPPING US-PATENTED, ORANGE BOOK-LISTED PRODUCTS OR COVERED APIS FACE THE FULL 100% RATE. SUN PHARMA (NSE: SUNPHARMA) AND AUROBINDO PHARMA (NSE: AUROPHARMA) ARE IN FOCUS, WITH SELECT SPECIALTY DRUGS AND INGREDIENTS REPORTEDLY EXEMPT VIA COMMERCE DETERMINATION.
WHAT ARE THE ZERO-RATE SPECIALTY CATEGORIES?
ORPHAN-DESIGNATED DRUGS, NUCLEAR MEDICINES, PLASMA-DERIVED THERAPIES, FERTILITY TREATMENTS, CELL AND GENE THERAPIES, AND ANTIBODY-DRUG CONJUGATES CAN QUALIFY FOR A 0% RATE, BUT ONLY THROUGH A COMMERCE DETERMINATION PUBLISHED IN THE FEDERAL REGISTER.
WHAT DECIDES WHETHER A SHIPMENT PAYS THE DUTY?
THE US ENTRY DATE, NOT THE SHIP DATE. CBP GUIDANCE CSMS #69395344 OF JULY 30, 2026 SETS THE RULES: GOODS ENTERED FOR CONSUMPTION ON OR AFTER 12:01 AM EDT ON SEPTEMBER 29, 2026 PAY THE DUTY, WITH THE LOWEST APPLICABLE RATE GOVERNING WHERE MORE THAN ONE RATE COULD APPLY.
