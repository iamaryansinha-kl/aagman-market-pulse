India Cotton Import Duty: Govt Weighs 6-7% Rate or Zero Duty in Fall

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Union agriculture minister Shivraj Singh Chouhan proposes a middle path on cotton import duty amid textile industry demand for zero duty imports till December 2026
  • 02/Current cotton stock at 47 lakh bales with CCI can meet domestic demand till August; production dipped to 290.91 lakh bales in 2025-26
  • 03/Two options under review: zero duty imports in September-October or reducing import duty to 6-7% from current 11%; sowing is already underway across major states

The Indian government is unlikely to fully scrap the 11% import duty on cotton. Union agriculture minister Shivraj Singh Chouhan has proposed a middle path to balance textile industry demands with farmer concerns.

The Textile Ministry had sought zero duty imports till December 2026. The Agriculture Ministry opposed this, warning it would send the wrong signal to farmers as sowing has already begun across states.

Two compromise options are now on the table. The first is allowing zero duty imports in September-October when the new harvest arrives. The second is reducing the duty to 6-7% from the current 11%.

An estimated 47 lakh bales of cotton are currently held by the Cotton Corporation of India. Combined with private sector stocks, domestic demand can be met till August without any additional imports.

India's cotton production fell to 290.91 lakh bales in 2025-26 from 297.24 lakh bales in 2024-25. Acreage has stagnated at 114.8 lakh hectares after dropping sharply from 129.27 lakh hectares in 2022-23.

The Cotton Association of India estimates 2025-26 season production at 324 lakh bales. Consumption is pegged at 315 lakh bales, with imports at 47 lakh bales and exports at 15 lakh bales.

Textile and garment exports fell 2.2% to $35.8 billion in 2025-26. Rising yarn and fabric costs have eroded competitiveness, prompting the industry to seek duty relief amid the ongoing West Asia crisis.
