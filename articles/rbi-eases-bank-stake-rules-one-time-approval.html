RBI Eases Bank Stake Rules: One-Time Approval Up to 10%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/RBI on Thursday finalised rules letting eligible mutual funds, insurers and pension funds seek one-time approval for subsequent bank stake acquisitions up to 10%
  • 02/The change replaces a regime where investors whose holdings dipped below 5% needed fresh RBI approval before crossing the threshold again
  • 03/Initial acquisitions of major shareholding still require prior RBI approval, and investors must report any move across the 5% threshold within three working days

The Reserve Bank of India on Thursday finalised its July proposal to simplify bank shareholding rules. Eligible institutional investors can now seek a one-time approval for subsequent acquisitions of major shareholding in the same bank.

The move covers mutual funds registered with Sebi, pension funds registered with PFRDA and insurance companies registered with IRDAI. These entities can obtain a single clearance for building stakes of up to 10 per cent.

The 10 per cent ceiling applies to paid-up share capital or voting rights of a banking company. RBI may grant the one-time approval individually or collectively for such subsequent acquisitions.

Under the earlier framework, the process was far more repetitive. An investor whose holding fell below 5 per cent after initially acquiring a major shareholding had to seek fresh RBI approval before crossing the threshold again.

The amended rules replace this repeated approval requirement with a one-time approval mechanism for eligible institutional investors. This cuts down the regulatory burden each time such an investor rebuilds a position in the same lender.

Importantly, prior approval continues to be mandatory for the initial acquisition of a major stake. The one-time mechanism applies only to subsequent acquisitions after that first clearance.

The amendments are effective immediately. They apply to commercial banks, small finance banks, payments banks and local area banks.

Applications must be made through RBI's PRAVAAH portal. The concerned bank is required to furnish its comments to the central bank as part of the process.

The approval is not unconditional. RBI can revoke it for non-compliance with conditions, or if the qualifying investor or any associated person is subsequently found not to be "fit and proper".

Investors operating under the one-time approval carry an ongoing reporting obligation. They must report to RBI and the concerned bank within three working days whenever their aggregate holding falls below or rises above the 5 per cent threshold.

The final rules follow a consultation process. RBI released draft amendments on July 14 and sought comments until August 4, with the feedback examined and suitable modifications incorporated.

The framework also clarifies an indirect acquisition point involving portfolio managers. A client's acquisition need not be treated as an indirect acquisition by its portfolio manager if three conditions are met.

First, the client must remain the registered owner of the shares and voting rights. Second, the manager must provide only non-binding advice. Third, any voting must be based on a specific mandate from the client.

The stated aim is to reduce repeated regulatory approvals for eligible institutional investors. At the same time, RBI retains oversight over major bank shareholdings through the initial approval gate and the revocation power.

For institutional investors, the practical effect is fewer trips to the regulator. Once the one-time approval is in place, adding to a position in the same bank within the 10 per cent limit no longer triggers a fresh approval cycle each time.

The reporting duty acts as the ongoing check. Because holdings must be flagged within three working days of crossing the 5 per cent line in either direction, RBI and the bank retain real-time visibility even without repeated prior approvals.

The revocation clause adds teeth to the framework. An approval is not a permanent pass, and it can be withdrawn if conditions are violated or if the investor or any associated person fails the fit-and-proper test later.

The change follows a standard RBI consultation cycle, from the July 14 draft to the August 4 comment deadline. The central bank said feedback received was examined and suitable modifications were incorporated before finalisation.

For now, the rules take immediate effect. Eligible institutional investors looking to build or rebuild major positions in banks can begin applying through the PRAVAAH route with the one-time approval option.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT HAS THE RBI ANNOUNCED ON BANK SHAREHOLDING RULES?
THE RBI ON THURSDAY FINALISED RULES ALLOWING ELIGIBLE MUTUAL FUNDS, INSURANCE COMPANIES AND PENSION FUNDS TO SEEK ONE-TIME APPROVAL FOR SUBSEQUENT ACQUISITIONS OF MAJOR SHAREHOLDING OF UP TO 10 PER CENT IN THE SAME BANK.
WHICH INVESTORS ARE ELIGIBLE FOR THE ONE-TIME APPROVAL?
MUTUAL FUNDS REGISTERED WITH SEBI, PENSION FUNDS REGISTERED WITH PFRDA AND INSURANCE COMPANIES REGISTERED WITH IRDAI ARE ELIGIBLE FOR THE ONE-TIME APPROVAL MECHANISM.
DOES THE 10 PER CENT CAP APPLY TO INITIAL ACQUISITIONS OF A MAJOR STAKE?
NO. PRIOR APPROVAL CONTINUES TO BE MANDATORY FOR THE INITIAL ACQUISITION OF A MAJOR SHAREHOLDING. THE ONE-TIME APPROVAL COVERS ONLY SUBSEQUENT ACQUISITIONS UP TO 10 PER CENT OF PAID-UP SHARE CAPITAL OR VOTING RIGHTS.
WHICH TYPES OF BANKS DO THE AMENDED RULES APPLY TO?
THE AMENDMENTS, WHICH ARE EFFECTIVE IMMEDIATELY, APPLY TO COMMERCIAL BANKS, SMALL FINANCE BANKS, PAYMENTS BANKS AND LOCAL AREA BANKS.
WHAT REPORTING OBLIGATIONS DO INVESTORS HAVE UNDER THE ONE-TIME APPROVAL?
INVESTORS MUST REPORT TO RBI AND THE CONCERNED BANK WITHIN THREE WORKING DAYS WHENEVER THEIR AGGREGATE HOLDING FALLS BELOW OR RISES ABOVE THE 5 PER CENT THRESHOLD. RBI CAN ALSO REVOKE THE APPROVAL FOR NON-COMPLIANCE OR IF THE INVESTOR IS FOUND NOT "FIT AND PROPER".
