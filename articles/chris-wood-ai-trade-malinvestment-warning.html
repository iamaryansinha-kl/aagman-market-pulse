Chris Wood Warns AI Boom Will End on Malinvestment, Not Chip Glut

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Chris Wood says AI boom will end when investors realize hyperscalers cannot earn adequate returns on massive AI capex, not from chip oversupply
  • 02/TSMC has lifted 2026 capex guidance to $56 billion from $41 billion last year, with AI demand now accounting for 31% of TSMC revenues
  • 03/Wood is repositioning towards memory and hardware names, adding SK Hynix and Kioxia while removing Alphabet and Alibaba from portfolios

Jefferies strategist Chris Wood has warned the AI trade will end not from a chip glut, but from investor realisation that hyperscalers cannot earn adequate returns on massive capex. He calls "malinvestment" the most likely trigger for the boom's end.

TSMC has lifted its 2026 capex guidance to $56 billion from $41 billion last year. Jefferies' Fubon Research now projects $65-70 billion for 2027. AI-related demand accounts for 31% of TSMC's 2026 revenues.

This surge is driving boom-like conditions in Taiwan. Real GDP growth hit 14.55% year-on-year in 1Q26, while export orders surged 53.4% year-on-year through May.

"Memory has evolved from a peripheral component into the core engine driving productivity in the AI era," said Micron CEO Sanjay Mehrotra. Micron has signed 16 strategic agreements covering roughly 20% of its DRAM volume.

A key risk is the commoditisation of large language models. Wood highlights Z.ai's GLM-5.2 as "almost equal to Anthropic" for corporate use at one quarter of the cost per token. On OpenRouter, top Chinese models processed 21.37 trillion tokens versus 5.76 trillion for US models.

Wood points to circular funding structures like Nvidia financing OpenAI so OpenAI can buy more Nvidia chips. This feedback loop could unwind sharply once doubts over long-term returns take hold.

Despite structural concerns, Wood says there is "zero sign of AI capex slowing." He is adding SK Hynix and Kioxia with 4% weightings while removing Alphabet and Alibaba from portfolios.
