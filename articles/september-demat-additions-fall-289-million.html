September Demat Additions Fall 11.5% to 2.89 Million Amid Selloff

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Demat account additions fell 11.5% to 2.89 million in September 2026, down from 3.27 million in August
  • 02/Sensex and Nifty each fell 6% in September, their worst monthly drop since March, cooling retail participation
  • 03/Despite the monthly dip, Q2 FY27 saw a record 9.04 million additions, powered by a bumper IPO season

Demat account openings in India slowed sharply in September 2026. New additions fell 11.5% to 2.89 million, from 3.27 million in August, according to NSDL and CDSL data.

The September number exactly matched July's 2.89 million. It was the sharpest monthly decline in six months.

The reason is straightforward: markets fell hard. The Sensex and Nifty each dropped 6% in September, their worst monthly showing since March, when both indices tumbled 11%.

Broader indices fared worse. The BSE MidCap 150 fell 6.6% and the BSE SmallCap 250 declined 3.2%, also their worst monthly falls since March.

Retail investors tend to open demat accounts when markets are climbing. A 6% single-month correction tends to pause that momentum, and September proved no exception.

Yet the monthly dip hides a bigger trend. In the September quarter of FY27, a record 9.04 million demat accounts were added.

That is the highest quarterly addition in seven quarters. It compares with 7.03 million in April-June 2026 and 7.92 million in the same quarter a year earlier.

The last time quarterly additions ran higher was October-December 2024, when 9.88 million accounts were opened.

The contrast with March is instructive. During the March selloff, both the Sensex and Nifty tumbled 11%, and retail account openings slowed visibly in the following weeks.

September's 6% fall was half that magnitude. The slowdown in openings was proportionately milder, which suggests the underlying appetite for equities remains intact.

Total demat accounts crossed 240.59 million at the end of September. That is up 3.9% quarter-on-quarter and 16.2% from a year ago.

One caveat matters here. Demat accounts are not the same as unique investors, since a single person can hold multiple accounts across depositories.

The unique investor base stood at 135 million as of August 2026, according to NSE data. That is the cleaner gauge of how many individuals actually participate in equities.

Account growth has been one of the defining stories of Indian markets over the past four years. Total demat accounts have roughly doubled since 2022, fuelled by cheap UPI-based investing, zero-brokerage platforms, and a wave of first-time savers.

That structural base is why market watchers are not alarmed by one weak month. The bigger question is whether the quarterly record survives a prolonged correction.

The September quarter also delivered the busiest IPO market on record. 69 IPOs raised ₹90,462 crore, the highest quarterly fundraising figure ever.

Listing gains kept retail interest alive. The average listing gain was around 20% per issue, compared with roughly 2% in January-June 2026.

The SME segment added to the pull. 92 SME companies raised ₹4,132 crore through IPOs, with average listing gains of about 19% versus 7% in the first half of 2026.

Applications for IPO allotments require a demat account. That mechanic alone funnels thousands of first-time investors into depositories every busy listing week.

Analysts say this IPO wave explains why account openings stayed strong despite the monthly selloff. "The recent IPO season has accelerated the pace of demat account openings," said Kranthi Bathini, equity strategist at WealthMills Securities.

Bathini expects the momentum to continue. "The Jio brand could provide an additional boost to new demat account openings," he said, referring to the expected push by new-age broking platforms into small-town India.

Others caution against reading too much into one soft month. "Demat account additions may have shown a slight dip in September because of short-term market fluctuations, but overall data shows continued increase despite market volatility," said Ravi Singh, chief research officer at Master Capital Services.

Singh's point is backed by the arithmetic. Even after the September slowdown, quarterly additions rose 28.6% sequentially, from 7.03 million to 9.04 million.

The quarter itself was not kind to indices. The Sensex and Nifty each fell 5.2% in Q2 FY27, while the BSE MidCap dropped 3% and the BSE SmallCap slipped 0.5%.

So the picture is mixed but not weak. Monthly additions cracked under a 6% selloff, yet the quarter still set a seven-quarter record.

What happens next depends on two factors. If markets stabilise in October, monthly additions could move back above 3 million, as they were in August.

The second factor is the primary market. A busy IPO calendar keeps pulling first-time investors into the system even when secondary markets are falling.

The risks are real, though. If the current correction deepens into a bear phase, history suggests monthly additions could fall below 2 million, as they did during the 2024 mid-cap rout.

For brokerages and depositories, the quarterly trend matters more than one soft month. A record 9.04 million additions in a falling quarter suggests retail participation is structural, not just momentum-driven.

Investors watching the data should track October's monthly numbers, due in early November. That print will show whether September was a blip or the start of a slower trend.

Depositories earn fees on every account, so the growth race between NSDL and CDSL has also intensified. CDSL has historically led retail additions, while NSDL dominates institutional holdings.

Market participants also note that September is seasonally a slower month for new accounts. The monsoon quarter typically sees lighter account openings, and this year's market slide compounded that seasonal effect.

Either way, the direction of travel is clear. India's equity investor base is expanding, and even a 6% monthly selloff has not been able to reverse it.

For now, the market correction has dented the pace, not the trend. That distinction will matter if volatility persists into the December quarter.

GLOSSARY: Demat account: An electronic account that holds shares, bonds, and other securities in digital form, removing the need for physical share certificates. NSDL: National Securities Depository Ltd, one of India's two central depositories, which maintains demat accounts and settles trades in electronic form. CDSL: Central Depository Services Ltd, the other Indian depository, which holds securities in dematerialised form for investors. IPO (Initial Public Offering): The process by which a company sells its shares to the public for the first time and lists on a stock exchange. Listing gains: The profit an investor makes when shares allotted in an IPO trade above the issue price on the first day of trading. SME IPO: An initial public offering by a small or medium enterprise on a dedicated exchange platform, with lower listing thresholds than the main board. Unique investors: The count of distinct individuals participating in the stock market, which is lower than the total demat account count because one person can hold multiple accounts.
