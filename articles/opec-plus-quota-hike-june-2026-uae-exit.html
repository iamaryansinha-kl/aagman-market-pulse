OPEC+ Agrees 188K B/D Quota Hike in First Meeting Since UAE Exits Cartel

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/OPEC+ will add 188,000 barrels per day in June, led by Saudi Arabia and Russia, at a virtual meeting on May 3
  • 02/UAE exited OPEC and OPEC+ on May 1 after 59 years, citing national interest and freeing itself from quota constraints
  • 03/The hike is largely symbolic as Strait of Hormuz remains blocked; actual output restoration depends on war resolution

Major OPEC+ nations agreed a modest 188,000 barrels per day production increase for June. Seven countries led by Saudi Arabia and Russia finalized the deal at a video conference on Sunday, May 3.

The hike was largely expected by delegates before the meeting. It comes just two days after the UAE formally exited OPEC and OPEC+ on May 1.

The UAE's departure ended nearly 60 years of membership. Abu Dhabi first joined OPEC in 1967, four years before the UAE federation was formed.

At the same moment, the UAE's flagship oil company Adnoc announced plans to accelerate growth. It will award 200 billion dirham ($55 billion) in upstream and downstream projects.

"OPEC+ is playing it cool," said Jorge Leon, head of geopolitical analysis at Rystad Energy. "By sticking to the same production path—just minus the UAE—it's acting as if nothing has happened."

The increase is symbolic for now. Middle East producers cannot implement it unless the Strait of Hormuz reopens. The waterway is blocked by the ongoing US-Israeli conflict with Iran.

The UAE was OPEC's third-largest producer, pumping about 3.4 million bpd before the war. Its output fell to 1.9 million bpd in March as Hormuz traffic collapsed.

With no quotas to bind it, the UAE can ramp to its 5 million bpd capacity once exports resume. That prospect raises the risk of future price wars within the Gulf.

Brent crude for June delivery was trading near $110.40 per barrel after the UAE exit announcement. West Texas Intermediate had crossed $100.

OPEC+ will hold its next meeting on June 7. The alliance must now manage output without one of its most influential members.

THE UAE ANNOUNCED ITS EXIT ON APRIL 28, 2026, AND FORMALLY DEPARTED BOTH OPEC AND OPEC+ ON MAY 1, 2026, ENDING NEARLY 60 YEARS OF MEMBERSHIP.
WHY DID THE UAE EXIT OPEC?
THE UAE CITED NATIONAL INTEREST AND A COMPREHENSIVE REVIEW OF ITS PRODUCTION POLICY. IT HAD LONG CHAFED UNDER QUOTAS THAT DID NOT REFLECT ITS EXPANDED CAPACITY OF NEARLY 5 MILLION BARRELS PER DAY.
WHAT IS ADNOC PLANNING AFTER THE EXIT?
ADNOC PLANS TO AWARD 200 BILLION DIRHAM ($55 BILLION) IN PROJECTS ACROSS UPSTREAM AND DOWNSTREAM OPERATIONS. THIS IS PART OF A LARGER PROGRAMME TO RAMP PRODUCTION TO 5 MILLION BPD BY 2027.
WHY IS THE QUOTA HIKE CONSIDERED SYMBOLIC?
THE ACTUAL RESTORATION OF THOSE BARRELS DEPENDS ON THE STRAIT OF HORMUZ BEING REOPENED. THE WATERWAY IS CURRENTLY BLOCKED BY THE ONGOING US-ISRAELI CONFLICT WITH IRAN, PREVENTING MOST PERSIAN GULF EXPORTS FROM REACHING GLOBAL MARKETS.
