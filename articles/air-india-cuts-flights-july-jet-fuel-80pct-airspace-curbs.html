Air India Cuts 100 Daily Flights Till July as Jet Fuel Soars 80%, Airspace Curbs Bite

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Air India will cut roughly 100 daily flights (9% of its 1,100-flight network) through July 2026 as jet fuel prices hit $179.46 per barrel, up 80% since late February.
  • 02/The Tata Group-owned flag carrier faces over ₹22,000 crore in FY2026 losses while West Asia airspace closures force longer routes on Europe, North America, and Australia flights.
  • 03/Watch for Strait of Hormuz reopening and whether India's 25% monthly ATF price cap extends to international airlines or if peers like IndiGo follow with broader cuts.

Air India will slash roughly 100 flights per day across its domestic and international network through July 2026, CEO Campbell Wilson told staff on May 2. The cuts represent nearly 9% of the airline's 1,100 daily departures.

Wilson blamed a "massive rise in jet fuel prices" combined with West Asia airspace closures that have made long-haul international routes unprofitable. The airline already trimmed April and May schedules.

Aviation turbine fuel (ATF) prices have surged 80% globally, climbing from $99.40 per barrel at end-February to $179.46 per barrel for the week ended April 24, 2026. Brent crude crossed $125 per barrel, its highest since 2022.

In India, international airline ATF prices in Delhi jumped 5.33% to USD 1,511.86 per kilolitre in the latest monthly revision. Domestic carriers saw prices capped at a 25% monthly increase to Rs 104,927.18 per kilolitre.

Airspace restrictions over Pakistan and broader West Asia have forced Air India to fly longer routes to Europe, North America, and Australia. Each detour burns extra fuel on already fuel-intensive long-haul sectors.

Air India Group is estimated to have incurred over ₹22,000 crore in losses for the financial year ended March 31, 2026. The airline is restructuring under Tata Sons after years of government-era financial stress.

Wilson, who announced plans to step down later this year, said the airline has "no choice" but to trim June and July schedules further. He hopes the Middle East conflict settles and the Strait of Hormuz reopens soon.

The Federation of Indian Airlines (FIA), which represents Air India, IndiGo, and SpiceJet, warned the Ministry of Civil Aviation that continued fuel inflation could force broader capacity reductions across India's aviation sector.
