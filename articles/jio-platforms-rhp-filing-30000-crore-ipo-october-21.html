Jio Set to File RHP Today for Record Rs 30,000 Crore IPO

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Jio Platforms is set to file its red herring prospectus with Sebi on Wednesday for an IPO of about Rs 30,000 crore.
  • •- The issue is likely to open for subscription from October 21 to 23, with listing targeted for October 28.
  • •- The IPO will be a 100% fresh issue with no offer-for-sale; marquee investors Meta, Google, PIF, KKR and others are not exiting.
  • •- The expected price band of Rs 1,065-1,119 implies a valuation of up to Rs 10.3 lakh crore.
  • •- Grey market premium has strengthened to about Rs 218, or 19%, implying a price of roughly Rs 1,337 per share.
  • •- Reliance Industries is likely to retain about 66.4% of Jio Platforms after the listing.

Jio Platforms Ltd is set to file its red herring prospectus with markets regulator Sebi on Wednesday for an initial public offering that could raise about Rs 30,000 crore, according to people familiar with the matter, formally launching the largest share sale in Indian capital markets history.

The offering by billionaire Mukesh Ambani-led Reliance Industries' telecom and digital services arm is expected to surpass Hyundai Motor India's Rs 27,800 crore IPO of October 2024. It would also mark Reliance's first major public offering in three decades and give investors direct access to one of the country's largest technology and consumer platforms.

The structure of the deal tells its own story. The IPO will comprise only a fresh issue, with no offer-for-sale component, meaning none of Jio's existing marquee investors — Meta Platforms, Google, Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, KKR, Vista Equity Partners, Silver Lake, Mubadala, General Atlantic, Abu Dhabi Investment Authority and TPG — are looking to exit. Some existing backers are instead seeking to increase their exposure through the offering.

That absence of an OFS is significant. It positions the IPO as a capital-raising and value-discovery exercise rather than an exit event, a signal that early investors who bought in during the 2020 fundraising rounds see value beyond even the mooted price band. Meta affiliate Jaadhu Holdings, the largest external investor with a 9.98 per cent stake, and Google International with 7.73 per cent, are staying put.

On valuation, market estimates put the sought-after market capitalisation at as much as Rs 10.3 lakh crore at a likely price band of Rs 1,065-1,119 per share; at the upper end the issue would raise about Rs 30,200 crore, with final details confirmed only after the RHP is filed. Nomura's analysis of the reported band puts Jio's equity valuation at Rs 9.8 lakh crore to Rs 10.3 lakh crore, with an implied enterprise value of about Rs 10.9 lakh crore to Rs 11.4 lakh crore once net debt, spectrum and lease liabilities are added and IPO proceeds adjusted.

At the upper end, Nomura estimates Jio would trade at about 10.9 times estimated FY28 enterprise value to EBITDA, while Motilal Oswal's analysis based on a potential Rs 11 lakh crore market capitalisation values the company at around 12 times. Both would place Jio at a premium to Bharti Airtel's India telecom business, which Nomura estimates at about 9.2 times FY28 EV/EBITDA — a valuation gap the market will debate fiercely through the subscription window.

Investor appetite is already visible. The IPO has drawn more than $20 billion in bids from prospective investors including Fidelity, Allianz and Capital Group, according to reports. In the grey market, Jio shares were trading at a premium of about Rs 218, or 19 per cent, implying a price of roughly Rs 1,337 per share against the presumed upper band of Rs 1,119.

The calendar is now concrete: the anchor book may open on October 19, public subscription from October 21 to 23, and listing targeted for October 28. Akash Ambani and Isha Ambani have completed international roadshows across the Americas, Europe and Southeast Asia, meeting investors personally, while domestic roadshows have drawn strong appetite from major mutual funds.

The scale underpinning the pitch is formidable. A Religare research report cited FY26 revenue from operations of Rs 1,46,885 crore, EBITDA of Rs 76,255 crore at a 51.9 per cent margin, net profit of Rs 30,049 crore and an active subscriber base of more than 524 million. The next phase of growth is expected to come from average revenue per user expansion, 5G premiumisation, higher data consumption, enterprise cloud and artificial intelligence services, even as moderating network capital expenditure improves cash-flow conversion.

For Reliance Industries, the listing is a structural event. The parent is likely to retain about 66.4 per cent of Jio Platforms, and Nomura estimates its stake could be worth Rs 481-506 per Reliance share, or Rs 385-405 after a 20 per cent holding company discount — value the brokerage believes Reliance's sharp pre-IPO correction has already largely priced in. Analysts at both Nomura and Motilal Oswal argue the listing could compress the holding company discount that has long weighed on Reliance's sum-of-the-parts valuation.

And for the telecom sector, the pricing debate moves to centre stage. Motilal Oswal expects a smartphone tariff hike of about 15 per cent in December 2026, arguing the case for higher tariffs will strengthen once Jio is listed and Vodafone Idea completes its proposed fundraise. If that plays out, the IPO will not just raise a record sum — it will reset the industry's economics.

For the primary market itself, the listing arrives at a fortuitous moment. Sebi's decision this week to extend easier IPO rules through March 2027 gives the Jio issue maximum procedural flexibility, and the exchange infrastructure — tested by the NSE listing last month — has already proven it can absorb record-scale books. The subscription numbers from October 21 will be the clearest live reading on Indian retail investor appetite in years.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHEN WILL THE JIO IPO OPEN?
The issue is likely to open for public subscription from October 21 to 23, with listing targeted for October 28, after the RHP is filed on Wednesday.
WHAT IS THE EXPECTED VALUATION?
Market estimates put the valuation at up to Rs 10.3 lakh crore at a price band of Rs 1,065-1,119 per share, with Nomura estimating 10.9 times FY28 EV/EBITDA at the upper end.
WILL EXISTING INVESTORS SELL?
No. The IPO is a 100% fresh issue with no offer-for-sale; investors including Meta, Google, PIF, KKR and Mubadala are not exiting, and some are seeking to increase their stakes.
HOW BIG IS JIO FINANCIALLY?
FY26 revenue from operations was Rs 1,46,885 crore with EBITDA of Rs 76,255 crore at a 51.9% margin and net profit of Rs 30,049 crore, on more than 524 million active subscribers. GLOSSARY: - Red herring prospectus (RHP): The preliminary offer document filed with Sebi detailing an IPO's terms before subscription opens. - Fresh issue: New shares sold by the company, with proceeds going to it rather than to selling shareholders. - Offer-for-sale (OFS): A component of an IPO where existing investors sell their stakes. - EV/EBITDA: Enterprise value relative to operating profit, a standard valuation multiple for telecom companies. - Grey market premium: The unofficial premium at which unlisted IPO shares trade ahead of listing.
