Sebi Calls Jane Street's Demand a Dilatory Tactic at SAT Hearing

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Sebi counsel Gaurav Joshi on Tuesday called Jane Street's demand for additional case documents a "dilatory" delaying tactic at the October 6 SAT hearing
  • 02/Sebi says it is only obliged to furnish documents it relied on for its July 3, 2025 interim order, which banned Jane Street and seized Rs 4,840 crore ($503 million) in alleged unlawful gains
  • 03/The SAT hearing continues Wednesday, October 7, and the outcome could shape how global trading firms assess regulatory risk in India's derivatives market

Sebi on Tuesday told the Securities Appellate Tribunal that Jane Street Group's demand for additional documents is a "dilatory" delaying tactic. A dilatory tactic is a legal manoeuvre used to stall proceedings rather than engage with the substance.

Sebi counsel Gaurav Joshi opened the day's arguments with a Hindi proverb. It roughly translates to "the thief reprimanding the police" — a suggestion that the firm was turning the accusations back on the regulator.

Joshi argued Jane Street should explain why its trading strategy does not amount to manipulation. Instead, he said, the firm is seeking to question Sebi's conduct.

The hearing is part of Jane Street's appeal against Sebi's July 3, 2025 interim order. That order temporarily banned the firm from trading Indian securities.

It also directed the seizure of Rs 48.4 billion in alleged unlawful gains. That is about $503 million, reported in Indian coverage as roughly Rs 4,840 crore.

Jane Street deposited the amount in an escrow account, a neutral third-party holding arrangement. The firm has not resumed trading in Indian securities since.

The case centres on alleged manipulation of the pricing of stocks in the NSE Nifty Bank Index. Those stocks form the base for heavily traded options contracts, making their pricing critical to the derivatives market.

On Monday, Jane Street pressed the tribunal for more information about the origins of the investigation. The firm sought documents showing how the probe began.

Jane Street argued that Sebi's surveillance department and the National Stock Exchange had earlier cleared it of manipulation.

Joshi countered that the NSE report was "preliminary." A preliminary report is an early-stage finding, not a final determination. He said the earlier regulator review was a "pre-investigative" finding.

He also cited a caution letter the NSE had issued to the firm. Jane Street, he said, disregarded it.

The regulator further argued it is only obliged to provide documents it actually relied on for the interim order. Furnishing more, Sebi said, could harm the ongoing probe.

Joshi said Jane Street has "succeeded in delaying" the matter for a year and a half. The interim order at the heart of the appeal is now more than fifteen months old.

Global proprietary trading firms are watching the case closely. India is one of the world's largest derivatives markets, and the outcome could shape how such firms assess regulatory risk there.

The stakes extend beyond one firm. The dispute has already kept one of the world's most active trading firms out of the Indian market for more than fifteen months.

The SAT hearing continues on Wednesday, October 7.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHY DOES SEBI SAY JANE STREET'S DOCUMENT DEMAND IS DILATORY?
SEBI COUNSEL GAURAV JOSHI ARGUED THE FIRM IS SEEKING TO QUESTION THE REGULATOR'S CONDUCT INSTEAD OF EXPLAINING WHY ITS TRADING STRATEGY DOES NOT AMOUNT TO MANIPULATION. JOSHI TOLD THE TRIBUNAL JANE STREET HAS "SUCCEEDED IN DELAYING" THE MATTER FOR A YEAR AND A HALF.
WHAT DOCUMENTS DOES SEBI SAY IT MUST PROVIDE?
SEBI ARGUES IT IS ONLY OBLIGED TO FURNISH THE DOCUMENTS IT RELIED ON FOR ITS JULY 3, 2025 INTERIM ORDER. PROVIDING ADDITIONAL MATERIAL BEYOND THAT ORDER, THE REGULATOR SAID, COULD HARM THE ONGOING PROBE.
WHAT WAS THE NSE REPORT THAT JANE STREET CITES AS CLEARANCE?
JOSHI TOLD THE TRIBUNAL THE NSE REPORT WAS "PRELIMINARY," MEANING AN EARLY-STAGE FINDING RATHER THAN A FINAL DETERMINATION. HE SAID THE EARLIER REGULATOR REVIEW WAS A "PRE-INVESTIGATIVE" FINDING AND CITED AN NSE CAUTION LETTER ISSUED TO THE FIRM.
WHAT DOES THE JULY 2025 INTERIM ORDER REQUIRE?
THE JULY 3, 2025 ORDER TEMPORARILY BANNED JANE STREET FROM TRADING INDIAN SECURITIES AND DIRECTED THE SEIZURE OF RS 48.4 BILLION — ABOUT $503 MILLION, OR ROUGHLY RS 4,840 CRORE — IN ALLEGED UNLAWFUL GAINS. THE FIRM DEPOSITED THE AMOUNT IN AN ESCROW ACCOUNT BUT HAS NOT RESUMED TRADING.
WHEN DOES THE HEARING CONTINUE?
THE SECURITIES APPELLATE TRIBUNAL HEARING CONTINUES ON WEDNESDAY, OCTOBER 7, 2026. THE OUTCOME COULD INFLUENCE HOW GLOBAL TRADING FIRMS ASSESS REGULATORY RISK IN INDIA'S DERIVATIVES MARKET. GLOSSARY: DILATORY TACTIC: A legal manoeuvre intended to delay or prolong proceedings rather than address their substance. INTERIM ORDER: A temporary regulatory directive that takes immediate effect while a full investigation or hearing proceeds. ESCROW ACCOUNT: A neutral third-party account holding funds until specified conditions are met; Jane Street's Rs 4,840 crore sits in one. SECURITIES APPELLATE TRIBUNAL (SAT): The statutory body that hears appeals against orders passed by SEBI and other market regulators. PRELIMINARY REPORT: An initial, non-final assessment that a regulator or exchange prepares before a full investigation concludes.
