CBIC Clarifies SEZ Goods Drawback: Duty Refund on Re-Exports Now Uniform

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/CBIC instructs field formations to treat SEZ-to-DTA goods as imports for Section 74 drawback on re-exports
  • 02/Previously, customs formations handled claims inconsistently — some allowed the benefit while others denied it
  • 03/Experts say the move will reduce litigation, improve cash flows, and bring uniformity across the system

The Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBIC) has issued a fresh instruction to its field formations. Goods moved from SEZ units to the domestic market after paying duties will now be treated as imported goods for drawback purposes.

The clarification applies when such goods are later re-exported from the Domestic Tariff Area (DTA). These will qualify for duty drawback under Section 74 of the Customs Act, 1962.

Section 74 allows drawback on re-export of duty-paid goods. The goods must be easily identified and previously imported into India.

A Special Economic Zone is treated as foreign territory within India for customs purposes. Movement of goods from an SEZ into the DTA is therefore construed as an import.

Previously, drawback claims were handled differently across customs formations. Some allowed the benefit while others denied it on the grounds that SEZ supplies were not imports.

"This should help bring consistency in assessments, reduce avoidable disputes, and ease cash flow issues for businesses dealing in re-export transactions," said Ikesh Nagpal, Lead-Indirect Tax at AKM Global.

"It will bring uniformity, reduce litigation, and ensure justice for exporters who have already paid applicable customs duties," said GTRI Founder Ajay Srivastava. He added that the change supports fairness and provides greater certainty to DTA buyers and SEZ suppliers.

The instruction was issued on April 28, 2026. It legally settles the position and removes ambiguity that had led to different practices across customs formations.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT HAS THE CBIC CLARIFIED REGARDING SEZ GOODS AND DUTY DRAWBACK?
THE CBIC HAS CLARIFIED THAT GOODS SUPPLIED FROM SEZ UNITS TO THE DOMESTIC TARIFF AREA AFTER PAYING APPLICABLE DUTIES WILL BE TREATED AS IMPORTED GOODS. THIS ALLOWS SUCH GOODS TO QUALIFY FOR DUTY DRAWBACK UNDER SECTION 74 OF THE CUSTOMS ACT WHEN THEY ARE LATER RE-EXPORTED.
WHAT IS SECTION 74 OF THE CUSTOMS ACT?
SECTION 74 OF THE CUSTOMS ACT ALLOWS DUTY DRAWBACK ON THE RE-EXPORT OF DUTY-PAID GOODS. THE GOODS MUST BE CAPABLE OF BEING EASILY IDENTIFIED AND MUST HAVE BEEN PREVIOUSLY IMPORTED INTO INDIA.
WHY IS THE CBIC TREATING SEZ-TO-DTA MOVEMENT AS AN IMPORT?
FOR CUSTOMS PURPOSES, A SPECIAL ECONOMIC ZONE IS TREATED AS FOREIGN TERRITORY WITHIN INDIA. THEREFORE, MOVEMENT OF GOODS FROM AN SEZ INTO THE DOMESTIC TARIFF AREA IS CONSTRUED AS AN IMPORT UNDER THE CUSTOMS ACT.
WHAT PROBLEM DID THIS CLARIFICATION SOLVE?
PREVIOUSLY, DRAWBACK CLAIMS ON RE-EXPORT OF SUCH GOODS WERE HANDLED DIFFERENTLY ACROSS CUSTOMS FORMATIONS. SOME ALLOWED THE BENEFIT WHILE OTHERS DENIED IT, CREATING UNCERTAINTY AND LITIGATION FOR BUSINESSES.
WHAT IS THE IMPACT OF THIS CLARIFICATION ON BUSINESSES?
EXPERTS SAY THE MOVE WILL BRING UNIFORMITY IN ASSESSMENTS, REDUCE AVOIDABLE DISPUTES, AND EASE CASH FLOW ISSUES. IT ENSURES JUSTICE FOR EXPORTERS WHO HAVE ALREADY PAID APPLICABLE CUSTOMS DUTIES AND PROVIDES GREATER CERTAINTY TO DTA BUYERS AND SEZ SUPPLIERS.
