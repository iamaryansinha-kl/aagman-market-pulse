Sebi to Use 30-Min VWAP for Derivative Settlement: Report

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Sebi will partly reverse its new closing auction session mechanism for determining derivatives settlement prices, sources with direct knowledge of the matter said.
  • 02/The volume-weighted average price (VWAP) of the last 30 minutes of trading is set to replace the auction for derivative settlement for at least one year.
  • 03/Sebi is expected to implement the changes by the end of this month; the plan is not final and the regulator has not officially confirmed it.

The Securities and Exchange Board of India (Sebi) plans to partly reverse its new closing auction session mechanism for determining derivatives settlement prices. The move follows sharp expiry-day swings in derivatives prices and 20,000 public suggestions on the rules, two sources with direct knowledge of the matter said.

Under the likely change, the volume-weighted average price (VWAP) of the last 30 minutes of trading would be used to determine derivative settlement prices. The closing auction would not be used for this purpose for at least a year, the sources said.

Sebi introduced the closing auction session, or CAS, last month. It applies to stocks that have futures and options contracts linked to them.

The CAS works as a short auction at the end of the trading day to determine the closing price. Similar systems operate in the US and Hong Kong markets.

The new process led to sharp swings in derivatives prices on expiry days. That market reaction prompted the regulator to review the mechanism.

In a post on X over the weekend, Sebi said it received 20,000 suggestions to tweak the rules. The feedback came in response to a consultation paper the regulator issued last month.

The sources indicated that for underlying stocks in the less liquid cash market, a closing auction will still be used. That portion of the mechanism is set to remain in place to determine end-of-day prices.

If implemented, the VWAP-based approach would align India more closely with US and European markets. In those markets, derivatives settlement is often determined using VWAPs over set trading periods rather than a single closing auction.

Sebi is expected to implement the changes by the end of this month. The timeline was described by the sources, not officially announced.

A Sebi spokesperson did not respond to a request for comment. The sources declined to be identified as they are not authorised to speak to the media.

Derivatives settlement prices matter because they determine cash flows between buyers and sellers at contract expiry. A mechanism that produces abrupt swings on expiry days can sharply raise settlement costs for one side of the trade.

The consultation paper drew 20,000 public suggestions within weeks of the mechanism's debut. That volume of feedback is what pushed the regulator toward a partial reversal rather than a full retreat.

The plan remains under review and is not final. Market participants will be watching for a formal circular from the regulator confirming any change to the settlement mechanism.
