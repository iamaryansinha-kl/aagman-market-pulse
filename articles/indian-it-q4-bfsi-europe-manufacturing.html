Indian IT Q4: BFSI Surges 28% as Manufacturing Drowns, Europe Steals the Show

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Indian IT firms closed Q4 on patchy footing, with BFSI and energy leading growth while manufacturing and telecom dragged
  • 02/Europe emerges as FY27 growth lever on sovereign AI and regulatory demand, reversing the US-led trend of FY22-FY24
  • 03/Persistent's BFSI vertical surged 28.4% in FY26; Infosys Daimler programme ramp-down highlights manufacturing headwinds

Indian IT services majors reported a mixed Q4 FY26, with growth driven by AI-led BFSI, energy, and life sciences while manufacturing, telecom, and retail dragged.

BFSI emerged as the clearest frontrunner. Persistent Systems saw its BFSI vertical surge 28.4% in FY26 on a full-year basis, hitting a $600 million annualised run rate.

"BFSI started AI-led modernisation 18 to 24 months ahead of other verticals," Sidhant Rastogi, President at Zinnov, said. "It is now converting that into scope expansion, not just cost optimisation."

Manufacturing is now a 100 to 150 basis point drag on cohort growth. Infosys's Daimler programme ramp-down is the highest-profile example of this weakness.

Europe is overtaking North America as the incremental growth pocket. Capgemini called out Europe's sovereignty-AI investments as a new demand pool. Tech Mahindra's Q4 beat was also led by European account wins.

TCS saw North America-led growth, with BFSI, energy, and tech holding up. Communications and utilities lagged behind the rest of the portfolio.

Infosys was driven by Europe and North America, with BFSI and energy strong. Manufacturing, retail, and communications remained under pressure from macro and tariff uncertainty.

Wipro's growth was led by Europe and APMEA regions. BFSI weakness in the Americas and cautious consumer and healthcare spending weighed on overall performance.

HCLTech saw traction in tech and BFSI but declines in telecom, retail, and manufacturing. The US market was steady while Europe remained soft for the company.

"Tariff uncertainty in US manufacturing is showing up in specific client programme ramp-downs," Rastogi said. "Clients in tariff-exposed sectors are deferring discretionary decisions until policy clarity emerges."

Sanchit Vir Gogia, Chief Analyst at Greyhound Research, noted that financial services and healthcare cannot delay technology investment. "Delaying investment is not an option. The systems being supported are critical and cannot be paused without consequence."

The Middle East also stands out for momentum, often driven by sovereign investment strategies and public sector programmes linked to long-term national priorities.

HCLTech CEO flagged "rising energy prices and supply chain disruptions" in Europe. He added that "the business environment remains highly fluid, making it difficult to form a definitive view of how the next 12 months will unfold."

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHICH VERTICALS DROVE GROWTH FOR INDIAN IT FIRMS IN Q4 FY26?
BFSI, ENERGY, AND LIFE SCIENCES EMERGED AS THE STRONGEST VERTICALS, DRIVEN BY AI-LED MODERNISATION AND NON-DISCRETIONARY SPENDING. PERSISTENT SYSTEMS' BFSI VERTICAL GREW 28.4% IN FY26 WITH A $600 MILLION ANNUALISED RUN RATE.
WHICH SECTORS DRAGGED PERFORMANCE IN Q4?
MANUFACTURING, TELECOM, AND RETAIL WERE THE WEAKEST VERTICALS. MANUFACTURING ALONE IS NOW A 100 TO 150 BASIS POINT DRAG ON COHORT GROWTH. INFOSYS'S DAIMLER PROGRAMME RAMP-DOWN IS THE HIGHEST-PROFILE EXAMPLE OF THIS WEAKNESS.
HOW DID REGIONAL PERFORMANCE VARY ACROSS GEOGRAPHIES?
NORTH AMERICA REMAINED BROADLY RESILIENT AND STEADY. EUROPE EMERGED AS THE NEXT GROWTH LEVER ON SOVEREIGN AI AND REGULATORY-DRIVEN DEMAND. THE MIDDLE EAST SHOWED STRONG MOMENTUM BACKED BY SOVEREIGN INVESTMENT STRATEGIES.
WHAT DID INDUSTRY ANALYSTS SAY ABOUT THE Q4 TRENDS?
SIDHANT RASTOGI, PRESIDENT AT ZINNOV, SAID BFSI BEGAN AI MODERNISATION 18 TO 24 MONTHS AHEAD OF OTHER VERTICALS AND IS NOW CONVERTING THAT INTO SCOPE EXPANSION. SANCHIT VIR GOGIA OF GREYHOUND RESEARCH NOTED THAT FINANCIAL SERVICES AND HEALTHCARE CANNOT DELAY TECHNOLOGY INVESTMENT AS IT IS TIED TO COMPLIANCE AND OPERATIONS.
WHAT IS THE OUTLOOK FOR FY27?
EUROPE IS EXPECTED TO BE THE INCREMENTAL GROWTH POCKET IN FY27, REVERSING THE US-LED TREND OF FY22 TO FY24. TARIFF UNCERTAINTY REMAINS THE PRIMARY MACRO HEADWIND, WITH CLIENTS IN EXPOSED SECTORS DEFERRING DISCRETIONARY DECISIONS UNTIL POLICY CLARITY EMERGES.
