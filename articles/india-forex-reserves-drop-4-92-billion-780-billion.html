India's Forex Reserves Fall $4.92 Billion to $780.78 Billion in Week Ended September 11

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

India's foreign exchange reserves slipped from their record high in the latest weekly data. Reserves declined by $4.92 billion to $780.78 billion in the week ended September 11, according to data released by the Reserve Bank of India on Friday.

The fall follows a surge the previous week, when reserves had jumped $44.90 billion to a record $785.71 billion in the week ended September 4.

The composition of the decline is notable. Gold reserves fell sharply by $12.77 billion during the week to $111.22 billion, accounting for the bulk of the overall drop. Foreign currency assets, the largest component of the reserves, declined by a more modest $2.37 billion to $645.8 billion.

Despite the weekly fall, the underlying trend remains strongly positive. Foreign currency assets are up $95.886 billion from end-March and $63.692 billion from a year earlier, reflecting the RBI's sustained reserve accumulation through the year as capital inflows strengthened and the central bank intervened to manage currency volatility.

The rupee has been under pressure in recent weeks, trading near 96 to the dollar as elevated crude prices widened India's import bill. In that context, the buffer matters: reserves of $780 billion cover more than 11 months of imports, among the strongest external positions among major emerging markets.

The weekly swings also reflect valuation effects. Gold prices have been volatile, and with over $111 billion of the reserves held in the metal, movements in bullion prices translate into sizeable weekly changes in the headline number.
