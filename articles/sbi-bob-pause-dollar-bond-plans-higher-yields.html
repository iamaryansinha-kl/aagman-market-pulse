SBI, BoB Pause Dollar Bond Plans as Investors Demand Higher Yields

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/State Bank of India (NSE: SBIN) and Bank of Baroda (NSE: BANKBARODA) shelved dollar bond issues as investors demanded wider spreads over US Treasuries
  • 02/HDFC Bank (NSE: HDFCBANK) raised $750M at 90 bps over US treasury; Power Finance Corp (NSE: PFC) paid 105 bps for $300M - 15 bps wider
  • 03/DFIs Nabard, Sidbi and NaBFID plan $1.5B in foreign-currency loans; NaBFID MD targets 6.5-7% pricing

State Bank of India (NSE: SBIN) and Bank of Baroda (NSE: BANKBARODA) have shelved their dollar bond issues this week. Investors demanded higher spreads over benchmark US Treasuries amid expectations of increased supply from Indian issuers.

HDFC Bank (NSE: HDFCBANK) raised $750 million through five-year bonds last week. The issue priced at 90 basis points above the five-year US treasury, the tightest spread for any private sector Indian bank.

State-run Power Finance Corp (NSE: PFC) followed on Monday, raising $300 million. It paid 105 basis points over US Treasuries, 15 bps wider than HDFC Bank despite raising less than half the amount.

"There is now a tussle between issuers and investors on what is the right price," said a banker involved in the issues. "Indian issuers are very price-conscious, and despite the RBI concessions, they do not want to overpay."

Development finance institutions are pivoting to foreign-currency loans. Nabard, Sidbi and NaBFID plan to borrow $1.5 billion through loans, Reuters reported.

NaBFID managing director Rajkiran Rai said the DFI plans to raise $500 million to $1 billion in bank loans. He expects pricing within the 6.5% to 7% range, with loans closing faster than a debut bond issue.

Banks and PSUs are also tapping the RBI's special swap window. The facility lets them sell dollars to RBI and buy back at maturity at a fixed 1.5% annual rate, removing hedging costs.

Issuers may wait for global bond markets to cool before returning. Bankers said the loan market offers more flexibility through bilateral relationship deals.

WHY HAVE SBI AND BANK OF BARODA PAUSED THEIR DOLLAR BOND PLANS?
STATE BANK OF INDIA AND BANK OF BARODA HAVE SHELVED THEIR DOLLAR BOND ISSUES BECAUSE INVESTORS ARE DEMANDING HIGHER YIELDS AND WIDER SPREADS OVER US TREASURIES. THE INCREASED SUPPLY EXPECTATIONS FROM INDIAN ISSUERS HAVE PROMPTED INVESTORS TO SEEK BETTER PRICING.
WHAT SPREADS DID RECENT INDIAN DOLLAR BOND ISSUES PRICE AT?
HDFC BANK RAISED $750 MILLION AT 90 BASIS POINTS ABOVE THE FIVE-YEAR US TREASURY, THE TIGHTEST SPREAD FOR ANY PRIVATE SECTOR BANK IN INDIA. POWER FINANCE CORP RAISED $300 MILLION AT 105 BASIS POINTS OVER US TREASURIES, WHICH IS 15 BASIS POINTS WIDER THAN HDFC BANK DESPITE THE SMALLER ISSUE SIZE.
HOW ARE DEVELOPMENT FINANCE INSTITUTIONS RESPONDING TO HIGHER BOND YIELDS?
NABARD, SIDBI AND NABFID PLAN TO BORROW A TOTAL OF $1.5 BILLION THROUGH FOREIGN-CURRENCY LOANS INSTEAD OF BONDS. THE LOAN MARKET OFFERS MORE FLEXIBILITY THROUGH BILATERAL RELATIONSHIP DEALS COMPARED TO THE PUBLIC BOND MARKET.
WHAT IS THE RBI SPECIAL SWAP ARRANGEMENT?
THE RBI OFFERS A SPECIAL SWAP WINDOW WHERE BANKS AND PUBLIC SECTOR ENTERPRISES CAN SELL DOLLARS TO THE RBI AND SIMULTANEOUSLY AGREE TO BUY BACK AT THE END OF THE LOAN TENURE AT A FIXED RATE OF 1.5% PER ANNUM, COMPOUNDED SEMI-ANNUALLY. THIS REMOVES THE NEED FOR THEM TO HEDGE THEIR FUTURE DOLLAR LIABILITIES.
WHAT ARE NABFID'S FUNDING PLANS AND EXPECTED PRICING?
NABFID MANAGING DIRECTOR RAJKIRAN RAI SAID THE DFI PLANS TO RAISE AT LEAST $500 MILLION AND UP TO $1 BILLION IN BANK LOANS. HE EXPECTS PRICING TO REMAIN WITHIN THE 6.5% TO 7% RANGE, NOTING THAT LOANS WILL HAPPEN FASTER SINCE A BOND ISSUANCE WOULD REQUIRE ROADSHOWS AND INVESTOR MEETINGS AS IT IS NABFID'S DEBUT ISSUE.
