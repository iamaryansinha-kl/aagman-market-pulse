Rupee Hits Record 95.25/USD Low; 10-Year Bond Yield Spikes To 7.6%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/The Indian rupee crashed to an all-time low of 95.2475 per US dollar on April 30, 2026, after opening 17 paise weaker at 95.01.
  • 02/The benchmark 10-year government bond yield surged past 7% to 7.6%, hardening 60 basis points from the previous close of 7%.
  • 03/The US Federal Reserve held rates unchanged at 3.5-3.75%, while Middle East tensions and surging crude oil prices kept pressure on Indian markets.

The Indian rupee crashed to an all-time low of 95.2475 per US dollar on April 30, 2026. The currency opened 17 paise weaker at 95.01 and extended losses through the session.

This marks the first time the rupee has breached the 95 mark since March 30, 2026. The domestic currency has now depreciated roughly 10% over the financial year 2025-26.

The US Federal Reserve's decision to hold interest rates unchanged at 3.5-3.75% amplified the pressure. A hawkish Fed stance limits the Reserve Bank of India's room to intervene aggressively.

"RBI remains constrained in the current scenario. A hawkish Fed adds to the RBI's policy constraints ahead, with the Middle East crisis persisting longer than expected," said Madhavi Arora, Chief Economist at Emkay Global Financial Services, and Harshal Patel, Research Associate.

Yields on Government Securities hardened sharply alongside the currency slide. The benchmark 10-year G-Sec yield jumped to 7.6% from the previous close of 7%, crossing the crucial 7% psychological barrier.

"Right now, the rupee is no longer trading in isolation -- it is reacting to three dominant forces: oil, capital flows and central bank policies," said Amit Pabari, Managing Director at CR Forex Advisors.

State Bank of India's economic research department warned that without timely balance of payments support measures, inflationary expectations could become unanchored. The exchange rate cannot function as a shock absorber indefinitely, SBI economists noted.

"The current rupee depreciation is not in line with India's macro fundamentals. It is therefore imperative to control the second-round effects," said Soumya Ghosh, Group Chief Economic Advisor at SBI. India's balance of payments could remain negative for the third consecutive year.

Global crude oil prices surged amid uncertainty over the West Asia conflict, the blockade of the Strait of Hormuz, and the UAE's withdrawal from OPEC. India imports nearly 85% of its crude requirements, making the economy highly vulnerable to oil shocks.

The RBI's options to stabilize the rupee remain limited, with some regulatory measures aimed at containing depreciation being rolled back. Foreign portfolio investment flows are expected to stay weak as long as Middle East tensions remain elevated.
