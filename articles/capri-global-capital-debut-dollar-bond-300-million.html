Capri Global Capital Debuts With $300M Dollar Bond Issue, Eyes $500M

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Capri Global Capital plans to raise $300 million via debut dollar-denominated bonds with a 3-year-3-month maturity
  • 02/Issue may scale to $500 million if investor demand is strong; pricing expected before end of June 26 week
  • 03/Move aligns with MD Rajesh Sharma's strategy to lift capital market borrowings from 20% to 40%-50% of total

Capri Global Capital Ltd (NSE: CGCL) is set to enter the foreign currency debt market. The NBFC plans to raise around $300 million through U.S. dollar denominated bonds.

The bonds will carry a maturity of three years and three months. Two merchant bankers familiar with the matter confirmed the plans on Monday, June 29, 2026.

Investor calls are scheduled for Tuesday, June 30. The company aims to finalise pricing before the end of this week.

"The company may target around $300 million, but if the demand is strong, we could not rule out a benchmark size issue of $500 million," one banker said. Both bankers requested anonymity as they are not authorised to speak to media.

This marks Capri Global's first foray into international debt markets. The move signals growing confidence among mid-sized Indian NBFCs to tap global liquidity.

In April 2026, the company raised ₹5 billion ($53.03 million) through a public issue of debt domestically. The new dollar bond would be nearly six times that size.

Managing Director Rajesh Sharma had earlier stated the company will focus more on bond issuances this year. Capri Global aims to increase capital market borrowings to 40%-50% of total, up from around 20% currently.

The company reported assets under management of approximately ₹300 billion ($3.18 billion). Gross NPA stood at 1.00% and net NPA at 0.70% as of March 2026.

Capri Global shares closed at ₹217.44 on June 25, 2026. The stock has gained 34.64% over the past year, though it trades below its 52-week high of ₹248.
