India-Korea Trade Target $50 Billion by 2030 as Leaders Sign 15 Pacts

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/India and South Korea signed 15 agreements on April 20, 2026, covering industrial cooperation, steel supply chains, and maritime partnerships
  • 02/Bilateral trade target doubled to $50 billion by 2030, requiring about 18% annual growth from current $27 billion
  • 03/Both sides agreed to fast-track the CEPA upgrade and establish a dedicated Korean industrial enclave in India

Prime Minister Narendra Modi and South Korean President Lee Jae Myung held bilateral talks at Hyderabad House in New Delhi on April 20, 2026. The two leaders signed 15 agreements spanning industrial cooperation, steel supply chains, shipbuilding, and maritime logistics.

The India-Korea Business Leaders' Dialogue brought together industry leaders from both nations. PM Modi said the dialogue highlighted the "immense potential" of the economic partnership across diverse sectors.

Both countries aim to nearly double bilateral trade to $50 billion by 2030. Current trade stands at approximately $27 billion. Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal stated this implies an annual growth rate of about 18% over the next four years.

The two sides agreed to fast-track the upgrade of the Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (CEPA). The original CEPA was signed on August 7, 2009, and came into force on January 1, 2010. Eleven rounds of upgrade negotiations have been completed since 2016.

Goyal said the upgraded pact will address non-tariff barriers and ease rules of origin. The goal is to reduce India's large trade deficit with South Korea and create a more balanced partnership.

A major shipbuilding agreement targets procurement of over 400 vessels valued at ₹2.2 lakh crore (approximately $25 billion). The framework includes upgrading Indian shipyards, establishing a greenfield shipyard in southern India, and training programs led by Korea International Cooperation Agency.

Korean FDI into India totalled $7.27 billion from 1980 through December 2021. A dedicated Korean industrial enclave is now planned in India to attract more firms and channel demand into bilateral partnerships.

Cooperation will expand into semiconductors, artificial intelligence, electric mobility, green energy, critical minerals, and nuclear energy. South Korea also plans to increase naphtha imports from India, which currently account for about 8% of Korean naphtha imports.

"India and South Korea are going to transform their trusted ties into a futuristic partnership," PM Modi said following the talks. President Lee noted that current trade volumes do not reflect the relationship's full potential.

Lee stressed the need for joint efforts to secure the Strait of Hormuz and diversify energy supply chains. Both countries depend heavily on the Middle East for crude oil and natural gas.

The Korean president is on a three-day state visit to India that began on April 20, 2026. He is scheduled to travel to Vietnam after concluding his India visit.

HOW MANY AGREEMENTS DID INDIA AND SOUTH KOREA SIGN ON APRIL 20, 2026?
THE TWO COUNTRIES SIGNED 15 AGREEMENTS COVERING INDUSTRIAL COOPERATION, STEEL SUPPLY CHAINS, SHIPBUILDING, MARITIME LOGISTICS, PORT DEVELOPMENT, AND MARITIME HERITAGE COOPERATION.
WHAT IS THE NEW BILATERAL TRADE TARGET BETWEEN INDIA AND SOUTH KOREA?
BOTH NATIONS AIM TO DOUBLE BILATERAL TRADE TO $50 BILLION BY 2030, UP FROM THE CURRENT LEVEL OF APPROXIMATELY $27 BILLION. THIS REQUIRES AN ANNUAL GROWTH RATE OF ABOUT 18%.
WHAT IS THE VALUE OF THE SHIPBUILDING AND MARITIME AGREEMENT?
THE FRAMEWORK COVERS INDIA'S PLAN TO PROCURE OVER 400 VESSELS WITH AN ESTIMATED VALUE OF ₹2.2 LAKH CRORE, OR APPROXIMATELY $25 BILLION. IT ALSO INCLUDES PORT MODERNISATION PROJECTS WORTH $13.3 BILLION OVER THE NEXT FIVE YEARS.
WHEN WAS THE INDIA-KOREA CEPA ORIGINALLY SIGNED, AND WHAT IS ITS STATUS NOW?
THE COMPREHENSIVE ECONOMIC PARTNERSHIP AGREEMENT WAS SIGNED ON AUGUST 7, 2009, IN SEOUL AND CAME INTO FORCE ON JANUARY 1, 2010. ELEVEN ROUNDS OF UPGRADE NEGOTIATIONS HAVE BEEN COMPLETED SINCE 2016, AND BOTH SIDES HAVE NOW AGREED TO FAST-TRACK THE REMAINING PROCESS.
WHAT SECTORS WILL SEE ENHANCED COOPERATION UNDER THE NEW AGREEMENTS?
KEY SECTORS INCLUDE SEMICONDUCTORS, ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE, ELECTRIC MOBILITY, GREEN ENERGY, SHIPBUILDING, DEFENSE, CRITICAL MINERALS, NUCLEAR ENERGY, DIGITAL TRADE, AND STEEL SUPPLY CHAINS. SOUTH KOREA WILL ALSO INCREASE NAPHTHA IMPORTS FROM INDIA TO DIVERSIFY ENERGY SUPPLY CHAINS.
