Sebi Chief: Expiry-Day Settlement Rules In A Week, AI Framework Soon

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Sebi chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey said on Saturday that guidelines on the expiry-day derivatives settlement-price mechanism will be issued in about a week.
  • 02/Speaking at the BSE Brokers' Forum Capital Market Confluence 2026, Pandey outlined four priorities: proportionate regulation, deeper cash markets, easier access and technological resilience.
  • 03/Sebi also plans an AI/ML framework aligned with IOSCO's toolkit and interoperability fixes for tender offers, buybacks and OFS targeted by end-November 2026.

Sebi chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey said on Saturday that the regulator will issue guidelines on the settlement-price mechanism for derivatives on expiry days within about a week.

"Following review of the Closing Auction Session, a consultation paper was put out to address concerns in respect of settlement price of derivatives on expiry-day. We are currently examining the comments and hope to issue guidelines in about a week's time," Pandey said.

He was speaking at the second Capital Market Confluence 2026, organised by the Bombay Stock Exchange Brokers' Forum in Mumbai. His address was titled "From Regulation to Global Leadership: Positioning India's Capital Markets as a Standard-Setter for the Next Decade."

The timing matters for traders. The Closing Auction Session, or CAS, is the mechanism that sets closing prices in the cash market. Equity and stock derivatives expire against prices derived from those closing levels, which is where expiry-day manipulation concerns live.

The CAS review is part of Sebi's broader effort to deepen India's cash markets, improve price discovery and enhance market efficiency. Pandey said wider participation, stronger securities borrowing and lending, and efficient hedging and arbitrage could help improve liquidity and the functioning of cash markets.

Pandey structured his address around four broad priorities. The first is proportionate regulation, and the centrepiece there is a graded approach to broker supervision.

Sebi is exploring a graded compliance framework for stock brokers based on their scale, client exposure and dependence on technology. "One size need not fit all," he said.

Alongside that, the regulator is reviewing the Depositories and Participants Regulations. The aim is to simplify rules and strengthen fraud prevention.

The second priority is deeper cash markets, and interoperability is the concrete fix. Sebi is addressing gaps in the interoperability framework for certain transactions, including tender offers, buybacks and offers for sale, or OFS.

These transactions currently require parallel clearing arrangements, adding to costs and compliance requirements. The regulator is targeting implementation of the proposed interoperability changes by the end of November 2026.

The changes are expected to improve capital efficiency and lower compliance costs while preserving settlement safety.

The third priority is easier access to Indian markets. Sebi has consulted on simplifying digital onboarding for Persons Resident Outside India, or PROIs, without requiring physical presence.

The proposal drew more than 400 comments. Pandey said the regulator will issue a circular shortly.

The fourth priority is technological resilience. Sebi will issue guidelines shortly for the responsible use of artificial intelligence and machine learning.

The AI framework will follow a tiered approach. It will include provisions for clear accountability, data controls, kill switches and human oversight, aligned with the International Organization of Securities Commissions' Supervisory Toolkit for AI.

Sebi is also strengthening business continuity, disaster recovery, cybersecurity safeguards and governance across market infrastructure institutions.

The speech leaned heavily on scale. India's market capitalisation stands at around Rs 472 lakh crore, with more than 15 crore unique investors, mutual fund assets of around Rs 87 lakh crore and outstanding corporate bonds of around Rs 61 lakh crore.

Pandey argued that market leadership will depend on more than scale. It will require stronger investor protection, efficient market infrastructure and responsible innovation.

His closing message to intermediaries was direct. "Compete on technology. Compete on service. Compete on efficiency. But above all, compete on trust," Pandey said, urging market intermediaries to make investor confidence central to their operations.
