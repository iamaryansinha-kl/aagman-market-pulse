Rupee Hits Record Low Near 97/USD: RBI Eyes 2013 Taper Tantrum Playbook

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Rupee slumps to record low near 97 per dollar, surpassing all previous levels against the US currency
  • 02/RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra mulls rate hikes, currency swaps, and overseas dollar-raising measures
  • 03/Foreign portfolio outflows in 2026 already exceed last year's record $19 billion, intensifying pressure

The Indian rupee has fallen to a record low of nearly 97 per dollar this week. The Reserve Bank of India is now weighing emergency measures to arrest the slide.

RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra is considering interest rate hikes, additional currency swaps, and steps to raise dollars from overseas investors. The goal is to break the negative feedback loop between currency expectations and depreciation.

The urgency follows a sustained exodus of foreign capital. Portfolio outflows in 2026 have already surpassed last year's record of $19 billion. Investor confidence has eroded further.

India faced a similar crisis in 2013 during the Federal Reserve's taper tantrum. The rupee weakened from 55 to nearly 69 per dollar within three months. Capital fled emerging markets.

Then-Governor D. Subbarao tightened liquidity and raised the marginal standing facility rate by 200 basis points. Newly appointed Governor Raghuram Rajan later unveiled a foreign-currency non-resident deposit program.

That 2013 FCNR program mobilized over $30 billion. Banks offered deposit rates of 3.5% to 5%. Today, comparable schemes would likely require 8% to 9% to attract funds.

"Capital augmentation is needed to break the cycle," said Sajjid Chinoy, India economist at JPMorgan Chase Bank. "If done, it should be done in scale to alter expectations in the FX market."

Economists warn that rate hikes to defend the rupee could inflict broader economic damage. Structural reforms remain the critical test for attracting durable capital inflows, said Standard Chartered.
