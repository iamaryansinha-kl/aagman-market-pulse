Apple India Crosses Rs 1 Lakh Crore Revenue, Profit Jumps 66%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Revenue from operations rose 31% YoY to Rs 1,03,853 crore in FY26, from Rs 79,060 crore; growth accelerated from 18% in FY25.
  • •- Net profit jumped 66% to Rs 5,311 crore, versus 16% growth the year before.
  • •- Apple overtook Samsung in India mobile revenue: Samsung's phone business fell 1.4% to Rs 81,472 crore.
  • •- iPhone volume growth more than doubled to 24% from 11% as the premium segment grew fastest.
  • •- The reported numbers exclude iPhone exports routed through Apple's contract manufacturers overseas.
  • •- Analysts credit premiumisation, tier-2 retail expansion and easier financing for the surge.

Apple's India business crossed a threshold in FY26 that most consumer companies in the country never reach: revenue from operations of Rs 1,03,853 crore in the year ended March 2026, up 31 per cent from Rs 79,060 crore the previous year, according to filings by Apple India Pvt Ltd with the Registrar of Companies accessed through business intelligence platform AltInfo. What makes the milestone more striking than the number itself is the acceleration — growth of 31 per cent, against 18 per cent in FY25, in a market where the overall smartphone industry grew just 2 per cent by volume and 9 per cent by value. Apple is not riding the market. It is leaving it behind.

The profit line tells the sharper story. Net profit jumped 66 per cent to Rs 5,311 crore, from Rs 3,196 crore a year earlier — and the year before that, profit had grown only 16 per cent. Margins are expanding as the mix shifts upward: the premium segment, defined as handsets priced above Rs 30,000, was the fastest-growing part of the Indian market in FY26 at 12 per cent volume growth, and Apple's own iPhone volumes grew 24 per cent, more than double the 11 per cent pace of FY25. The company did not specify the reasons for the acceleration in its filings, and did not respond to emailed questions. It did not need to; the market-research read is consistent, and the scale of the milestone has already been noticed well beyond the trade press.

"Apple's growth in India is being driven by the steady shift towards premium smartphones," said Shubham Singh, research analyst at Counterpoint Technology Market Research. "Strong brand aspiration, wider retail reach, deeper distribution across tier-2 and beyond markets, along with easy financing options are making iPhones accessible to a much larger set of buyers." Singh added a subtler point: with prices of all phones rising, the gap between Android flagships and iPhones has narrowed, and at near-parity prices the brand pull and ecosystem lock-in tilt decisions Apple's way. A buyer who once compared a Rs 45,000 Android flagship against a Rs 65,000 iPhone now often compares two devices separated by a few thousand rupees — and Apple wins those comparisons more often than not.

The competitive framing matters too. Samsung Electronics India reported FY26 revenue of Rs 1.12 lakh crore — still larger than Apple's India number — but that figure spans televisions, home appliances, network equipment and exports. In the mobile business, the two are now head to head, and moving in opposite directions: Samsung's India mobile phone revenue declined 1.4 per cent to Rs 81,472 crore last fiscal, while Apple's crossed Rs 1 lakh crore. The reversal lands after a decade in which Samsung treated India as its most important smartphone market outside China, building the world's largest phone factory outside that country in Noida. The world's two smartphone superpowers have effectively traded places in the Indian market's most profitable segment, in the same year that Apple kept expanding its retail footprint and India-assembled iPhones kept flowing to both domestic shelves and export markets.

There is an asterisk worth keeping on the number, and it flatters rather than diminishes it. Apple India's reported revenue does not capture the full scale of iPhone manufacturing and exports from the country: those exports are executed by contract manufacturers such as Foxconn and Tata-run operations, with sales recorded through multiple Apple group entities overseas rather than routed through Apple India's own accounts. The true economic footprint of the Apple ecosystem in India — assembly jobs, component sourcing, exports — is materially larger than the Rs 1 lakh crore in the filing. The milestone, in other words, is the floor of Apple's India story, not the ceiling.

The strategic read extends beyond phones. A 66 per cent profit surge on a 31 per cent revenue rise implies operating leverage kicking in as the installed base grows — services attach, accessories, trade-in programmes and the ecosystem effect that turns one iPhone sale into a decade of spending. It also marks a shift in how Apple earns in India: margin-rich hardware at scale, rather than a thin import-and-sell model, which is what makes the India P&L increasingly resemble Apple's more mature Asian markets rather than a frontier outpost. With Apple's market share in India still in the low double digits by volume, the penetration runway remains long, and every rupee of financing innovation, tier-2 store expansion and price-segmentation widens the funnel. The iPhone 17 cycle brings its own questions — early reads suggest price-conscious buyers pausing on standard models while Pro models gain ground — but FY26's numbers show a franchise that has learned to win across price tiers, not just at the top. Crossing Rs 1 lakh crore took Apple roughly a decade of serious India commitment. On current growth rates, the next lakh will come considerably faster.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT WERE APPLE INDIA'S FY26 NUMBERS?
Revenue rose 31% to Rs 1,03,853 crore and net profit jumped 66% to Rs 5,311 crore in the year ended March 2026.
HOW DOES APPLE COMPARE WITH SAMSUNG IN INDIA?
Samsung India's total revenue of Rs 1.12 lakh crore includes TVs, appliances and exports; in mobile phones, Samsung fell 1.4% to Rs 81,472 crore while Apple crossed Rs 1 lakh crore.
WHAT IS DRIVING APPLE'S GROWTH?
Premiumisation of the smartphone market, wider tier-2 retail, easier financing and a narrowing price gap with Android flagships.
DO THE NUMBERS INCLUDE IPHONE EXPORTS?
No — exports through Apple's contract manufacturers are booked via overseas group entities, so the true footprint is larger than reported. GLOSSARY: - Premiumisation: The shift of consumer demand toward higher-priced product segments. - Operating leverage: Profit growing faster than revenue as fixed costs spread over a larger sales base. - Contract manufacturer: A third-party company that assembles products on behalf of a brand. - Registrar of Companies (RoC): India's registry where companies file statutory financial statements.
