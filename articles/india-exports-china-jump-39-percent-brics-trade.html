China's Appetite for Indian Goods Grows: Exports to Beijing Jump 39% in April-August

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

India's export engine is finding new buyers in old rival territory. Shipments to the four core BRICS economies, China, South Africa, Brazil and Russia, rose 34% to $19.9 billion in April-August 2026-27, according to commerce ministry data, with China leading the surge.

Exports to China alone jumped 39% to $9.6 billion during the five-month period. South Africa saw the sharpest growth at 58%, while shipments to Brazil and Russia rose 13% and 11% respectively. The four countries accounted for 9.2% of India's total exports, up from 8.1% a year earlier, when the comparable figure was $14.9 billion.

A commerce ministry official told PTI that the strongest momentum is coming from the bloc's founding partners, with core BRICS exports growing nearly three times faster than exports to the broader BRICS grouping, which expanded in 2024 to include Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, the UAE and Saudi Arabia, with Indonesia joining in 2025.

The broadening is not limited to BRICS. India's exports to Japan rose 43% to $3.43 billion in April-August, driven by a 76%, or $231.7 million, jump in mineral fuels, alongside growth in electronics and aluminium. Shipments to Italy rose about 30% to $3.92 billion from $3.02 billion a year earlier, while exports to South Korea increased 22% to $3.21 billion on the back of minerals, fuels, electronics, aluminium, iron and steel and chemicals.

The commerce ministry sees the trend as strategic integration. Officials noted that India's exports to South Korea are increasingly linked to that country's role as a manufacturing hub, with India supplying industrial raw materials, energy products and intermediate goods.

The data lands amid a complex trade backdrop. India is navigating steep US tariffs under the new Russia sanctions law while simultaneously negotiating a trade deal with Washington, and the rise of China, Japan, Italy and Korea as growth markets gives exporters some room to manoeuvre.

For policymakers, the April-August numbers support the push to widen India's export base beyond its traditional destinations. For exporters, they mark a rare period in which the fastest-growing major market for Indian goods is China itself.
