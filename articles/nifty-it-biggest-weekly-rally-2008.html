Nifty IT Rallies 11.7% in Biggest Weekly Surge Since 2008

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Nifty IT jumped 11.7% for the week, its biggest gain since April 2008.
  • •- Tech Mahindra led with an 18% weekly rise; TCS and Infosys gained 14% each.
  • •- The rally added Rs 2.8 lakh crore to constituents' market capitalisation.
  • •- Asian semiconductor markets crashed: Kospi -14.5%, its worst week since October 2008.
  • •- The index still sits 16% below its January level despite the surge.
  • •- TCS reported annualised AI revenue of $3.1 billion; HCLTech's AI business grew 78% YoY.

The trade of the week was hiding in plain sight: while Asia's AI hardware complex burned, India's software exporters staged their biggest weekly rally in eighteen years. The Nifty IT index surged 11.7 per cent over the week — the largest gain for the period since April 2008, the weeks before Lehman Brothers collapsed — closing Friday up 3.9 per cent at 31,924, its highest level since February 22. The rally added roughly Rs 2.8 lakh crore to the combined market capitalisation of constituents, which now carry a 7.52 per cent weight in the Nifty.

The mirror image makes the story. In Seoul, the Kospi plunged 14.5 per cent for the week — its worst since October 2008 — as investors questioned the profitability of massive AI infrastructure spending. Japan's Nikkei and Taiwan's market each fell nearly 5 per cent, unwinding a chunk of the semiconductor mania that made South Korea and Taiwan the year's hottest AI destinations. The money did not leave the AI trade; it rotated within it, from the companies building the hardware to the companies that put the software to work. Legacy outsourcing firms with proven revenue streams and decent operating margins — battered for two years on fears that AI would destroy their business — are being repriced as a hedge against the AI trade itself.

Earnings lit the fuse. HCLTech's better-than-expected September quarter set the tone, and the season has broadly delivered: rupee-reported revenue growth of 11-15 per cent year-on-year across the sector, deal flows holding steady, and hiring returning — TCS onboarded 24,000 freshers in the first half, while HCLTech added 6,450 employees during the quarter. Accenture, the sector's global proxy, logged its biggest daily gain ever earlier this month on results and guidance that beat expectations, signalling to overseas funds that the demand cycle has turned. "The sector is being reset," said Manish Bhandari of Vallum Capital. "Indian IT companies are adapting their services and business models to AI." Contract structures are shifting too: fixed-price and outcome-based deals are gaining ground over time-and-materials billing, which links revenue to results rather than headcount and should support margins if it persists.

AI revenue is now material enough to quote, not just project. TCS reported annualised AI revenue of $3.1 billion — more than 10 per cent of its total — while HCLTech's advanced AI business grew 78 per cent year-on-year. These are the numbers that let a portfolio manager justify the pivot: the same AI capex that is straining chip economics is generating services demand at the application layer, and Indian firms are the application layer's plumbers.

The leaderboard tells a quality-plus-beta story. Tech Mahindra, the most beaten-down of the large caps, surged 18 per cent for the week; TCS and Infosys rose 14 per cent each; Wipro and HCLTech gained 13.5 per cent apiece; LTIMindtree and Mphasis advanced 8 and 6 per cent respectively. Cheapness did the rest: after a prolonged correction, the sector was trading well below historical multiples, over-owned by overseas funds but abandoned by momentum traders. The combination of a valuation floor and a fundamental spark is precisely what produces 11.7 per cent weeks.

The caveats deserve equal billing. Even after the surge, Nifty IT remains 16 per cent down for the year — this was a rebound inside a bear market, not yet a breakout. The Nifty itself gained 2.8 per cent on the week, so the index effect flatters nobody. And the rally's logic has a circular edge: if Asian chip stocks keep falling because the market doubts AI profitability, the services demand that Indian firms are riding could eventually face the same question from the same clients. For now, though, the rotation is the trade, the rupee's recovery below 95 added a currency tailwind, and a sector that spent two years apologising for its existence closed its best week since the iPhone was a rumour — a genuinely long time in this market's memory.

For the earnings season that produced it, the week rewrote the narrative arc. Two weeks ago, IT was the sector investors loved to short: AI was coming for its billing model, US clients were squeezing budgets, and the stocks had underperformed for eighteen months. The Q2 prints did not refute any of that; they simply showed revenue growing anyway, at double digits in rupee terms, with AI contributing measurable dollars rather than just consuming them. TCS's $3.1 billion AI run-rate and HCLTech's 78 per cent AI growth gave the rotation its fundamental cover, and the freshers' hiring — 24,000 at TCS alone in the first half — suggested management confidence that the demand is not a blip.

The coming weeks supply the tests. December-quarter guidance from US peers will confirm or deflate the demand signal; the rupee's behaviour above 95 will determine whether the currency tailwind persists; and the same investors who bought this week will demand evidence that outcome-based contracts are actually lifting margins rather than just featuring in slide decks. But after eighteen months of being told the sector was a legacy asset in an AI world, the bulls finally had a week that belonged to them — and one with a genuinely historic signature, since 2008 references in this market are not deployed lightly.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
HOW BIG WAS THE IT RALLY?
Nifty IT rose 11.7% for the week, the biggest weekly gain since April 2008, adding Rs 2.8 lakh crore in market value.
WHAT DROVE IT?
Strong Q2 results led by HCLTech, Accenture's record gain on better-than-expected guidance, attractive valuations after a long correction, and rotation out of Asian chip stocks.
WHICH STOCKS LED?
Tech Mahindra +18%, TCS and Infosys +14% each, Wipro and HCLTech +13.5% each.
IS THE SECTOR'S PROBLEM SOLVED?
The index is still down 16% YTD; the rally reflects AI services demand and cheap valuations, but the durability depends on client spending and margin delivery. GLOSSARY: - Time-and-materials billing: Charging clients by hours worked; fixed-price and outcome-based models instead charge for results. - Rotation: Shifting investment from one sector or theme to another without leaving the market. - Kospi: South Korea's benchmark stock index, heavy in semiconductor makers. - Annualised revenue: The most recent quarter's revenue run-rate projected over a full year.
