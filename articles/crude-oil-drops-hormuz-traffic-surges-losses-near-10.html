Crude Oil Drops 4% to $71.99 as Hormuz Traffic Surges, Losses Near 10%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Brent crude crashed to $71.99 per barrel on June 27, down 4.34% for the day and 10.86% for the week
  • 02/Tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz surged to highest levels since the U.S.-Israeli conflict with Iran began in February
  • 03/Saudi Aramco resumed loading at Ras Tanura after a nearly four-month halt, adding to oversupply concerns

Brent crude futures settled at $71.99 a barrel on Friday, down $3.27 or 4.34%. U.S. West Texas Intermediate finished at $69.23, down $2.69 or 3.74%.

Since the market closed last Thursday, the Brent benchmark fell 10.86%, while WTI fell 9.62% for the week. The market was closed for a public holiday last Friday.

Tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz surged to its highest since the U.S.-Israeli conflict with Iran began at the end of February. This eased supply concerns that had kept prices elevated.

Saudi Aramco resumed oil loading at its Ras Tanura terminal on Friday after a nearly four-month halt. Two very large crude carriers loaded crude while another waited nearby.

"There is a growing sense that oil is going to keep moving through the Strait of Hormuz," said Phil Flynn, senior analyst at Price Futures Group.

"The predominant view remains one of imminent oversupply," said PVM analyst Tamas Varga. "We're going to get a flood of oil," Flynn added.

China's crude demand has not yet picked up, adding pressure to prices. June Goh, analyst at Sparta Commodities, noted a general selloff on increased Hormuz flows.

Russia is considering a diesel export ban for several months after Ukrainian drone attacks damaged its refineries. This could tighten product markets even as crude prices slump.
