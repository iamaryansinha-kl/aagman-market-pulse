Q1 2026: Domestic Capital Drives Indian Real Estate at $1.2 Billion

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Indian real estate attracted $1.6 billion in institutional investments during Q1 2026, a 26% year-on-year increase
  • 02/Domestic investors contributed $1.2 billion or 76% of total inflows, surpassing foreign capital in four of the last five quarters
  • 03/Industry experts see this shift driving transparency, governance, and long-term stability over the next three to five years

Institutional investments in Indian real estate stood at $1.6 billion in Q1 2026. This marks a 26% increase compared to the same period last year, per Cushman & Wakefield data.

Domestic investors contributed $1.2 billion, accounting for 76% of total inflows. Foreign investments made up the remaining 24%.

Domestic capital has now exceeded foreign inflows in four of the last five quarters. This signals a sustained structural shift in the sector's funding dynamics.

Pratik Tibrewala, Senior Vice President at M3M India, said domestic investors are now active across office, retail, warehousing, and yield-oriented segments. He attributed this to strong domestic liquidity and rising financial savings.

He noted that REITs, InvITs, AIFs, private credit, and public markets now support a diversified capital ecosystem. "India has entered the early stages of real estate financialization," Tibrewala said.

Ace Consulting founders Raunaq Arora and Maanu Dewan said domestic institutional investments rose 53% in the first half of 2025. They also flagged $41 billion in untapped domestic institutional capital.

Pushpender Singh, Managing Director at JMS Group, said domestic investors are approaching real estate with a long-term strategic mindset. This is expected to push developers toward greater transparency and execution discipline.

Manik Malik, CEO and President of BPTP, expects the trend to accelerate consolidation toward branded developers over the next three to five years. Integrated communities and lifestyle-led developments are likely to attract stronger interest.
