RBI Repo Rate Hike Expected in October Policy: Poll

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/A PTI poll of 16 economists and bankers expects the RBI to raise the repo rate by 25 basis points at its October 2026 policy review on Wednesday, with hawkish guidance.
  • 02/Retail inflation accelerated to an eight-month high of 4.82% in August and has stayed above the RBI's 4% target for three straight months.
  • 03/Experts broadly expect at least two hikes in FY27 and an upward revision to the RBI's FY27 CPI forecast from 5.0%.

The Reserve Bank of India is expected to raise the repo rate by 25 basis points at its monetary policy review this week, according to a PTI poll of 16 economists and bankers. A majority of poll participants expect the hike to come with hawkish guidance.

If the central bank moves on Wednesday, it would mark a reversal in interest rate policy. The RBI delivered rate cuts in 2025 and then held an extended pause. The repo rate currently stands at 5.25%.

The expected hike is a response to mounting inflationary pressure. The re-escalation of the West Asia crisis has pushed up energy and commodity prices.

Rate hikes by global central banks add to the case for action. Kanika Pasricha, chief economic advisor at Union Bank of India, said: "Coordination with global central bank hikes, rising inflation risks and strong growth momentum provide policy space to hike."

Dipti Deshpande, principal economist at Crisil, said inflationary pressures have built since the last policy review. She attributed the rise mainly to the re-escalation of the West Asia conflict and pressure on energy and commodity prices.

If these pressures persist, further rate hikes are likely, Deshpande indicated. Poll participants broadly see at least two increases in FY27, with several expecting two to three hikes in total.

Not everyone in the poll agrees on a move this week. Sachchidanand Shukla, group chief economist at Larsen & Toubro, expects the RBI to opt for status quo.

Shukla's reasoning: there is no evidence yet of demand-led inflation or overheating. The debate reflects how divided the expert community is on the timing.

Opinion is also split on the policy stance. Views among poll participants range from 'no change' and 'withdrawal of accommodation' to 'calibrated tightening'.

Despite that divide, a majority expect a hawkish tone from the central bank. Guidance, rather than the rate action itself, is where most participants see common ground.

Gaura Sengupta, economist at IDFC First Bank, said: "Gradual normalisation of policy as headline inflation is higher. The rate hike is needed to ensure real rates are not negative in the coming quarters."

The inflation data supports the hawkish camp. India's retail inflation accelerated to an eight-month high of 4.82% in August, up from 4.45% in July.

CPI has now stayed above the RBI's 4% target for three straight months. It printed 3.93% in May, 4.38% in June and 4.45% in July.

The August reading was the highest under the new CPI series, which uses a 2024 base year. It underscores how quickly price pressures have returned.

Those pressures are also broadening across the consumption basket. Around 19% of the 358 items in the CPI basket recorded inflation above 6% in August, according to Rajani Sinha, chief economist at CareEdge Ratings. That compares with 13% in March.

Experts expect the RBI to revise its FY27 CPI inflation forecast upward at the review. The central bank had projected FY27 CPI at 5.0% in the August policy.

Aditi Nayar, chief economist at ICRA, said the recent surge in crude oil prices above USD 100 per barrel could lift retail selling prices of petrol and diesel. That could further generalise price pressures, she said, necessitating an upward revision in CPI forecasts.

Apoorva Javadekar, chief economist at Shriram Group, expects a mild upward revision of 0.10% for the third and fourth quarters of FY27. "In fact, the upward revision to future inflation is almost a pre-condition for a rate hike, as otherwise the inflation is progressing on the projected path," he said.

Strong growth gives the RBI additional room to act. Experts largely expect an upward revision to the FY27 GDP forecast after stronger-than-expected activity in the first half of the fiscal year.

Radhika Rao, senior economist and executive director at DBS Bank, expects a "slight upward revision to above 7 per cent growth". The RBI had projected FY27 real GDP growth at 6.7% in the August policy.

On liquidity, experts expect the RBI to continue absorbing surplus funds. The central bank is seen using VRRR operations, open market operations and FX swaps for the purpose.

The October review will be the first real test of the RBI's inflation-fighting credibility since the easing cycle began. Whatever the decision on Wednesday, the poll makes one thing clear: the era of easy policy is expected to end.

WHY IS A RATE HIKE EXPECTED NOW?
RETAIL INFLATION ACCELERATED TO AN EIGHT-MONTH HIGH OF 4.82% IN AUGUST AND HAS STAYED ABOVE THE RBI'S 4% TARGET FOR THREE STRAIGHT MONTHS. THE RE-ESCALATION OF THE WEST ASIA CRISIS HAS PUSHED CRUDE OIL ABOVE USD 100 PER BARREL, AND GLOBAL CENTRAL BANKS HAVE BEEN RAISING RATES.
HOW MANY RATE HIKES ARE EXPECTED IN FY27?
POLL PARTICIPANTS BROADLY EXPECT AT LEAST TWO RATE INCREASES IN FY27. SEVERAL EXPECT TWO TO THREE HIKES IN TOTAL IF ENERGY AND COMMODITY PRICE PRESSURES PERSIST, ACCORDING TO ECONOMISTS INCLUDING CRISIL'S DIPTI DESHPANDE.
WILL THE RBI REVISE ITS INFLATION AND GROWTH FORECASTS?
EXPERTS EXPECT AN UPWARD REVISION TO THE FY27 CPI FORECAST FROM THE 5.0% PROJECTED IN AUGUST. APOORVA JAVADEKAR OF SHRIRAM GROUP SEES A MILD 0.10% UPWARD REVISION FOR Q3 AND Q4 OF FY27. ON GROWTH, THE FY27 GDP FORECAST OF 6.7% MAY ALSO BE REVISED UP, WITH RADHIKA RAO OF DBS EXPECTING ABOVE 7%.
WHAT WOULD A HIKE MEAN FOR THE INTEREST RATE CYCLE?
A 25 BASIS POINT HIKE WOULD BE THE FIRST INCREASE SINCE FEBRUARY 2023, WHEN THE REPO RATE WAS RAISED TO 6.50%. THE RBI HELD RATES UNCHANGED THROUGH 2023-24 AND BEGAN A RATE-CUT CYCLE IN 2025.
