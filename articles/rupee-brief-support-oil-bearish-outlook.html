Rupee Sees Brief Support on Softer Oil, Bearish Outlook Holds

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Rupee expected to open at 94.86-94.88 per dollar on Monday, supported by pullback in Brent crude prices
  • 02/Currency hit record low of 95.33 on April 30; has slid nearly 2% over past eight trading sessions
  • 03/Foreign portfolio outflows reached $6.5 billion in April; 2026 cumulative withdrawals at roughly $20.6 billion

The Indian rupee is expected to inch higher at the open on Monday, supported by a pullback in oil prices. It is seen opening in the 94.86 to 94.88 range against the US dollar.

The rupee settled at 94.91 on Thursday after hitting an all-time low of 95.33 during the session. Indian financial markets were shut on Friday for Labour Day.

Brent crude July futures slid to $105.50 in Asian trade. The pullback followed US President Donald Trump's announcement that America would help clear ships stuck in the Strait of Hormuz.

Trump described the move as a humanitarian effort for neutral nations. He offered scant details on the operation.

Brent crude was last trading near $108 a barrel. The lack of progress toward a US-Iran peace deal is expected to limit further downside in oil, analysts said.

The rupee has slid nearly 2% over the past eight trading days. Sustained weakness reflects a feedback loop from high oil prices that has eroded market sentiment.

Heavier hedging by importers and steady dollar demand from oil refiners have added pressure. High oil prices have also kept foreign investors on the sidelines of Indian equities.

Portfolio outflows reached nearly $6.5 billion in April. Cumulative withdrawals in 2026 stand at roughly $20.6 billion, surpassing outflows seen in all of 2025.

"The rupee's outlook remains challenging from both sentiment and flow perspectives, with the ballooning RBI FX forward book an added headwind," a currency trader at a bank said.
