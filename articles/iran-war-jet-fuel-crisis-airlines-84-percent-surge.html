Iran War Jet Fuel Crisis: Airlines Face 84% Surge, Summer Clouded

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Jet fuel prices surged 84% since Iran war began February 28, threatening airlines' summer profit outlook
  • 02/Gulf airlines hardest hit with flights down 50% YoY in March; global capacity still up 2% in 2026
  • 03/IATA's Willie Walsh warns of rationing risk in Asia and Europe as price hedges begin to expire

Jet fuel prices have surged 84% since the Iran war began on February 28, 2026. European airlines now face their biggest crisis since COVID-19 as the summer holiday season approaches.

Carriers have used hedges to tame costs so far. But these protections are running out, and supply shortages could emerge if the conflict continues.

"There is a risk that we'll see rationing of fuel supply, particularly in Asia and Europe," said Willie Walsh, head of IATA. He told Reuters on April 28 that supply remains robust for now.

Walsh said the situation is not yet as severe as the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020. The underlying demand for aviation remains robust, which is a positive for the sector.

Ryanair CEO Michael O'Leary played down concerns, saying supply disruption risk is receding. But Wizz Air CEO Jozsef Varadi warned fuel prices won't quickly return to pre-war levels even if fighting stops.

Gulf airlines suffered the worst blow. Cirium Ascend data shows Middle Eastern operator flights dropped 50% year-on-year in March. Q2 and Q3 bookings via Gulf hubs are down 42.5%.

Global passenger capacity remains up nearly 2% in 2026 versus 2025. Finnair reported a net positive impact from the crisis, while Norwegian brushed off supply risks entirely.

Air France-KLM, IAG and Lufthansa are set to report first-quarter results this week. All three have raised ticket prices and cut flight capacity to offset soaring fuel bills.

Cirium Ascend's George Dimitroff said airlines are far more agile today than in previous decades. He agreed COVID-19 was a much bigger hit than the current fuel crisis.
