Commercial LPG Hike Hits ₹993: Restaurants to Pass 10-15% Costs to Consumers

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Commercial LPG hiked by ₹993 per 19-kg cylinder on May 1, 2026, the third consecutive increase after ₹195.50 in April and ₹114.50 in March
  • 02/Total three-month price rise reaches ₹1,332; restaurant industry warns of 10-15% menu price hikes to pass on costs to consumers
  • 03/Packaged food companies face 1-2% manufacturing cost pressure; smaller eateries and micro players expected to be hit hardest

Commercial LPG prices were raised by ₹993 per 19-kg cylinder on May 1, 2026. This marks the third consecutive hike after ₹195.50 in April and ₹114.50 in March.

The total increase over three months now stands at ₹1,332. Hotels and restaurants, among the largest consumers of commercial LPG, face unprecedented cost escalation.

Restaurant industry players said menu prices will need to rise by at least 10-15 per cent. Pradeep Shetty, VP of the Federation of Hotel & Restaurant Associations of India, called the increase catastrophic for the sector.

Aroop Verma, founder of Seven Hills Tower, expects a 15-20 per cent hike at owned outlets. Standalone restaurants could raise prices by 20-50 per cent, he added.

A spokesperson for snacks brand Cornitos said packaged food companies face a 1-2 per cent manufacturing cost hit. Processes like frying and baking are energy-intensive and directly exposed to LPG pricing.

M Venkatasubbu, president of Tamil Nadu Hoteliers Association, said only 25 per cent of cooking can shift to induction currently. Just 50 per cent of restaurants use induction stoves today.

Ajay Sahai, DG of Federation of Indian Export Organisations, noted MSMEs in food processing, ceramics and glass need cost stability. Tiruppur garment units face indirect impact through higher dyeing costs.

Many Andhra Pradesh hotels have already stopped running in-house restaurants. The latest hike may force more eatery closures and job losses, an association member warned.
