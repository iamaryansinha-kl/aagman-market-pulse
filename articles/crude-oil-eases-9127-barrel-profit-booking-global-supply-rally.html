Crude Oil Eases to ₹9,127/Barrel as Profit-Booking Trims Global Supply Rally

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/MCX crude oil for May delivery fell ₹48 or 0.52% to ₹9,127 per barrel in futures trade on April 24
  • 02/Brent Crude surged $2.18 or 2.07% to $107.25 on Strait of Hormuz closure and Middle East tensions
  • 03/Profit-booking in domestic markets offset the global supply-driven rally; 12,549 lots traded on MCX

Crude oil for May delivery slipped ₹48, or 0.52 per cent, to ₹9,127 per barrel on the Multi Commodity Exchange. The contract traded in 12,549 lots on Friday, April 24.

Analysts attributed the domestic decline to profit-booking by local traders. Global benchmarks, however, moved higher on escalating supply concerns.

Brent Crude futures for June delivery rose $2.18, or 2.07 per cent, to $107.25 per barrel. West Texas Intermediate gained $1.90, or 2 per cent, to $97.75 for the same month.

"Crude oil futures moved higher as Middle East tensions intensified, with the Strait of Hormuz remaining closed," said Kaveri More, Commodity Analyst at Choice Broking. She noted fresh military activity around Tehran has kept supply concerns elevated.

President Donald Trump's order to target vessels laying mines in the Strait of Hormuz added to fears of prolonged disruption. Iran has shown no immediate willingness to negotiate under continued naval restrictions.

Stronger demand for US exports and falling domestic fuel inventories provided additional support to global prices. The standoff continues to tighten global crude supply.

The divergence between domestic and global crude prices reflects differing risk appetites. Indian traders locked in gains while international markets priced in geopolitical risk.
