India External Debt Hits $762.8 Billion in FY26, Debt-to-GDP at 20.8%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/India's external debt reached $762.8 billion by March 2026, up $26.3 billion from a year ago
  • 02/Debt-to-GDP ratio climbed to 20.8% from 19.8%; dollar strength accounted for $24.6 billion valuation effect
  • 03/Short-term debt share rose to 19.6% while debt service ratio improved to 5.8% of current receipts

India's external debt stood at $762.8 billion at the end of March 2026. This marks an increase of $26.3 billion compared to March 2025. The Reserve Bank of India released the data on Monday.

The external debt-to-GDP ratio rose to 20.8% at end-March 2026. This is up from 19.8% recorded a year earlier. The increase reflects both fresh borrowings and currency valuation effects.

The US dollar's appreciation against the rupee and other major currencies contributed significantly. The valuation effect alone amounted to $24.6 billion during the period. Excluding this, the debt increase would have been $51 billion year-on-year.

Long-term debt with original maturity above one year stood at $613.5 billion. This represents an increase of $11.6 billion over its level at end-March 2025. Short-term debt's share in total external debt increased to 19.6% from 18.3%.

The ratio of short-term debt to foreign exchange reserves climbed to 21.6%. This is up from 20.1% reported at the end of March 2025. The trend signals rising near-term repayment obligations relative to reserve cover.

US dollar-denominated debt remained the largest component at 55.5% of total external debt. This was followed by Indian rupee debt at 29.4%, yen at 6.4%, special drawing rights at 4.3%, and euro at 3.7%.

Loans constituted the largest share of external debt at 34.7%. Currency and deposits accounted for 22.3%, trade credit and advances for 19%, and debt securities for 16.1%. The composition reflects diverse funding sources.

Debt service, covering principal repayments and interest payments, declined to 5.8% of current receipts. This is down from 6.6% a year ago. The improvement indicates better debt sustainability despite the higher absolute debt level.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT IS INDIA'S EXTERNAL DEBT AS OF MARCH 2026?
INDIA'S EXTERNAL DEBT STOOD AT $762.8 BILLION AT THE END OF MARCH 2026. THIS REPRESENTS AN INCREASE OF $26.3 BILLION COMPARED TO THE LEVEL RECORDED AT END-MARCH 2025.
WHAT IS THE DEBT-TO-GDP RATIO AND HOW HAS IT CHANGED?
THE EXTERNAL DEBT-TO-GDP RATIO INCREASED TO 20.8% AT END-MARCH 2026 FROM 19.8% AT END-MARCH 2025. THE RISE REFLECTS BOTH FRESH BORROWINGS AND THE VALUATION IMPACT OF A STRONGER US DOLLAR.
HOW MUCH DID THE US DOLLAR'S STRENGTH IMPACT THE DEBT FIGURE?
THE VALUATION EFFECT DUE TO THE APPRECIATION OF THE US DOLLAR VIS-A-VIS THE INDIAN RUPEE AND OTHER MAJOR CURRENCIES AMOUNTED TO $24.6 BILLION. EXCLUDING THIS EFFECT, EXTERNAL DEBT WOULD HAVE INCREASED BY $51 BILLION YEAR-ON-YEAR.
WHAT IS THE BREAKDOWN BETWEEN LONG-TERM AND SHORT-TERM DEBT?
LONG-TERM DEBT WITH ORIGINAL MATURITY ABOVE ONE YEAR STOOD AT $613.5 BILLION. SHORT-TERM DEBT'S SHARE IN TOTAL EXTERNAL DEBT ROSE TO 19.6% FROM 18.3%, AND THE RATIO OF SHORT-TERM DEBT TO FOREIGN EXCHANGE RESERVES INCREASED TO 21.6% FROM 20.1%.
HOW HAS THE DEBT SERVICE RATIO CHANGED?
DEBT SERVICE, COMPRISING PRINCIPAL REPAYMENTS AND INTEREST PAYMENTS, DECLINED TO 5.8% OF CURRENT RECEIPTS AT END-MARCH 2026 FROM 6.6% A YEAR EARLIER. THIS INDICATES IMPROVED DEBT SUSTAINABILITY DESPITE HIGHER ABSOLUTE DEBT LEVELS.
