Global Crude Oil Supply Deficit Hits 4.8 mbpd Amid Hormuz Disruptions: Report

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Global crude oil market faces a 4.8 million barrels per day supply deficit due to West Asia conflict and Strait of Hormuz disruptions, per PL Capital report
  • 02/IEA released 400 million barrels from strategic reserves and alternative routes added 6.2 mbpd, but a 15 mbpd supply loss leaves a gap
  • 03/Demand destruction expected to rebalance markets as Brent trades near $108; PL Capital remains positive on Indian OMCs IOC, BPCL and HPCL

The global oil market is facing a supply deficit of approximately 4.8 million barrels per day (mbpd). This gap has emerged due to ongoing disruptions in West Asia and restricted flows through the Strait of Hormuz, according to a PL Capital report dated May 4, 2026.

The Strait of Hormuz disruption has caused a supply loss of nearly 15 mbpd. The IEA has partially offset this by releasing about 400 million barrels from strategic reserves globally.

Alternative export routes bypassing Hormuz have contributed around 6.2 mbpd. Despite these measures, the supply shortfall remains at 4.8 mbpd.

PL Capital stated: "Supply deficit of 4.8mbpd needs to be compensated... Against a supply balance of ~4.8mbpd, such expected demand destruction will partially rebalance market dynamics further, putting a downward pressure on crude prices."

Brent crude traded near $108 per barrel on May 3, 2026, down from a Q2 2026 peak of $115 forecast by the EIA. Prices have risen about 79% over the past year.

The IEA expects global oil demand to contract by around 1.5 mbpd in Q2 2026. April 2026 could see a sharper decline of about 2.3 mbpd, compared to industry estimates of around 4.0 mbpd.

The demand slowdown is initially concentrated in the Middle East and Asia-Pacific. India imports roughly 88-90% of its crude, with 50-53% transiting Hormuz.

PL Capital remains positive on Indian oil marketing companies. It expects lower crude prices driven by demand destruction to improve marketing margins for Indian Oil Corporation (NSE: IOC), Bharat Petroleum Corporation (NSE: BPCL) and Hindustan Petroleum Corporation (NSE: HPCL).

However, UBS has downgraded these same OMCs, cutting IOC target to ₹175, BPCL to ₹365 and HPCL to ₹340. Elara Securities warned that OMC earnings could plummet 90-190% if Brent stays above $110 without fuel price hikes.

ICRA estimates domestic LPG losses for Indian OMCs could reach ₹80,000 crore in FY27 if current price trends continue. The government raised commercial LPG prices by up to 48% on May 1, 2026, while shielding retail consumers.
