India Restricts Silver Imports: What It Means for Investors and Domestic Prices

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •• India restricted imports of 99.9% purity silver bars and semi-manufactured silver products with immediate effect
  • •• Import duties on gold and silver were raised to 15% from 6% just days earlier
  • •• India imported a record $12 billion worth of silver in FY26, up from $4.8 billion the previous year
  • •• Analysts say domestic silver premiums will rise, making physical silver more expensive for Indian buyers

India has placed imports of silver bars with 99.9% purity and several semi-manufactured silver products under the "restricted" category. The move took effect immediately.

This comes just days after the government raised import duties on gold and silver to 15% from 6%. Both measures aim to curb imports and ease pressure on foreign exchange reserves.

India is the world's largest silver consumer. The country imports more than 80% of its silver requirements.

Silver imports surged to a record $12 billion in FY26. This is sharply higher than $4.8 billion in the previous financial year. In April alone, silver imports jumped 157% year-on-year to $411 million.

"The silver import restriction does not mean India has shut the door. It means the entry is now guarded," said Jateen Trivedi, Vice President at LKP Securities.

Imports will now be channelled mainly through nominated agencies. These include RBI-authorised banks, DGFT-approved entities, and jewellers operating through bullion exchanges.

For domestic investors, this almost always translates into higher premiums. Indian buyers may end up paying more for physical silver compared with global benchmark prices.

Analysts say the MCX-LBMA spread is the key indicator to watch. This gap shows exactly how much extra Indians are paying versus the world price.

"Silver had been trading at a discount after the government raised import duties. But it is likely to start trading at a premium in the coming weeks," said Chirag Thakkar, CEO of Amrapali Group Gujarat.

The impact on global silver prices may remain limited. India is a large consumer but not a price setter for global benchmarks.

However, trade flows could shift regionally. Demand may get rerouted through channels such as the UAE Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement tariff quota mechanism.

Silver ETFs and listed refiners could also be affected. Investors may increasingly turn to financial products when physical supply becomes harder to access.

Apart from jewellery and investment, silver is widely used in industrial applications. These include solar panels, electronics, and electrical equipment.

India imports most of its silver from the UAE, Britain, and China.
