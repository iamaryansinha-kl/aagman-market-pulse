Vivriti Asset Management Returns Rs 3,400 Crore Across Fund Vintages

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Vivriti Asset Management has returned more than Rs 3,400 crore to investors across two fund vintages over the past seven years
  • 02/Vintage II moderate scheme delivered ~15% gross IRR (~13% net); conservative scheme ~12% gross (~10.5% net) investing only in 'A'-rated companies
  • 03/The on-schedule exits of three performing credit schemes answer the liquidity question dogging India's private credit boom

Vivriti Asset Management has completed the exit of its Vintage II funds. The three performing credit schemes were wound up within their stipulated fund life and in line with stated objectives.

With these exits, VAM has returned more than Rs 3,400 crore to investors. The total covers capital repayments and income distributions across two fund vintages over the past seven years.

Vintage II launched in early 2022 and reached final close by September 2023, after raising all committed capital. The vintage collected Rs 1,700 crore in investor commitments and deployed more than Rs 3,500 crore across over 45 portfolio companies.

The investor base comprised insurance companies and all-India financial institutions. Family offices and high-net-worth individuals also participated, across schemes with conservative and moderate risk profiles.

Deployments spanned infrastructure, including roads, ports, airports and digital infrastructure. The vintage also invested in clean energy, manufacturing such as fertilisers and electronic devices, and services including logistics, co-working, financial services and enterprise SaaS.

Vintage II alone returned more than Rs 2,260 crore in capital and income to investors. The moderate scheme delivered a gross internal rate of return of about 15%, which works out to roughly 13% net of fees and expenses.

The conservative scheme posted a gross IRR of about 12%, or around 10.5% net of fees. That scheme invests only in portfolio companies rated 'A', a filter that capped risk but also capped return relative to the moderate pool.

"Private credit addresses financing requirements that sit between conventional bank lending and equity capital," said Vineet Sukumar, Founder and Managing Director of Vivriti Asset Management.

"Some fundamentally sound, cash-generating businesses need more flexible capital than traditional lending structures provide," Sukumar said. The approach, he said, is to structure transactions where returns are supported by strong cash flows, with downside protection through security and diversification across sectors and businesses.

"These repayments highlight the role of credit in portfolios and the opportunity cost of not allocating to the asset class," said Soumendra Ghosh, Chief Investment Officer at VAM.

Ghosh pointed to the funds' track record of steady income, predictable repayment of capital and low volatility in outcomes across the fund life. The funds operated through changing business conditions, shifting policy rates, liquidity swings and a volatile geopolitical environment.

The exits land in the middle of a live debate: whether India's fast-growing private credit industry can actually return money on schedule. Vintage II offers a full lifecycle data point — launch in 2022, deployment of Rs 3,500 crore, exit on time, capital returned.

Net returns of 10.5% to 13% also compare favourably with conventional fixed income over the same period, giving yield-hungry institutional investors a reference point for credit allocation. The conservative-moderate split gives credit AIFs a working template for risk-tiering — one pool for 'A'-rated paper, another reaching for higher yield.

The next test is repetition. One clean exit cycle is proof of concept; the asset class will be judged on whether managers across the industry can match on-schedule wind-ups as competition for deals intensifies.

MORE THAN RS 3,400 CRORE THROUGH CAPITAL REPAYMENTS AND INCOME DISTRIBUTIONS ACROSS TWO FUND VINTAGES OVER THE PAST SEVEN YEARS. THIS INCLUDES OVER RS 2,260 CRORE RETURNED BY THE VINTAGE II FUNDS ALONE.
WHAT RETURNS DID VIVRITI'S VINTAGE II FUNDS DELIVER?
THE MODERATE SCHEME DELIVERED A GROSS IRR OF ABOUT 15%, OR ROUGHLY 13% NET OF FEES AND EXPENSES. THE CONSERVATIVE SCHEME RETURNED ABOUT 12% GROSS, OR AROUND 10.5% NET.
WHO INVESTED IN VIVRITI'S VINTAGE II FUNDS?
THE INVESTOR BASE INCLUDED INSURANCE COMPANIES AND ALL-INDIA FINANCIAL INSTITUTIONS, ALONGSIDE FAMILY OFFICES AND HIGH-NET-WORTH INDIVIDUALS, ACROSS SCHEMES WITH CONSERVATIVE AND MODERATE RISK PROFILES.
WHAT DID THE VINTAGE II FUNDS INVEST IN?
MORE THAN RS 3,500 CRORE WAS DEPLOYED ACROSS OVER 45 PORTFOLIO COMPANIES IN INFRASTRUCTURE, CLEAN ENERGY, MANUFACTURING AND SERVICES. THE CONSERVATIVE SCHEME INVESTED ONLY IN PORTFOLIO COMPANIES RATED 'A'.
WHY DO THESE EXITS MATTER FOR INDIA'S PRIVATE CREDIT MARKET?
THEY PROVIDE A FULL LIFECYCLE EXAMPLE — LAUNCH IN EARLY 2022, FINAL CLOSE BY SEPTEMBER 2023, EXIT WITHIN STIPULATED FUND LIFE — ADDRESSING THE LIQUIDITY CONCERN AROUND PRIVATE CREDIT AND DEMONSTRATING STEADY INCOME WITH LOW VOLATILITY IN OUTCOMES. GLOSSARY: Private credit: Loans and debt investments made directly by funds (not banks) to companies, typically offering higher yields than public bonds in exchange for less liquidity. IRR (internal rate of return): The annualised return of an investment over its life, accounting for the timing of cash flows. Gross IRR is before fees; net IRR is what investors actually receive. Fund vintage: The year or cohort in which a fund is raised. Comparing returns across vintages shows whether a manager's performance is repeatable.
