Rahee Group Plans ₹600 Crore Investment Over 3 Years for Expansion

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Rahee Group will invest up to ₹600 crore over the next three years to scale operations across its key verticals
  • 02/A ₹200 crore foundry in Odisha will manufacture cast manganese and steel castings; another ₹100 crore goes to Pandrol Rahee for fastening systems
  • 03/Rahee Infratech targets ₹2,000 crore standalone revenue and may pursue an IPO within the next 2-3 years

Rahee Group plans to invest up to ₹600 crore over the next three years. The infrastructure firm aims to scale operations and strengthen manufacturing capabilities across its businesses.

CMD Pradeep Khaitan said the group is targeting ₹400-500 crore in direct investments across verticals. The allocation covers foundry, fastening systems, and core infrastructure capex.

Rahee Track Technologies Ltd, a wholly-owned subsidiary of Rahee Infratech, is setting up a ₹200 crore foundry in Odisha. The facility will manufacture cast manganese and steel castings for the railway sector.

Rahee Infratech Ltd will spend ₹60-70 crore annually over three years on capex. This will enhance its core infrastructure capabilities in track laying, bridges, and ballastless track systems.

Pandrol Rahee Technologies, a subsidiary where Rahee holds a 40 per cent stake, will receive around ₹100 crore. The funds will set up a dedicated line for modern fastening systems and plastic components under the Pandrol brand.

The Rahee Group's combined turnover stands at approximately ₹1,800 crore. Rahee Infratech, founded in 1948, has evolved from a track component maker into a full railway EPC and civil infrastructure player.

Rahee Infratech is targeting ₹2,000 crore in standalone revenue. The company may consider an initial public offering in the next 2-3 years, Khaitan indicated.

Pandrol Rahee is exploring export markets including the US, UK, Japan, and emerging economies. The group is also developing sustainable rail solutions using recycled rubber from end-of-life tyres.

"India is not only moving towards self-reliance but also becoming a competitive manufacturing hub for global rail markets. 'Make in India' is now evolving into 'Make for the World'," Khaitan said.

Indian Railways is undertaking one of the world's largest modernisation programmes. Metro rail expansion across multiple cities is expected to drive demand for advanced track solutions.
