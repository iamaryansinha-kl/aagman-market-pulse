Micron Stock Surges 17% on Blockbuster Results, Ignites Global Chip Rally

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Micron Technology surged over 17% in U.S. premarket trading after forecasting quarterly profit and revenue well above analyst expectations
  • 02/Customers have committed $22 billion to lock in memory chip supplies, underscoring how AI-driven demand is tightening the semiconductor market
  • 03/The rally spread across U.S., European and Asian chip stocks, with SK Hynix jumping 13% and Qualcomm soaring nearly 12%

Micron Technology surged more than 17% in U.S. premarket trading on Thursday. The company forecast quarterly profit and revenue well above Wall Street expectations.

Customers have committed $22 billion to lock in supplies of memory chips. This underscores how AI-driven demand is tightening the semiconductor market.

The stock was on track to add roughly $214 billion in market value. At $1,237.91 per share, Micron's total market capitalization would reach $1.39 trillion.

That puts Micron right behind Meta Platforms at $1.41 trillion and Tesla at $1.42 trillion. The company's shares have more than tripled in value so far this year.

Micron is a key supplier for Nvidia's AI processors. It is the only U.S.-based producer of high bandwidth memory (HBM) chips.

Analysts at D.A. Davidson said Micron has entered "a new era" marked by unprecedented visibility. They bumped the stock's price target to a Wall Street high of $2,000, nearly double its last close of $1,048.51.

"We expect tight conditions to persist beyond calendar 2027," Micron CEO Sanjay Mehrotra said. He pointed to strong demand and structural supply constraints.

J.P. Morgan analysts said the company's shift toward multi-year customer agreements is "fundamentally transforming" its business model. They recommended investors "add on any dips" while maintaining an overweight stance on South Korea.

Other U.S. chip stocks also rallied. Western Digital and SanDisk jumped more than 10% each, while Seagate Technology rose 9%.

Qualcomm soared nearly 12% after saying it expects $15 billion in data center sales by 2029. Arm Holdings and Marvell gained between 4% and 6%, while Nvidia rose 1.2%.

In Europe, ASML rose nearly 4% after two days of heavy losses. Infineon, STMicroelectronics and ASM International gained between 3% and 6%.

In Asia, South Korean chip heavyweights SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics closed higher at 13% and 5.3%, respectively. SK Hynix also plans to raise up to $29.4 billion through a U.S. stock market listing.
