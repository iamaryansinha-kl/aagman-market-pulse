Rallis India FY26 Net Profit Jumps 47% to ₹184 Crore on Higher Revenues

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Rallis India FY26 net profit rises 47% to ₹184 crore versus ₹125 crore in FY25 on 9% revenue growth
  • 02/Q4FY26 loss narrows to ₹15 crore from ₹32 crore loss year-ago; revenue up 6% at ₹456 crore
  • 03/Board recommends ₹3 per share dividend for FY26; stock closed at ₹261.75 on NSE

Rallis India Ltd has reported a 47% jump in net profit for FY26 at ₹184 crore. Revenue for the full year rose 9% to ₹2,897 crore from ₹2,663 crore in FY25.

The company is a Tata Chemicals subsidiary listed on NSE. It manufactures crop protection products, seeds, and plant nutrients.

For the March quarter Q4FY26, the company narrowed its net loss to ₹15 crore. This compares with a loss of ₹32 crore in Q4FY25. Q4 revenue grew 6% to ₹456 crore.

The board has recommended a dividend of ₹3 per equity share for FY26. The payout, representing 300% on face value, is subject to shareholder approval at the AGM.

During FY26, the Crop Care business grew 8%. Domestic formulations delivered 5% growth. B2B exports including CSM surged 17% on improved realisations.

The Seeds business posted 15% growth despite supply constraints in Cotton and Mustard. The Soil and Plant Health segment grew 8% amid regulatory headwinds on biostimulants.

The company launched two new insecticide products, Fiplam and Alstor, during the year. Earnings per share for FY26 improved to ₹9.46 from ₹6.43 in FY25.

Dr Gyanendra Shukla, Managing Director & CEO, said: "FY26 reflects our continued focus on strengthening the business through focused execution, portfolio expansion, and customer engagement. As we move forward, we will continue to build on our product portfolio, digital capabilities, and innovation pipeline."

Rallis shares closed at ₹261.75 on the NSE on April 28. The stock has gained 11% over the past year. The company trades at a market capitalisation of approximately ₹5,900 crore.

The agrochemical sector has faced mixed conditions recently. Peers including UPL, PI Industries, and Sumitomo Chemical India have reported quarterly declines in revenue or profit.

The Q4 seasonal loss reflects the agri-inputs industry's inherent cyclicality. Full-year profitability suggests Rallis has managed input costs and pricing better than some competitors.
