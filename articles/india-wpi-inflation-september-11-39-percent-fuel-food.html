India WPI Inflation Quickens to 11.39% on Fuel, Food Costs

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Wholesale price inflation rose to 11.39% year-on-year in September from 9.92% in August, official data showed on Wednesday.
  • •- The print came in well above the 10.18% that economists had forecast in a Reuters poll.
  • •- Fuel and power inflation jumped to 25.85% from 22.93%, while manufactured product inflation rose to 9.76% from 8.37%.
  • •- The wholesale food index recorded inflation of 8.66%, up from 7.05% in August.
  • •- The data follows retail inflation hitting a near two-year high of 5.54% in September.
  • •- The RBI raised the repo rate to 5.5% earlier this month, its first increase since February 2023.

Wholesale prices in India accelerated sharply in September, rising 11.39 per cent year-on-year against 9.92 per cent in August, official data released by the commerce and industry ministry on Wednesday showed — a reading that blows past forecasts and hardens the inflation case behind the Reserve Bank of India's recent hawkish turn.

Economists polled by Reuters had expected wholesale inflation at 10.18 per cent for the month. The actual number landed more than a full percentage point above consensus, making this one of the largest upside surprises in the history of the series.

The drivers were broad-based. Inflation in fuel and power jumped to 25.85 per cent from 22.93 per cent in August, reflecting elevated crude oil and energy costs. Manufactured product inflation — the category that captures the pass-through into factory-gate prices — rose to 9.76 per cent from 8.37 per cent. Primary articles, which include food articles, non-food articles and minerals, saw inflation rise to 8.67 per cent from 7.76 per cent. The wholesale food index climbed to 8.66 per cent from 7.05 per cent.

The ministry singled out the key pressure points: mineral oils, food articles, crude petroleum and natural gas, coal and lignite, basic metals, chemicals, textiles, rubber and plastic products, and electrical equipment. That is not a story about one crop or one commodity — it is an economy-wide cost push, arriving from energy, metals and food simultaneously.

Wednesday's data lands on top of an already-uncomfortable retail picture. Consumer price inflation hit 5.54 per cent in September, a near two-year high, driven largely by vegetables and food. The gap between wholesale and retail inflation — more than five percentage points — signals that producers are absorbing part of the cost surge for now, but with manufactured goods inflation already at 9.76 per cent, some of that pressure will inevitably reach shop shelves in the coming months.

The policy response has already begun. In early October, the RBI raised its benchmark repo rate to 5.5 per cent, the first increase since February 2023, with Governor Sanjay Malhotra flagging that inflation and its outlook "are not benign as they were last year." The September WPI print validates that judgment in uncomfortable fashion: the RBI acted before the full wholesale data was in, and the data now says it did not act early by mistake.

The forward view is not reassuring. An ET Online survey of economists found food inflation likely to stay elevated, with some expecting 7-8 per cent over the next three to six months. Fresh Kharif arrivals and winter Rabi supplies could bring relief from late 2026 or early 2027, but the risks run the wrong way: El Nino conditions, uneven monsoon rainfall and below-average reservoir levels threaten winter crops. ICRA's Aditi Nayar and ANZ's Dhiraj Nim have both flagged reservoir levels as a key variable for food prices, with the impact depending on crop output and adverse weather.

Global commodity prices add a second layer of pressure. Elevated crude raises farm, irrigation and transportation costs; higher liquefied natural gas prices lift fertiliser costs; and India's dependence on imported edible oils leaves that category exposed to world markets. Freight, packaging and crop-protection costs compound the bill. With Brent crude holding above $100 a barrel and the Iran conflict keeping shipping risk premia alive, the imported-cost channel is not about to switch off.

There is a technical footnote worth knowing. The September WPI data was released under the revised series introduced in June with 2022-23 as the base year, designed to better reflect the economy's current structure. The new series expands the commodity basket to 957 items from 697 and includes newer energy sources such as solar, wind and nuclear electricity. Even on the broader, modern basket, the direction is unambiguous.

For markets, the arithmetic is stark. Wholesale inflation above 11 per cent, retail inflation at a two-year high, oil above $100 and a central bank already back in hiking mode adds up to a higher-for-longer rate environment. Bond yields, which have been creeping up all month, have one more reason to stay elevated — and the RBI's October 3 rate action now looks less like a one-off insurance move than the first step of a cycle.

The pass-through question is the one that should worry consumers most. Wholesale inflation historically leads retail inflation by a few months as producers gradually pass input costs down the chain, and with the WPI-retail gap this wide, the 5.54 per cent CPI print may itself understate what is coming. Economists call this pipeline pressure: costs that have already landed at the factory gate but have not yet appeared on store shelves.

For corporate India, the picture splits. Companies with pricing power — consumer brands, infrastructure contractors with cost-escalation clauses, lenders with floating-rate books — can ride the wave. Margin-takers without it, particularly in textiles, chemicals and smaller manufacturing, will absorb the 9.76 per cent manufactured-input inflation in their profit and loss statements over the next two quarters. Earnings season, now underway, will show which camp each company belongs to.

Wholesale price inflation rose to 11.39% year-on-year in September 2026 from 9.92% in August, well above the 10.18% economists had forecast.
WHAT IS DRIVING WHOLESALE INFLATION?
Fuel and power inflation jumped to 25.85%, manufactured products to 9.76% and the wholesale food index to 8.66%, with mineral oils, crude petroleum, coal, basic metals and chemicals among the key drivers.
HOW DOES THIS RELATE TO RBI POLICY?
The RBI raised the repo rate to 5.5% in early October, its first hike since February 2023, citing inflation risks that the September WPI data has now confirmed.

GLOSSARY: - Wholesale Price Index (WPI): A measure of price changes at the producer and bulk level, covering goods before retail markups. - Base year: The reference period against which index values are compared; the WPI series was rebased to 2022-23 in June 2026. - Repo rate: The RBI's benchmark lending rate to banks, currently 5.5% after the October hike. - Primary articles: Unprocessed goods in the WPI basket, including food articles, non-food articles and minerals. - El Nino: A warming of Pacific Ocean waters that typically disrupts monsoon rainfall in India, affecting farm output.
