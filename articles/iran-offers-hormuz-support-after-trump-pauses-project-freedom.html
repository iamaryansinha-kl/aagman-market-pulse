Iran Offers Hormuz Support After Trump Pauses Project Freedom

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Iran's Ports and Maritime Organisation announced full preparedness to offer maritime, technical, and medical support to commercial vessels in the Strait of Hormuz on May 7, 2026
  • 02/The advisory comes a day after Trump temporarily paused "Project Freedom," an initiative aimed at facilitating commercial ship movement through the waterway
  • 03/Ship masters can contact Iranian Vessel Traffic Service centres on VHF channel 16; the blockade of Iran's ports remains in full effect

Iran's Ports and Maritime Organisation announced it is fully prepared to offer maritime support to commercial vessels. The advisory covers the strategically critical Strait of Hormuz and surrounding regional waters.

The announcement was issued on May 7, 2026, and carried by Islamic Republic News Agency. It was directed at ship commanders to ensure maritime safety and safeguard crew welfare.

The organisation stated ships may access provisions, fuel supply, medical assistance, and authorised repair materials. These services are available through Iranian ports and maritime communication systems.

The message will be broadcast three times daily for three consecutive days. Ship masters and owners can contact Vessel Traffic Service centres at Iranian ports on VHF channel 16.

The advisory comes a day after US President Donald Trump paused "Project Freedom." The initiative aimed to help commercial vessels trapped in the Strait of Hormuz amid escalating Gulf tensions.

Trump announced the pause on May 6 following requests from Pakistan, which is mediating between Washington and Tehran. The naval blockade of Iran's ports remains in place.

The US President cited "tremendous Military Success" and progress toward a potential agreement. The pause is intended to see whether a deal can be finalised and signed.

The Strait of Hormuz handles approximately one-fifth of global oil shipments. Disruptions to this route directly affect energy prices and worldwide supply chains.
