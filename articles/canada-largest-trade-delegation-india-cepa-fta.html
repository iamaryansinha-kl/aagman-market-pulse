Canada's Largest Trade Delegation In 30 Years Amid India FTA Push

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Canada is leading its largest trade delegation to India in over 30 years — nearly 300 business delegates — as CEPA negotiations advance.
  • 02/A WTO-model-based study estimates India's FTAs could add about $140 billion to GDP by 2035 if the EU and New Zealand deals kick in from 2027.
  • 03/Canada aims to double bilateral trade with India to $70 billion by 2030; both sides target concluding CEPA by end-2026.

Canada is leading its largest trade delegation to India in more than 30 years. Nearly 300 business delegates are travelling to explore partnerships as both countries advance a Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement.

Canadian International Trade Minister Maninder Sidhu announced the mission in a post on X. "Next stop: India," he wrote, adding that the delegation would build new partnerships across six sectors.

The mission focuses on aerospace, clean energy, technology, life sciences, agri-food and forestry. Canadian Foreign Minister Anita Anand is also visiting India on October 13.

Canada has set an ambitious goal for the relationship. It wants to more than double bilateral trade with India to $70 billion by 2030.

The fifth round of CEPA negotiations took place in Ottawa earlier this month. Both countries are targeting the conclusion of talks by the end of 2026.

The timing coincides with a broader acceleration in India's trade diplomacy. New Delhi has ended years of caution and is rapidly expanding its free-trade network.

A new analysis published by the Economic Times puts numbers on that push. Using a World Trade Organization-based computable general equilibrium model, it estimates India's trade deals could add about $140 billion to GDP by 2035.

The base calculation: agreements already in force should raise India's GDP by 1.2 per cent by 2035. That rises to 1.7 per cent if the European Union and New Zealand agreements are implemented from 2027.

A US-India interim deal would add another 0.2 percentage point. If negotiations with the Eurasian Economic Union, the Gulf Cooperation Council and Israel conclude, GDP could end up 2.1 per cent higher — about $170 billion — by 2035.

Trade volumes would grow even faster than GDP. Indian exports could be almost 10 per cent above baseline by 2035 if all the deals are implemented.

India's FTA map has filled out quickly. During 2025-26, agreements with the European Free Trade Association, the United Kingdom and Oman entered into force.

Those join the deals with Australia and the United Arab Emirates that took effect in 2022. India has also signed an FTA with New Zealand and concluded negotiations with the EU.

The urgency comes partly from Washington. The average tariff on Indian goods sold to the US has risen to 11.6 per cent from 2.4 per cent in 2024, reflecting the Trump administration's tariff regime.

By 2035, US tariffs would cut Indian goods exports to America by about one-fifth, the study estimates. The GDP hit, though, should be negligible because less than 2 per cent of India's output relies on US markets.

Relations have thawed somewhat this year. The US removed its Russia-related 25 per cent tariff on India and lowered reciprocal rates to 18 per cent in February 2026.

Under the Section 301 forced-labour action, India has been set a rate of 10 per cent — lower than the 12.5 per cent imposed on many other partners. That is paving the way for an interim bilateral deal.

Signing agreements, however, is only the first step. The study sketches two domestic scenarios that would multiply the gains.

The first is a further fall in production costs. Reforms, infrastructure investment and skills programmes worth a roughly 2 per cent efficiency gain could add about half a percentage point to GDP gains by 2035.

The second is labour. More than 40 per cent of Indian workers remain in agriculture, with high underemployment; shifting them toward manufacturing and services could add about 2 percentage points to GDP gains.

The comparison is Vietnam. There, agriculture's share of workers fell from over 40 per cent to around 25 per cent during industrialisation, and farm output per worker now exceeds India's by almost 60 per cent.

For Canada specifically, CEPA would slot into this expanding network. Energy, critical minerals and aerospace have been identified as priority areas, and the 300-delegate mission is designed to convert tariff access into actual deals.

The near-term calendar is busy: CEPA talks continue through this quarter, the EU and New Zealand agreements await implementation, and a US interim deal is under negotiation. India's trade agenda is as crowded as it has ever been.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
HOW BIG IS CANADA'S TRADE DELEGATION TO INDIA?
NEARLY 300 BUSINESS DELEGATES — CANADA'S LARGEST TRADE MISSION TO INDIA IN MORE THAN 30 YEARS, FOCUSED ON AEROSPACE, CLEAN ENERGY, TECHNOLOGY, LIFE SCIENCES, AGRI-FOOD AND FORESTRY, ACCORDING TO TRADE MINISTER MANINDER SIDHU.
WHAT IS THE GDP GAIN FROM INDIA'S FTAS?
ACCORDING TO A WTO-BASED MODEL ANALYSIS, FTAS IN FORCE SHOULD RAISE INDIA'S GDP BY 1.2% BY 2035, RISING TO 1.7% — ABOUT $140 BILLION — IF THE EU AND NEW ZEALAND DEALS ARE IMPLEMENTED FROM 2027, AND UP TO 2.1% ($170 BILLION) WITH MORE DEALS.
WHAT IS THE TARGET FOR CANADA-INDIA TRADE?
CANADA AIMS TO MORE THAN DOUBLE BILATERAL TRADE TO $70 BILLION BY 2030. BOTH COUNTRIES ARE TARGETING CONCLUSION OF THE CEPA NEGOTIATIONS BY THE END OF 2026.
HOW HAVE US TARIFFS AFFECTED INDIA'S EXPORTS?
THE AVERAGE TARIFF ON INDIAN GOODS SOLD TO THE US HAS RISEN TO 11.6% FROM 2.4% IN 2024. THE STUDY ESTIMATES THIS COULD CUT INDIAN GOODS EXPORTS TO THE US BY ABOUT ONE-FIFTH BY 2035.
WHAT WOULD MAXIMISE THE GAINS FROM FTAS?
THE STUDY CITES A ROUGHLY 2% DECLINE IN PRODUCTION COSTS FROM REFORMS (ADDING 0.5 PPT TO GDP GAINS) AND SHIFTING UNDEREMPLOYED FARM WORKERS — OVER 40% OF THE WORKFORCE — TO INDUSTRY AND SERVICES (ADDING ABOUT 2 PPTS). GLOSSARY: CEPA: Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement — a deep free-trade pact covering goods, services and investment rules. Computable general equilibrium model: An economic model that simulates how policy changes ripple through an entire economy. Non-tariff measures: Barriers other than tariffs — quotas, standards, licensing — that restrict trade. Section 301: A US trade law provision used to impose tariffs, including the forced-labour action covering India. Eurasian Economic Union: A Russia-centred bloc of five post-Soviet states with which India is pursuing a trade pact. Gulf Cooperation Council: A bloc of six Gulf Arab states including the UAE and Saudi Arabia, targeted for an India trade deal.
