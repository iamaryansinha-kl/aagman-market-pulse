India's Households Miss Global Shift to Financial Assets: UBS Report

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

India's savings habits are out of sync with the world.

A UBS report has found that Indian households are missing the global shift toward financial assets, continuing to prefer physical stores of wealth like gold and real estate even as equity markets hit record highs and mutual fund SIPs surge. The structural gap between India's savings patterns and global trends represents both a challenge and an opportunity for the country's financial sector.

Globally, households have been rotating into financial assets. In the US, Europe, and even emerging markets like China, the share of household wealth held in equities, bonds, and mutual funds has risen steadily over the past two decades. This shift has been driven by low interest rates, strong equity returns, and the democratisation of investing through technology platforms.

India's household balance sheet looks different. Gold and real estate together account for over 60% of household wealth, compared to less than 20% in the US. Financial assets — equities, mutual funds, bank deposits, and insurance — make up a much smaller share. Even within financial assets, bank fixed deposits dominate, with equities and mutual funds representing a tiny fraction.

The reasons are cultural and structural. Gold has deep cultural significance in India, used in weddings, festivals, and as a store of value passed down generations. Real estate is seen as a tangible, inflation-hedged asset that also provides rental income. Both have historically delivered returns that, while modest, felt safer than the volatility of equity markets.

The opportunity for financial services is massive. If Indian households were to shift even a small percentage of their physical asset holdings into financial assets, the inflow would be enormous. The mutual fund industry, which currently manages around Rs 60 lakh crore, could potentially grow several-fold if penetration rates approached global averages.

SIPs are changing the narrative, but slowly. The surge in systematic investment plans, with monthly inflows crossing Rs 25,000 crore, shows that a new generation of investors is embracing financial assets. But this is still a small fraction of total household savings. The vast majority of India's wealth remains locked in gold lockers and property deeds.
