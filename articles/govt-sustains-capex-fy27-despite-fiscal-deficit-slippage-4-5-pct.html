Govt Sticks to ₹12.22 Lakh Crore Capex as FY27 Deficit Hits 4.5% of GDP

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Govt plans ₹12.22 lakh crore capex for FY27, prioritising highways, railways, ports and urban development despite fiscal stress
  • 02/FY27 fiscal deficit revised to 4.5% of GDP from budgeted 4.3%, after downward revision in nominal GDP under new series
  • 03/Crude oil hit $126/barrel, excise duty cuts cost ₹7,000 crore; tax buoyancy and import risks remain key watchpoints

The government will maintain its ₹12.22 lakh crore capital expenditure plan for FY27 despite mounting fiscal stress. Expenditure Secretary V Vualnam confirmed the commitment at the ICPP Growth Conference on Friday.

The FY27 Budget had pegged the fiscal deficit at 4.3% of GDP. It is now seen at 4.5% following a downward revision in India's nominal GDP under the new series.

Fiscal stress stems from the ongoing West Asia conflict that began on February 28. Crude oil prices soared to a four-year high of $126 per barrel from about $73 before the war.

To contain retail fuel prices, the government cut excise duties on petrol and diesel in late March. The move is estimated to cost the exchequer ₹7,000 crore for a 15-day period.

"The fiscal stress is indeed very much a reality, but at the same time... the capex would really be a priority item," Vualnam said. He stressed highways, railways, shipping, ports and urban development as focus areas.

India imports 60% of its LPG, and 90% of that flows through the now-closed Strait of Hormuz. This poses a "very challenging situation" for energy security and import costs.

Tax buoyancy could take a hit from the excise duty cut, further squeezing fiscal space. The next few quarters and the coming year will possibly see "a lot of stress points", the official warned.

The government has also levied an export duty of ₹23 per litre on diesel and ₹33 per litre on aviation turbine fuel. These duty changes are reviewed every fortnight. Despite the pressures, India's fiscal prudence has put the country in "very good stead", Vualnam noted, and the Centre remains committed to funding capex.

THE FY27 BUDGET PEGGED THE FISCAL DEFICIT AT 4.3% OF GDP. FOLLOWING A DOWNWARD REVISION IN INDIA'S NOMINAL GDP UNDER THE NEW SERIES, IT IS NOW SEEN AT 4.5% OF GDP.

THE ONGOING WEST ASIA CONFLICT HAS PUSHED CRUDE OIL TO $126 PER BARREL FROM ABOUT $73. EXCISE DUTY CUTS ON PETROL AND DIESEL COST THE EXCHEQUER ₹7,000 CRORE FOR 15 DAYS, IMPACTING TAX BUOYANCY.

HIGHWAYS, RAILWAYS, SHIPPING, PORTS AND URBAN DEVELOPMENT SECTORS ARE THE KEY FOCUS AREAS FOR CAPITAL EXPENDITURE IN THE CURRENT FISCAL YEAR, AS STATED BY THE EXPENDITURE SECRETARY.

THE GOVERNMENT HAS LEVIED AN EXPORT DUTY OF ₹23 PER LITRE ON DIESEL AND ₹33 PER LITRE ON AVIATION TURBINE FUEL TO ENSURE ADEQUATE DOMESTIC AVAILABILITY. THESE DUTIES ARE REVIEWED EVERY FORTNIGHT.
