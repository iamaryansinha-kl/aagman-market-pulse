Non-Financial CPSE Dividends May Hit Record ₹80,000 Crore in FY27

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Centre expects non-financial CPSE dividends to touch record ₹80,000 crore in FY27
  • 02/FY26 collections stood at ₹78,438.07 crore; FY27 budget estimate is ₹75,000 crore
  • 03/Driven by oil and gas, coal, power, mining companies; dividend policy requires 30% of PAT or 5% of net worth

The Centre expects dividend receipts from non-financial Central Public Sector Enterprises to touch a record ₹80,000 crore in 2026-27. This would exceed the budget estimate of ₹75,000 crore, according to people aware of the matter.

If the estimates materialize, collections would surpass the ₹78,438.07 crore collected in FY26. This reflects sustained profitability across government-owned enterprises despite continued capital expenditure commitments in key sectors.

"The anticipated record payout is being driven by healthy earnings from oil and gas, coal, power, mining and infrastructure companies. This is coupled with the government's policy of encouraging profitable CPSEs to maintain consistent dividend payouts while balancing their capex requirements," said a person familiar with the development.

Dipam has long followed a dividend policy under which CPSEs are encouraged to pay a minimum annual dividend of 30% of profit after tax or 5% of net worth, whichever is higher. This is subject to the financial health and investment requirements of the enterprise.

Dividend receipts from non-financial CPSEs have risen steadily over the past few years. The Centre collected about ₹39,750 crore in FY21, ₹59,294 crore in FY22, ₹59,533 crore in FY23, ₹64,030 crore in FY24, ₹74,128.63 crore in FY25, and ₹78,438.07 crore in FY26.

"The steady increase in dividend receipts also reflects a pattern of actual collections consistently exceeding the government's budget estimates. The FY27 projection of around ₹80,000 crore suggests that this trend is likely to continue," the person added.

Although collections in the current fiscal year have begun modestly at around ₹2,025 crore, dividend inflows are expected to gather pace. Major CPSEs will declare interim and final dividends over the course of the year.

The Union budget has projected total receipts under the head "Dividends and Profits" at ₹3.91 trillion for FY27. This is higher than the ₹3.75 trillion for FY26 revised estimates. Dividends from public sector enterprises have been budgeted at ₹75,000 crore for FY27, compared with ₹71,000 crore revised estimates for FY26.

Dividends from non-financial CPSEs primarily represent payouts from operational profits of enterprises engaged in production, services, and infrastructure activities. These form the core of the government's non-tax revenue buoyancy.

"The fact that non-financial PSUs are earning profits and paying dividends to the government is a positive development. It means they are supporting the government's finances while also enhancing shareholder value," said Madan Sabnavis, chief economist at Bank of Baroda.

"The current year has seen expenditure rise due to the West Asia war and the possibility of lower agricultural output affecting consumer spending. Higher dividend receipts will strengthen non-tax revenue and make it an important contributor to the government's overall revenue generation," he added.

In contrast, dividends from financial CPSEs, including nationalized banks and the Reserve Bank of India, are accounted under a separate sub-head. RBI surplus transfers and bank dividends pushed total dividends and profits to ₹3,08,424 crore in FY24-25 actuals.
