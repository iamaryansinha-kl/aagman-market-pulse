Core Sector Output Jumps 7.9% in September on Coal, Steel

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Output across India's nine core infrastructure sectors rose 7.9% year-on-year in September, versus 3.1% in the same month last year.
  • •- August growth was revised to 5.9%.
  • •- Iron ore led with 18.4% growth, followed by electricity at 11.4%, coal at 10.7% and refinery products at 10.3%.
  • •- Steel grew 4.9% and cement 9.8%.
  • •- Fertilisers, natural gas and crude oil contracted during the month.
  • •- The core index carries roughly 40% weight in the Index of Industrial Production.

India's heavy industry put in its strongest month of the year just as the central bank was turning cautious on inflation. Output across the nine core infrastructure sectors — coal, crude oil, natural gas, refinery products, fertilisers, steel, cement, electricity and iron ore — rose 7.9% year-on-year in September, government data released on Wednesday showed, more than double the 3.1% recorded in the same month of 2025 and accelerating from a revised 5.9% in August. Because the core index accounts for about 40% of the weight in the Index of Industrial Production, September's print points to firm industrial momentum heading into the festive quarter and, more broadly, to an economy whose supply side is still accelerating into the year's final stretch.

The composition is the interesting part. Iron ore was the standout, growing 18.4% — the fastest of any core sector and a number that speaks to both steel demand and export appetite for raw material. Electricity generation rose 11.4%, consistent with an economy running hot through an extended summer and early festive demand. Coal output grew 10.7% and refinery products 10.3%, the latter reflecting healthy crude throughput at India's refineries even with global supply risks elevating input costs. Cement, the sector closest to ground-level construction activity, expanded 9.8% — a strong confirmation that the building cycle remains intact, with the government's infrastructure pipeline and private capex on data centres and manufacturing plants both feeding demand. Steel production grew a more modest 4.9%, respectable in a month when global steel sentiment was clouded by Chinese overcapacity concerns.

Three sectors went backwards. Fertilisers, natural gas and crude oil all contracted in September. The crude and gas declines are structural as much as cyclical — India's maturing fields continue their long depletion curve even as demand rises — while the fertiliser slip sits oddly against the year's crop cycle and may reflect inventory timing rather than demand weakness. For the inflation debate, the mixed message matters: the index confirms real-economy strength, but the same oil-and-gas weakness that drags on output also feeds the import bill now that crude trades above $110 a barrel.

One methodological footnote deserves mention. Wednesday's release came from the revamped Index of Core Industries, which uses 2022-23 as its base year and counts iron ore as a ninth sector — the series that replaced the older 2011-12-based index. Base-year revisions typically flatter fast-growing new-economy weights, and the iron ore inclusion gives the index more exposure to export-linked commodities than its predecessor had; some of September's 7.9% reflects that arithmetic as well as genuine acceleration. Even discounting for the rebasing effect, however, a print more than double the year-ago number, with seven of nine sectors expanding, is not a statistical mirage. It is also worth holding the sector-level dispersion in view: the spread between iron ore's 18.4% and crude oil's contraction is the widest in months, and dispersion that wide usually signals an economy in transition — old-economy inputs accelerating, hydrocarbon intensity fading — rather than one uniformly overheating.

The data lands hours after the Reserve Bank of India published the minutes of its October policy meeting, which raised the growth forecast for 2026-27 to 7.1% while flagging building inflationary pressure. Wednesday's core-sector numbers validate the growth side of that judgment — an economy producing 7.9% more coal, power and cement than a year ago is not one visibly throttled by tighter financial conditions. They complicate the inflation side in equal measure: electricity generation running 11.4% above last year implies demand so robust that utilities have pricing room, and refinery products up 10.3% reflects throughput discipline rather than demand destruction, even as crude sits above $110. The question the index cannot answer is whether that momentum survives the oil shock: every sector in September's strong print runs on energy, and the energy input price has jumped since the month closed. October's core-sector data, due next month, will be the first to show whether strength or cost wins that contest.

For markets, the print is mildly bond-negative and equity-neutral. Firm core output reduces the risk of a growth scare justifying aggressive rate hikes, but it also does nothing to soften the inflation case the MPC minutes laid out; swap markets already price roughly 100 basis points of tightening over a year. It also reframes the evening's other signal — the 6.94% 2036 benchmark yield touching its highest since March 2023 on Wednesday was not a market fighting the RBI but a market agreeing with it, and strong activity data removes the one argument (growth fragility) that might have pushed the committee to delay. For policymakers, the comfort is that India's industrial engine was accelerating before the oil premium arrived — which means the economy enters the shock from strength, with the cushion of the RBI's own upgraded forecast rather than against a downgraded one.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT DOES THE CORE SECTOR DATA SHOW FOR SEPTEMBER?
Output across the nine core infrastructure industries grew 7.9% year-on-year, up from 3.1% a year earlier and 5.9% in August (revised).
WHICH SECTORS GREW FASTEST?
Iron ore rose 18.4%, electricity 11.4%, coal 10.7%, refinery products 10.3% and cement 9.8%.
WHICH SECTORS CONTRACTED?
Fertilisers, natural gas and crude oil registered declines in September.
WHY DOES THE DATA MATTER?
The core industries carry about 40% of the weight in the Index of Industrial Production, so the reading is a leading indicator of broader factory output and economic momentum. GLOSSARY: - Core sector: Nine infrastructure industries whose output is tracked monthly as a proxy for industrial activity. - Index of Industrial Production: The broader monthly measure of factory output, of which core sectors form about 40%. - Refinery products: Outputs such as petrol, diesel and jet fuel produced from crude oil processing. - Kharif: India's monsoon-season crop cycle, sown June-July and harvested in autumn.
