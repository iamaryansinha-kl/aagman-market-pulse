GIFT City Eyes Direct Equity Listings Without IPO, Says IFSCA Official

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/IFSCA is pushing a framework for companies to list equity shares directly on GIFT City exchanges without an initial public offering, an official said at the ET Alpha Wealth Summit 2.0 on September 30, 2026
  • 02/Proposed eligibility thresholds include minimum revenue of $20 million, pre-tax profit of $1 million, and expected post-listing market capitalisation of $50 million
  • 03/The framework was first laid out in a consultation paper issued on July 13, 2026, and would be enabled via Regulation 40 of the IFSCA (LODR) Regulations, 2024

The International Financial Services Centres Authority is pushing a framework that would let companies list equity shares directly on GIFT City exchanges. An IFSCA official said this at the ET Alpha Wealth Summit 2.0 on September 30, 2026.

The framework was first laid out in a consultation paper released on July 13, 2026. Public comments on the paper closed on August 3, 2026.

Under a standard initial public offering, a company launches a fresh share sale and price discovery happens through book-building. The proposed GIFT City route removes that step entirely.

In a direct listing, shares list on the exchange without a fresh public offer. Existing shareholders can sell into the market, and the company can still list new shares directly.

The eligibility bar is set for growth-stage companies. Proposed thresholds include minimum revenue of $20 million and pre-tax profit of $1 million.

There is also a size requirement at listing. The company must have an expected post-listing market capitalisation of $50 million.

Price discovery would work differently from an IPO book-building process. An independent registered valuer would set a valuation for the company.

That valuation would then be tested through a pre-open auction on the exchange. The auction, rather than institutional book-building, would determine the opening price.

Disclosure requirements are lighter than a standard public issue. Companies would file an "information document" prepared with the help of an investment banker.

That document is a scaled-down version of the draft red herring prospectus used in Indian IPOs. The intent is to cut both listing cost and time.

Foreign issuers get a distinct advantage under the proposal. The minimum public shareholding for them is set at 10%.

That compares with the 25% minimum public shareholding required for domestic Indian listings on mainland exchanges. The lower bar is designed to attract foreign companies seeking a second listing venue.

The legal basis already exists. The framework would be enabled through Regulation 40 of the IFSCA (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2024.

The rationale is market depth. GIFT IFSC wants to attract growth-stage companies and already-listed or foreign companies that want an additional venue.

GIFT City, or Gujarat International Finance Tec-City, is India's flagship international financial services centre. Its exchanges include NSE IX, BSE IX and India INX.

These venues already host GIFT Nifty derivatives, debt listings and alternative investment fund vehicles. Equity direct listings would add a new asset class to that stack.

For growth companies, the appeal is speed and cost. A direct listing without an IPO avoids the book-building process and its associated fees.

For foreign issuers, the 10% public float requirement lowers the dilution hurdle. That could make GIFT City a cheaper second venue than a full Indian IPO.

What happens next depends on the consultation outcome. The final shape of the regulations and the timing of the first direct listing remain to be seen.

WHAT DID THE IFSCA OFFICIAL ANNOUNCE AT THE ET ALPHA WEALTH SUMMIT 2.0?
ON SEPTEMBER 30, 2026, AN IFSCA OFFICIAL SAID THE AUTHORITY IS PUSHING A FRAMEWORK THAT WOULD ALLOW COMPANIES TO LIST EQUITY SHARES DIRECTLY ON GIFT CITY EXCHANGES WITHOUT AN INITIAL PUBLIC OFFERING. THE STATEMENT WAS REPORTED BY THE ECONOMIC TIMES.
WHAT ARE THE PROPOSED ELIGIBILITY THRESHOLDS FOR A DIRECT LISTING AT GIFT CITY?
THE CONSULTATION PAPER PROPOSES A MINIMUM REVENUE OF $20 MILLION, A PRE-TAX PROFIT OF $1 MILLION, AND AN EXPECTED POST-LISTING MARKET CAPITALISATION OF $50 MILLION FOR COMPANIES SEEKING A DIRECT LISTING.
HOW WOULD PRICE DISCOVERY WORK WITHOUT AN IPO BOOK-BUILDING PROCESS?
VALUATION WOULD BE SET BY AN INDEPENDENT REGISTERED VALUER. THE OPENING PRICE WOULD THEN BE DISCOVERED THROUGH A PRE-OPEN AUCTION ON THE EXCHANGE INSTEAD OF INSTITUTIONAL BOOK-BUILDING.
WHAT DISCLOSURE DOCUMENT IS REQUIRED UNDER THE PROPOSED FRAMEWORK?
COMPANIES WOULD PREPARE AN "INFORMATION DOCUMENT" WITH THE HELP OF AN INVESTMENT BANKER. THIS IS LIGHTER THAN THE FULL DRAFT RED HERRING PROSPECTUS REQUIRED FOR A STANDARD PUBLIC ISSUE.
WHAT IS THE MINIMUM PUBLIC SHAREHOLDING PROPOSED FOR FOREIGN ISSUERS?
FOREIGN ISSUERS WOULD NEED A MINIMUM PUBLIC SHAREHOLDING OF 10%, COMPARED WITH THE 25% REQUIREMENT FOR DOMESTIC INDIAN LISTINGS ON MAINLAND EXCHANGES. THE FRAMEWORK WOULD BE ENABLED VIA REGULATION 40 OF THE IFSCA (LODR) REGULATIONS, 2024.
