SpaceX Eyes $1.75 Trillion IPO: How Elon Musk Turned PayPal Cash into the Biggest Debut in History

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •• SpaceX is targeting an IPO as early as June 12 on Nasdaq at a valuation near $1.75 trillion, potentially raising $75 billion
  • •• The company was founded by Elon Musk in 2002 using proceeds from the PayPal sale, and nearly went bankrupt before its first successful launch in 2008
  • •• BlackRock is reportedly discussing a $5-10 billion investment in the IPO, and Musk recently combined SpaceX with his xAI startup in a $250 billion deal

SpaceX is preparing for what could become the biggest initial public offering in stock market history, with reports indicating a targeted valuation of nearly $1.75 trillion and a potential raise of around $75 billion.

According to Reuters and multiple reports, the company founded by Elon Musk is aiming for a Nasdaq listing as early as June 12, following a confidential filing.

The journey began in 2002 when Musk used proceeds from the sale of PayPal to launch SpaceX, at a time when private space exploration was widely considered unrealistic and financially dangerous.

The company's early years were filled with failures, including the Falcon 1 rocket failing during its first launch in 2006, with several subsequent attempts also going wrong.

SpaceX finally achieved a breakthrough in September 2008 when Falcon 1 successfully reached Earth's orbit, becoming the first privately developed liquid-fuel rocket to achieve this milestone.

Just months later, NASA awarded SpaceX its first major contract to transport cargo and supplies to the International Space Station, providing the financial credibility that helped the company survive a period when Musk later admitted it was close to running out of money.

Over the next decade, SpaceX steadily transformed the global space industry, with a Dragon capsule becoming the first privately built spacecraft to dock with the ISS in 2012.

The company's biggest breakthrough came in December 2015 when it successfully landed a Falcon 9 rocket vertically after launch, demonstrating reusable rockets and dramatically changing launch economics.

SpaceX later expanded beyond rockets, launching the Starlink satellite internet business in 2019, which has since become one of the company's largest revenue drivers with thousands of satellites orbiting Earth.

The company deepened its NASA relationship by completing the first operational crew mission in 2020 and winning contracts linked to NASA's Artemis moon mission.

At the same time, SpaceX aggressively pursued deep-space ambitions through Starship, its next-generation heavy rocket designed for missions to the moon and Mars, though development has suffered delays and multiple explosive failures.

Investor appetite remains extremely strong because SpaceX now operates across satellite internet, launch services, defense technology, AI-linked infrastructure, and future space transportation.

According to The Information, BlackRock has discussed investing between $5 billion and $10 billion in the IPO through its actively managed funds.

Earlier this year, SpaceX also acquired Musk's artificial intelligence startup xAI in a deal reportedly valued at $250 billion, combining AI and space businesses under a broader technology strategy.
