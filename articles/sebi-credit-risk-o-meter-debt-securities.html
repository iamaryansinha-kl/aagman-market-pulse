Sebi Mandates Colour-Coded Credit Risk-O-Meter for Debt Securities

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Sebi on October 7, 2026 made a six-tier colour-coded credit risk-o-meter mandatory for all debt securities, from AAA-rated "lowest credit risk" to B+ to D "high to very high risk of default"
  • 02/Disclosure is required in offer documents, abridged prospectuses, private placement memorandums, advertisements, and on the web and mobile channels of online bond platform providers
  • 03/Where a security carries multiple ratings, the meter must reflect the lowest one; unsecured instruments must carry the word "unsecured" in bold red text

The Securities and Exchange Board of India (Sebi) on Wednesday, October 7, 2026, introduced a mandatory colour-coded credit risk-o-meter for debt securities. The regulator wants credit risk to be easier for potential investors to understand.

The meter translates Sebi's existing credit rating framework into six colour-coded categories. It runs from "lowest credit risk" for AAA-rated paper to "high to very high risk of default" for B+ to D ratings.

The intermediate bands are very low, low and moderate credit risk, followed by moderate risk of default. Each colour band maps to a defined slice of the rating scale, so the grade is visible before the alphabet soup.

The mandate covers all listed and proposed-to-be-listed non-convertible securities, commercial papers, securitised debt instruments, security receipts, and structured or market-linked debentures. It applies to public issues and private placements alike.

Retail participation in corporate bonds has surged through online bond platforms and mutual funds. But rating alphabets — AAA, AA, A, BBB — confuse many first-time buyers. The colour-coded meter turns those letters into an at-a-glance risk signal.

Disclosure is mandatory in offer documents, the abridged prospectus, private placement memorandums and advertisements. Online bond platform providers (OBPPs) must also display the meter on their web and mobile channels.

Issuers and OBPPs must disclose the name of the credit rating agency and the actual rating below the meter. The colour band supplements the rating; it does not replace it.

Where a security carries multiple ratings, the meter must reflect the lowest one. This closes a marketing loophole where an issuer could spotlight its most generous rating while softer ones stayed buried in the fine print.

For unsecured debt instruments, the word "unsecured" must be displayed in bold red text. That flag is the sharpest consumer-protection element in the framework, since retail buyers often miss the absence of security cover.

If a rating agency has classified an issuer as "Issuer Not Cooperating", the meter must specifically indicate that status. Short-term debt instruments carry separate prescribed disclosures under the framework.

Issuers must include a disclaimer stating that the meter evaluates the credit risk of the issuer and does not constitute investment advice. The band describes the issuer's credit profile, not whether the bond suits a particular buyer's goals.

The framework standardises how credit risk is presented across prospectuses, platforms and advertisements. Its real test will be whether OBPPs surface the meter prominently at the point of sale — and whether the lowest-rating rule steers buyers away from selectively marketed paper.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT HAS SEBI INTRODUCED FOR DEBT SECURITIES?
SEBI ON OCTOBER 7, 2026 INTRODUCED A MANDATORY SIX-TIER COLOUR-CODED CREDIT RISK-O-METER FOR DEBT SECURITIES. IT TRANSLATES THE EXISTING CREDIT RATING FRAMEWORK INTO SIX COLOUR BANDS, FROM "LOWEST CREDIT RISK" FOR AAA-RATED PAPER TO "HIGH TO VERY HIGH RISK OF DEFAULT" FOR B+ TO D RATINGS. THE MOVE IS AIMED AT MAKING CREDIT RISK EASIER FOR POTENTIAL INVESTORS TO UNDERSTAND.
WHICH INSTRUMENTS DOES THE RISK-O-METER RULE COVER?
THE MANDATE APPLIES TO ALL LISTED AND PROPOSED-TO-BE-LISTED NON-CONVERTIBLE SECURITIES, COMMERCIAL PAPERS, SECURITISED DEBT INSTRUMENTS, SECURITY RECEIPTS, AND STRUCTURED OR MARKET-LINKED DEBENTURES. IT COVERS BOTH PUBLIC ISSUES AND PRIVATE PLACEMENTS, SO NO ISSUANCE ROUTE ESCAPES THE DISCLOSURE REQUIREMENT.
WHERE MUST THE RISK-O-METER BE DISCLOSED?
DISCLOSURE IS MANDATORY IN OFFER DOCUMENTS, THE ABRIDGED PROSPECTUS, PRIVATE PLACEMENT MEMORANDUMS AND ADVERTISEMENTS. ONLINE BOND PLATFORM PROVIDERS (OBBPS) MUST DISPLAY THE METER ON THEIR WEB AND MOBILE CHANNELS. ISSUERS AND OBBPS MUST ALSO SHOW THE NAME OF THE CREDIT RATING AGENCY AND THE ACTUAL RATING BELOW THE METER.
WHAT HAPPENS WHEN A SECURITY HAS MULTIPLE RATINGS?
WHEN A SECURITY CARRIES RATINGS FROM MORE THAN ONE CREDIT RATING AGENCY, THE METER MUST REFLECT THE LOWEST RATING. ISSUERS CANNOT HIGHLIGHT A HIGHER RATING WHILE DOWNPLAYING A LOWER ONE. IF AN AGENCY HAS CLASSIFIED THE ISSUER AS "ISSUER NOT COOPERATING", THE METER MUST SPECIFICALLY INDICATE THAT STATUS.
HOW ARE UNSECURED DEBT INSTRUMENTS FLAGGED?
FOR UNSECURED DEBT INSTRUMENTS, THE WORD "UNSECURED" MUST BE DISPLAYED IN BOLD RED TEXT. ISSUERS MUST ALSO INCLUDE A DISCLAIMER STATING THAT THE METER EVALUATES THE CREDIT RISK OF THE ISSUER AND DOES NOT CONSTITUTE INVESTMENT ADVICE. SHORT-TERM DEBT INSTRUMENTS, MEANWHILE, CARRY SEPARATE PRESCRIBED DISCLOSURES. GLOSSARY: Credit rating: A grade assigned by a registered agency (CRISIL, ICRA, CARE etc.) measuring an issuer's ability to repay debt. AAA is the highest grade. OBPP (Online Bond Platform Provider): SEBI-regulated apps and websites, such as BondSahulat-style marketplaces, where retail investors buy corporate bonds. Examples include platforms like Jiraaf, Wint Wealth and bond sections of broker apps. Commercial paper: Short-term unsecured debt issued by companies to fund working capital, typically for 7 days to one year. Debenture: A debt instrument — essentially an IOU — through which a company borrows from investors at a fixed interest rate.
