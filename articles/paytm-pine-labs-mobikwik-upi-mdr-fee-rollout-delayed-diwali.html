Paytm, Pine Labs Fall 7% as UPI MDR Rollout Delayed Past Diwali

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- NPCI delayed the rollout of merchant discount rate fees on select UPI transactions beyond Diwali, missing the festive season window.
  • •- The new fee structure was scheduled to take effect from October 15 and applies to select person-to-merchant UPI transactions above Rs 2,000.
  • •- Paytm parent One 97 Communications fell more than 7% to an intraday low of Rs 1,571; Pine Labs dropped over 5% to a low of Rs 154.42.
  • •- Mobikwik shares slipped about 4% in Tuesday's session.
  • •- Trade bodies AICPDF and AIMRA had sought deferment, a higher threshold and exclusion of merchant-to-merchant transactions.

Shares of India's listed fintech companies sold off sharply on Tuesday afternoon after the National Payments Corporation of India said the rollout of merchant discount rate fees on select Unified Payments Interface transactions would be delayed beyond Diwali. The deferral pushes the new fee regime past the festive season that payment platforms had expected to convert into revenue.

Paytm parent One 97 Communications plunged more than 7% during the session, hitting an intraday low of Rs 1,571 apiece. Pine Labs dropped more than 5% to its day's low of Rs 154.42. Mobikwik shares declined about 4%. All three companies had been positioned as the biggest beneficiaries of the MDR regime, making the delay a direct hit to near-term earnings expectations.

NPCI, which operates the UPI rails, said in a statement on Tuesday that the introduction of MDR on select UPI payments was pushed beyond Diwali and that a final implementation date would be communicated to stakeholders soon. The fee structure had been scheduled to take effect from October 15.

The announced structure covers select person-to-merchant UPI transactions above Rs 2,000, with NPCI announcing the merchant discount rate last month. If implemented as announced, it would mark the first time a large share of UPI merchant payments carry an explicit transaction fee, ending India's long-standing stance of zero-cost UPI payments for merchants.

The delay follows sustained pushback from retail trade associations. The All India Consumer Products Distributors Federation and the All India Mobile Retailers Association sought deferment of the proposed MDR in view of the festive season and heightened business activity. They also asked for a review of the Rs 1 lakh threshold, arguing it should be raised to Rs 5 lakh, and sought exclusion of merchant-to-merchant transactions from the fee regime, describing such transactions as an important part of the trading ecosystem.

The trade bodies had earlier called for a "New UPI Day" protest on October 2 but withdrew the call after meeting Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman. "In view of the constructive discussions and the assurance that the concerns raised by the trading community will receive due consideration, the All India Consumer Products Distributors Federation and the All India Mobile Retailers Association have decided to withdraw the protest call," Praveen Khandelwal, secretary general of the Confederation of All India Traders, said after the meeting.

For investors, the math is straightforward. Payment platforms earn nothing on the vast majority of UPI transactions today, and the MDR regime was expected to open a monetisation channel precisely during the festive quarter, when transaction volumes peak. Brokerage commentary had framed Paytm as the likely biggest winner in absolute terms, given its merchant base, with Pine Labs positioned to gain faster on its point-of-sale-heavy mix. A delay beyond Diwali removes that seasonal tailwind from the current fiscal half.

The stocks' reaction also reflects how much expectation had been built in. Fintech shares have rallied repeatedly on MDR headlines over the past month, and Tuesday's intraday losses unwound a portion of those gains even as Paytm recovered from its session lows later in the afternoon. Even with that partial recovery, the stock closed the day sharply lower, with traders repricing the near-term revenue opportunity that the Diwali window had promised. The longer-term thesis — that UPI monetisation eventually arrives in some form — was not invalidated by the delay, but its timing is now squarely in NPCI's hands.

For merchants, the deferral keeps UPI acceptance free through the entire festival season and into the new year. That preserves the status quo that small traders have defended since the MDR proposal surfaced, and it means the debate over who pays for India's digital payment infrastructure will continue into the next calendar quarter.

The episode highlights a structural question the industry has debated for years. UPI has run without merchant fees since its launch, subsidised by banks and payment platforms that treated it as a customer-acquisition engine. As volumes scaled into the tens of billions of transactions a month, the economics of permanently free payments became harder to sustain, and NPCI's MDR proposal was the first serious attempt to attach a fee to the merchant side of the network.

What happens next depends on three moving parts: the revised date NPCI communicates, whether the trade bodies' demands on threshold and merchant-to-merchant exclusions are accommodated, and how payment companies model their revenue run-rate once a firm schedule exists. Until then, every MDR headline is likely to remain a tradable event for the fintech cohort. The sector's shares have also been among the market's more volatile largecaps this year, and quick profit-taking after the afternoon fall showed some investors locking in recent gains rather than reassessing the longer monetisation story, which most analysts still expect to unfold over several quarters once a revised implementation calendar is published and absorbed into earnings models.

GLOSSARY: - MDR (Merchant Discount Rate): A fee charged on digital payment transactions, shared between the payment service provider and issuing entities. - UPI (Unified Payments Interface): India's real-time payments system operated by NPCI, historically free for person-to-merchant transactions. - NPCI: National Payments Corporation of India, the umbrella organisation operating retail payment systems including UPI. - P2M (Person-to-Merchant): UPI transaction category where an individual pays a business, as distinct from person-to-person transfers. - Intraday low: The lowest price a stock trades at during a single trading session.
