Rupee Faces Fresh Strain as Fed Hawkish Tilt and $121 Oil Worsen Outlook

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Rupee expected to open at 94.90-94.95, heading for third straight weekly decline after wiping out April gains
  • 02/Brent crude near $121/barrel, set for ~14% weekly jump; Fed's most divided vote since 1992 lifts dollar and US yields
  • 03/Foreign investors sold net $210.7 million Indian shares and $10.9 million bonds on April 28

The Indian rupee is set to face fresh headwinds on Thursday. Hawkish signals from some Federal Reserve policymakers have lifted the dollar and US bond yields, compounding pressure on the currency.

The rupee is expected to open in the 94.90-94.95 range. It settled at 94.8450 on Wednesday, according to traders in Mumbai.

The currency is on course for its third consecutive weekly decline. It has wiped out nearly all gains made earlier in April following rare central bank measures aimed at curbing excessive speculation.

The Federal Reserve left policy rates unchanged overnight. The decision was its most divided since 1992, with three officials dissenting over guidance that still signalled a bias towards easing.

Oil prices extended gains in Asia trade on Thursday. Brent crude futures were last quoted at nearly $121 per barrel, putting them on course for a weekly jump of about 14%.

Surging oil prices and weakness in capital flows are contributing to a persistent bias towards rupee weakness. Foreign investors sold a net $210.7 million worth of Indian shares on April 28, as per NSDL data.

"Pending price pressures are clearly seeping into more cautious views, but the bar to hike rates is still high," said Tai Hui, APAC chief market strategist at J.P. Morgan Asset Management.

Offshore markets remain tilted towards positioning for further rupee depreciation. Onshore moves are being driven largely by real dollar demand after the central bank's curbs on banks' positions dampened speculative activity.
