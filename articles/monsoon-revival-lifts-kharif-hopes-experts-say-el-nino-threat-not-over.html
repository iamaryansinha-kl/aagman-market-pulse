Monsoon Revival Lifts Kharif Hopes, but Experts Say El Nino Threat Is Not Over

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

The monsoon is showing signs of life, but caution remains.

A revival in monsoon rainfall across key agricultural regions has lifted hopes for kharif crop sowing, but weather experts warn that the El Nino threat is not yet over. The weather pattern could still disrupt rainfall distribution in the coming weeks, making it too early to declare the monsoon season a success.

Kharif crops depend heavily on the June-September monsoon. Rice, cotton, soybean, and pulses are all sown during this period, and adequate rainfall is critical for germination and early growth. The delayed start to the monsoon had caused concern about lower acreage and yields.

The revival has been notable in Central and South India. Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Telangana, and Karnataka have received above-normal rainfall in recent days, allowing farmers to accelerate sowing. However, Northwest India, particularly Punjab and Haryana, remains relatively dry.

El Nino is the lingering concern. The weather phenomenon, associated with warming of the Pacific Ocean, typically suppresses monsoon rainfall in India. While the current El Nino is weak, meteorologists warn that its impact could still be felt in the second half of the monsoon season, which is crucial for crop maturation.

Water reservoir levels are a key indicator. The Central Water Commission monitors 150 major reservoirs, and their levels determine irrigation availability for the winter rabi crop. If monsoon rainfall is inadequate, reservoir levels could remain low, affecting agricultural output for the entire year.

The government is monitoring closely. The agriculture ministry tracks sowing progress weekly, and contingency plans are in place if rainfall remains patchy. Drought-prone regions have been identified, and seed and fertiliser subsidies are being directed to areas where sowing has been delayed.
