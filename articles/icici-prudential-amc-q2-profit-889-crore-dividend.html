ICICI Prudential AMC Q2 Profit Up 6% to Rs 889 Crore, Rs 30 Dividend

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

ICICI Prudential Asset Management Company, India's second-largest asset manager, on Monday reported a steady July-September quarter, with profit rising 6.1% year-on-year to Rs 889 crore even as a sharp climb in operating expenses capped margin expansion.

Revenue for the second quarter of FY27 grew 16.3% to Rs 1,674 crore, reflecting the continued flood of household savings into mutual funds. Total expenses, however, climbed faster at 17.9% to Rs 493 crore, a trend the company has flagged as it invests in distribution reach, technology and brand building amid intense competition for the systematic investment plan flows that now dominate industry inflows.

The scale numbers underline why asset managers are viewed as one of the cleanest proxies on India's financialisation theme. Quarter-end average assets under management, or QAAUM, stood at Rs 11.61 lakh crore, up 14.4% from Rs 10.15 lakh crore a year ago, giving the company a 13.3% share of the industry's managed pool. Within that, actively managed mutual fund QAAUM was Rs 9.59 lakh crore, up 11.1%, also with a 13.3% market share.

Equity remains the engine. Equity AUM grew 16.3% year-on-year to Rs 6.59 lakh crore, translating to a 13.7% market share, while the equity and hybrid complex expanded 19.7% to Rs 2.29 lakh crore with a dominant 26.4% share. Those numbers matter because equity and hybrid products carry higher fee rates than passive or debt funds, so mix shifts within the book directly drive revenue per rupee of AUM.

The first-half picture was stronger than the standalone quarter. Profit after tax for the six months ended September 2026 rose 14.3% to Rs 1,854 crore, suggesting that whatever margin pressure built up in Q2 was largely cost-timing related rather than structural. The company's systematic franchise continued to compound, with monthly systematic transactions running at Rs 4,672 crore in September 2026, a figure that captures the SIP treadmill powering the entire industry.

Beyond plain-vanilla mutual funds, the alternatives book has become a meaningful second engine. Assets in alternative segments totalled Rs 80,965 crore, split between portfolio management services at Rs 30,791 crore, alternative investment funds at Rs 24,260 crore, and advisory mandates at Rs 25,914 crore. These higher-ticket, stickier pools appeal to ultra-high-net-worth and institutional clients, and they diversify the revenue base away from retail equity flows.

The customer base is broadening at pace. ICICI Prudential AMC served 17.8 million unique customers as of September 2026, up from 15.5 million a year earlier, supported by a network of more than 118,000 empanelled distribution partners across 287 offices. That physical and digital distribution muscle has become decisive as competition from passive funds, fintech platforms and rival houses intensifies.

The board rewarded shareholders with a generous interim dividend of Rs 30 per share, with October 21 set as the record date for determining eligible shareholders. At the current market price, the payout keeps ICICI Prudential AMC among the more rewarding listed asset managers for income-oriented investors, and it signals management confidence in the cash-generative nature of the model even as costs rise.

The market's reaction on Monday was muted: the stock slipped 2.02% to close at Rs 2,967.40, though it remains up roughly 13% year-to-date with a market capitalisation of about Rs 1.47 lakh crore. The decline came on a soft day for broader markets, with the Nifty down around 157 points to 22,363, and may also reflect profit-taking after a strong run rather than any disappointment with the quarter itself.

The bigger story around the numbers is the structural one. Indian households continue to shift savings from physical assets and bank deposits into market-linked products, and each month of rising SIP flows adds a predictable annuity-like revenue layer to asset managers. Regulatory tailwinds, including easier onboarding and growing pension-linked investment options, are reinforcing that shift, and the largest players with trusted brands are capturing a disproportionate share.

Risks remain. Market corrections shrink AUM mechanically and can dent sentiment-driven flows; fee pressure from passive products is a slow but persistent drag on yields; and the expense growth seen this quarter, if sustained above revenue growth, will compress margins over time. Competition for distribution has also turned expensive, with rivals matching commissions and marketing spends rupee for rupee.

For now, though, the quarter reads as steady execution on a favourable cycle: double-digit revenue growth, AUM compounding in the mid-teens, a widening customer base and a fat dividend. As the festive-season quarter typically brings strong inflows, the December quarter will show whether the company can convert top-line momentum into faster profit growth while keeping expenses in check.

GLOSSARY: Asset management company (AMC): A firm that pools investor money and manages it across mutual fund schemes and other investment products. QAAUM: Quarterly average assets under management, the average of daily AUM over a quarter, used as the basis for calculating fees. SIP (systematic investment plan): A facility allowing investors to contribute a fixed sum at regular intervals into a mutual fund scheme. Portfolio management services (PMS): Customised investment portfolios managed for wealthy individuals and institutions, with higher minimum investments than mutual funds. Alternative investment funds (AIF): Pooled investment vehicles regulated by Sebi that invest in assets beyond traditional stocks and bonds, including private equity and venture capital. Record date: The cut-off date set by a company to determine which shareholders are eligible to receive a declared dividend. Financialisation: The long-term shift of household savings from physical assets like gold and property into financial assets such as mutual funds and equities. Passive funds: Funds that replicate an index rather than actively picking stocks, typically charging much lower fees than active schemes.
