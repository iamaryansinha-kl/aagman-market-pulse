RBI's Rs 50,000 Crore OMO Sale Oversubscribed: Bids Worth Rs 66,590 Crore at Yields Above Market

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

The Reserve Bank of India's first open market operation sale of the year attracted stronger demand than expected, with market participants bidding Rs 66,590 crore against the notified Rs 50,000 crore.

The operation is part of the central bank's effort to drain excess liquidity from the banking system and align the weighted average call rate with the policy rate. Surplus funds in the system stood at Rs 7.37 lakh crore on September 16, while the WACR has held at 5.05%, below the 5.25% repo rate, since late July.

The most striking detail was pricing. The RBI accepted bonds at yields higher than prevailing market levels, meaning it was willing to pay up in yield terms to get the liquidity out. The 6.68% GS 2031 five-year paper was taken at a cut-off yield of 6.90%, against a market close of 6.78% on Thursday. The 6.79% GS 2029 three-year paper was accepted at 6.70%, versus a 6.66% close.

The 8.28% GS 2032 was the most in-demand security, drawing bids of Rs 19,700 crore, while the 2031 paper garnered Rs 7,970 crore.

"The yields were slightly higher than prevailing market levels and expectations, but the OMO was well subscribed. Since the sale was fully subscribed, it did not lead to any significant negative reaction in yields," said Alok Singh, head of treasury at CSB Bank.

The operation comes ahead of a Rs 28,000 crore weekly bond auction scheduled for Friday, and the RBI has already lined up a Rs 25,000 crore OMO sale for September 21. A three-day variable rate reverse repo operation for Rs 2.25 lakh crore is also on the calendar for Friday.

The sequence shows the central bank normalising its liquidity toolkit after years of surplus management. OMO sales drain durable liquidity, VRRR operations mop up shorter-term funds, and the Friday auction will test the market's appetite for fresh supply.

For bond markets, the message is two-sided. Heavy absorption can push yields up, but by accepting higher cut-offs without disrupting the market on Thursday, the RBI signalled it wants adjustment without disorder.
