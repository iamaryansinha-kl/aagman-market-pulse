AWL Flags 20% Rise in Oil-Linked Costs Amid West Asia Conflict

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/AWL CEO Shrikant Kanhere flags roughly 20% surge in crude-linked input costs including chemicals, packing material and coal amid West Asia conflict
  • 02/Brent crude has surged from low $70s to above $110 per barrel; cost impact translates to roughly 25-50 basis points for the company
  • 03/AWL expects 8-9% sales volume growth in FY2027 with per-tonne margins broadly stable; edible oils to grow mid-single-digit, foods double-digit

AWL, the consumer goods maker behind Fortune cooking oil and Kohinoor rice, is facing a roughly 20 per cent surge in crude-linked input costs. The spike follows the West Asia conflict, which has driven global oil prices sharply higher.

"Costs have gone up for us in terms of chemicals, packing material and coal, so that is something which remains a cause of concern even today," Shrikant Kanhere, AWL's managing director and CEO, told Reuters.

The cost impact translates to roughly 25 to 50 basis points for the company. AWL is absorbing part of the increase while passing the rest to consumers through price adjustments.

Global oil prices have surged on supply disruption fears. Brent crude has climbed from the low $70s a barrel before the conflict to above $110, according to market data.

The pressure reflects a broader industry trend. Peers including bottled water maker Bisleri and Hindustan Unilever have also raised prices to counter higher conflict-linked input costs.

AWL is cutting packaging and fuel use at its plants to limit the profit hit. The company expects per-tonne margins to remain broadly stable in fiscal 2027.

Kanhere forecast sales volume growth of 8 per cent to 9 per cent in fiscal 2027. That is nearly double last year's pace, with edible oils growing at a mid-single-digit rate and foods posting double-digit growth.

The company is expanding distribution and investing in online channels and large-format grocers. These channels together posted nearly 50 per cent growth last year as AWL pushes to scale volumes.
