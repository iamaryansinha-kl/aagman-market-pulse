New PSU Closure Push: Government Targets Nine Ailing CPSEs for Winding Up

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •• The government has moved a draft note to close nine ailing CPSEs including NTC, STC, and PEC after years of delay.
  • •• NTC lost ₹344 crore in FY21, while STC has negative net worth of ₹955 crore and accumulated losses of ₹1,083 crore.
  • •• The move follows the 2021 PSE policy and a disinvestment drive targeting ₹47,000 crore in FY26.

The government has initiated a fresh push to wind up nine loss-making central public sector enterprises after years of policy paralysis and stop-start delays.

A draft Cabinet note is under discussion within the government and is currently under consideration at the Prime Minister's Office, officials familiar with the matter said.

The nine CPSEs earmarked for closure include National Textile Corporation and its five subsidiaries, State Trading Corporation of India, PEC Limited, and British India Corporation Limited.

The move follows the 2021 public sector enterprise policy that allows the government to exit non-strategic sectors through privatisation, merger, or outright closure.

National Textile Corporation has not carried out meaningful operations for several years due to obsolete technology and working capital constraints, posting a net loss of ₹344 crore in FY21.

State Trading Corporation did not undertake any business activity during FY24 and survives on rental income despite carrying accumulated losses of ₹1,083 crore and negative net worth of ₹955 crore.

PEC Limited has suspended all trading activity since September 2019 and reported a net loss of ₹340 crore in FY24, largely on account of interest payments to lenders.

The ailing firms drain the exchequer because the Centre continues to fund employee salaries and statutory dues even though commercial operations have ceased.

The Department of Public Enterprises issued revised time-bound closure guidelines in 2018 and fresh guidelines in 2021 to fast-track the winding-up process.

The government has granted in-principle approvals for disinvestment or closure to roughly 50 PSUs, of which about 12 closures and 13 strategic sales have been completed.

Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman reaffirmed the Centre's commitment to privatisation in the Union Budget 2026, setting a disinvestment and asset monetisation target of ₹47,000 crore for FY26.

The proposed closure aligns with the broader strategy of reducing the government's footprint in non-strategic sectors while protecting employee interests through voluntary retirement schemes.

DIPAM is separately pursuing the strategic sale of the government's stake in IDBI Bank, a transaction that could raise approximately ₹45,000 crore if concluded this fiscal year.

Analysts remain divided on execution timelines, as previous closure and divestment plans have faced opposition from administrative ministries and employee unions.

The Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs must approve the closure note before the Department of Public Enterprises can initiate the formal winding-up process.
