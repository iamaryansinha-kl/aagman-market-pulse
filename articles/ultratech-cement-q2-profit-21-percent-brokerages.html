UltraTech Q2 Profit Jumps 21% as Brokerages Split

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- UltraTech Cement reported consolidated Q2 net profit of Rs 1,494 crore, up 21% from Rs 1,232 crore a year earlier.
  • •- Revenue rose 19% year-on-year to Rs 23,295 crore.
  • •- Jefferies raised its target to Rs 14,380; Nomura cut to Rs 13,700 and Citi to Rs 13,200, all with Buy ratings.
  • •- Q2 EBITDA of Rs 3,700 crore was 7% above Nomura's estimate, with 14% volume growth.
  • •- Management expects demand to stay strong through March 2029, led by data centres, private capex, metros and urban housing.
  • •- Cost pressure remains the watch item: fuel and fixed costs drove a 7% sequential rise in operating costs.

India's largest cement maker delivered the earnings and got an argument instead of a verdict. UltraTech Cement reported a consolidated net profit of Rs 1,494 crore for the July-September quarter, up 21% from Rs 1,232 crore a year earlier, on revenue that rose 19% to Rs 23,295 crore. The numbers beat the Street's operating estimates — Q2 EBITDA of Rs 3,700 crore came in 7% above Nomura's forecast, driven by slightly better realisations and 14% volume growth that was 2 points ahead of estimate. Yet the stock, after falling over 2% on results day Monday, bounced 3% on Wednesday on the back of three brokerage notes that all said Buy and could not agree on much else. The split is the story: UltraTech's earnings power is not in question; what the market will pay for it is.

Jefferies maintained Buy and raised its target to Rs 14,380 from Rs 14,065, calling it another strong quarter and flagging management's signal that the next phase of organic expansion will be announced soon — a programme Jefferies expects to drive further volume market share gains from the current 31-32%. Nomura stayed Buy but trimmed its target to Rs 13,700 from Rs 13,900, not on demand concerns but on costs: higher fuel and fixed expenses drove a 7% sequential increase in operating costs, 5% above its estimate, and the brokerage cut FY27, FY28 and FY29 EBITDA by 5%, 1% and 1% respectively to account for an escalated cost structure. Citi went furthest, cutting its target to Rs 13,200 from Rs 14,750 while holding Buy, with spot cement prices currently 5% above the second-quarter average supporting expectations of further price hikes.

Underneath the brokerage arithmetic sits a simple divergence in emphasis. Jefferies chose to pay for the demand and share-gain story, costs be damned; Nomura and Citi chose to charge for the cost story, demand be assumed. Neither camp doubts the franchise — all three ratings are Buy, and even the lowest target implies 24% upside from Monday's close. What the split really prices is uncertainty about fuel: with crude crossing $110 a barrel this week on Middle East supply worries, every cement maker's cost curve is hostage to the same geopolitical variable, and analysts are effectively taking different views of the West Asia premium. UltraTech's quarterly print was good enough to make that the only question worth asking, which is itself a statement about the quality of the results.

The demand commentary is where the quarter's real signal sits. Management expects strength to persist through March 2029, supported by data centres, private capex, metro extensions and urban housing — a list that marks how thoroughly India's cement demand has rotated from rural housing-led to infrastructure-and-investment-led over the past decade. The caveats are specific: a weaker monsoon typically means softer rural demand, which Nomura flags as a risk for FY28, and management itself acknowledged that costs may not have peaked and that the West Asia situation remains unpredictable — a euphemism worth unpacking, with crude above $110 and fuel the cement industry's largest variable cost.

There is also a governance footnote with strategic weight: UltraTech announced a board reshuffle alongside results week, with managing director KC Jhanwar moving to non-executive vice chairman from January 1. Leadership transitions at India's largest cement producer rarely coincide with expansion announcements by accident, and the market read the pairing as a handoff into the capacity announcement Jefferies expects imminently.

For the stock, the year has been unkind regardless of the quarter: shares are down 10.5% in 2026 and nearly 14% over one year, even as three-year and five-year returns of 26% and 49% remind holders what the franchise has compounded. The brokerage spread — Rs 13,200 to Rs 14,380, all labelled Buy — says the market's argument is about multiple, not model. Cement is a volume story again, which is what bulls wanted; the cost of fuel is the tax on that story, and West Asia is setting the rate.

Scale remains UltraTech's quiet advantage in that argument. The company has been consolidating capacity relentlessly — it recently commissioned a Rs 2,650 crore cement plant in Andhra Pradesh in just fourteen months, a build-speed that smaller rivals cannot match — and its consistent improvement at India Cement, which Nomura singled out as a key positive, shows the integration playbook working in practice. With roughly a third of the industry's volumes and the balance sheet to keep building through cycles, UltraTech can treat margin compression as a filtering event that hurts leveraged regional players first. Whether that translates into share-price performance depends on the fuel line item resolving; every brokerage note this week, in its own direction, said the same thing in different words. The results themselves arrive at an inflection for the cycle: capacity utilisation is climbing, pricing power is returning, and the next expansion announcement will set the industry's supply agenda for the rest of the decade.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT WERE ULTRATECH'S Q2 NUMBERS?
Consolidated net profit of Rs 1,494 crore, up 21% year-on-year, on revenue of Rs 23,295 crore, up 19%; EBITDA was Rs 3,700 crore.
HOW DID BROKERAGES REACT?
Jefferies raised its target to Rs 14,380; Nomura cut to Rs 13,700; Citi cut to Rs 13,200. All three maintained Buy ratings.
WHAT IS DRIVING CEMENT DEMAND?
Management pointed to data centres, private capex, metro extensions and urban housing supporting demand through March 2029.
WHAT ARE THE KEY RISKS?
Higher fuel and fixed costs — operating costs rose 7% sequentially — softer rural demand from a weak monsoon, and West Asia-related uncertainty. GLOSSARY: - EBITDA: Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation. - Realisations: The average price realised per unit of product sold. - Volume growth: The rate of increase in physical quantities sold, as distinct from value. - Spot prices: Current market prices for immediate delivery, as distinct from contract prices.
