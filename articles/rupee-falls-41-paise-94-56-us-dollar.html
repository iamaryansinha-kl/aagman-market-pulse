Rupee Falls 41 Paise to Close at 94.56 Against US Dollar

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/The rupee depreciated 41 paise to close at 94.56 against the US dollar on April 28, 2026, hitting an intraday low of 94.58.
  • 02/Brent crude surged past $111 per barrel while FIIs have pulled out over $19 billion from Indian equities so far this year.
  • 03/Forex traders warn the rupee could face further pressure if West Asia conflict escalates and crude prices climb higher.

The Indian rupee depreciated 41 paise to close at 94.56 (provisional) against the US dollar on Tuesday, April 28, 2026. The currency opened at 94.35 in the interbank market and touched an intraday low of 94.58 before settling.

On the previous trading day, the rupee had gained just one paise to close at 94.15. Tuesday's decline erased that marginal recovery entirely.

Elevated crude oil prices and sustained foreign capital outflows drove the rupee lower. Brent crude, the global oil benchmark, jumped 2.62% to trade at $111.07 per barrel.

Foreign institutional investors have pulled out over $19 billion from Indian equities so far this year. On Monday alone, FIIs offloaded equities worth ₹1,151.48 crore.

Forex traders said the rupee has already weakened sharply, factoring in a likely widening of the Current Account Deficit and volatile capital flows. Geopolitical uncertainties in West Asia added to the pressure.

"The primary pressure on the rupee stems from weak capital inflows. Net FDI inflows have been subdued, while FPI flows have been affected by elevated global uncertainty," said Rajani Sinha, Chief Economist, CareEdge Ratings.

The dollar index, which gauges the greenback's strength against a basket of six currencies, rose 0.24% to 98.73. A stronger dollar typically weighs on emerging market currencies.

On the domestic equity front, the Sensex dropped 416.72 points (0.54%) to settle at 76,886.91. The Nifty declined 97 points (0.40%) to end at 23,995.70.

Sinha expects crude to average $85–90 per barrel in FY27 if the conflict eases. Under that baseline, the rupee could average 92–93, but an escalation would push it weaker.
