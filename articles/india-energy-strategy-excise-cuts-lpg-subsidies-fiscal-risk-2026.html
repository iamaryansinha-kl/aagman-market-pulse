India's Three-Prong Energy Strategy: Subsidies, Price Caps, and Fiscal Risks

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/India reduced excise duty by INR 10/litre on petrol and diesel on March 27, 2026, costing an estimated INR 1.3 lakh crore (0.4% of GDP) if maintained through FY27
  • 02/LPG under-recoveries could exceed INR 60,000 crore in FY26-27 if global prices persist at current elevated levels
  • 03/HSBC economist recommends oil subsidies, gas price caps, and power tariff shields as a three-prong strategy to curb energy shock implications

India's government responded to surging global energy prices with a mix of tax cuts and subsidies. On March 27, 2026, the finance ministry reduced excise duty by INR 10 per litre on petrol and diesel.

The fiscal cost is substantial. If the current duty structure is maintained through FY27, revenue losses will reach an estimated INR 1.3 lakh crore, equivalent to 0.4% of GDP. This comes on top of other existing subsidy pressures.

Global oil prices surged from around USD 66 per barrel in late February to approximately USD 113 by early April 2026. The Strait of Hormuz closure threatened 40% of India's crude oil imports.

LPG under-recoveries pose an even larger fiscal risk. Saudi Aramco benchmark prices rose 44% between March and April 2026. If sustained, annual LPG under-recoveries could exceed INR 60,000 crore in FY26-27.

HSBC economist recommends a three-prong strategy. First, maintain oil subsidies to shield consumers. Second, cap gas prices to protect households and industries. Third, shield power tariffs to prevent electricity cost spikes.

The oil ministry raised LPG allocation to commercial and industrial users by 20%, bringing total supply to 70% of pre-crisis levels. Priority goes to steel, automobiles, textiles, and other essential industries.

Windfall taxes were imposed on aviation fuel and diesel exports to capture gains from refiners. However, prolonged price suppression weakens incentives for fuel efficiency and EV adoption.

An HSBC economist warned that abrupt price pass-throughs could generate short-term macroeconomic pressures. A phased and calibrated approach beginning with petrol would ease the fiscal burden while limiting welfare impacts on lower-income households.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
HOW MUCH DID INDIA CUT EXCISE DUTIES ON PETROL AND DIESEL?
INDIA REDUCED EXCISE DUTY BY INR 10 PER LITRE ON PETROL AND DIESEL ON MARCH 27, 2026. THE SPECIAL EXCISE DUTY ON PETROL WAS CUT TO INR 3 PER LITRE FROM INR 13 EARLIER, AND DIESEL DUTY WAS CUT TO ZERO FROM INR 10 PER LITRE.
WHAT IS THE ESTIMATED FISCAL COST OF THE EXCISE DUTY CUTS?
IF MAINTAINED THROUGH FY27, THE REVENUE LOSSES WILL REACH AN ESTIMATED INR 1.3 LAKH CRORE, EQUIVALENT TO 0.4% OF GDP. THIS IS IN ADDITION TO OTHER EXISTING SUBSIDY PRESSURES.
HOW MUCH COULD LPG UNDER-RECOVERIES REACH IN FY26-27?
IF GLOBAL LPG PRICES PERSIST AT CURRENT ELEVATED LEVELS, INDIA'S ANNUAL LPG UNDER-RECOVERIES COULD EXCEED INR 60,000 CRORE (USD 7 BILLION) IN FY 2026-27. EVEN WITH A 10% PRICE INCREASE, UNDER-RECOVERIES COULD REACH INR 26,000 CRORE.
WHAT IS THE THREE-PRONG STRATEGY RECOMMENDED BY HSBC?
THE THREE-PRONG STRATEGY INCLUDES: FIRST, MAINTAIN OIL SUBSIDIES TO SHIELD CONSUMERS; SECOND, CAP GAS PRICES TO PROTECT HOUSEHOLDS AND INDUSTRIES; THIRD, SHIELD POWER TARIFFS TO PREVENT ELECTRICITY COST SPIKES.
WHAT WINDFALL TAXES WERE IMPOSED ON FUEL EXPORTS?
THE GOVERNMENT IMPOSED A DIESEL EXPORT TAX OF INR 21.5 PER LITRE AND AN AVIATION FUEL EXPORT TAX OF INR 29.5 PER LITRE TO CAPTURE REFINER GAINS AMID SURGING GLOBAL PRICES.
