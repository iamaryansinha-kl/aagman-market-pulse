FPI IPO Inflow: $1 Billion in September Amid Sell-Off

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Foreign portfolio investors pumped $1 billion (₹9,676 crore) into Indian IPOs in September even as they dumped $4.8 billion (₹45,537 crore) of listed equities.
  • 02/The secondary-market exit was the highest in five months, and 34 of the year's 96 IPOs were crammed into September alone to beat audited-filing deadlines.
  • 03/Watch the Jio Platforms IPO in the second half of October, sized at over ₹35,000-40,000 crore, which could keep primary market money flowing.

Foreign portfolio investors put $1 billion into Indian IPOs in September. The same set of investors offloaded $4.8 billion (₹45,537 crore) worth of equities in the secondary market during the month, ET Intelligence Group data shows.

The contrast could hardly be sharper. Primary market buying of ₹9,676 crore ran alongside the biggest secondary-market exit in five months.

September's secondary outflow was the highest since April 2026. That month's exit was even larger at $6.7 billion (₹63,167 crore).

The primary market, meanwhile, was running at full throttle. Of the 96 IPOs hosted in 2026 so far, 34 came up in September alone. That is one out of every three IPOs this year squeezed into a single month.

The reason was a filing deadline, not a sudden glut of new businesses. IPO-bound companies scrambled to launch before they were required to file audited financials for recent quarters of the current fiscal year.

The rush capped a record fundraising first half for the primary market in FY27. But the pace is unlikely to hold.

October offerings may cool off, since companies now need time to gather audited financial data for the June quarter and the quarters that follow. A fresh wave of filings is unlikely until those numbers are in place.

One deal could still anchor the month. The expected launch of the Jio Platforms IPO in the second half of October, at a size of over ₹35,000-40,000 crore, may offer support to primary market investments even as the broader pipeline thins.

The year-to-date numbers frame the split clearly. Through end-September 2026, FPIs have invested $5.9 billion in the primary market while selling $33.7 billion of equities in the secondary market.

The same period of the previous year showed the same pattern at smaller scale. FPIs logged a net inflow of $5 billion in the primary market against an outflow of $22.7 billion from the secondary market.

For issuers, the arithmetic was simple. Launch in September and price offers on the older audited numbers, or wait and compile fresh statements for the June quarter and every quarter after. Many chose speed.

ET Intelligence Group's reading is that foreign investors continue to favour new equity paper while unloading listed stakes. Primary issues let them buy into businesses with strong growth potential, usually at a discount to the valuations of listed peers.

There is a mechanical advantage too. Buying in the primary market avoids the price impact of bulk deals that hits secondary market trades.

Whether that preference persists will depend on the October pipeline. A Jio Platforms listing of that scale could absorb a meaningful share of primary market appetite on its own.
