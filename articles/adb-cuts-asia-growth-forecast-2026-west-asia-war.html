ADB Cuts Asia Growth Forecast to 4.7% on West Asia War Impact

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/ADB slashes Asia-Pacific growth forecast to 4.7% in 2026 and 4.8% in 2027, down from 5.1% for both years
  • 02/Inflation forecast raised sharply to 5.2% in 2026 from 3.6% earlier; IMF also cut 2026 global outlook to 3.1%
  • 03/If conflict escalates, growth could fall to 4.2% in 2026 with inflation surging to 7.4%

The Asian Development Bank on April 29 slashed its economic growth forecast for developing Asia and the Pacific to 4.7% for 2026 and 4.8% for 2027. Both figures are down from the earlier projection of 5.1%.

The revision reflects the economic fallout from the war in West Asia. The conflict has raised energy prices, tightened financial conditions, and weighed on activity across the region.

"We are confronting systemic, long-lasting disruptions to global energy and trade networks, not just temporary volatility," said ADB President Masato Kanda. He called the revision "significant."

The ADB also raised its inflation forecast for the region to 5.2% in 2026, up sharply from the earlier projection of 3.6%. Energy costs are driving the surge.

Earlier this month, the International Monetary Fund cut its 2026 global growth outlook to 3.1%, also citing the Iran war. Major institutions are converging on a bleaker outlook.

The ADB warned that an escalation could deepen the damage. If oil prices spike in May and remain elevated, growth could slow to 4.2% this year.

In that severe scenario, inflation would surge to 7.4% in 2026, with growth further dropping to 4.0% in 2027. The downside risk is substantial.

The ADB advised central banks to limit excessive market volatility while monitoring inflation expectations. Policymakers face a balancing act between growth support and price stability.
