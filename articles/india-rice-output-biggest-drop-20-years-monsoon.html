India's Rice Output Set for Biggest Drop in Nearly 20 Years as Weak Rains Hit Yields

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- 01/ Rice production could fall ~10 million tonnes, about 6.5%, from last year's record 154 million tonnes.
  • •- 02/ It would be the biggest drop since 2009/10 and the first decline in a decade.
  • •- 03/ Monsoon rainfall is 15% below normal, with deficits as high as 42% in some rice-growing states.
  • •- 04/ Sown area is down 4% at 42.68 million hectares as of September 11.
  • •- 05/ Record state reserves of 59.6 million tonnes should keep exports flowing.

## A 10-million-tonne hole

India's rice production is set to fall this year for the first time in a decade — and by the most in nearly 20 years. The Rice Exporters Association estimates output could decline by around 10 million metric tonnes, nearly 6.5% below last year's record 154 million tonnes.

That would be the biggest decline since 2009/10, when an El Nino-induced drought curtailed production.

A prolonged dry spell during the crop's maturation period has hit yield potential across southern and eastern states. India has received 15% below-normal rainfall since the monsoon began on June 1, and in some rice-growing states the deficit runs as high as 42%.

## Area and winter crop at risk

The area under summer-sown rice stood at 42.68 million hectares as of September 11, nearly 4% lower than a year earlier. Summer-sown crops account for more than 80% of total rice production.

The winter crop may shrink too. Reservoirs are holding less water than normal, which will limit planting, according to Nitin Gupta, deputy country head at Olam Agri India.

## Prices already moving

Local rice prices have started rising on expectations of lower production, pushing export prices to their highest level in more than a year. Thailand and Vietnam, rival exporters, are also seeing prices climb.

Farmers growing premium varieties should get higher open-market prices this year — which also means lower sales to government procurement agencies, according to association president B.V. Krishna Rao.

## Stocks buy room to breathe

The buffer is inventory. State reserves of rice, including unmilled paddy, totalled a record 59.6 million tonnes as of September 1 — nearly six times the government's target of 10.3 million tonnes by October 1.

With those stockpiles, India should be able to maintain exports at record levels without imposing restrictions, traders said. That matters globally: India is the world's biggest rice producer and exporter, and past export curbs have sent world prices spiralling.

## FAQ Q: How big is the expected production drop? A/01/ Around 10 million tonnes, roughly 6.5% below last year's record 154 million tonnes — the biggest decline since 2009/10.

Q: What is causing it? A/02/ Below-normal rainfall during the maturation period: monsoon rains are 15% below normal nationally and up to 42% short in some rice states.

Q: Will India restrict rice exports? A/03/ Unlikely in the near term — state reserves of 59.6 million tonnes are nearly six times the government's October target.

Q: What happens to prices? A/04/ Local and export prices are already rising; export prices are at their highest in more than a year.

## KEY TAKEAWAYS - The worst rice output drop in ~20 years: about 10 million tonnes lost. - Weak monsoon rains, not policy, is the driver. - Record 59.6-million-tonne state stockpile is the shock absorber. - Global rice prices are already at 1-year highs; India's export stance is the swing factor.
