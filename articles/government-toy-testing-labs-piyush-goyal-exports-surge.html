Government to Set Up Modern Toy Testing Labs; Toy Exports Surge 239% in Four Years

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Government to set up modern toy testing labs across manufacturing clusters via BIS, National Test House and other laboratories
  • 02/Toy exports surged 239% over past four years while imports fell 32%; imports now ₹2,500-3,000 crore in ₹18,000 crore market
  • 03/Over 50 toy manufacturing clusters established with nearly 21,000 MSMEs; India-UK FTA comes into force on 15 July

The government will set up state-of-the-art testing facilities across major toy manufacturing clusters. Union Commerce and Industry Minister Piyush Goyal announced this on Saturday at the 17th Toy Biz International B2B Exhibition 2026 in New Delhi.

The new infrastructure will be developed through the Bureau of Indian Standards, the National Test House and other government and semi-government laboratories. Goyal urged manufacturers to identify the testing equipment needed so Indian-made toys can achieve the highest quality certifications for both domestic and export markets.

"The Government would establish modern testing facilities across toy manufacturing clusters in the country," Goyal said.

The minister asked manufacturers to prepare a comprehensive list of testing equipment requirements. This would help create an ecosystem capable of supporting world-class quality assessment and certification.

Goyal encouraged the industry to leverage India's growing network of Free Trade Agreements. Duty-free access to European markets and the India-UK Free Trade Agreement, scheduled to come into force on 15 July, would open significant opportunities for exporters.

He also called on manufacturers to embrace modern production technologies such as Computer-Aided Design and Computer-Aided Manufacturing systems and CNC machining. Adopting advanced manufacturing processes will enhance product quality, improve precision and increase production efficiency.

The minister assured industry players of support under the Export Promotion Mission. He encouraged companies to participate in global trade fairs, establish warehousing facilities in key export destinations and strengthen engagement with international brands, retailers and e-commerce platforms.

Goyal urged greater investment in domestic production of motors, electronics, dies and moulds used in toy manufacturing. Building a self-reliant supply chain would enhance competitiveness while lowering production costs.

Toy exports have surged by 239% over the past four years. Imports have fallen by 32%, reflecting the success of the Make in India initiative and the National Toy Action Plan.

Domestically manufactured toys once accounted for only around 12% of India's toy market. Now imports have declined to about ₹2,500-3,000 crore in a market valued at nearly ₹18,000 crore. Indian manufacturers now supply the bulk of domestic demand.

More than 50 toy manufacturing clusters have been established across the country. Nearly 21,000 MSMEs are operating in the sector.

Goyal called on manufacturers to target tenfold expansion through stronger branding, better quality, continuous innovation and increased exports.
