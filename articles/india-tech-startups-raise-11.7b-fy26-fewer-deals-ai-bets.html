India Tech Startups Raise $11.7B in FY26: Fewer Deals, Bigger AI Bets

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Indian tech startups raised $11.7 billion in FY26, an 18% decline from $14.3 billion in FY25, per Tracxn's India Tech Annual Funding Report 2026
  • 02/Early-stage funding bucked the trend, surging 33% to $4.8 billion, while late-stage funding fell 38% to $5.6 billion
  • 03/AI-driven sectors led large deals including Nxtra's $710M round and Neysa's $600M Series B; 47 IPOs marked a 52% jump

Indian technology startups raised $11.7 billion in FY 2025-26. The figure marks an 18% decline from $14.3 billion in FY25, according to Tracxn's India Tech Annual Funding Report 2026.

Despite the headline drop, funding was up 20% compared to $9.7 billion in FY24. India ranked as the fourth-highest funded startup ecosystem globally, behind only the US, UK, and China.

Early-stage funding showed strong momentum, rising to $4.8 billion. This represents a 33% jump from $3.6 billion in FY25 and a 37% increase from FY24 levels.

In contrast, seed-stage investments slipped 15% to $1.3 billion. Late-stage funding fell sharply by 38% to $5.6 billion from $9.2 billion in FY25.

The number of $100 million-plus rounds dropped to 13 in FY26. This compares with 23 such mega-deals in FY25, though it matched the 13 seen in FY24.

Large deals were concentrated in enterprise infrastructure, enterprise applications, and fintech. Nxtra raised $710 million in a private equity round, Neysa secured $600 million in Series B funding, and Inox Clean Energy raised $344 million in Series D.

Enterprise Applications attracted $3.6 billion in FY26, unchanged from FY25. FinTech funding rose 14% to $2.4 billion, while retail startup funding declined 32% to $2.4 billion from $3.5 billion.

The public markets offered a brighter picture. India Tech recorded 47 IPOs in FY26, up 52% from 31 in FY25. Major listings included Lenskart, Groww, and Meesho.

The ecosystem created six unicorns in FY26, up from four in FY25. Bengaluru retained its position as India's startup capital with 33% of total funding, followed by Mumbai at 21%.

"While overall funding saw moderation, strong momentum in early-stage investments highlights continued investor confidence in startups building differentiated and scalable solutions," said Neha Singh, Co-Founder of Tracxn.
