Rupee Hits All-Time Low of 95.20 Against US Dollar on April 30, 2026

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/The Indian rupee depreciated 32 paise to an all-time low of 95.20 against the US dollar in early trade on April 30, 2026.
  • 02/Brent crude hovering near $122 per barrel and fears of wider conflict in West Asia are amplifying import cost pressures and investor anxiety.
  • 03/Forex traders warn the USD/INR pair may see further downside, with 95.25 now breached and the next support zone uncertain.

The Indian rupee opened at 95.01 against the US dollar at the interbank foreign exchange market on Thursday, April 30, 2026. It quickly lost ground and touched an all-time low of 95.20 in initial trade, marking a 32 paise decline.

The previous all-time intraday low was 95.22 recorded on March 30, 2026. On Wednesday, April 29, the rupee had closed at 94.82, just 3 paise shy of the record closing low of 94.85 set on March 27.

Brent crude oil prices hovering around $122 per barrel are the primary drag. India imports nearly 85% of its crude requirement, making any spike in oil prices a direct hit on the current account deficit.

Forex traders said the USD/INR pair may see further downside from here. Rising crude prices are likely to sharply impact India's import bill, while concerns over potential wider conflict in West Asia are fuelling investor anxiety.

Foreign Institutional Investors sold equities worth Rs 2,103.74 crore on April 28, according to exchange data. Sustained FII outflows have added persistent selling pressure on the rupee throughout April.

"The trend remains weak, with the currency consistently facing selling pressure on rebounds, indicating a lack of strong support at higher levels. In the near term, 94.40 is likely to act as resistance, while 95.25 remains the next key support," said Jateen Trivedi, VP Research Analyst – Commodity and Currency, LKP Securities.

The dollar index, which measures the US currency against six major peers, stood firm near 98.72. A stronger greenback typically triggers capital flight from emerging markets including India.

Analysts said higher oil prices are also increasing inflation risks and limiting any meaningful recovery. Retail investors should watch RBI intervention levels and the upcoming US Federal Reserve policy signals for directional cues.
