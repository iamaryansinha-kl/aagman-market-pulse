India Manufacturing PMI Rises to 54.7 in April, Input Inflation Surges

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/India's manufacturing PMI rose to 54.7 in April from 53.9 in March, marking the second-slowest improvement in operating conditions in nearly four years
  • 02/Input costs surged at the fastest pace since August 2022, driven by Middle East conflict spillovers; output prices rose at the quickest rate in six months
  • 03/Hiring activity hit a 10-month high as manufacturers expanded staffing, but input inventory growth fell to a near 5-year low

India's manufacturing PMI rose to 54.7 in April from 53.9 in March, S&P Global reported on May 4. The index tracks 400 companies and signals expansion above the 50 mark.

The April reading marks the second-slowest improvement in operating conditions in nearly four years. It remains below the long-run average of 54.2.

Input cost inflation surged to the fastest pace since August 2022. Panellists cited higher prices for aluminium, chemicals, fuel, leather, petroleum products and rubber.

Producers raised output prices at the quickest rate in six months. The pass-through reflects intense cost pressures from the Middle East conflict.

Hiring activity strengthened to a 10-month high. Manufacturers recruited additional workers despite only marginal growth in outstanding business volumes.

Input inventories rose at the slowest pace in nearly five years. Firms kept stocks lean amid subdued sales performance and cautious planning.

Finished goods inventories increased for the first time in six months. The accumulation rate remained slight overall.

"India's manufacturing PMI rose to 54.7 in April, up from 53.9 in March, but still marking the second-slowest improvement in operating conditions in nearly four years. Further, spillovers from the Middle East conflict are becoming more evident, particularly through inflation," said Pranjul Bhandari, Chief India Economist at HSBC.

Consumer goods was the only sector to see a slowdown in cost inflation. It still topped output charge inflation rankings among sub-sectors.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT WAS INDIA'S MANUFACTURING PMI IN APRIL 2026?
INDIA'S MANUFACTURING PURCHASING MANAGERS' INDEX (PMI) ROSE TO 54.7 IN APRIL 2026, UP FROM 53.9 IN MARCH. THE INDEX IS BASED ON RESPONSES FROM 400 COMPANIES AND READINGS ABOVE 50 SIGNAL EXPANSION.
WHAT DROVE THE SURGE IN INPUT COSTS FOR MANUFACTURERS?
INPUT COSTS INCREASED AT THE FASTEST PACE SINCE AUGUST 2022. PANELLISTS REPORTED HIGHER PRICES FOR ALUMINIUM, CHEMICALS, ELECTRICAL COMPONENTS, FUEL, LEATHER, PETROLEUM PRODUCTS AND RUBBER, OFTEN ATTRIBUTED TO THE MIDDLE EAST CONFLICT.
HOW DID MANUFACTURERS RESPOND TO RISING COSTS?
PRODUCERS RAISED OUTPUT PRICES AT THE QUICKEST RATE IN SIX MONTHS. HIRING ALSO HIT A 10-MONTH HIGH AS FIRMS EXPANDED STAFFING TO SUPPORT EXPANSION PLANS.
WHAT HAPPENED TO INVENTORY LEVELS IN APRIL?
INPUT INVENTORIES ROSE AT THE SLOWEST PACE IN NEARLY FIVE YEARS AS FIRMS MAINTAINED CAUTIOUS STOCK LEVELS. FINISHED GOODS INVENTORIES INCREASED FOR THE FIRST TIME IN SIX MONTHS, BUT THE RATE OF ACCUMULATION WAS SLIGHT.
WHAT DOES THE APRIL PMI READING SIGNAL FOR THE ECONOMY?
THE 54.7 READING SIGNALS CONTINUED EXPANSION BUT REMAINS THE SECOND-SLOWEST IMPROVEMENT IN NEARLY FOUR YEARS. OUTPUT, NEW ORDERS, EXPORTS AND EMPLOYMENT ALL GREW MODERATELY, POINTING TO RESILIENCE DESPITE PERSISTENT INFLATIONARY CHALLENGES.
