India Private Capex Pipeline Jumps 39% to Rs 99.7 Lakh Crore

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- India's announced private capital expenditure pipeline rose 39% year-on-year to Rs 99.7 lakh crore in September 2026, according to Union Bank of India Research.
  • •- The pipeline has expanded 87% in two years and 4.1 times since March 2014, but the number of projects rose only 18% — growth is coming from megaprojects, not breadth.
  • •- IT-enabled services and conventional power drove 87% of the latest annual increase, adding Rs 16.8 lakh crore and Rs 7.6 lakh crore respectively.
  • •- Maharashtra alone accounts for Rs 28.6 lakh crore, or 29% of the total, and five states contributed 94% of the year's increase — concentration is the report's central warning.

Every few quarters a number arrives that describes the Indian economy's ambition more honestly than any budget speech. The latest is Rs 99.7 lakh crore: the stock of announced private capital expenditure in the country as of September 2026, up 39% from a year earlier and up 87% over two years, according to Union Bank of India's research desk. For scale, the pipeline is now 4.1 times its March 2014 level, and the last two years alone added Rs 46.4 lakh crore of announced project costs — against an addition of Rs 7 lakh crore in the entire eight years to March 2022. Whatever else is true about India's investment cycle, corporate India is at least announcing capital spending at a pace the country has never seen. Whether that intent converts into steel and concrete is the question the rest of the data bears on — and the report is careful, pointedly careful, to separate the two.

The shape of the growth, however, is more interesting than its size. The project count behind the pipeline rose only 18% over the same two years in which announced costs nearly doubled — meaning the expansion is being carried by fewer, much larger projects rather than a broad mobilisation of smaller ones. That distinction matters because announced pipeline is a measure of intent, not of activity, and megaprojects have a longer, lumpier path from announcement to earth-moving than mid-sized factory expansions. A pipeline concentrated in a handful of giant projects is more fragile than one spread across thousands: one stalled refinery-scale development moves the total far more than fifty postponed workshops. It also tells banks something important about underwriting — concentration risk in a loan book mirrors concentration risk in the pipeline itself, and the lenders best positioned to finance this cycle will be the ones whose exposure is diversified across the long tail of mid-capex projects rather than stacked against a few flagship announcements.

The sector split tells the story of what India Inc is betting on. IT-enabled services — data centres and the infrastructure around them — added Rs 16.8 lakh crore in a single year, taking that segment's pipeline from Rs 2.2 lakh crore to Rs 18.9 lakh crore across 160 projects; conventional power added another Rs 7.6 lakh crore. Together the two sectors accounted for 87% of the year's net increase. Manufacturing (Rs 41.3 lakh crore) and electricity (Rs 32.2 lakh crore) now hold 74% of the outstanding pipeline, while services as a whole grew fastest at 347% to Rs 21.9 lakh crore. The read is unambiguous: the artificial-intelligence buildout and the power generation that feeds it have become the twin engines of Indian private investment, much as roads and ports were a decade ago. The pipeline's own history cautions against straight-line extrapolation — announced costs sat nearly flat through the eight years to March 2022 before exploding — but the composition this time is different: the two leading sectors are the ones with the clearest demand visibility in the economy.

Geography is just as concentrated as sector. Maharashtra remains the single largest destination with Rs 28.6 lakh crore — about 29% of the national total — and five states (Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Odisha and Uttar Pradesh) together contributed 94% of the pipeline's annual increase. The report's framing is diplomatic — the cycle is "firmly aligned with Viksit Bharat 2047 aspirations" but "not yet broad-based" — which translates to: the announced money is real, but it is flowing to a shortlist of states that have mastered land acquisition, clearances and power-evacuation logistics, while the rest of the country watches. The policy lesson is not new — investment follows the states that make it easy — but at 94% concentration in five states, the divergence between leader and laggard states is becoming the defining fault line of India's regional economy.

The most pointed warning in the report concerns a mismatch the headline number hides. Planned power-generation investment stands at Rs 32 lakh crore; planned transmission investment is Rs 0.17 lakh crore. Generation without evacuation is a stranded asset in waiting, and the ratio is so extreme that lenders will have to price it into every power-sector sanction. The same logic extends to the data-centre surge: ITES projects announce quickly and cluster around the same metro corridors, which means power, water, land and connectivity all compete in the same few places. That is the report's closing caution in essence: the pipeline offers significant lending opportunities, but banks must assess execution capability, financing structures and evacuation arrangements before committing — because in a two-year-doubling pipeline, the difference between announced and completed is where credit losses live. For the economy, the figure is still unambiguously good news: private capex intent at Rs 99.7 lakh crore is the demand-side counterpart to this week's MSME confidence data, and both point the same way — India Inc is planning to spend.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
HOW BIG IS INDIA'S PRIVATE CAPEX PIPELINE?
Announced private capex reached Rs 99.7 lakh crore in September 2026, up 39% year-on-year and 87% over two years, per Union Bank of India Research.
WHICH SECTORS ARE DRIVING THE GROWTH?
IT-enabled services (up to Rs 18.9 lakh crore across 160 projects) and conventional power, which together contributed 87% of the latest annual increase. Manufacturing remains the largest segment at Rs 41.3 lakh crore.
WHAT ARE THE RISKS?
Concentration — five states drove 94% of the increase and megaprojects outnumber small ones — plus a huge gap between Rs 32 lakh crore of planned power generation and only Rs 0.17 lakh crore of transmission investment, which threatens project viability. GLOSSARY: ANNOUNCED CAPEX PIPELINE — The cumulative value of private investment projects publicly announced; a leading indicator of intent rather than a measure of spending already underway. IT-ENABLED SERVICES — Outsourced digital work including data centres, cloud infrastructure and business-process operations; the current boom driver. POWER EVACUATION — The transmission infrastructure that moves generated electricity to consumers; investment here is lagging generation investment severely. VIKSIT BHARAT 2047 — The government's policy framework targeting developed-economy status for India by the centenary of independence.
