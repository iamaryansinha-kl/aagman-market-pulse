Net Direct Tax Collections Rise 13.21% to Rs 13.38 Lakh Crore

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Net direct tax collections rose 13.21% YoY to Rs 13.38 lakh crore till October 14 of FY27.
  • •- Gross collections grew 14.89% to Rs 16.03 lakh crore; refunds surged 24.22% to Rs 2.65 lakh crore.
  • •- Net corporate tax rose 20.5% to Rs 5.99 lakh crore; non-corporate collections grew 5.8% to Rs 6.92 lakh crore.
  • •- Securities Transaction Tax net collections jumped 51.6% to Rs 46,840 crore.
  • •- The data covers corporate tax, personal income tax, HUFs, firms and other direct levies.

India's direct tax engine kept up its brisk pace through the first half of the financial year, with net collections rising 13.21 per cent year on year to Rs 13.38 lakh crore as of October 14, the Income Tax department said on Thursday — a number that will bring quiet relief to a finance ministry watching both an energy shock and a volatile equity market.

The gross side of the ledger is stronger still. Gross direct tax collections rose 14.89 per cent to Rs 16.03 lakh crore, from Rs 13.95 lakh crore a year earlier, with the figures capturing taxes paid by companies, individuals, Hindu Undivided Families, firms, associations of persons, local authorities and other juridical entities. The gap between gross and net — refunds of Rs 2.65 lakh crore — actually grew faster than collections themselves, up 24.22 per cent. That is not a red flag; faster refunds are the deliberate result of the department's push to settle claims quickly, which front-loads cash to taxpayers and suppresses net collections in the short run while improving compliance over time.

The corporate engine is doing most of the pulling. Net corporate tax collections rose to Rs 5.99 lakh crore from Rs 4.97 lakh crore a year earlier — a gain of roughly 20.5 per cent — while net non-corporate collections, which include personal income tax, grew a more modest 5.8 per cent to Rs 6.92 lakh crore from Rs 6.54 lakh crore. On a gross basis, corporate taxes rose to Rs 7.53 lakh crore from Rs 6.44 lakh crore, and non-corporate gross collections to Rs 8.03 lakh crore from Rs 7.20 lakh crore. The corporate outperformance is consistent with the strong profit numbers Indian companies have been reporting through the September quarter — the same results season that has given the stock market its IT-led rebound.

The most eye-catching line sits at the bottom of the table. Net collections from the Securities Transaction Tax — the levy on every equity market transaction — climbed to Rs 46,840 crore from Rs 30,902 crore in the same period last year, a jump of about 51.6 per cent. STT growth of this magnitude is the fiscal footprint of a market trading at record volumes: more transactions, higher turnover, and a growing derivatives book all feed directly into the line. It also sharpens a policy irony the finance ministry is actively debating — the government is collecting far more from market participants than it budgeted for, at the same moment that foreign investors are pleading for relief from the capital-gains tax increases of July 2024.

For fiscal watchers, the trajectory matters more than the level. The Budget's full-year direct tax target will be revised at the end of the quarter, but collections growing in the low teens through mid-October put the government comfortably ahead of the run-rate needed, even after absorbing the cost of income-tax relief under the new regime and elevated refunds. The cushion matters because the expenditure side of the year is only getting started — the festival-season spending calendar, fertilizer and food subsidies through the rabi season, and any additional energy-shock support all land in the second half.

There are caveats worth flagging. Corporate advance-tax instalments for the September quarter were due this week, so part of the strength reflects timing as much as trend; and the final quarter of the half-year will lap the softer base of late FY26. The 'other taxes' line, a small residual category, was negative at Rs 1.64 crore against positive collections of Rs 282 crore a year earlier — immaterial in a Rs 13.38 lakh crore total, but a reminder that category-level quirks persist beneath the headline.

The compliance backdrop is part of the story too. Tax officials have for several years credited faceless assessment, e-verification of returns and the widening of the tax base for keeping collections growing faster than nominal GDP — a claim supported by the steady rise in the number of returns filed each year. Whether that structural buoyancy fully survives the base effects of FY27 will only be clear by February, when the fourth-quarter instalment lands. For now, though, the treasury's biggest revenue engine is performing exactly as the Budget assumed it would — and slightly better.

Still, the through-line is consistent with nearly every other high-frequency indicator of the past month: tax buoyancy near or above nominal GDP growth, corporate profitability holding up, and household financial-market participation deepening. For a government running a deficit-reduction path while absorbing an oil shock, a 13 per cent first-half pace is about as much as fiscal planners could possibly ask for — and a data point that will strengthen the finance ministry's hand whenever the next round of fiscal arithmetic, from rising subsidy bills to the ongoing capital-gains tax debates, reaches the negotiating table.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT ARE INDIA'S NET DIRECT TAX COLLECTIONS SO FAR IN FY27?
Net collections rose 13.21% YoY to Rs 13.38 lakh crore as of October 14; gross collections rose 14.89% to Rs 16.03 lakh crore.
WHICH COMPONENT GREW FASTEST?
Securities Transaction Tax, up about 51.6% to Rs 46,840 crore, followed by corporate tax, up roughly 20.5% to Rs 5.99 lakh crore net.
WHY DID REFUNDS GROW FASTER THAN COLLECTIONS?
Refunds rose 24.22% to Rs 2.65 lakh crore, reflecting the Income Tax department's faster settlement of claims — deliberate policy that improves compliance over time.
WHAT DOES THE DATA MEAN FOR THE FISCAL DEFICIT?
A 13% growth pace through mid-October keeps collections ahead of the full-year run-rate, giving the government room for second-half spending even as it absorbs energy-shock costs. GLOSSARY: - Direct taxes: Taxes levied on income and profits — corporate tax and personal income tax — as opposed to indirect taxes like GST. - STT: Securities Transaction Tax, charged on every purchase and sale of equity shares and derivatives on Indian exchanges. - Advance tax: Instalment payments of estimated tax liability due quarterly by companies and individuals. - Net collections: Gross tax receipts minus refunds paid back to taxpayers.
