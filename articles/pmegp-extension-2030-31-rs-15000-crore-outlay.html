PMEGP Extension to 2030-31: Govt Weighs ₹15,000 Crore Outlay

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/The government is examining a proposal to extend the Prime Minister's Employment Generation Programme (PMEGP) until 2030-31, with an estimated outlay of ₹15,000 crore.
  • 02/The scheme, launched in 2008-09 and running through 2025-26, has sanctioned loans worth ₹91,000 crore and helped set up over 400,000 micro-enterprises.
  • 03/A final decision will follow consultation with the finance ministry; simpler documentation and no-collateral lending for vulnerable and first-time entrepreneurs are on the table.

The government is examining a proposal to extend the Prime Minister's Employment Generation Programme (PMEGP) by five years, until 2030-31. The extension involves an estimated outlay of ₹15,000 crore.

A final decision will be taken after consultation with the finance ministry. People familiar with the developments said the scheme will also undergo modifications to gain momentum.

PMEGP is the flagship self-employment scheme of the MSME ministry. It supports aspiring entrepreneurs by providing margin money subsidies on bank loans.

The programme funds first-time business owners who might otherwise struggle to raise capital. By subsidising the margin money component, it lowers the entry barrier for small borrowers.

The scheme was launched in 2008-09. It was extended for a five-year period from 2021-22 to 2025-26, and is now approaching the end of that window.

Loans worth ₹91,000 crore have been sanctioned under the scheme so far. Between 2021-22 and 2025-26, it generated an estimated 3.63 million jobs and helped establish more than 400,000 micro-enterprises.

The government's money has flowed through margin money subsidies. A total of ₹13,453 crore was disbursed to more than 400,000 units between 2021-22 and 2025-26.

The outreach has been distinctly rural and female. According to the MSME ministry, 80% of the enterprises established under the scheme were located in rural areas.

That rural tilt matters for policy. It suggests the subsidy has reached borrowers far from major commercial centres, where formal credit access is thinnest.

Women accounted for 40% of the micro-enterprises supported under PMEGP. They also received around 45% of the total margin money subsidy disbursed.

The proposed modifications focus on access. These include less documentation and no collateral requirements for vulnerable, rural and first-time entrepreneurs.

Other possible changes may include a pre-sanction physical verification report and geo-tagging, according to an executive who did not wish to be identified.

An official, on condition of anonymity, said the evaluation is about stretching the money further. "We are evaluating certain parameters to optimise the use of funds to enable support to a larger number of beneficiaries. A final decision will be taken after consultation with the finance ministry."

Industry sees room to widen the net. Anil Bhardwaj, secretary general of the Federation of Indian Micro and Small & Medium Enterprises, said: "The scheme is very good, and it is helping to establish new micro-enterprises. It has yielded substantial results on the ground, and the extension of the scheme would be helpful."

Bhardwaj also pointed to technology as the next lever. Integrating modern technology and digital platforms could make the scheme more transparent, accessible and easier to monitor, he said.

Better monitoring could also curb leakages and speed up disbursals. Geo-tagging of units, one of the modifications under discussion, would let officials verify enterprises on the ground.

Third parties or intermediaries could facilitate the onboarding of self-help groups and others who are unaware of the scheme, Bhardwaj added. That could help increase the number of beneficiaries.

The proposal remains under examination. Nothing has been approved yet, and the finance ministry's consultation will determine the final shape and size of the extension.
