Rupee Hits All-Time Low Of 95.20 Against US Dollar On Fed Hold, Oil Shock

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/The Indian rupee plunged to a fresh all-time low of 95.20 against the US dollar on April 30, 2026, falling 32 paise from its previous close.
  • 02/The US Federal Reserve held interest rates unchanged at 3.5-3.75%, while Brent crude prices near $115 per barrel and persistent West Asia tensions piled pressure on the currency.
  • 03/The benchmark 10-year government bond yield spiked past 7.6%, and SBI economists warned India faces a third consecutive year of negative balance of payments without urgent structural fixes.

The Indian rupee crashed to a record low of 95.20 against the US dollar in morning trade on April 30, 2026. The currency opened 17 paise weaker at 95.01 and slid further to hit the all-time low, down 32 paise from its previous close of 94.88. At the time of writing, the rupee was trading at 95.2475 per dollar.

The sharp depreciation follows the US Federal Reserve's decision to hold policy rates unchanged at 3.5-3.75%. A hawkish Fed has constrained the Reserve Bank of India's room to manoeuvre.

"RBI remains constrained in the current scenario," said Madhavi Arora, Chief Economist at Emkay Global Financial Services, in a note co-authored with Research Associate Harshal Patel. "A hawkish Fed adds to the RBI's policy constraints ahead, with the Middle East crisis persisting longer than expected."

The RBI's options to stabilise the rupee remain limited. Some regulatory measures aimed at containing depreciation have already been rolled back. Foreign portfolio investor flows are expected to stay weak as long as West Asia tensions remain elevated.

"Right now, the rupee is no longer trading in isolation -- it is reacting to three dominant forces: oil, capital flows and central bank policies," said Amit Pabari, Managing Director at CR Forex Advisors. Brent crude oil prices are hovering near $115 per barrel. The Strait of Hormuz blockade and the UAE's decision to pull out of OPEC have added fresh uncertainty to energy markets.

The benchmark 10-year government security yield hardened 60 basis points to trade at 7.6%, breaching the crucial 7% mark. Rising oil prices and volatile capital flows are driving bond market stress. State Bank of India's economic research department has cautioned that without timely balance of payments support, inflationary expectations could become unanchored.

"The current rupee depreciation is not in line with India's macro fundamentals," said Soumya Ghosh, Group Chief Economic Advisor at SBI. SBI economists observed that the exchange rate cannot act as a shock absorber forever.

Persistent weakness will transform it into a pass-through mechanism for imported inflation. India's balance of payments could remain negative for the third consecutive year.
