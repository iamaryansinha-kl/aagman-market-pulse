SEBI Proposes Dynamic Options Strike Framework to Tackle Market Volatility

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/SEBI is evaluating a uniform framework to allow exchanges to introduce new options strike prices intraday during market hours
  • 02/Current system is fragmented across exchanges; outdated strikes remain listed while relevant ones are unavailable during sharp price moves
  • 03/Proposal discussed with exchanges and brokers; no broker system changes required during live trading under the plan

SEBI is considering a uniform framework for managing options strike prices. The proposal aims to improve trading efficiency and price discovery during volatile market conditions.

Currently, exchanges follow different practices for adding and managing strike prices. Some exchanges allow intraday strike additions, while others do not.

During sharp price movements, relevant strike prices are often unavailable. Outdated strikes remain listed even when far from current market levels.

The key proposal would allow exchanges to introduce new strike prices during market hours. This would align options with prevailing market levels during volatility spikes.

The framework is designed to avoid operational disruptions. No broker system modifications would be required during live trading sessions.

SEBI's existing regulatory framework primarily covers long-dated index options. Stock, currency, and commodity options follow exchange-specific practices.

Derivatives trading volumes have surged significantly with increased retail participation. Timely availability of appropriate strikes is critical for risk management and smooth market functioning.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT IS SEBI'S PROPOSED DYNAMIC OPTIONS STRIKE FRAMEWORK?
SEBI IS EVALUATING A UNIFORM FRAMEWORK THAT WOULD ALLOW STOCK EXCHANGES TO INTRODUCE NEW OPTIONS STRIKE PRICES DURING MARKET HOURS, PARTICULARLY IN THE DIRECTION OF SHARP MOVEMENTS IN THE UNDERLYING ASSET.
WHY DOES SEBI WANT TO CHANGE THE CURRENT STRIKE PRICE SYSTEM?
THE CURRENT SYSTEM IS FRAGMENTED ACROSS EXCHANGES AND ASSET CLASSES. DURING SHARP PRICE MOVEMENTS, RELEVANT STRIKE PRICES ARE OFTEN UNAVAILABLE, WHILE OUTDATED STRIKES REMAIN LISTED WITH LITTLE OR NO ACTIVITY.
WILL BROKERS NEED TO CHANGE THEIR SYSTEMS FOR THIS FRAMEWORK?
NO. THE PROPOSAL IS DESIGNED SO THAT INTRADAY CHANGES TO STRIKE PRICES DO NOT REQUIRE ANY SYSTEM MODIFICATIONS BY BROKERS OR MARKET PARTICIPANTS DURING LIVE TRADING.
WHICH OPTIONS SEGMENTS WOULD BE COVERED UNDER THE PROPOSED FRAMEWORK?
THE UNIFORM FRAMEWORK WOULD COVER EQUITY, COMMODITY, CURRENCY, AND INDEX OPTIONS. CURRENTLY, ONLY LONG-DATED INDEX OPTIONS HAVE SPECIFIC SEBI REGULATIONS.
WHAT IS THE CURRENT STATUS OF SEBI'S PROPOSAL?
SEBI HAS DISCUSSED THE PROPOSAL WITH STOCK EXCHANGES, BROKERS, AND OTHER STAKEHOLDERS. THE REGULATOR IS LIKELY TO ISSUE A CIRCULAR AFTER CONSULTATIONS ARE COMPLETE.
