FII Stake in India's Top 10 Companies Falls to 20-Year Low, Valuations Drop Below Long-Term Average

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

Foreign institutional investors are holding the smallest stake in India's 10 largest companies in two decades.

The decline in FII ownership comes as valuations for these blue-chip stocks have dropped below their long-term averages. This combination of low foreign ownership and compressed valuations is creating a potential contrarian entry point.

Analysts note that the current under-ownership is unusual. Historically, FIIs have maintained significant positions in India's largest companies, which are typically the most liquid and globally recognised names.

The reduction in FII stakes has coincided with a period of elevated geopolitical risk. Middle East tensions, currency volatility, and global monetary tightening have driven foreign investors to reduce their India exposure.

Experts suggest the trend could reverse. As geopolitical risks ease and the rupee stabilises, foreign flows may return to large-caps. Any reversal would likely drive outperformance in the top 10 names relative to the broader market.

The data points to a potential inflection point. Long-term investors with a contrarian bent may find the current setup attractive, particularly if earnings growth remains intact.
