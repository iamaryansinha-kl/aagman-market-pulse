Reliance Industries' 21% Slide Wipes Out Rs 4 Lakh Crore of Market Value

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

The biggest stock in India has become one of its biggest disappointments. Shares of Reliance Industries have fallen 21% in 2026 so far, wiping off Rs 4 lakh crore from the market value of India's most valuable company, and analysts are advising investors to think twice before buying the dip.

The underperformance is stark against a weak benchmark. The Nifty50 has itself fallen around 11% this year amid a sharp surge in oil prices, AI-driven global volatility and other headwinds, and RIL, which carries nearly an 8% weight in the index, has done nearly twice as badly.

The first blow came in March, when the government reintroduced windfall taxes on diesel and ATF exports, reversing its earlier decision to scrap them, as authorities sought to recalibrate revenue from the energy sector when the Middle East conflict began roiling global oil markets. Reliance is a major exporter of both fuels: its two Jamnagar refineries produce nearly 5 million tonnes of aviation turbine fuel, much of it exported, and about one-fourth of India's total ATF output. The stock has stayed range-bound even as refining and petrochemical margins have risen.

The second worry is structural. Investors fear the Jio listing, structured as a fresh issue, may widen the holding company discount at Reliance instead of unlocking value for shareholders, even as the consumer engine holds up. Jio and Retail now contribute over half of consolidated EBITDA, and the June quarter came in ahead of expectations.

Analysts are split on the way out. Vaqarjaved Khan of Angel One said the 21% fall reflects pressure on near-term earnings and valuation rather than a breakdown in core businesses, noting that the oil-to-chemicals segment has been caught in the West Asia disruption, where higher crude premiums, freight costs and fuel retail under-recoveries have diluted the benefit of strong refining cracks. His advice: do not chase a bottom, but long-term investors can accumulate in tranches over the coming months, with Jio IPO pricing, the next tariff hike, refining margin stability and new energy progress as the key triggers.

Technicals tell a colder story. Sudeep Shah of SBI Securities said the stock's RSI has slipped below 40, indicating weak bearish momentum, with the price trading well below key moving averages. He pegs Rs 1,275-1,280 as immediate resistance and says the bearish bias remains intact below that zone, advising investors to avoid the dip until strong reversal signals emerge.

For a stock that long anchored the Indian market, Rs 4 lakh crore of lost value is more than a bad run. It is a question hanging over the index itself: if RIL does not stabilise, the Nifty's path higher gets harder, and if it does, the recovery trade has an obvious first candidate.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHY HAS RIL FALLEN 21% IN 2026?
The March reimposition of windfall taxes on diesel and ATF exports hit its energy business, while fears that a fresh-issue Jio IPO could widen the holding company discount have capped the stock.
WHAT DO ANALYSTS RECOMMEND?
Angel One's fundamental view: much of the bad news is priced in, accumulate in tranches. SBI Securities' technical view: avoid the dip until a reversal above Rs 1,275-1,280.
HOW MUCH HAS THE SELLOFF ERASED?
About Rs 4 lakh crore of market value, against an 11% decline in the Nifty50 this year.
