Adani Group Restructuring 2026: Three-Layer Model, $100B Capex Plan

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Adani Group announces three-layer organizational restructuring in Labour Day memo to nearly 400,000 employees, partners and contractors
  • 02/Conglomerate doubles capital spending target to $100 billion in 5-6 years, up from decade-long plan; raised $2 billion locally last year
  • 03/Restructuring comes amid US SEC fraud case against founder, copper plant technical setbacks, and Adani Enterprises slipping into quarterly loss

The Adani Group unveiled plans to overhaul its operating model on May 1, 2026. Founder Gautam Adani announced a three-layer organizational structure in an internal memo to employees.

The new structure will have fewer decision-makers. Leaders will be pushed closer to project sites.

The overhaul aims to cut decision-making time from days to hours. It will also strengthen accountability across business units.

The group will place greater focus on liquidity and access to capital. This comes as the conglomerate scales up its investment activity across infrastructure, energy and consumer-facing sectors.

This marks Adani's second major restructuring since 2015. That year, the group spun off its ports and power businesses into separately listed entities.

The conglomerate operates more than 700 sites across 24 states. It houses India's largest private sector ports and airports operators.

Adani Group has doubled its capital spending plan. It now intends to outlay $100 billion in five to six years instead of spreading it over a decade.

The group raised $2 billion from the local market last year. Chief Financial Officer Jugeshinder Singh previously said plans are to scale this up to $10 billion over three years.

Adani Enterprises Ltd (NSE: ADANIENT) approved raising ₹150 billion by selling shares on April 30, 2026. The company reported slipping into a loss in the last quarter.

The group is also spending around ₹50 billion ($527 million) to build a township across more than 175 acres in Mundra, Gujarat. The accommodation will house 50,000 workers.

The $1.2 billion copper plant in Kutch has hit technical setbacks since commissioning 10 months ago. The plant closed for repair work in late March and has yet to produce meaningful volumes.

The group faces a US fraud case against its founder that has dragged on for almost 18 months. Lawyers for Adani last month sought to have the Securities and Exchange Commission case dismissed.
