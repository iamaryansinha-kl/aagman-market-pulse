Reliance Jio, Amazon Leo Spar Over Satellite Network Framework at TRAI

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Reliance Jio and Amazon Leo clash over TRAI's proposed satellite communication network framework
  • 02/Jio argues only licensed telecom operators should hold spectrum through auctions
  • 03/Amazon Leo wants SCN entities to hold feeder link spectrum; Bharti Airtel and Vodafone Idea advocate a middle path

Telecom operator Reliance Jio and Amazon Leo have locked horns over the government's proposed satellite communication network framework. Spectrum ownership has emerged as a key source of disagreement.

The framework seeks to allow satellite companies to act as infrastructure providers. They would supply capacity to telecom and internet providers. This creates a new category of companies that would operate satellite communication networks, including ground stations.

Licensed telecom and satellite internet service providers would then use this infrastructure. They would deliver satellite internet services to users through this arrangement.

Jio has opposed granting spectrum rights to these infrastructure providers. The company argues that only licensed telecom operators should hold spectrum through auctions.

On Thursday, TRAI held an industry discussion on the proposed framework. This is for satellite communication network authorization and related spectrum terms. The framework is different from licences currently held by Starlink, OneWeb, and Jio Satellite.

Reliance Jio argued that spectrum for satellite communication network should be auctioned. U.K. Shrivastava, president of regulatory affairs at Reliance Jio, said the spectrum should be assigned only to authorized access service providers. No other entity, including the proposed SCN entity, should hold any right to use spectrum.

Shrivastava argued that a separate SCN category is unnecessary. He noted that a separation between service and network layers already exists. Introducing an SCN provider would create a regulatory imbalance and be detrimental to the sector's orderly growth.

Amazon Leo, which is in the fray to obtain authorisation to provide satellite internet services in India, opposed any auctions of such spectrum. Faheem Shaikh, lead of licensing and regulatory affairs at Amazon Leo, said the feeder link spectrum should be held by the SCN entity. The user link spectrum should be held by the partnering entity.

Under the Telecommunications Act 2023, the government has decided to administratively allocate satellite communication spectrum without auctions.

According to Shaikh, restricting ground segment spectrum assignment to Indian service providers would lock the infrastructure to domestic use cases only. SCN entities with global constellations would lose their ability to serve neighboring countries from Indian gateways.

Shaikh said there are landlocked countries like Nepal and Bhutan that depend on India or China for internet connectivity. The government should allow SCN to provide internet breakout through Indian gateways for them.

He warned that if this becomes a bottleneck, investment in ground infrastructure in India might shift to Bangladesh. Bangladesh has easier policies with local internet breakout.

Bharti Airtel and Vodafone Idea both advocated a middle path. The two operators stated that while user link spectrum should strictly remain with service providers, SCN entities must be allowed to hold the feeder link spectrum to effectively operate their gateways.

Jio also faced opposition from its peers on the rollout of direct-to-device satellite technology. This would allow smartphones to access mobile connectivity directly through satellites. Jio wants the government to wait for the World Radiocommunication Conference 2027 outcome before permitting D2D services.

Vodafone Idea's Ambika Khurana argued that waiting until 2027 means commercial rollouts would not happen until 2029-30. This would deny the country the ability to leverage the advantages that D2D brings.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT IS THE PROPOSED SATELLITE COMMUNICATION NETWORK FRAMEWORK?
THE FRAMEWORK ALLOWS SATELLITE COMPANIES TO ACT AS INFRASTRUCTURE PROVIDERS, SUPPLYING CAPACITY TO TELECOM AND INTERNET PROVIDERS. THIS CREATES A NEW CATEGORY OF SCN ENTITIES THAT OPERATE SATELLITE COMMUNICATION NETWORKS AND GROUND STATIONS.
WHAT IS RELIANCE JIO'S POSITION ON THE FRAMEWORK?
JIO OPPOSES GRANTING SPECTRUM RIGHTS TO SCN ENTITIES. IT ARGUES ONLY LICENSED TELECOM OPERATORS SHOULD HOLD SPECTRUM THROUGH AUCTIONS. JIO ALSO WANTS THE GOVERNMENT TO WAIT FOR WRC 2027 BEFORE PERMITTING DIRECT-TO-DEVICE SATELLITE SERVICES.
WHAT IS AMAZON LEO'S POSITION?
AMAZON LEO OPPOSES AUCTIONS OF SATELLITE SPECTRUM. IT WANTS SCN ENTITIES TO HOLD FEEDER LINK SPECTRUM WHILE PARTNERING ENTITIES HOLD USER LINK SPECTRUM. IT ARGUES RESTRICTING GROUND SEGMENT SPECTRUM WOULD LOCK INFRASTRUCTURE TO DOMESTIC USE ONLY.
WHAT IS THE MIDDLE PATH ADVOCATED BY AIRTEL AND VODAFONE IDEA?
BOTH OPERATORS STATE THAT USER LINK SPECTRUM SHOULD REMAIN WITH SERVICE PROVIDERS. BUT SCN ENTITIES MUST BE ALLOWED TO HOLD FEEDER LINK SPECTRUM TO EFFECTIVELY OPERATE THEIR GATEWAYS.
WHAT IS THE GOVERNMENT'S POSITION ON SATELLITE SPECTRUM ALLOCATION?
UNDER THE TELECOMMUNICATIONS ACT 2023, THE GOVERNMENT HAS DECIDED TO ADMINISTRATIVELY ALLOCATE SATELLITE COMMUNICATION SPECTRUM WITHOUT AUCTIONS.
