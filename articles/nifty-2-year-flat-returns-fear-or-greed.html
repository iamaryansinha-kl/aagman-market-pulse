Nifty 2-Year Flat Returns: Is It Time for Fear or Greed?

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/The Nifty 50 has delivered near-zero returns over two years, round-tripping from 26,277 in September 2024 back to the 24,000 zone
  • 02/Historical data across eleven comparable periods since 2001 shows one-year post-stagnation returns ranging from 5% to 50%
  • 03/Large-cap forward P/E has compressed to 17x, below the 18.8x average, while earnings growth is projected to accelerate to 14.3% in FY27

The Nifty 50 has delivered near-zero returns over the past two years. After peaking at 26,277 in September 2024, the index fell on global tariff shocks. It recovered to 26,373 in January 2026 before sliding back to the 24,000 zone.

Nifty is now trading around 24,095, effectively back to June-end 2024 levels. The two-year round trip has erased gains for long-term investors.

Historical data offers a clear pattern. Across eleven comparable stagnation periods since 2001, one-year returns ranged from 5% to 50%. There is no instance where flat two-year returns led to prolonged further stagnation.

After June 2001–June 2003 stagnation, the next one-year return was 33%. The July 2018–July 2020 period delivered 42% in the following year. The August 2018–August 2020 Covid overlap saw 50% gains.

Valuations have compressed sharply. Large-cap one-year forward P/E stands at 17x, below the seven-year average of 18.8x. The index trades at a 16% discount to its own history. Nearly 44% of large-cap stocks now trade below their 10-year median valuation.

Earnings growth is set to accelerate. Nifty 50 median EPS growth was stuck at 9-10% through FY25 and FY26. Forward estimates now pencil in 14.3% for FY27 and 16.5% for FY28.

"Valuations in the large cap space have come down and could be a good entry point," said Sandeep Bagla, CEO of TRUST Mutual Fund. Vinod Karki of ICICI Securities sees falling oil prices as a tailwind for Indian equities.

Not all are bullish. Nuvama remains guarded, noting GST cut tailwinds are fading and El Niño threatens farm output. The brokerage expects range-bound markets and favours defensives over cyclicals.
