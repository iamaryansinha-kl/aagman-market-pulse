Danish Brewing Giant Carlsberg Files IPO Papers for India Unit With SEBI

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Danish brewing giant Carlsberg confidentially files draft IPO papers with SEBI for its India unit
  • 02/Follows trend of multinationals tapping India equity markets; Hyundai and LG already listed
  • 03/Carlsberg operates Tuborg, Carlsberg Elephant, Kronenbourg 1664 Blanc brands in India

Danish brewing giant Carlsberg has confidentially filed draft papers with markets regulator SEBI for an initial public offering of its Indian business. People familiar with the development confirmed this on Thursday.

The confidential filing route allows companies to submit draft offer documents to SEBI for review. This happens without immediately making commercially sensitive information public.

With this move, Carlsberg joins a growing list of multinational companies looking to tap India's equity markets. South Korean conglomerates Hyundai Motor and LG Electronics have already listed their Indian subsidiaries to unlock shareholder value.

In February, Carlsberg Group CEO Jacob Aarup-Andersen had said the company was considering listing its India business. This was part of its strategy to unlock value for shareholders.

The size, valuation, and timing of the proposed IPO will depend on regulatory approvals. Market conditions and other factors will also influence the final terms, people familiar with the development added.

Carlsberg operates in India through popular beer brands such as Tuborg, Carlsberg Elephant, and Kronenbourg 1664 Blanc. The company launched the French wheat beer brand Kronenbourg 1664 Blanc in India last year. This strengthened its presence in the fast-growing premium beer segment.

The filing comes at a time when activity in India's primary market is gaining momentum. Easing geopolitical tensions and improving investor sentiment are supporting this trend. In June alone, six companies launched their maiden public offerings.

Jio Platforms and the National Stock Exchange filed their draft papers with SEBI in June. These are paving the way for two of the largest public offerings in the country's capital market history.

The Carlsberg India IPO would add to a growing pipeline of multinational listings. This reflects the attractiveness of India's capital markets for global companies seeking to unlock value in their local operations.

India's beer market has been growing steadily, driven by rising disposable incomes and a young demographic. Premium beer segments have shown particularly strong growth in recent years.

Carlsberg's move to list its India unit could set a precedent for other multinational beverage companies. Many are looking at similar strategies to capitalise on India's growth story.
