US Wants $500 Billion India Trade in Three Years: Gor

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- US Ambassador to India Sergio Gor set a target of exceeding $500 billion in bilateral trade within three years, from a record $240 billion now.
  • •- Gor was speaking at the Freedom 250 reception in Mumbai alongside US Secretary of State Marco Rubio.
  • •- The focus sectors named: semiconductors, critical minerals, energy, defence and superintelligence.
  • •- India's merchandise exports to the US stood at $87.31 billion in FY26, with imports of $53.45 billion.
  • •- Rubio called the India-US alignment "extraordinary" and said Washington should visit India more often.
  • •- An interim trade agreement framework announced in February remains under negotiation.

The number is the biggest one yet attached to the India-US trade reset. Addressing business leaders and diplomats at the Freedom 250 reception in Mumbai on Tuesday night, US Ambassador to India Sergio Gor said Washington has set an ambitious target of driving bilateral trade past $500 billion within the next three years — more than double the record $240 billion the two countries currently share. The venue and timing were deliberate: the reception, held during US Secretary of State Marco Rubio's three-day India visit, doubled as a statement that the economic relationship is now the load-bearing pillar of the wider alignment. "We are laser-focused on driving that number past $500 billion in the next three years," Gor said. "President Trump sent me to India with a clear mandate: deliver results. Don't just talk about the future. Make it happen." He added that in their recent discussions, Trump and Prime Minister Narendra Modi had reaffirmed precisely that commitment, putting a leader-level stamp on a target that would otherwise be an ambassador's aspiration.

The sector map Gor sketched shows where the growth is expected to come from: semiconductors, critical minerals, energy, defence and superintelligence — a list that reads like the negotiating agenda of the interim trade agreement framework India and the US announced in February, which covers market access for textiles, leather, engineering goods and pharmaceuticals, with tariff concessions still subject to conclusion. India's official trade data frames the distance to the target: merchandise exports to the US stood at $87.31 billion in FY2025-26 against imports of $53.45 billion, a goods total of roughly $141 billion that would need to more than triple, or be supplemented by an enormous services surge, to carry the bilateral figure to $500 billion. The $240 billion current total already includes services trade, which has been the quiet workhorse of the relationship — Indian IT exports to American enterprises remain the largest single services flow either country books.

Gor's Mumbai specifics grounded the rhetoric in corporate reality. More than 500 major American companies operate in the city, he noted, running through a list that spans Google, Meta and IBM in technology; Procter & Gamble and Johnson & Johnson in consumer goods; Pfizer, Abbott and Merck in pharmaceuticals; and Citi, Bank of America and JPMorgan Chase in finance. American institutional investors, he added, represent the largest single block of foreign capital in Indian equities traded on Mumbai's exchanges — "a massive vote of confidence in India's economic trajectory," as he put it. The ambassador's theatrical line, describing a partnership that "begins in the ocean depths with critical data infrastructure" and extends into deep space, was calibrated for the room; the portfolio data was calibrated for the dealmakers in it.

Rubio's own remarks at the reception supplied the strategic frame around the commercial target. Calling the alignment between the two countries "extraordinary," he conceded that "no two nations on Earth will ever be perfectly aligned on every issue," but argued that on the core issues defining national interests, India and the US are aligned to a degree capable of shaping geopolitics, economics and trade for decades. He characterised the opportunity as authoring the history of the 21st century together — the US as the planet's largest economy and India as a country nearing 1.5 billion people — and observed, pointedly, that Washington "doesn't come enough" to one of its most vital partners, a line that doubles as a promise of more senior-level engagement. The energy dialogue between Rubio and External Affairs Minister S Jaishankar, reported alongside the reception, supplies the immediate agenda: energy trade is both a deficit-reducer for the bilateral balance and the lever most directly linked to sanctions-waiver decisions hanging over the relationship.

The $500 billion figure lands in a specific political context. The interim trade agreement framework announced in February has been under negotiation since, with tariff concessions on Indian exports still unannounced; Rubio's comments earlier in the visit that the deal is "very, very close" and his reported linkage of progress to national-security waiver decisions — including those covering India's purchases of Russian oil — give the target a bargaining function as much as an aspirational one. A public number, once spoken by an ambassador at a flagship reception, becomes a benchmark against which both governments will be measured. Whether the three-year clock is tied to the interim agreement's conclusion, or runs independently of it, will determine whether $500 billion reads in 2029 as prophecy or as diplomacy.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT IS THE NEW US TRADE TARGET FOR INDIA?
US Ambassador Sergio Gor said Washington aims to exceed $500 billion in bilateral trade within three years, from a record $240 billion currently.
WHICH SECTORS WILL DRIVE THE GROWTH?
Gor named semiconductors, critical minerals, energy, defence and superintelligence as the focus sectors.
WHAT ARE CURRENT TRADE LEVELS?
India's merchandise exports to the US were $87.31 billion in FY26 with imports of $53.45 billion; total bilateral trade including services has crossed $240 billion.
WHERE DOES THE TRADE DEAL STAND?
An interim trade agreement framework announced in February is still under negotiation, with Rubio saying this week that a deal is "very, very close." GLOSSARY: - Bilateral trade: The total flow of goods and services between two countries. - Interim trade agreement: A preliminary trade pact covering select sectors, intended as a step toward a fuller deal. - Tariff concession: A reduction in import duties granted as part of a trade agreement. - Waiver decision: An official exemption from sanctions requirements, granted at executive discretion.
