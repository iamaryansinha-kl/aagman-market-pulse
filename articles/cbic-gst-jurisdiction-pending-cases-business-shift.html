CBIC: New GST Jurisdiction to Take Over Pending Cases on Business Shift

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/CBIC mandates that new GST jurisdictional authority takes over all pending tax proceedings when a taxpayer shifts principal place of business
  • 02/Investigations, audits, show cause notices and adjudication actions initiated by the previous authority remain valid and must be continued
  • 03/AMRG Global's Rajat Mohan says the move fills a procedural gap and removes ambiguity that caused delays in adjudication

The Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBIC) has clarified that new jurisdictional authorities will take over pending proceedings when a taxpayer shifts their principal place of business. The circular was issued on June 27, 2026.

Any investigation, audit, show cause notice, or adjudication initiated by the previous tax officer remains valid. The new authority must act upon it as if it had itself initiated the same action.

The clarification was issued after field formations sought guidance on the validity of actions taken when a taxpayer's jurisdiction changed. The move addresses a procedural gap that had created uncertainty for migrating businesses.

If fresh issues emerge after the shift, the earlier authority must intimate the new jurisdictional officer for further action. The transferor authority cannot continue proceedings on its own once the taxpayer has migrated.

The transferee authority will take over and conclude proceedings from the stage at which they stood at the time of migration. It can also initiate any consequential proceedings arising from the case.

"By clearly defining the responsibilities of transferor and transferee authorities, CBIC has removed ambiguity that often resulted in jurisdictional objections and delays in adjudication," said AMRG Global Managing Partner Rajat Mohan.

The move fills a procedural gap under GST. It is expected to bring greater consistency in handling cases involving the migration of taxpayers across jurisdictions.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT HAPPENS TO PENDING GST PROCEEDINGS WHEN A BUSINESS SHIFTS JURISDICTION?
THE NEW JURISDICTIONAL AUTHORITY (TRANSFEREE) TAKES OVER AND CONCLUDES ALL PENDING PROCEEDINGS FROM THE STAGE AT WHICH THEY STOOD AT THE TIME OF MIGRATION. THIS INCLUDES INVESTIGATIONS, AUDITS, SHOW CAUSE NOTICES, AND ADJUDICATION UNDER CENTRAL GST LAW.
DO ACTIONS INITIATED BY THE PREVIOUS AUTHORITY REMAIN VALID?
YES. ANY ACTION OR PROCEEDING UNDERTAKEN BY THE PREVIOUS (TRANSFEROR) JURISDICTIONAL AUTHORITY REMAINS VALID EVEN AFTER THE TAXPAYER SHIFTS TO A NEW JURISDICTION. THE NEW AUTHORITY SHALL ACT UPON AND PROCEED AS IF IT HAD INITIATED THE SAME.
WHAT IF A FRESH ISSUE COMES TO LIGHT AFTER THE BUSINESS HAS SHIFTED?
IF THE EARLIER JURISDICTIONAL AUTHORITY DISCOVERS ANY FRESH ISSUE AFTER THE TAXPAYER HAS SHIFTED, IT MUST INTIMATE THE NEW JURISDICTIONAL OFFICER FOR FURTHER ACTION.
WHY DID CBIC ISSUE THIS CLARIFICATION?
CBIC ISSUED THE CIRCULAR AFTER RECEIVING REFERENCES FROM FIELD FORMATIONS SEEKING CLARITY ON THE VALIDITY OF ACTIONS TAKEN AND THE COMPETENT AUTHORITY WHEN A TAXPAYER'S JURISDICTION CHANGED DUE TO A SHIFT IN PRINCIPAL PLACE OF BUSINESS.
WHAT IS THE SIGNIFICANCE OF THIS MOVE FOR TAXPAYERS?
THE CLARIFICATION FILLS A CRITICAL PROCEDURAL GAP UNDER GST. IT REMOVES AMBIGUITY THAT OFTEN RESULTED IN JURISDICTIONAL OBJECTIONS AND DELAYS IN ADJUDICATION, BRINGING GREATER CONSISTENCY FOR MIGRATING BUSINESSES.
