Trump Made 3,700+ Stock Trades in Q1, Tens of Millions in Volume

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Trump's Q1 2026 financial disclosure shows over 3,700 trades, averaging 40+ per day over three months
  • 02/Purchases included at least $1M each in Nvidia, Oracle, Microsoft, Boeing, Costco; sales included $5-25M of Microsoft, Meta, Amazon on Feb 10
  • 03/Ethics experts call volume "insane" and "baffling"; Trump paid $200 fines for missing federal disclosure deadlines

President Donald Trump's latest financial disclosure reveals over 3,700 trades in the first quarter of 2026. The transactions totaled tens of millions of dollars and involved major companies with government dealings.

The trades, detailed in 100+ pages filed Thursday with the US Office of Government Ethics, list purchases and sales in broad ranges. The exact value is hard to calculate. But the volume stands out: more than 40 trades per day over a three-month period.

"This is an insane amount of trades," said Matthew Tuttle, CEO of Tuttle Capital Management. He said it looks like "a hedge fund with massive algo trades" rather than a personal account.

In Q1, Trump bought at least $1 million each in Nvidia Corp., Oracle Corp., Microsoft Corp., Boeing Co., and Costco Wholesale Corp. Other trades involved eBay, Abbott Laboratories, Uber Technologies, AT&T, and Dollar Tree.

The disclosure reignites conflict-of-interest concerns. Critics accuse Trump of mixing official duties with business interests. Unlike predecessors, he did not divest assets or use a blind trust.

His biggest sales came on Feb 10. He unloaded holdings in Microsoft, Meta Platforms, and Amazon.com in amounts between $5 million and $25 million each.

Six trades involved Intel Corp. His administration hammered out an agreement to take a 10% stake for nearly $9 billion in August. Intel shares gained 20% in Q1 and more than doubled in April.

"All of this raises questions that you'd rather not raise as a president," said Tuttle. "When you're the president you know everything, so any stock you buy, there's a huge question mark."

Federal ethics law requires trade reporting within 45 days. Both filings missed the deadline. The penalty: a $200 fine per late filing. Trump's disclosures show he paid the fee.

"I'm baffled," said Eric Diton, president of The Wealth Alliance. "In 40-plus years on Wall Street, this is an unusual amount of trading by any standards."

Trump's son-in-law Jared Kushner manages billions for Qatar, Saudi Arabia, and UAE while serving as a "volunteer" envoy on Middle East issues. The White House dismissed conflict questions, saying Trump "only acts in the best interests of the American public."
