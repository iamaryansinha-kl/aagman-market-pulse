Hindustan Zinc Q2 Profit Skyrockets 142% to Rs 6,412 Crore

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Consolidated net profit rose 142% YoY to Rs 6,412 crore from Rs 2,649 crore.
  • •- Revenue surged 76.5% to Rs 15,093 crore; EBITDA doubled to Rs 9,285 crore.
  • •- EBITDA margin expanded to 62% from 52% in the year-ago quarter.
  • •- Silver sales jumped 141% to Rs 4,111 crore; zinc sales rose 54% to Rs 8,224 crore.
  • •- Cost of production fell 29% YoY to $703 per tonne excluding royalty.
  • •- Record quarterly output: 271 KT mined metal and 264 KT refined metal.

If Q2 results season has a single chart that captures the commodity repricing of 2026, it is Hindustan Zinc's margin line. On Monday the Vedanta-group metals major reported consolidated net profit of Rs 6,412 crore for the July-September quarter, up 142 per cent from Rs 2,649 crore a year earlier, on revenue that rose 76.5 per cent to Rs 15,093 crore. EBITDA more than doubled to Rs 9,285 crore, and the EBITDA margin expanded ten full percentage points to 62 per cent — a level most software companies would recognise and almost no miner achieves.

The revenue engine was silver. Net silver sales surged 141 per cent year-on-year to Rs 4,111 crore, riding prices that have been on a historic run as industrial demand from solar and electronics collided with safe-haven flows. Zinc sales rose 54 per cent to Rs 8,224 crore, and lead and other metals jumped 35 and 151 per cent respectively. What makes the quarter more than a price story is the cost side: the cost of production excluding royalty fell 29 per cent year-on-year to $703 per tonne, driven by better mined grades, higher metal production, greater renewable power consumption and richer by-product realisations — partly offset, the company noted, by lower domestic coal usage and input inflation linked to the global geopolitical crisis. Falling unit costs into rising prices is the oldest margin trade in mining, executed at record scale.

Production set records while it was at it. Mined metal output reached 271 kilotonnes and refined metal production hit 264 kilotonnes, both all-time quarterly highs, giving the company operating leverage exactly when the price environment rewards it most. Chief executive Amarendu Prakash framed the result around capability rather than luck, citing disciplined growth, AI-led innovation and the critical-minerals portfolio the company is building to support India's energy transition. Chief financial officer Amit Gupta pointed to the 62 per cent margin, strong cash generation and balance-sheet strength as evidence of financial discipline — the kind of language that typically precedes capital-allocation decisions shareholders welcome.

The stock market's verdict, as with most metal names this year, has lagged the P&L. Shares are down about 2 per cent over the past week, 10 per cent over the month and more than 12 per cent in 2026, even after an 8 per cent gain over the past year; the three-year return of 71 per cent is the truer picture of what the franchise has built. The market capitalisation stands above Rs 2.28 lakh crore. The gap between a doubling profit and a falling share price is the market pricing a commodity peak: the question embedded in the discount is whether silver and zinc prices at these levels are a level or a plateau. Valuations across global miners have compressed this year as funds rotated out of anything with a spot-price beta, and Indian metals names were swept along despite reporting in rupees and selling into a domestic demand story that has nothing to do with the global growth scare.

What argues for the plateau view is the demand structure underneath the metals themselves — silver's solar and electronics consumption, zinc's galvanisation of the infrastructure buildout, and India's strategic interest in both as critical minerals. The government's critical-minerals mission has made domestic output of exactly the metals Hindustan Zinc produces a policy priority, which gives the company a second tailwind beyond price: a regulatory and strategic environment that wants its capacity expanded, not merely maintained. What argues for caution is that 142 per cent profit growth always has a price component that mean-reverts. Hindustan Zinc's answer to that cyclicality is already visible in this quarter's numbers: record volumes, structurally lower costs and a margin cushion wide enough to absorb a price giveback that would gut a higher-cost producer. Miners cannot choose their prices. They can choose their cost curve, and this quarter Hindustan Zinc chose decisively.

There is also a cash-flow dimension the headline profit obscures. A 62 per cent EBITDA margin on Rs 15,093 crore of revenue implies quarterly operating cash generation measured in thousands of crores, and Hindustan Zinc has historically been among the more generous dividend payers in the Indian metals complex — a trait that matters more, not less, in a year when its parent group's broader funding dynamics have kept investors attentive to cash movement up the ownership chain. The critical-minerals pivot, meanwhile, is not rhetoric: silver, zinc and lead all sit on India's declared critical list, and the company's commentary about AI-led operations is part of a genuine industry shift toward sensor-driven mining that reduces both cost and safety risk. None of that eliminates commodity risk. But it reframes the stock from a pure zinc proxy into something closer to a diversified, cash-generative metals franchise whose quarter just demonstrated what its best case looks like.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT WERE HINDUSTAN ZINC'S Q2 RESULTS?
Net profit rose 142% YoY to Rs 6,412 crore, revenue rose 76.5% to Rs 15,093 crore, and EBITDA doubled to Rs 9,285 crore at a 62% margin.
WHAT DROVE THE SURGE?
Higher metal prices, record production, stronger by-product realisation and a weaker rupee; silver sales rose 141% to Rs 4,111 crore and zinc sales 54% to Rs 8,224 crore.
HOW DID COSTS PERFORM?
Cost of production excluding royalty fell 29% YoY to $703 per tonne on better grades, higher output and more renewable power.
WHAT WAS RECORD PRODUCTION?
Mined metal of 271 KT and refined metal of 264 KT, both quarterly records. GLOSSARY: - EBITDA margin: Operating profit as a share of revenue, before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation. - Cost of production (COP): The all-in cost to produce one tonne of metal, excluding government royalty. - Mined metal: Ore extracted and processed into concentrate; the first stage of output. - By-products: Secondary metals such as silver and lead recovered alongside the primary metal.
