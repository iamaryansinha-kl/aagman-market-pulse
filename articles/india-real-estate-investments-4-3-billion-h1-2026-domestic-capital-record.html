India Real Estate Investments Hit $4.3 Billion in H1 2026, Domestic Capital at Record 64%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Institutional real estate investments in India rose 23% YoY to USD 4.3 billion in H1 2026 across a record 54 transactions
  • 02/Domestic investors accounted for a record 64% of total capital at USD 2.8 billion, up 165% YoY, while foreign investment fell 37%
  • 03/Office sector reclaimed top position with USD 2.3 billion (54% share); JLL expects 2026 full-year inflows of USD 8.5-9 billion

India's institutional real estate market attracted USD 4.3 billion in investments during the first half of 2026. This marks a 23% year-on-year increase, according to a JLL report released on June 25, 2026.

The rebound follows a subdued H1 2025. A record-breaking second half of 2025 had pushed full-year institutional investments to USD 10.5 billion.

A total of 54 transactions were recorded in H1 2026, the highest ever for a six-month period. However, the average deal size dropped nearly 40% from USD 133 million to USD 80 million.

Investors spread capital across more transactions rather than concentrating on a few large deals. This reflects a more risk-calibrated approach amid global uncertainty.

The most striking shift was the surge in domestic institutional participation. Domestic investors contributed USD 2.8 billion, a robust 165% YoY jump, capturing a record 64% share of total investments.

Foreign institutional investments declined 37% amid inflationary pressures, currency concerns, and capital repatriation requirements. Domestic PE funds and REITs together contributed 72% of domestic capital.

Domestic investors have increasingly favoured equity investments. Equity accounted for 83% of domestic capital deployment in H1 2026, compared with a more balanced debt-equity mix before 2025.

The office sector emerged as the biggest beneficiary, reclaiming the top spot with a 54% share. Office investments rose 34% YoY to USD 2.3 billion across 17 transactions.

Domestic investors dominated office deals, accounting for 89% of capital deployed in the segment. JLL attributed this to India's expanding Global Capability Centre ecosystem and stabilising return-to-office trends.

Bengaluru, Chennai, and Delhi-NCR remained the leading investment destinations. Together, these three cities accounted for 46% of total institutional investment volumes in H1 2026.

"The most significant development is the unprecedented surge in domestic institutional participation, which now commands 64% of total capital flows, the highest level ever recorded. This growth, driven by domestic PE players and REITs, signals the early stages of maturation of India's domestic investment landscape and substantially reduced vulnerability to external shocks," said Lata Pillai, Senior Managing Director & Head of Capital Markets, India, JLL.

Looking ahead, JLL expects foreign investors to return as geopolitical risks ease and inflation moderates. Historically, H1 contributes 50-52% of annual institutional investments, suggesting full-year 2026 inflows could reach USD 8.5-9 billion.
