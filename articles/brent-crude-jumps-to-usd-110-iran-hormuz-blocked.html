Brent Crude Jumps 5.8% to USD 110.44 as Iran Keeps Hormuz Blocked

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Brent crude July contract surged 5.8% to settle at USD 110.44 per barrel as Iran keeps Strait of Hormuz closed
  • 02/US stocks held near records with S&P 500 nearly unchanged at 7,135.95 despite oil shock and Fed holding rates steady
  • 03/Traders per CME Group eliminated nearly all bets for a 2026 rate cut, favoring a small chance for a Fed hike instead

Brent crude for July delivery jumped 5.8% to settle at USD 110.44 per barrel on Wednesday. The contract reached an intraday high of USD 111.84 during the session.

Oil prices surged as President Donald Trump maintains the US blockade of Iranian ships. Iran responded by keeping the Strait of Hormuz closed to other oil tankers carrying crude worldwide.

The June Brent contract briefly breached USD 120 during trading. That topped the previous high of USD 119.50 reached last month since the war with Iran began.

MCX crude oil futures in India traded at ₹10,385 per barrel, up ₹259. The Sensex and Nifty opened lower as the crude shock weighed on market sentiment.

The S&P 500 slipped just 2.85 points to 7,135.95, nearly unchanged. The Dow Jones dropped 280.12 points, or 0.6%, while the Nasdaq composite added 9.44.

The Federal Reserve held interest rates steady on Wednesday. Three Fed officials opposed including any language suggesting more rate cuts may be coming.

Treasury yields climbed after the Fed's announcement. The 10-year Treasury yield rose to 4.41% from 4.36%, while the two-year yield jumped to 3.93% from 3.84%.

Strong earnings from Visa and Starbucks helped support US stocks. Visa jumped 8.3% and Starbucks climbed 8.4% after beating forecasts. "Consumer spending remained resilient in the quarter," said Ryan McInerney, CEO of Visa.
