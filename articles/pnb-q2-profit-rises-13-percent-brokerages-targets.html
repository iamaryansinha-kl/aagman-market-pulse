PNB Q2 Profit Jumps 13% to Rs 5,532 Crore; Stock Soars 8%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Standalone net profit rose ~13% YoY to Rs 5,532 crore in Q2FY27, from Rs 4,904 crore a year earlier.
  • •- Net interest income grew ~11% YoY to Rs 11,601 crore, with margins expanding 8 basis points sequentially.
  • •- Gross NPA ratio improved 90 basis points YoY to 2.55%; net NPA fell to 0.25%.
  • •- The stock jumped around 8% intraday, its biggest single-day gain in over three years.
  • •- Jefferies, Motilal Oswal and JM Financial all set target prices of Rs 140; JM Financial upgraded to Buy.
  • •- Management guided for return on assets above 1% and credit cost of 0.25-0.30%.

Results season on Dalal Street has produced an unusual pattern this quarter: private lenders reporting solid numbers and getting sold, public sector banks reporting solid numbers and getting bought. Punjab National Bank was Monday's clearest example. The state-run lender reported a July-September standalone net profit of Rs 5,532 crore, up roughly thirteen per cent from Rs 4,904 crore a year earlier, powered by stronger net interest income, higher treasury income and controlled provisions — and the market rewarded it with an eight per cent intraday rally, the stock's biggest single-day gain in more than three years.

The quarter's engine was the margin. Net interest income rose about eleven per cent year-on-year to Rs 11,601 crore from Rs 10,469 crore, and the margin expanded eight basis points sequentially as the bank's funding mix improved — a lower share of bulk deposits on the liability side and a continued shift away from low-yielding corporate and IBPC arrangements on the asset side. Operating profit rose four per cent to Rs 7,532 crore, and return on assets reached 1.05 per cent. JM Financial, which upgraded the stock to Buy from Add and raised its target to Rs 140 from Rs 120, noted the highest-ever return on assets of 1.09 per cent, twenty-four per cent fee income growth and negligible credit cost of just three basis points. Loan growth of sixteen per cent year-on-year, with the advance book continuing its tilt toward retail, agriculture and MSME segments, gave the margin story an earnings base to compound against.

Asset quality continued its long cleanup. The gross NPA ratio fell ninety basis points year-on-year to 2.55 per cent, and the net NPA ratio dropped to 0.25 per cent. Motilal Oswal, which maintained its Buy with a Rs 140 target, flagged the marginal uptick in slippages from a single infrastructure corporate account — noise, in its view, against an otherwise clean book — and noted the bank has been building additional standard-asset provisions to smooth its transition to the expected credit loss framework. Management's guidance was confident: return on assets above one per cent and credit cost between 0.25 and 0.30 per cent, with a further seven to nine basis points of margin improvement expected in coming quarters as the repo-linked book reprices and funding costs stay stable.

Brokerage reaction was uniform. Jefferies, which also carries a Buy and raised its target to Rs 140 — implying more than twenty per cent upside from Friday's close of Rs 116 — said profit came in ahead of estimates with better NII, higher treasury income and lower provisions offsetting elevated operating costs, and lifted its earnings estimates explicitly because of the rate-hike cycle and the NIM trajectory. All three houses landing on the same Rs 140 target price is a rare display of consensus, and it frames the debate: at roughly twelve times earnings with an improving margin profile, the cheapest major bank in the index is being repriced as a normalization story rather than a value trap.

For PNB itself, the strategic question is what the new profitability pays for. The bank has spent a decade repairing a legacy book that once carried the industry's worst bad-loan ratio, and the emerging question of its ownership — the government's stake sale discussions hover in the background of every PSU bank story — becomes easier to advance when the franchise earns its cost of equity convincingly for several consecutive quarters. Monday's numbers were one more quarter of that proof, and the first in which the market paid for it in full. Whether the re-rating continues depends on the guidance holding: margins rising another seven to nine basis points, credit costs contained near a quarter of a per cent, and the RoA floor of one per cent surviving the next phase of the rate cycle. On Monday's evidence, the cheapest big bank in India is done apologizing for being cheap, and the market has stopped asking it to.

The wider significance is the rotation the rally captures. As the RBI's tightening cycle lifts net interest margins across the system, PSU banks — with their sticky low-cost deposit franchises and legacy cheap funding — are the cycle's leveraged beneficiaries, while private lenders face questions about deposit competition and premium valuations. The pattern was visible across the tape on Monday: Bank of Baroda and Canara Bank rose in step with PNB while HDFC Bank and Axis fell after their own results, a cross-sectional divergence that says more about the cycle's incidence than any single earnings print. The eight per cent pop in PNB was therefore about more than one quarter's numbers: it was the market pricing a regime in which margin expansion favors the unfashionable end of the banking sector, and in which a decade of balance-sheet repair at state-run banks has finally made their earnings investable rather than merely tolerable.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT WERE PNB'S Q2FY27 NUMBERS?
Standalone net profit rose 13% YoY to Rs 5,532 crore, NII rose 11% to Rs 11,601 crore, gross NPAs improved to 2.55% from 3.45%, and RoA reached 1.05%.
WHAT DID BROKERAGES SAY?
Jefferies, Motilal Oswal and JM Financial all set Rs 140 target prices; JM Financial upgraded to Buy from Add, citing record RoA of 1.09% and 24% fee income growth.
WHY DID THE STOCK JUMP 8%?
The beat was driven by margin expansion, lower provisions and strong asset quality, with management guiding to further margin improvement of 7-9 basis points.
WHAT IS MANAGEMENT'S OUTLOOK?
PNB targets RoA above 1% and credit cost of 0.25-0.30%, supported by repricing of its repo-linked loan book. GLOSSARY: - Net interest margin: The difference between interest earned on loans and interest paid on deposits, as a share of assets. - IBPC: Inter-bank participation certificate — a short-term arrangement where banks sell loan exposure to each other. - ECL: Expected credit loss — the forward-looking accounting framework for loan-loss provisioning. - Credit cost: Provisions for bad loans as a percentage of total advances.
