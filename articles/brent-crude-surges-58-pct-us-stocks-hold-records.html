Brent Crude Surges 5.8% to $110.44 as US Stocks Hold Near Record Highs

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Brent crude for July delivery surged 5.8% to settle at $110.44 per barrel, with intraday highs touching $111.84.
  • 02/The S&P 500 slipped less than 0.1% to 7,135.95, while the Dow Jones fell 0.6% to 48,861.81 as corporate earnings offset oil shock fears.
  • 03/The Federal Reserve held rates at 3.5%-3.75%; traders now price in zero chance of a 2026 rate cut per CME Group data.

Brent crude for July delivery jumped 5.8% to settle at $110.44 per barrel on Wednesday. The contract traded as high as $111.84 during the session.

The June Brent contract briefly breached $120. That surpassed the $119.50 peak reached last month, the highest since the war with Iran began.

President Donald Trump is maintaining the US blockade of Iranian ships. Iran has responded by keeping the Strait of Hormuz closed to tankers carrying crude to global customers.

The S&P 500 finished nearly unchanged, slipping just 2.85 points to 7,135.95. The Nasdaq composite added 9.44 to 24,673.24, while the Dow Jones dropped 280.12 points to 48,861.81.

Strong earnings from big companies helped US stocks remain resilient. Visa jumped 8.3% after beating analyst expectations, and Starbucks climbed 8.4% on better-than-forecast results.

Companies that missed expectations were punished sharply. GE Healthcare Technologies dropped 13.2% after falling short of forecasts, and Robinhood Markets sank 13.2% on disappointing profit growth.

The Federal Reserve announced it is holding interest rates steady at 3.5%-3.75%. High oil prices and inflation risks pushed the central bank to pause any rate cut plans.

Three Fed officials did not want the policy statement to suggest more cuts were coming. Treasury yields climbed immediately after the decision.

The 10-year Treasury yield rose to 4.41% from 4.36%. The two-year yield, which tracks Fed expectations more closely, jumped to 3.93% from 3.84%.

Traders now largely expect the Fed to hold rates steady through year-end, per CME Group data. They have nearly eliminated bets for a rate cut in 2026 and now see a small chance of a hike.

Global markets were mixed. Hong Kong's Hang Seng jumped 1.7%, while London's FTSE 100 fell 1.2%.
