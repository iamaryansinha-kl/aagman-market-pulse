Iran War Drains Global Oil Buffer at Record 4.8M Barrels/Day

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Global oil stockpiles dropped 4.8 million barrels per day between March 1 and April 25, the fastest quarterly drawdown on record per IEA data.
  • 02/The Strait of Hormuz has been nearly closed for two months, cutting more than a billion barrels of supply and pushing global visible stocks to their lowest since 2018.
  • 03/JPMorgan warns OECD inventories could hit "operational stress" in early June and "operational minimum" by September if the strait does not reopen.

The Iran war has burned through global oil inventories at an unprecedented pace. Morgan Stanley estimates stockpiles fell 4.8 million barrels per day from March 1 to April 25.

That drawdown far exceeds any previous quarterly decline in International Energy Agency records. Crude accounts for roughly 60 per cent of the drop, with refined fuels making up the rest.

More than a billion barrels of supply have been lost since the Strait of Hormuz nearly closed two months ago. The buffer protecting against supply shocks is shrinking fast.

"Inventories are acting as the shock absorber of the global oil system," said Natasha Kaneva, head of global commodities research at JPMorgan Chase & Co. "But not every barrel can be drawn."

Global visible oil stocks are already near their lowest level since 2018, according to Goldman Sachs Group Inc. The bank noted some slowdown recently due to weaker Chinese demand, but the overall picture remains tight.

Asia-Pacific inventories outside China have been hit hardest, falling about 70 million barrels since the conflict began, per geospatial firm Kayrros. Japan's stocks are down 50 per cent, while India's are off 10 per cent.

The US has become the supplier of last resort and has already drawn domestic crude and fuel inventories below historical averages. US distillate stockpiles hit their lowest since 2005, while gasoline stocks hover near seasonal lows not seen since 2014.

The Strategic Petroleum Reserve is poised to fall to its lowest level since 1982. Washington has released only 79.7 million of the 172 million barrels it pledged under the IEA-coordinated emergency release.

JPMorgan's Kaneva warned that OECD inventories could reach "operational stress levels" in early June. By September, the world may hit the bare minimum oil needed for pipelines, tanks, and terminals to function.

Europe faces a jet fuel crisis ahead of summer. ARA hub stocks have plunged a third since the war began to a six-year low, per Insights Global. "Everybody's scrambling for whatever jet fuel they can get," said Lars van Wageningen at Insights Global.

Chevron Corp. CFO Eimear Bonner told Bloomberg TV on May 1: "A lot of the inventory and spare capacity has been depleted already. We are going to start to see some import-dependent countries potentially start to face critical shortages as we get into the June-July time-frame."

Pakistan's petroleum ministry said in late April it holds roughly 20 days of commercial refined product reserves. India's oil ministry claimed adequate crude on May 3, though state refiners privately acknowledged sizable drawdowns.

Diesel, the lifeblood of global commerce, is also under pressure. "Vietnam, Philippines are in a more dire situation," said Xavier Tang, senior market analyst at Vortexa Ltd.

Even if the Strait of Hormuz reopens, Gulf output and shipping will not normalise quickly. Post-war restocking could create an additional demand layer. "We would not be surprised to see several countries restock their SPRs above pre-war levels," said Willie Chiang, CEO of Plains All American Pipeline LP.
