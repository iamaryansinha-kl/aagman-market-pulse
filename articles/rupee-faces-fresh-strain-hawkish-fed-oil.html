Rupee Faces Fresh Strain as Hawkish Fed and $121 Oil Compound Weakness

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/The Indian rupee settled at 94.8450 on April 29 and is expected to open at 94.90-94.95, heading for its third consecutive weekly decline.
  • 02/Hawkish signals from Federal Reserve policymakers lifted the dollar index to 98.91 and pushed Brent crude toward $121 per barrel, compounding import pressure.
  • 03/Foreign portfolio investors sold a net $210.7 million in Indian equities on April 28, adding to capital outflow headwinds.

The Indian rupee is set to face fresh headwinds on April 30 after hawkish remarks from Federal Reserve policymakers lifted the dollar and US bond yields. The currency is expected to open in the 94.90-94.95 range after settling at 94.8450 on Wednesday.

The rupee is on course to log its third consecutive weekly decline. It has wiped out nearly all of the gains made earlier in the month after the Reserve Bank of India rolled out rare measures to curb excessive speculation.

Overnight, the Federal Reserve left policy rates unchanged. However, the decision was its most divided since 1992, with three officials dissenting over guidance that still signalled a bias towards easing. The dollar index traded at 98.91 while the ten-year US Treasury note yield stood at 4.42%.

"Pending price pressures are clearly seeping into more cautious views, but the bar to hike rates is still high," said Tai Hui, APAC chief market strategist at J.P. Morgan Asset Management. He added that the Fed can be patient as the conflict and its impact on the economy and prices unfolds.

Oil prices climbed again on Wednesday and extended gains in Asia trade on Thursday. Brent crude futures were last quoted at nearly $121 per barrel, putting them on course for a weekly jump of about 14%. Surging oil prices and weakness in capital flows are contributing to a persistent bias towards rupee weakness.

Offshore markets remain tilted towards positioning for further depreciation. Onshore moves are being driven largely by real dollar demand after the RBI curbs on banks' positions dampened speculative activity, a trader at a foreign bank said.

Foreign portfolio investors sold a net $210.7 million worth of Indian shares on April 28, as per NSDL data. They also sold a net $10.9 million worth of Indian bonds on the same day.

Concerns over persistent rupee weakness have prompted state-run refiners to scale back the use of a special foreign exchange credit line introduced to reduce spot dollar purchases for oil imports.

The one-month non-deliverable rupee forward was quoted at 95.32 with NDF points at 35 paisa. The RBI had earlier capped banks' net open forex positions at $100 million and barred them from offering rupee non-deliverable forwards to clients.
