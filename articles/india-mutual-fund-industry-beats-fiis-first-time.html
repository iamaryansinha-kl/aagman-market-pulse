India's Mutual Fund Industry Beats FIIs for the First Time Ever

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

India's mutual fund industry has crossed a historic milestone.

Domestic mutual funds have overtaken foreign institutional investors in a key measure for the first time ever. The shift reflects the growing dominance of domestic institutional capital in Indian markets.

The mutual fund industry's assets under management have surged to record levels. Systematic investment plans have been the primary engine of growth, with monthly SIP inflows consistently exceeding Rs 20,000 crore.

Retail participation has expanded dramatically. The number of mutual fund folios has crossed 20 crore, up from just a few crore a decade ago. Equity-oriented schemes have attracted the bulk of new money as investors shift from traditional savings instruments to market-linked products.

The surpassing of FIIs represents a structural shift in India's capital markets. For years, foreign portfolio investors were the dominant force, with their buying and selling often dictating market direction. Now, domestic institutions have the scale and flows to provide a stabilising counterweight.

Market participants say the trend is likely to continue. India's savings pool is vast, and financialisation of savings remains in early stages. As more households move from physical assets like gold and real estate to financial assets, mutual funds stand to benefit.

The milestone also has implications for market volatility. Domestic investors tend to be more sticky than foreign investors, who can redeem quickly during global risk-off events. A larger domestic base could reduce the amplitude of market swings.
