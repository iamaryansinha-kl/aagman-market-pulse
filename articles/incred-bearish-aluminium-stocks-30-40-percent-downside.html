InCred Equities Sees 30-40% Downside in Aluminium Stocks, Advises Exit

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/InCred Equities has turned bearish on the aluminium sector, warning of a 30-40% downside across aluminium stocks
  • 02/The brokerage argues aluminium is an above-ground circular metal, not a consumed commodity like oil, with 1.5 billion tonnes still available
  • 03/Vedanta Aluminium fell 4% on Thursday, NALCO dropped 3%, and Hindalco declined 2% as aluminium stocks fell up to 16% in the past month

InCred Equities has advised investors to exit all aluminium stocks immediately. The domestic brokerage sees a potential 30-40% downside amid mounting headwinds across the sector.

The brokerage argues the market is incorrectly valuing aluminium as a supply-constrained primary metal. Aluminium is actually an above-ground circular metal, unlike consumed commodities such as crude oil or coal.

Nearly 1.5 billion tonnes of aluminium remains available above the ground. Almost 80% of all aluminium ever produced is still part of the usable metal pool today.

China's primary aluminium output rose from 41.6 million tonnes in 2023 to 44.0 million tonnes in 2024. It is now close to the 45 million tonnes per annum policy cap.

However, this ignores the secondary aluminium pool. China's secondary aluminium consumption rose from 12.7 million tonnes in 2024 to 13.35 million tonnes in 2025.

Scrap imports into China increased from 1.7 million tonnes in 2023 to 2.02 million tonnes in 2025. Around 80% of China's scrap supply is domestic, replenishing the visible primary deficit.

The Middle East disruption also appears temporary rather than structural. Around 2.2 million tonnes per annum of primary capacity was affected, but supply from Qatar Aluminium and Alba can normalise relatively quickly.

With aluminium prices vulnerable to an $800 per tonne fall, InCred believes current valuations of NALCO (NSE: NATIONALUM), Vedanta Aluminium, and Hindalco Industries (NSE: HINDALCO) look stretched. The brokerage has a 'Reduce' call on NALCO and Hindalco.

Vedanta Aluminium Metal shares dropped over 4% on Thursday. The stock is overall down over 10% since listing on the market last week after the mega demerger.

NALCO shares declined over 3% while Hindalco Industries fell around 2%. These stocks have fallen up to 16% in the past one month as aluminium prices continue to slide.
