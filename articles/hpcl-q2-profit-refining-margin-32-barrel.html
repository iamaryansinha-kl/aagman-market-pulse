HPCL Swings to Rs 3,625 Crore Profit, Refining Margin Hits $32

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- HPCL posted consolidated net profit of Rs 3,625 crore in Q2 FY27, recovering from a Rs 12,265 crore net loss in the preceding June quarter.
  • •- Profit was still down 6.1% year-on-year from Rs 3,859 crore, while revenue rose 26.1% to Rs 1,39,140 crore.
  • •- Gross refining margin before export cess surged to $32.15 per barrel from $8.80 a year earlier.
  • •- Refineries ran at 100% of combined capacity, processing 6.20 million tonnes of crude in the quarter.
  • •- HPCL refinanced or repriced Rs 51,970 crore of debt at lower cost and commissioned India's first refinery blue hydrogen unit at Visakh.

Hindustan Petroleum Corporation delivered one of the most dramatic quarterly swings in the Indian energy sector this earnings season. After posting a consolidated net loss of Rs 12,264.67 crore in the June quarter — a period when oil marketers were squeezed by inventory losses and weak marketing margins — the state-run refiner reported a consolidated net profit of Rs 3,625.22 crore for the September quarter of FY27. That is a sequential turnaround of roughly Rs 15,890 crore in three months. The year-on-year comparison is more modest: profit was down 6.1% from the Rs 3,859 crore earned in the same quarter last year.

Revenue from operations rose 26.1% year-on-year to Rs 1,39,140 crore from Rs 1,10,323 crore, reflecting both higher volumes and a richer realisation environment. For the first half of FY27, revenue climbed to Rs 2,84,267 crore from Rs 2,30,458 crore in the corresponding period a year earlier.

The engine of the recovery was the refinery gate. HPCL's gross refining margin before export cess surged to $32.15 per barrel in Q2 FY27, compared with $8.80 per barrel in the year-ago quarter — nearly a fourfold expansion that reflects how violently the global cracks swung in the company's favour during the period. For the first half, the margin stood at $27.89 per barrel against $5.95 a year earlier. These are historically extraordinary numbers for an Indian refiner, and they did the heavy lifting in flipping the quarterly result from loss to profit.

Operations ran flat out. HPCL's refineries processed 6.20 million metric tonnes of crude during the quarter, operating at 100% of combined capacity. The Visakh Refinery handled 3.60 MMT at 95% utilisation, while the Mumbai Refinery processed 2.60 MMT — running at 109% of its nameplate capacity. Throughput for the first half reached 12.72 MMT, equivalent to 104% utilisation.

Marketing volumes kept pace. Total sales including exports rose 5.5% year-on-year to 12.73 MMT in the quarter, with domestic sales up 4.9%. Combined petrol and diesel sales grew 10% to 7.78 MMT, while LPG sales stood at 2.03 MMT. Pipeline throughput was 6.99 MMT.

Beyond the quarter's numbers, HPCL spent the period fixing its balance sheet and extending its infrastructure. The company refinanced or repriced debt arrangements worth Rs 51,970 crore with lenders at lower cost, and its HPCL Rajasthan Refinery subsidiary received a credit-rating upgrade to AA+ from CRISIL with a stable outlook. Capital expenditure for the quarter came in at Rs 2,085 crore, taking the half-year total to Rs 3,819 crore. The 201-km Haldia-to-Panagarh LPG pipeline in West Bengal was mechanically completed and is in pre-commissioning, while an open-access fuel farm at Bhogapuram Airport was commissioned and refuelling operations commenced.

The retail network and energy-transition build-out continued to widen. As of September 30, HPCL operated 25,245 retail outlets and 6,394 LPG distributorships, alongside 2,296 CNG outlets and 5,880 EV charging stations. Some 24,172 outlets — 96% of the network — have been solarised. The company commissioned India's first refinery blue hydrogen unit at the Visakh Refinery during the quarter, based on its patented Higas technology and HP Novel Amine, launched a 10-kg composite LPG cylinder under the HP Navya brand, and entered the quick-commerce segment through a partnership with Swiggy Instamart. Its lubricants business expanded into Turkey and Sudan, taking its international footprint to 35 countries.

Under the operating surface, two quieter programmes are worth noting. The company's EBITDA improvement programme, Project Samriddhi 2.0, generated accruals of Rs 327 crore during the first half — equivalent to $0.18 per barrel — which is the difference between a good quarter and a great one in a business where margins are measured to the cent. And its HP Green R&D Centre had filed a cumulative 800 patents as of September 30, of which 339 have been granted. On the green energy front, HP Renewable & Green Energy secured consent to establish nine compressed biogas projects and won awards for four municipal solid waste-based projects in Punjab and Bihar, while a 70 MWp solar project at Gaaliveedu received Central Electricity Authority approval. None of these will move next quarter's profit; collectively they sketch the portfolio a state-run refiner is trying to own for the decade after this one.

One cautionary signal sits under all of this: the stock. HPCL shares ended Thursday at Rs 340.80 on the NSE, down 1.87% on the day, and are down 31.65% year-to-date, leaving a market capitalisation of about Rs 60,803 crore. The market is pricing the refining windfall as exactly that — a windfall, not a run-rate. Refining margins at $32 a barrel are the kind of number that arrives with a geopolitical premium and leaves with it, and investors have learned across cycles to capitalise a state-run marketer's normalised earnings rather than its cyclical peak. The debt repricing, the HRRL upgrade, the throughput records and the hydrogen commissioning all speak to a company running its operations well; the share price speaks to a market that does not believe the margin environment behind this quarter is here to stay.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT DROVE HPCL'S SWING BACK TO PROFIT?
A surge in gross refining margins to $32.15 per barrel from $8.80 a year earlier, combined with 100% refinery utilisation and a 26% rise in revenue, flipped the company from a Rs 12,265 crore June-quarter loss to a Rs 3,625 crore profit.
HOW DID HPCL'S REVENUE AND VOLUMES PERFORM?
Revenue rose 26.1% year-on-year to Rs 1,39,140 crore. Total sales volume including exports grew 5.5% to 12.73 MMT, with combined petrol and diesel sales up 10%.
WHAT BALANCE-SHEET STEPS DID HPCL TAKE?
The company refinanced or repriced Rs 51,970 crore of debt at lower cost, and its Rajasthan refinery subsidiary was upgraded to AA+ by CRISIL with a stable outlook.
WHAT NEW PROJECTS DID HPCL COMMISSION?
India's first refinery blue hydrogen unit at Visakh, an open-access fuel farm at Bhogapuram Airport, and the mechanical completion of the 201-km Haldia-Panagarh LPG pipeline. GLOSSARY: - Gross refining margin: The difference between the value of refined products a refinery produces and the cost of the crude it processes, measured per barrel. - Throughput: The amount of crude oil processed by a refinery in a given period. - Blue hydrogen: Hydrogen produced from natural gas with carbon capture, as distinct from green hydrogen made with renewable power.
