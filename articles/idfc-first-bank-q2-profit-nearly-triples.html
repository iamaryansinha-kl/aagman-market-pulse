IDFC First Bank Q2 Profit Nearly Triples to Rs 1,045 Crore

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Net profit nearly tripled year-on-year to Rs 1,045 crore in Q2 FY27, from Rs 352 crore in the same quarter last year.
  • •- Net interest income rose 21% to Rs 6,184 crore from Rs 5,113 crore, continuing the margin-led earnings run that produced record profits in the June quarter.
  • •- The print is the third straight quarter of roughly Rs 1,000-crore-plus profitability for the bank built by MD V Vaidyanathan from the ruins of a merged infra lender.
  • •- The result lands in a brutal tape for bank stocks — ten weeks of FII selling and a hawkish RBI — making operating performance the only metric that matters.

There is a particular kind of banking story that only India produces: a lender assembled from the wreckage of infrastructure finance, run by a founder-manager who compiles profits quarter after quarter until the market finally has to pay attention. IDFC First Bank added another chapter on Sunday, reporting a second-quarter net profit of Rs 1,045 crore — nearly three times the Rs 352 crore it earned in the same quarter a year ago — on the back of a 21% rise in net interest income to Rs 6,184 crore. Coming after a record Rs 1,075 crore in the June quarter, the September print confirms that the first half of FY27 is not a flash. This is what the model looks like when it works.

The arithmetic of the turnaround is worth restating, because it is easy to forget how improbable it once seemed. IDFC Bank merged with Capital First — Vaidyanathan's retail-lending venture — in 2018, inheriting a wholesale-heavy loan book stuffed with slow-moving infrastructure credit and a deposit franchise that barely existed. The bet was that a high-yielding retail book, funded by a CASA deposit base built from nothing, could eventually out-earn the legacy wholesale drag. For years the bet consumed capital faster than it produced profits. The last four quarters have flipped the ledger: retail and MSME lending now drives the book, funding costs have fallen as deposits compound, and the legacy wholesale portfolio is a shrinking share of a much larger balance sheet. A near-tripling of profit is the cleanest single-quarter proof that operating leverage has arrived.

Margins are doing the heavy lifting. The 21% NII growth — Rs 6,184 crore against Rs 5,113 crore a year earlier — outpaces loan-book growth, which means net interest margin is holding at the elevated levels the bank has guided toward through the year. In a quarter when the RBI's calibrated tightening raised funding costs across the system, holding margin is itself an achievement; growing NII at a fifth in that environment is better. Fee income and treasury will fill out the rest of the P&L when the full numbers are tabled, but the core engine — borrow cheap, lend well, lose little — is clearly running. The sequential comparison flatters the story in a different way: matching within 3% of a record June quarter, seasonally the strongest for loan growth, implies the September book kept compounding rather than pausing. Banks that decelerate after a record print usually blame the base; IDFC First appears simply to have continued.

Asset quality will draw the usual scrutiny, and the bank enters the quarter with the wind at its back: gross NPA had fallen to 1.51% by June from 1.97% a year earlier, net NPA to 0.44%, with provision coverage deep enough to absorb the MFI-portfolio claims that occasionally flare in micro-lending books. The September-quarter question is whether that trajectory held while growth continued; the profit trajectory suggests it did, because deteriorating asset quality and near-tripled profit do not coexist. The microfinance segment, where the bank carries meaningful exposure and where a Rs 515-crore CGFMU claim was booked prudentially in the June quarter, remains the one pocket investors will probe on the analyst call. But the direction of travel on credit cost has been the bank's friend for eight consecutive quarters, and nothing in a 21% NII print with stable margins suggests that changed.

Context matters for how this read lands. The market in which IDFC First reported is, by any measure, hostile: ten consecutive weeks of foreign selling, crude near six-month highs, bond yields at multi-decade stress levels, and valuations for mid-tier lenders compressed to levels that assume the worst. In that tape, a 190%-plus profit print with 21% NII growth is not just a company event — it is a test of whether fundamentals can reassert themselves over macro fear. IDFC First has spent six years building the deposits, the retail book and the governance record to pass exactly that test. The second half of FY27 will show whether the market awards it for doing so; the bank, at least, has done its part.

The competitive read is also favourable. This results season has now delivered strong prints from Shriram Finance, AU Small Finance Bank and Kotak Mahindra Bank alongside IDFC First — a sweep that says the lenders with genuine retail deposit franchises and disciplined underwriting are compounding straight through the stress, while the market prices them as if the stress is company-specific. Somewhere between those two views lies the trade, and earnings are on the side of the lenders. The deposit franchise is the quiet hero of the story: CASA has compounded at a pace most mid-tier banks would envy, and in a system where the RBI's tightening makes every incremental rupee of funding dearer, a sticky low-cost base is worth more each quarter.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT WERE IDFC FIRST BANK'S Q2 RESULTS?
Net profit nearly tripled YoY to Rs 1,045 crore from Rs 352 crore; net interest income rose 21% to Rs 6,184 crore from Rs 5,113 crore.
HOW DOES THIS COMPARE TO RECENT QUARTERS?
It follows a record Rs 1,075 crore profit in Q1 FY27, making H1 FY27 roughly Rs 2,120 crore of profit — the strongest half-year in the bank's history.
WHAT DROVE THE PROFIT GROWTH?
Margin-led NII growth of 21%, a compounding retail and MSME loan book, falling funding costs as the deposit franchise scales, and a shrinking legacy wholesale portfolio. GLOSSARY: NET INTEREST INCOME — The difference between what a bank earns on loans and pays on deposits; the core revenue line for any lender. OPERATING LEVERAGE — The point at which a bank's fixed costs stop growing as fast as revenue, so incremental profit flows disproportionately to the bottom line. CASA DEPOSITS — Current and savings account balances, a bank's cheapest funding source; building CASA from scratch was IDFC First's foundational challenge. INFRASTRUCTURE LENDING LEGACY — The wholesale project-finance book inherited from the original IDFC Bank, historically the drag on the merged lender's returns.
