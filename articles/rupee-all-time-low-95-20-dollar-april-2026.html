Rupee Hits Record Low of 95.20 Against Dollar on Oil, Geopolitical Fears

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/The rupee depreciated 32 paise to an all-time low of 95.20 against the US dollar in early trade on April 30, 2026
  • 02/Brent crude near $122 per barrel and escalating West Asia conflict are fuelling import cost fears and investor anxiety
  • 03/Forex traders warn of further downside as RBI faces limited policy room amid a hawkish US Fed holding rates at 3.5-3.75%

The rupee opened at 95.01 against the US dollar on Thursday. It quickly slid to a record low of 95.20, down 32 paise from its previous close of 94.88.

This marks the weakest level the Indian currency has ever traded against the greenback. The breach comes amid a sustained depreciation trend.

The rupee has fallen roughly 12% over the past year. It stood at approximately 85.03 against the dollar in April 2025.

Elevated Brent crude oil prices near $122 per barrel are a key driver. India imports over 85% of its oil needs, making the bill significantly costlier.

Concerns over a wider conflict in West Asia are amplifying the pressure. The Strait of Hormuz blockade and OPEC uncertainty are keeping energy markets volatile.

Amit Pabari, MD at CR Forex Advisors, said the rupee is reacting to three forces: oil, capital flows, and central bank policies. The currency is no longer trading in isolation.

The US Federal Reserve held interest rates unchanged at 3.5-3.75%. A hawkish Fed stance limits the Reserve Bank of India's room to intervene or cut rates.

Madhavi Arora, Chief Economist at Emkay Global, noted the RBI remains constrained. FPI flows are expected to stay weak as long as Middle East tensions persist.

Soumya Ghosh, Group Chief Economic Advisor at SBI, warned that without BoP support measures, inflationary expectations could become unanchored. The current depreciation is not in line with India's macro fundamentals.

The benchmark 10-year government bond yield hardened past 7% to 7.6%. Rising crude and currency weakness are feeding into imported inflation across the economy.
