ICICI Bank Q2 Profit Rises 20% to Rs 14,843 Crore, NII Up 18%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Net profit rose 20.1% YoY to Rs 14,843 crore from Rs 12,359 crore.
  • •- Net interest income grew 17.7% to Rs 25,339 crore; NIM came in at 4.18%.
  • •- Gross NPAs fell to 1.20% from 1.58% a year earlier; net NPAs eased to 0.30%.
  • •- Loans grew 30% YoY to Rs 18.31 lakh crore; deposits rose 27.1%.
  • •- Provisions declined to Rs 851 crore, with Rs 13,100 crore of contingency buffers held.
  • •- CET-1 capital ratio stood at 16.01%, far above regulatory minimums.

ICICI Bank has produced the cleanest large-bank quarter of the season so far. The private lender on Saturday reported net profit of Rs 14,843 crore for Q2FY27, up 20.1 per cent year-on-year from Rs 12,359 crore, powered by 17.7 per cent net interest income growth to Rs 25,339 crore and an asset-quality trajectory that most banks can only describe in presentations. Loans grew 30 per cent year-on-year to Rs 18.31 lakh crore. Profitability, growth and credit quality all moved in the right direction simultaneously — a combination that has become ICICI's signature rather than its surprise.

The margin story holds the quarter's main tension. Net interest margin came in at 4.18 per cent — comfortably the best among India's large banks, but down from 4.36 per cent in the June quarter and 4.30 per cent a year earlier. The compression is the cost of the growth strategy: lending at 30 per cent in a rising-rate environment means paying up for deposits, and ICICI's deposit growth of 27.1 per cent year-on-year (to Rs 20.49 lakh crore) was itself flattered by the FCNR window — excluding those flows, deposit growth was a still-healthy 17 per cent. The bank is deliberately trading a few basis points of margin for balance-sheet share, and the market has historically rewarded that trade from this management.

Asset quality did the quiet, decisive work. Gross NPAs fell to 1.20 per cent of advances from 1.38 per cent in June and 1.58 per cent a year ago — an 18-basis-point sequential improvement that is extraordinary at this scale — while net NPAs eased to 0.30 per cent. Fresh slippage stayed controlled at Rs 4,833 crore of gross additions, below the year-ago quarter's Rs 5,034 crore, and actual provisions declined to Rs 851 crore from Rs 914 crore. Behind those numbers sits a fortress of caution: Rs 13,100 crore of contingency provisions plus Rs 1,283 crore of additional standard-asset provisioning for the agricultural priority-sector book that the RBI had directed in Q3FY26. ICICI could absorb a meaningful credit cycle without touching the P&L's composure.

Capital is not a constraint on anything the bank wants to do. The total capital adequacy ratio stood at 16.68 per cent at September-end, with Common Equity Tier 1 at 16.01 per cent against regulatory minimums of 11.70 per cent and 8.20 per cent respectively. That headroom matters in the current environment: with the RBI tilting toward tighter policy and the bond market repricing bank treasury books, a 16 per cent CET-1 is the difference between strategy and survival. ICICI also used the quarter to deepen its insurance ownership, acquiring an additional 2 per cent of ICICI Life Insurance for Rs 1,470 crore — a signal that surplus capital is being pointed at the consolidated franchise rather than idling.

On a consolidated basis, the group's net income rose to Rs 16,333 crore from Rs 13,357 crore, and total assets expanded 22.6 per cent to Rs 32.93 lakh crore. The subsidiaries — life and general insurance, asset management, securities — continue to convert the bank's distribution into fee income, which is the structural answer to the margin question: if NIMs compress toward 4 per cent over time, the franchise's economics increasingly ride on the non-lending businesses that the deposit base feeds.

Set against the day's other prints, ICICI's quarter defines the top of the private-bank league table. Axis Bank reported a 38 per cent profit jump on 23 per cent loan growth an hour earlier — faster profit growth off a smaller base, with NIMs of 3.36 per cent. HDFC Bank, reporting alongside, grew profit 4.6 per cent on margins of 3.16 per cent as it continues digesting the mortgage merger. ICICI's 20 per cent profit growth on a 4.18 per cent NIM and a 1.20 per cent GNPA is the middle path that the market tends to reward most durably: not the fastest grower, not the biggest balance sheet, but the best risk-adjusted machine. The 30 per cent loan growth number, if sustained through December, will invite the RBI's macroprudential attention eventually — but that is a high-class problem, and one the bank has flagged it would welcome managing.

There is also a macro dimension to the print that sits awkwardly with the week's other big story. ICICI's loan book growing 30 per cent and deposits 27 per cent describes a banking system still pumping credit at full throttle into an economy facing an oil spike, a nine-week bond slide and an external deficit being defended with reserves. That is not a contradiction so much as a division of labour: the RBI handles the external war while private balance sheets keep the domestic engine funded. The risk case writes itself — if $110 oil persists and the December hike lands, credit costs eventually follow funding costs — but ICICI's Rs 13,100 crore contingency buffer is precisely the kind of provisioning that says the bank has already priced that scenario into its arithmetic. The quarter's real message is confidence, in the oldest banking sense: lend aggressively when your balance sheet can afford the worst day.

GLOSSARY: - Net interest margin (NIM): Interest earned minus interest paid, as a share of interest-earning assets — the core profitability gauge for a lender. - Contingency provisions: Funds set aside against potential future losses beyond identified NPAs — rainy-day capital on the liability side. - CET-1: Common Equity Tier 1 — the highest-quality regulatory capital, mostly equity and reserves. - FCNR(B) deposits: Dollar-denominated deposits raised from non-resident Indians under a special RBI window.
