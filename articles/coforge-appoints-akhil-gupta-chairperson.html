Coforge Appoints Ex-Bharti Vice-Chairman Akhil Gupta as Chairperson

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Coforge board approves Akhil Kumar Gupta as chairperson on September 28, 2026; he also joins as non-executive independent director for five years from September 29
  • 02/Appointment ends a boardroom crisis that began with OP Bhatt's resignation and DK Singh's exit two days later, after a KPMG audit flagged evaluation irregularities
  • 03/Shares rose about 2% on the news; watch the FY27 board evaluation cycle and Advent International's 21.18% stake for signs the governance reset is holding

Coforge Ltd (NSE: COFORGE) has appointed Akhil Kumar Gupta as its chairperson. The board approved the appointment on September 28, 2026, the company said in a press release.

Gupta also joins the board as a non-executive independent director. His five-year term starts September 29, 2026.

He is the former vice-chairman of Bharti Enterprises, the holding company of the Bharti Airtel group. Gupta spent more than 16 years at Bharti Enterprises.

Coforge shares rose about 2% on the news. The stock had fallen nearly 9% earlier in September, when the boardroom crisis became public.

The appointment closes a turbulent chapter for the Noida-based IT services firm. Two directors, including the previous chairperson, quit within four days in September.

Between Bhatt's resignation and Gupta's appointment, Coforge made about half a dozen filings to the stock exchanges. Most were clarifications on the circumstances of the two exits.

Gupta is a chartered accountant with four decades of experience. He currently chairs ONE WAM Ltd and Bharti Life Insurance Ltd.

He also serves on the boards of Zepto, Snapdeal, Eutelsat Communications and Lodha Developers. That mix spans insurance, e-commerce, satellite communications and real estate.

The selection was run with executive search firm Egon Zehnder. The board picked Gupta unanimously from a list of 60 candidates.

"We ran a very rigorous and intense global search process in partnership with Egon Zehnder, with a focus on finding a candidate with the highest governance experience, professional expertise, and strategic perspectives" - Vivek Sharma, interim chairperson, Coforge (company press release, September 28).
"Mr. Gupta was instrumental in Bharti Enterprises successfully navigating multiple business shifts during his watch and association with Bharti Enterprises" - Sudhir Singh, chief executive, Coforge (company press release, September 28).

The board now has eight members, of whom four are independent directors. The independents are Beth Boucher, Vivek Sharma, Anil Chanana and Gupta.

Gupta succeeds veteran banker OP Bhatt, the former chairman of State Bank of India. Bhatt resigned in September after 27 months as chairman, and his exit letter cited disagreements with board members over his "good faith actions."

Two days later, DK Singh, who headed the nomination and remuneration committee, also quit. Singh cited "differences and tension" between executive and independent directors, and a lack of independence to operate.

The trigger was an internal audit by KPMG, the company's internal auditor. KPMG found irregularities in the board evaluation exercise for the year ended March 2026.

On August 21, KPMG shared initial findings with the board. Its review said Bhatt received the lowest rating among all board members, and that the findings were withheld from the rest of the board.

In stock exchange filings on September 10 and 11, Coforge said the evaluation report was suppressed on Bhatt's direction. The company said both Bhatt and Singh accepted second board terms despite citing internal differences.

Private equity firm Advent International is Coforge's largest shareholder with a 21.18% stake. It has three nominees on the board, who joined in April 2026 and were not part of the FY26 evaluation.

Advent voted against Bhatt's re-appointment at the shareholder meeting. The resolution was approved by only 65% of shareholders, falling short of the 75% required for a special resolution.

Less than a month before that vote, the nomination and remuneration committee had cleared Bhatt's re-appointment. That panel included an Advent nominee, making the shareholder rebuke more pointed.

Advent also had reservations about Bhatt continuing as chairman until 2032, when he would have turned 82, according to a Mint report. Bhatt was seeking a second term when the audit findings surfaced.

Coforge is India's seventh-largest technology services company by revenue. It ended FY26 with revenue of $1.87 billion, up 29.2% year on year.

The company, formerly known as NIIT Technologies, is one of the fastest-growing mid-tier Indian IT firms. Its key verticals include travel, banking and financial services, insurance, and manufacturing.

It competes with Persistent Systems, Mphasis and LTIMindtree in the mid-tier IT services space. It is also integrating Encora, the US-based engineering services firm it agreed to acquire earlier this year.

Its market capitalisation stands at roughly ₹78,500 crore. The stock traded at ₹1,773 on September 25, about 11% below its 52-week high of ₹1,988.90 hit on December 8, 2025.

Even after the September slide, the shares have nearly doubled from their 52-week low of ₹1,008.50, recorded on March 17, 2026.

For investors, the appointment signals a governance reset. An independent chair with deep audit and boardroom experience addresses the exact weakness the KPMG review exposed.

Indian listing rules treat separation of the chairperson and CEO roles as a key safeguard. Coforge already follows that structure, with Sudhir Singh as chief executive.

The change does not fix the business challenges. Coforge's growth depends on discretionary technology spending in the US and Europe, and the industry faces pricing pressure as AI reshapes traditional services work.

The next markers are the annual shareholder meeting and the FY27 board evaluation. A clean evaluation cycle, stable filings and no further exits would confirm the board has moved past the crisis.

Gupta's arrival also aligns with Coforge's AI-led pivot under CEO Sudhir Singh. At Bharti, he guided the group through multiple technology and business shifts, experience the board clearly wants on its side.
