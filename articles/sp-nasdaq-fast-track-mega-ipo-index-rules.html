Index providers are rewriting the rulebook for benchmark inclusion. S&P Dow Jones Indices and Nasdaq are both proposing changes that would allow newly public mega-cap companies to enter flagship indices far sooner than current rules allow.

The changes come as a wave of high-profile IPOs looms. SpaceX, OpenAI, and Anthropic are all preparing to go public, potentially at valuations exceeding $1 trillion. Current index rules would delay their inclusion for months or exclude them entirely.

S&P Dow Jones Indices announced a formal review on April 30. The proposals would cut the minimum listing period from 12 months to 6 months for megacap companies. The profitability requirement could be waived entirely for the largest entrants.

Nasdaq already moved faster. In March, it approved a "fast entry" rule allowing certain large IPOs to join the Nasdaq-100 within approximately 15 trading days. The index can temporarily exceed 100 constituents to accommodate these additions.

The traditional rules were designed for a different era. Companies historically went public earlier in their lifecycle at smaller valuations. Today's mega-IPOs arrive with massive market caps and immediate liquidity but may lack sustained profitability.

SpaceX illustrates the scale involved. Reports suggest a potential $2 trillion valuation at IPO, which would place it among the top 10 most valuable US companies immediately. OpenAI's most recent funding round valued it at $852 billion.

Passive capital tracking these indices is enormous. Over $27 trillion is benchmarked to S&P Dow Jones Indices globally. The Nasdaq-100 alone is tracked by more than 200 investment products with over $600 billion in assets under management.

When a company enters an index, passive funds must buy automatically. This creates mechanical demand that can drive up the stock price, especially when free float is limited. Fast-track inclusion compresses this price discovery into a much shorter window.

The profitability waiver represents a philosophical shift. The S&P 500 has historically required GAAP profitability over 12 months and in the most recent quarter. Removing this for megacaps changes what the index represents as a quality filter.

FTSE Russell is also exploring similar changes. The competitive pressure among index providers means no single provider wants to lose relevance by excluding the market's most talked-about companies.

S&P's market consultation remains open until May 28. If adopted, changes could be implemented as early as June 8. Nasdaq's fast entry rule took effect May 1.
