Crude Oil Prices Fall 2%, Head for Weekly Losses as Hormuz Fears Ease

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Brent crude falls below $75 to $74.33, WTI drops over 2% to $70.46 as Hormuz supply fears ease
  • 02/Both benchmarks heading for weekly losses of around 7% as tanker traffic through Hormuz recovers
  • 03/Indian crude basket averaged $86.31 in June vs $114.48 in April, easing inflation concerns

Brent crude futures fell more than 1% to $74.33 a barrel on Friday. US West Texas Intermediate crude dropped over 2% to $70.46 a barrel.

The decline came despite renewed Middle East tensions. A cargo vessel was struck near Oman on Thursday, yet oil markets focused on improving supply.

Two US officials said Iran fired at the vessel as it passed through the Strait of Hormuz. Iranian authorities later said they could not guarantee safety for ships outside designated routes.

Crude shipments through the Strait of Hormuz climbed to their highest level since the February conflict began. However, vessel movement remains below the pre-conflict average of about 125 ships a day.

Both Brent and WTI are on track to post weekly losses of around 7%. The decline follows a ceasefire between Iran and Israel that eased fears of prolonged disruption.

For India, softer crude prices offer relief after the recent energy cost spike. The Indian basket averaged $86.31 per barrel in June compared to $114.48 in April.

Brent had briefly climbed to nearly $120 at the height of the conflict. The retreat to $74 eases concerns over inflation and fuel import costs for India.
