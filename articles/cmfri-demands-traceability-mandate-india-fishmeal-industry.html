CMFRI Demands Traceability Mandate in India's $10.6B Fishmeal Industry

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/CMFRI-led stakeholder consultation demands mandatory end-to-end traceability across India's fishmeal supply chain to curb IUU fishing
  • 02/Global fishmeal market valued at $10.64 billion in 2025; Asia-Pacific holds 41.15% share with India as key producer
  • 03/First-of-its-kind pilot study on mesopelagic resources for fishmeal presented; CMFRI pledges stock assessments for sustainable expansion

ICAR-CMFRI held a stakeholder consultation on April 29, 2026, calling for mandatory end-to-end traceability in India's fishmeal and fish oil sector. Marine scientists, industry representatives and policymakers attended the meeting.

The demand aims to curb Illegal, Unreported and Unregulated (IUU) fishing. Participants stressed that traceability from harvest to processing to consumers is critical for sustainability and regulatory compliance.

CMFRI Director Grinson George said the institute will undertake fish stock assessments. Any expansion of the fishmeal sector must be grounded in scientific evidence, he added.

ICAR-Central Institute of Fisheries Technology Director George Ninan said sustainable practices are needed to conserve resources. Fishmeal must fetch better prices in domestic and international markets through proper certification.

A collaborative pilot study led by CMFRI was presented at the meeting. It indicated that mesopelagic resources — deep-sea species in midwater zones — could be economically viable for fishmeal production.

Deputy Commissioner of Fisheries Sanjay Pandey urged stakeholders to implement the National Traceability Framework. Coordinated efforts among research institutions and industry players are essential.

"The industry must come forward to adopt transparent practices, while stronger industry–research linkages are crucial for developing reliable systems and technologies," said Shubdeep Ghosh, Assistant Director of ICAR.

The global fishmeal market was valued at $10.64 billion in 2025. Asia-Pacific dominates with over 41% share, driven by aquaculture expansion in China, India and Vietnam.

India's seafood exports hit a record 1.78 million tonnes worth $7.38 billion in 2023-24. Post-harvest losses remain high at about 20%, highlighting infrastructure gaps.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT DID CMFRI DEMAND AT THE STAKEHOLDER CONSULTATION ON FISHMEAL?
CMFRI DEMANDED THAT TRACEABILITY BE MADE MANDATORY ACROSS THE FISHMEAL AND FISH OIL SECTOR TO CURB ILLEGAL, UNREPORTED AND UNREGULATED (IUU) FISHING. EXPERTS CALLED FOR END-TO-END TRANSPARENCY FROM HARVEST TO PROCESSING TO CONSUMERS.
WHAT IS THE GLOBAL FISHMEAL MARKET SIZE?
THE GLOBAL FISHMEAL MARKET WAS VALUED AT USD 10.64 BILLION IN 2025 AND IS PROJECTED TO REACH USD 19.70 BILLION BY 2034, GROWING AT A CAGR OF 7.16%. ASIA-PACIFIC HOLDS THE LARGEST SHARE AT 41.15%.
WHAT IS THE MESOPELAGIC RESOURCES PILOT STUDY?
CMFRI PRESENTED A COLLABORATIVE PILOT STUDY, THE FIRST OF ITS KIND IN INDIA, EXAMINING WHETHER MESOPELAGIC RESOURCES — DEEP-SEA SPECIES FOUND IN MIDWATER ZONES — COULD BE ECONOMICALLY VIABLE FOR FISHMEAL PRODUCTION.
WHO ATTENDED THE CMFRI STAKEHOLDER CONSULTATION?
THE MEETING WAS ATTENDED BY MARINE SCIENTISTS, INDUSTRY REPRESENTATIVES AND POLICYMAKERS. NOTABLE PARTICIPANTS INCLUDED CMFRI DIRECTOR GRINSON GEORGE, ICAR-CIFT DIRECTOR GEORGE NINAN, DEPUTY COMMISSIONER SANJAY PANDEY AND ICAR ASSISTANT DIRECTOR SHUBDEEP GHOSH.
WHAT IS THE NATIONAL TRACEABILITY FRAMEWORK?
THE NATIONAL TRACEABILITY FRAMEWORK IS A REGULATORY MECHANISM URGED BY DEPUTY COMMISSIONER OF FISHERIES SANJAY PANDEY FOR IMPLEMENTATION IN THE FISHMEAL SECTOR. IT REQUIRES COORDINATED EFFORTS AMONG RESEARCH INSTITUTIONS AND INDUSTRY PLAYERS TO ENSURE ACCOUNTABILITY.
