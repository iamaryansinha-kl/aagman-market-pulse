Engineering Exports Rise 21% YoY in June, Showing Sector Resilience

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

Engineering exports are defying the global slowdown.

India's engineering exports rose 21% year-on-year in June, a strong performance that reflects the sector's resilience despite global headwinds, trade policy uncertainty, and the broader deceleration in merchandise trade. The numbers, reported by the Engineering Export Promotion Council, suggest that demand for industrial machinery, auto components, electrical equipment, and steel products from key markets in the United States, Europe, and the Middle East remains robust.

The 21% growth is impressive in context. India's overall merchandise exports have been sluggish in recent months, dragged down by weak demand for textiles, gems and jewellery, and agricultural products. Engineering exports, which account for nearly 25% of total merchandise exports, have been a bright spot, consistently growing at double-digit rates even as other sectors falter. The June numbers extend this trend and suggest that the sector has found a sustainable growth path.

Industrial machinery is the largest component. India's machinery exports — including machine tools, construction equipment, and process plant equipment — have benefited from the global infrastructure push, particularly in the Middle East and Africa. Indian manufacturers have become competitive in the mid-market segment, offering products that are cheaper than European or Japanese alternatives but higher quality than Chinese options. This positioning has created a sweet spot that is driving consistent demand.

Auto components are benefiting from the global supply chain reshuffle. The diversification of automotive supply chains away from China has created opportunities for Indian component manufacturers, particularly in precision parts, castings, forgings, and electronics. Companies like Bharat Forge, Motherson Sumi, and Sundram Fasteners have been expanding their export footprints, winning contracts from global OEMs that are seeking to de-risk their supply chains.

Electrical equipment and steel products are also performing well. India's electrical equipment exports, including transformers, switchgear, and cables, have grown as developing countries invest in grid modernisation and renewable energy infrastructure. Steel product exports, including pipes, fittings, and structural steel, have benefited from infrastructure investment in the Middle East and Southeast Asia, though the sector faces headwinds from anti-dumping duties in some markets.

The US and Europe remain the largest markets. Engineering exports to the United States have been resilient despite the trade tensions and tariff uncertainty, driven by demand for industrial machinery and auto components. Europe, particularly Germany and the UK, has been a strong market for precision engineering products and automotive components. The Middle East is a growing market, particularly for construction machinery and infrastructure-related equipment.

The sustainability of the growth depends on global demand. The 21% year-on-year growth partly reflects a favourable base effect from June 2025, when exports were weak due to global supply chain disruptions. If global demand for industrial goods continues to soften, the growth rate could moderate in the coming months. However, the structural trend of supply chain diversification and India's improving manufacturing competitiveness suggests that engineering exports will remain a growth driver for the trade sector.
