Sula Vineyards Q4 FY26: Revenue Up 7% But PAT Drops 34% On Cost Surge

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Sula Vineyards Q4 revenue climbs 7.1% to ₹142.6 crore, but PAT sinks 34% to ₹8.6 crore as costs surge 57%
  • 02/FY26 full-year PAT drops 63% to ₹25.7 crore from ₹70.2 crore; stock down 37.6% over past year
  • 03/Wine tourism hits record ₹23.9 crore in Q4; company acquires Chandon estate to expand tourism footprint

Shares of Sula Vineyards Limited (NSE: SULA) were trading marginally lower at ₹172.69 on Thursday morning, down ₹0.98 or 0.56 per cent from the previous close of ₹173.67. Sell orders dominated early trade with 73.67 per cent of total order quantity against 26.33 per cent on the buy side, reflecting cautious market sentiment.

The stock has endured a punishing run over the past year. It trades 37.63 per cent below its level a year ago and is down nearly 21 per cent year-to-date, significantly underperforming the Nifty 50.

For Q4 FY26, revenue from operations rose 7.1 per cent year-on-year to ₹142.6 crore. Own brand revenue grew 5.2 per cent to ₹115.3 crore, while wine tourism revenue climbed 17.5 per cent to a record ₹23.9 crore for the quarter.

However, cost of goods sold surged 57 per cent to ₹34.1 crore from ₹21.7 crore in Q4 FY25. Gross profit fell 3.1 per cent to ₹100.8 crore and gross margins contracted sharply by 745 basis points to 70.7 per cent.

Operating EBITDA slipped 2.5 per cent to ₹27.8 crore with EBITDA margins narrowing 192 basis points to 19.5 per cent. Profit after tax for the quarter came in at ₹8.6 crore, down 34 per cent from ₹13 crore in Q4 FY25, with PAT margins at 6 per cent versus 9.7 per cent a year ago.

For the full year FY26, revenue declined 3.7 per cent to ₹596.2 crore from ₹619.4 crore in FY25. Full-year EBITDA fell 30.6 per cent to ₹103.5 crore with margins at 17.4 per cent versus 24.1 per cent in FY25. PAT stood at ₹25.7 crore, down sharply from ₹70.2 crore.

Wine tourism remained a bright spot with full-year revenue growing 20.7 per cent to ₹72.8 crore. Q4 visitor footfall crossed 1,12,300, up 12 per cent year-on-year, aided by strong festive and wedding demand and improved road connectivity from Mumbai.

The company has signed an agreement to acquire Chandon's 19-acre wine estate in Dindori, Nashik, to further expand its tourism footprint. The balance sheet shows a debt-to-EBITDA ratio of under 3x and a return on capital employed of 11 per cent for FY26.
