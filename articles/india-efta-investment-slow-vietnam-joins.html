India's $100 Billion EFTA Pact Stalls as Vietnam Joins the Club

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Vietnam signed a free trade agreement with EFTA — Switzerland, Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein — on October 18 in Hanoi.
  • •- India's TEPA with EFTA took effect October 1, 2025, and includes a pledge to raise investment into India by $100 billion over 15 years.
  • •- India's exports to EFTA fell nearly 11% in 2025-26 and over 9% in January-July 2026.
  • •- Cumulative Swiss FDI into India stood at $11.32 billion through March 2026 — rising only about $440 million after the pact took effect.
  • •- Experts are split on whether Vietnam's deal threatens Indian exporters, given EFTA already levies zero tariffs on most industrial goods.
  • •- The investment commitment has a review mechanism at 5, 10 and 15 years, with rebalance remedies if unmet.

Vietnam's signature has turned India's European Free Trade Association honeymoon into an audit. The four-nation bloc — Switzerland, Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein — signed a pact with Vietnam in Hanoi on October 18, witnessed at the top level by Swiss President Guy Parmelin and Vietnam's industry minister. That makes two Asian manufacturing economies with EFTA deals, and the comparison is uncomfortable for New Delhi: Vietnam runs one of the world's most aggressive export machines, and it has just acquired the same preferential access to a wealthy European market that India secured eighteen months ago and has so far failed to convert into growth.

The scorecard is the awkward part. India's Trade and Economic Partnership Agreement was signed on March 10, 2024, and entered into force on October 1, 2025 — offering EFTA market access on 92.2% of tariff lines covering 99.6% of India's exports, in return for India opening 82.7% of lines covering 95.3% of EFTA's exports. The asymmetry in those headline numbers was always the pact's selling point: India gave market access to a bloc of ten million consumers in exchange for preferential entry to four of the world's wealthiest economies, plus the investment pledge. The market access has not translated into shipments. India's exports to the bloc fell nearly 11% in 2025-26 and dropped over 9% between January and July 2026, according to development economist Biswajit Dhar, who was blunt about the implications: duty-free access for Vietnam poses a significant challenge to India's ambitions in EFTA markets, and Indian exporters have struggled to use the opportunities the agreement created. Exports to Switzerland, the largest EFTA partner, crossed $1.2 billion in 2025-26; engineering goods, one of the bright spots, were worth $315 million in 2024-25, up 18%.

The $100 billion question sits in Article 7.1(3). Under it, the EFTA states "shall aim to increase" foreign direct investment into India by $50 billion within ten years of the agreement taking effect, and another $50 billion in the five years after. The Press Information Bureau describes the commitment as binding, and the text builds in enforcement teeth: a sub-committee reviews progress at the five-, ten- and fifteen-year marks, and if the objectives are unmet at the final review India can seek consultations and ultimately take temporary measures to rebalance its tariff concessions. Whether that machinery ever bites is speculative; what is observable is the flow. DPIIT data show cumulative Swiss equity inflow at $11.32 billion from April 2000 through March 2026 — a rise of only about $440 million from the $10.88 billion recorded up to June 2025, a period spanning the agreement's entry into force.

Expert opinion splits on how much of this is Vietnam and how much is optics. Ajay Srivastava, founder of the Global Trade Research Initiative, argues the Vietnam deal changes nothing on tariffs because EFTA countries already levy zero duties on most industrial products — the preferential advantage of an FTA over the normal route is narrower than the headline suggests, and competition will turn on quality, price and delivery reliability regardless. He calls the $100 billion figure an aspirational target tied to India's growth story rather than a contractual obligation. Dhar's counter is that preferences are not automatic — importers must claim them and goods must meet rules of origin, and on the overlapping products where India and Vietnam genuinely compete, including telephones, electronics, textiles and clothing, Vietnamese exporters have spent a decade building the compliance infrastructure that Indian firms are only now assembling.

The Vietnam comparison also frames India's second European front. India's deal with the European Union was concluded on January 27, 2026, and on September 11 the European Commission sent proposals to the EU Council seeking authorisation to sign it — a process Vietnam completed years ago, its EU pact having been in force since August 2020. New Delhi's commerce ministry counts $33 billion of labour-intensive exports, from textiles to leather and footwear, facing EU duties of 4% to 26% that would move to zero at entry into force. The prize is larger than anything EFTA offers; the lesson of the EFTA experience is that market access on paper and market share on the ground are separated by export compliance, logistics and product competitiveness — the unglamorous machinery where Vietnam excels and India, so far, does not.

That machinery is where the next eighteen months will be decided. EFTA's own trade dashboard is explicit that preferences are not automatic: importers must claim them, goods must satisfy rules of origin, and utilisation is measured only where a genuine preferential margin exists — a compliance discipline Dhar says Indian exporters have struggled with since the pact took effect, and one Vietnamese exporters have practised daily since their EU deal entered into force in 2020. Hanoi joining the club does not cost India its European opportunity. It does put a stopwatch on it.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT DID VIETNAM SIGN ON OCTOBER 18?
A free trade agreement with EFTA — Switzerland, Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein — signed in Hanoi; it awaits entry into force.
WHAT DOES INDIA'S EFTA PACT COMMIT TO?
A target, described as binding, to raise EFTA investment into India by $50 billion in 10 years and another $50 billion in the following five, with reviews at 5, 10 and 15 years.
HOW ARE INDIA'S EXPORTS TO EFTA PERFORMING?
They fell nearly 11% in 2025-26 and over 9% in January-July 2026, even as EFTA opened 92.2% of its tariff lines to Indian goods.
DOES VIETNAM'S DEAL HURT INDIA?
Experts disagree — EFTA already charges zero tariffs on most industrial goods, so the marginal tariff advantage is limited; the real contest is on quality, price and delivery, where Vietnam is strong in overlapping categories like electronics and textiles. GLOSSARY: - EFTA: The European Free Trade Association — Switzerland, Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein — a non-EU European trading bloc. - Rules of origin: Criteria determining where a product was made, required to claim FTA tariff preferences. - Rebalance measures: Temporary trade remedies a party may apply if agreed objectives are not met. - DPIIT: India's Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade, which tracks FDI inflows.
