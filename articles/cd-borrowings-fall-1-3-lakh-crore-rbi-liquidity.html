CD Borrowings Fall ₹1.3 Lakh Crore as RBI Liquidity Surges

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Outstanding bank CDs fell ₹1.306 lakh crore, or 19%, to ₹5.60 lakh crore as of September 15 from about ₹6.90 lakh crore in mid-August.
  • 02/The drop was driven by the RBI's FCNR(B) deposit swap window, which pushed system liquidity to a peak of around ₹11.2 lakh crore in early September.
  • 03/Watch short-term rates: markets are pricing in a possible rate hike at the October RBI review, and the central bank has begun absorbing excess cash by selling government bonds.

Indian banks have cut their outstanding certificate-of-deposit borrowings by ₹1.3 lakh crore in a single month. The pull-back follows a surge in system liquidity created by the Reserve Bank of India's special FCNR(B) deposit swap window.

Outstanding CDs stood at ₹5.60 lakh crore as of September 15, according to RBI data. That compares with about ₹6.90 lakh crore in mid-August — a decline of ₹1.306 lakh crore, or 19%, in barely a month.

The pace of the rundown accelerated in the most recent fortnight. Outstanding CDs dropped by ₹76,400 crore in that period alone, as banks let maturing paper run off rather than roll it over.

The contraction marks a sharp slowdown in CD growth on a year-on-year basis. Growth in outstanding CDs cooled to 11.5% from 28.4% at the end of August — a drop of nearly 17 percentage points in a matter of weeks.

The trigger is the RBI's special foreign currency non-resident (bank), or FCNR(B), deposit swap window. The facility allowed banks to swap dollars raised overseas with the central bank for rupees, flooding the banking system with liquidity.

System liquidity peaked at around ₹11.2 lakh crore in early September. With cash that abundant, banks no longer needed to lean on comparatively expensive wholesale funding.

Instead of replacing maturing CDs, lenders simply let them expire. That mechanical run-off accounts for the bulk of the ₹1.3 lakh crore decline.

"With ample funds in hand, banks cut back on certificates of deposit issuance, and the call rate fell to 4.97%," said Vineet Jain, senior director for BFSI at Care Ratings. The call rate is the rate at which banks lend to each other overnight.

Jain cautioned that the funding advantage is unlikely to persist. "This cost advantage is unlikely to last," he said, noting that short-term rates have already begun to rise as the market prices in a possible rate hike at the October review.

The RBI has also started to absorb the extra cash by selling government bonds, Jain noted. That drains liquidity from the system and puts upward pressure on short-term rates.

The repricing is already visible in issuance behaviour. Fresh CD issuance recovered to ₹39,900 crore from ₹19,500 crore in the previous period, as some banks returned to the market.

The cost of new issuance, however, has fallen unevenly. The lower end of issuance rates dropped to 5.57% from 6.24%, while the upper end was broadly unchanged at 7.18%.

The result is a wider spread between issuers. Stronger banks are raising money near the bottom of that range, while weaker names continue to pay close to the top.

For banks that tapped the window, the benefit to funding costs is tangible. Prateek Ancha of Axis Securities said 12-month CD rates are now about 60 basis points lower than they were on June 1.

Lower CD rates reduce the incremental funding cost on certificates of deposit and non-retail term deposits, Ancha said. That should support bank profitability in the coming quarters.

Cheaper, more stable funding also improves banks' liquidity coverage ratios, Ancha noted. It increases the pool of deposits available for lending at a time when credit demand remains firm.

The shift is not uniform across the market. Prakash Agarwal, partner at Geofin Capital, said the move is playing out differently for borrowers — a sign that the transmission of cheaper funding to loan rates will be uneven.

For now, the direction of travel is clear. Banks used the RBI's dollar swap window to swap expensive wholesale money for central bank liquidity, and they are in no hurry to rebuild their CD books.

The key risk to that calculus is policy. If the RBI tightens at the October review, short-term rates will reprice higher and the window of cheap funding will start to close.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
HOW MUCH DID BANKS CUT THEIR CD BORROWINGS BY?
OUTSTANDING CDs FELL BY ₹1.306 LAKH CRORE, OR 19%, TO ₹5.60 LAKH CRORE AS OF SEPTEMBER 15 FROM ABOUT ₹6.90 LAKH CRORE IN MID-AUGUST. THE LATEST FORTNIGHT ALONE ACCOUNTED FOR A ₹76,400 CRORE DROP.
WHY DID BANKS CUT CD ISSUANCE?
THE RBI'S SPECIAL FCNR(B) DEPOSIT SWAP WINDOW LET BANKS SWAP DOLLARS RAISED OVERSEAS FOR RUPEES. SYSTEM LIQUIDITY PEAKED AT AROUND ₹11.2 LAKH CRORE IN EARLY SEPTEMBER, REMOVING THE NEED FOR EXPENSIVE WHOLESALE FUNDING.
HOW MUCH HAVE CD RATES FALLEN?
THE LOWER END OF FRESH CD ISSUANCE RATES FELL TO 5.57% FROM 6.24%, WHILE THE UPPER END WAS BROADLY UNCHANGED AT 7.18%. AXIS SECURITIES ESTIMATES 12-MONTH CD RATES ARE NOW ABOUT 60 BASIS POINTS BELOW THEIR JUNE 1 LEVELS.
WILL THE CHEAPER FUNDING LAST?
NOT NECESSARILY. VINEET JAIN OF CARE RATINGS SAYS THE COST ADVANTAGE IS UNLIKELY TO LAST, AS SHORT-TERM RATES ARE RISING ON RATE-HIKE EXPECTATIONS AT THE OCTOBER RBI REVIEW. THE RBI HAS ALSO BEGUN ABSORBING EXCESS CASH BY SELLING GOVERNMENT BONDS.
WHAT DOES THIS MEAN FOR BANK PROFITS?
LOWER CD RATES REDUCE INCREMENTAL FUNDING COSTS ON CDs AND NON-RETAIL TERM DEPOSITS. ACCORDING TO AXIS SECURITIES, THIS SHOULD SUPPORT BANK PROFITABILITY, IMPROVE LIQUIDITY COVERAGE RATIOS, AND EXPAND THE POOL OF DEPOSITS AVAILABLE FOR LENDING. GLOSSARY: CERTIFICATE OF DEPOSIT (CD): A short-term money-market instrument issued by banks to raise wholesale funds, typically at a fixed rate for tenures from a few days up to a year. FCNR(B): Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank) — an account that lets non-resident Indians park foreign-currency deposits with Indian banks. Under the RBI's special window, banks swapped dollars raised overseas for rupee liquidity. CALL RATE: The interest rate at which banks lend to each other overnight in the interbank money market. It fell to 4.97% during the recent liquidity surge. LIQUIDITY COVERAGE RATIO (LCR): A regulatory requirement that banks hold enough high-quality liquid assets to cover net cash outflows over a 30-day stress period. BASIS POINT: One-hundredth of a percentage point. 100 basis points equal 1 percentage point; Axis Securities estimates 12-month CD rates are about 60 basis points below June 1 levels.
