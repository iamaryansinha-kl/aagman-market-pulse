SBI Research Projects Q4 FY26 GDP Growth at 7.2%, Below Advance Estimates

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/SBI Research forecasts India's Q4 FY26 GDP growth at 7.2%, slightly below the 7.3% advance estimate projected in February 2026.
  • 02/Full FY26 growth is pegged at 7.5%, while FY27 is expected to moderate to 6.6% amid global headwinds and a high base effect.
  • 03/Credit growth surged to 16.1% in FY26; official GDP data will be announced by the government on May 29, 2026.

India's economy is likely to grow at 7.2 per cent in the January-March quarter (Q4) of FY26, according to a research report by State Bank of India (SBI). The estimate is marginally below the advance estimates projection of 7.3 per cent released in February 2026.

The government will formally announce Q4 and full FY26 GDP numbers on May 29, 2026. The official release will confirm whether the economy met, exceeded, or fell short of expectations.

SBI Research tracked 50 leading indicators across consumption, agriculture, industry, and services. The data points to a slowdown in Q4 compared to Q3 FY26, though momentum remains intact.

About 85 per cent of indicators showed acceleration in Q4, compared to 83 per cent in Q3. Rural consumption stays strong on positive farm and non-farm signals. Urban consumption has picked up consistently since the last festive season, supported by fiscal stimulus.

For the full fiscal year FY26, SBI Research expects GDP growth at 7.5 per cent. For FY27, the projection drops to 6.6 per cent, reflecting moderation after a robust year.

Credit growth accelerated sharply to 16.1 per cent in FY26, up from 11 per cent in FY25. Total incremental credit reached ₹29.5 lakh crore, with ₹5 lakh crore in H1 and ₹24.5 lakh crore in H2.

As of April 30, 2026, credit growth stood at 16 per cent. SBI expects it to remain robust in H1 FY27 before tapering to 13-14 per cent for the full year due to a high base effect.

The report flagged rupee weakness driven by external macro uncertainty and speculative pressures. It called for structural changes on the balance of payments front to boost export competitiveness and global value chain integration.
