Oil Prices Surge as Trump Briefed on US Military Strike Options Against Iran

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Brent crude June futures rose $5.27 (4.5%) to $123.30 a barrel, marking the ninth consecutive day of gains
  • 02/President Trump is scheduled to receive a CENTCOM briefing Thursday on potential military strikes against Iran per an Axios report
  • 03/The Strait of Hormuz, which carries 20% of global energy supplies, remains effectively closed since Iran retaliated on February 28

Brent crude oil futures for June surged $5.27, or 4.5%, to $123.30 a barrel as of 0347 GMT on April 30. The contract has now risen for nine consecutive sessions.

West Texas Intermediate crude futures for June climbed $2.42, or 2.3%, to $109.30 a barrel. WTI has gained in eight of the past nine trading sessions.

The June Brent contract expires on Thursday. The more active July contract traded at $113.10, up $2.66 or 2.4%, after surging 5.8% in the previous session.

Both Brent and WTI are on track for their fourth consecutive month of price gains. Brent had already jumped 6.1% on Wednesday, while WTI surged 7%.

US President Donald Trump is slated to receive a military briefing Thursday on plans for strikes against Iran, according to an Axios report late Wednesday. Admiral Brad Cooper, commander of US Central Command, is expected to present options for a "short and powerful" wave of strikes targeting Iranian infrastructure.

The US and Israel began air strikes on Iran on February 28, 2026. Iran retaliated by closing the Strait of Hormuz, a chokepoint that carries roughly 20% of global energy supplies. Traffic through the strait has plummeted to just 10% of normal levels.

"The oil market has moved from over-optimism to the reality of the supply disruption we are seeing in the Persian Gulf," ING analysts said in a note.

The conflict has caused what analysts describe as the world's biggest energy supply disruption ever. Talks to end the war have deadlocked, with the US insisting on nuclear programme discussions and Iran demanding strait control and war reparations.

"Prospects for any near-term resolution to the Iran conflict or a reopening of the Strait of Hormuz remain dim," said IG market analyst Tony Sycamore.

Trump spoke with oil companies on Wednesday about mitigating the impact of a possible months-long US blockade on Iranian ports, a White House official confirmed.

OPEC+ is likely to agree a small output increase of around 188,000 barrels per day at its Sunday meeting. However, the UAE's withdrawal from OPEC, effective May 1, is expected to weaken the cartel's price control ability.

"Gulf countries, including the UAE, will take months to return to pre-war production volumes," Wood Mackenzie analysts said in a note. Analysts do not expect any near-term supply relief.
