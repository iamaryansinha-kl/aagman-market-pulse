Crisil Raises India FY27 GDP Growth Forecast to 7% From 6.6%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Crisil raised its India FY27 GDP growth forecast to 7% from 6.6%, citing strong first-half momentum and resilient domestic demand
  • 02/The agency expects growth to slow in the second half and assumes crude oil at $87-93 a barrel in FY27 versus $70 in FY26
  • 03/Crisil sees two 25-bps RBI rate hikes in October and December, and the rupee at 93.5 per dollar by March 2027

Domestic rating agency Crisil on Wednesday, September 30, 2026, raised its India FY27 GDP growth forecast to 7% from 6.6%. The upgrade follows a strong 7.8% growth print in the first quarter of the fiscal year.

Crisil Chief Economist D K Joshi, speaking to reporters in Mumbai, said the economy was doing strong. "The economy is doing strong. We expect the first half to witness fast growth, while the growth will slow down in the second half," Joshi said.

The upgrade rests on resilient consumers, investment and government spending. These factors are cushioning the economy from a volatile global backdrop, with strong domestic demand doing the heavy lifting.

Crisil has assumed crude oil prices will average $87-93 a barrel in FY27. That is sharply higher than the $70 a barrel average seen in FY26, and energy costs remain a key swing factor for the inflation outlook.

Weather is a live risk. Joshi said the monsoon was 12% below the long-term average and could affect the winter rabi crop, with reservoir levels and soil moisture now critical for farm output.

A strengthening El Nino coupled with elevated energy prices could trigger a fresh inflation shock. That matters in an economy where food prices have an outsized influence on household budgets, and could leave the RBI with less room to ease monetary policy.

On rates, Crisil expects two 25-basis-point hikes by the RBI in October and December. Joshi said the hikes were unlikely to hurt private capital investment, as companies were sitting on large cash balances and had deleveraged balance sheets.

Joshi also said the rupee was undervalued and could appreciate to 93.5 against the US dollar by March 2027. Past depreciation episodes, he noted, had been followed by rupee strength. He added that the RBI would continue using various tools to manage excess liquidity in the financial system.

Crisil is not alone in turning more optimistic. ADB raised its FY27 forecast to 7%, S&P Global Ratings lifted its view to 7% from 6.6%, and Moody's raised its forecast to 7% from 6%. S&P's rationale cited weather risks and the West Asia conflict adding to price pressures.

Fitch expects 6.9% growth, up from 6.4%, while the World Economic Forum expects 6.7% and Jefferies sees 6.5-7%. ADB expects India's retail inflation to average 5.2% in 2026, and S&P expects the RBI to raise its policy rate by 25 bps during the fiscal year.

Looking further out, Crisil flagged skilling as important for strengthening the growth process. Joshi said AI could automate some work but also create new jobs, as past technological revolutions had done.

What to watch: the rabi crop outcome after a weak monsoon, the RBI's October and December policy decisions, the trajectory of crude oil in the $87-93 assumed range, and whether the rupee's move toward 93.5 against the dollar materialises.
