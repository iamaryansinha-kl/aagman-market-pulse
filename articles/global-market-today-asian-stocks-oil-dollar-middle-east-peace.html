Global Market Today: Asian Stocks Up, Oil & Dollar Slide on Peace Hopes

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Asian stocks and US futures gained ground as investor risk appetite improved on fresh hopes for a Middle East peace deal
  • 02/Oil prices and the US dollar both slipped, reflecting easing geopolitical risk premium and reduced safe-haven demand
  • 03/President Trump signalled progress toward a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for global energy trade

Asian stock markets opened higher on Monday, tracking gains in US stock futures. The mood across equity markets improved overnight as new developments emerged around a potential Iran peace deal.

US stock futures pointed to a positive open, with investors buying back into risk assets. The optimism follows signs of diplomatic movement in the Middle East.

Oil prices declined, giving up some of the gains recorded in recent sessions. Traders unwound positions built around fears of prolonged supply disruption.

The US dollar also weakened against a basket of major currencies. Safe-haven demand eased as the prospect of de-escalation reduced uncertainty in the region.

President Trump indicated that progress was being made toward a peace agreement. A key element is reopening the Strait of Hormuz, through which roughly one-fifth of global oil shipments pass.

A closure or partial blockage of the Strait would have significant implications for global energy markets. The prospect of maintaining open sea lanes supports more stable supply expectations.

Markets remain sensitive to further headlines from diplomatic channels. Any reversal in negotiations could quickly reignite volatility across equities, commodities, and currencies.
