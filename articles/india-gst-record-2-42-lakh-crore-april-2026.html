India's GST Collections Hit Record ₹2.42 Lakh Crore in April 2026, Up 8.7% YoY

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/India's gross GST collections reached a record ₹2,42,702 crore in April 2026, marking an 8.7% increase over the ₹2,23,265 crore collected in April 2025.
  • 02/Import-linked revenue drove the surge with 25.8% growth to ₹57,580 crore, while domestic collections rose just 4.3% to ₹1,85,122 crore, exposing a growing divergence.
  • 03/Watch the 57th GST Council meeting for fixes to the inverted duty structure, and expect sequential dips in monthly collections as the year-end recalibration effect fades.

India's gross Goods and Services Tax collections for April 2026 hit an all-time high of ₹2,42,702 crore, according to official data released by the finance ministry on May 1, 2026. This marks an 8.7% jump from the ₹2,23,265 crore collected in the same month last fiscal year. Unlike prior record months, this milestone was achieved entirely without compensation cess.

The headline number masks a sharp divergence between domestic and import-linked revenue. Gross import revenue surged 25.8% year-on-year to ₹57,580 crore, while gross domestic revenue grew a modest 4.3% to ₹1,85,122 crore. Net revenue from customs duties actually jumped 42.9%, far outpacing the near-flat 0.3% growth in net domestic revenue.

Central GST stood at ₹52,140 crore, State GST at ₹61,331 crore, and Integrated GST at ₹71,651 crore for the month. Total refunds climbed 19.3% to ₹31,793 crore, pulling net collections to ₹2,10,909 crore — a 7.3% increase year-on-year. Domestic refunds alone spiked 54.6% to ₹19,996 crore, primarily due to the inverted duty structure.

Saurabh Agarwal, Tax Partner at EY India, called the headline encouraging but warned the gap between modest domestic growth and soaring import taxes needs a policy response. "We must critically re-examine our policy frameworks to further incentivize domestic manufacturing and ensure 'Make in India' keeps pace with global supply chain shifts," he said.

Vivek Jalan, Partner at Tax Connect, noted that net domestic collections remained flat as refunds under the inverted duty structure ballooned. He flagged the growing input tax credit accumulation on input services as a concern already raised with the GST Council ahead of its upcoming 57th meeting.

Agarwal also cautioned that April's spike reflects year-end target pushes by both industry and tax administrators. He expects a sequential dip in collections over the coming months as the market recalibrates into the new fiscal year. April figures primarily reflect goods consumed and services availed in March.

State-level data showed uneven momentum. Maharashtra grew 40%, Karnataka 42%, Telangana 36%, and Puducherry surged 108%. However, Bihar, Assam, West Bengal, and Jharkhand saw declines or zero growth, highlighting concentration in large consumption-driven states.

Manoj Mishra, Partner at Grant Thornton Bharat, tied the GST performance to India's broader macro story. "Set against global headwinds, India's GST performance mirrors its macro story, the fastest-growing major economy at 6.5%, underscoring a structurally formalising, demand-led growth cycle," he said.

WHAT DROVE THE SURGE IN IMPORT REVENUE?
GROSS IMPORT REVENUE JUMPED 25.8% TO ₹57,580 CRORE, WITH NET CUSTOMS REVENUE SURGING 42.9%. THIS WAS FUELLED BY STRONG MOP-UP FROM IMPORTED GOODS AND YEAR-END STOCK CLEARANCE.

DOMESTIC REVENUE ROSE ONLY 4.3% TO ₹1,85,122 CRORE, AND NET DOMESTIC REVENUE GREW JUST 0.3% AS REFUNDS UNDER THE INVERTED DUTY STRUCTURE SPIKED 54.6% TO ₹19,996 CRORE.
