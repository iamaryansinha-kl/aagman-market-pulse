SBI Research Urges LTCG Abolition To Stem Rs 2.6 Lakh Crore

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/SBI Research has called for abolishing the long-term capital gains tax on equities and extending the holding period to 2-3 years to attract foreign capital.
  • 02/FPIs have sold Rs 3.51 lakh crore of Indian equities between 2021 and September 2026, including over Rs 2.61 lakh crore in 2026 alone.
  • 03/The report argues RBI's monetary and forex interventions alone cannot fix the rupee; structural tax reform is needed as the BoP surplus narrows to $55-60 billion in FY27.

State Bank of India's research wing has made a bold recommendation. In its latest Ecowrap report, SBI Research called for abolishing the long-term capital gains tax on equities.

The report goes further. It suggests extending the minimum holding period for equities to two or three years to qualify for the most favourable tax treatment, anchoring hot money into longer-term bets.

The rationale is the persistent foreign selling. Foreign portfolio investors have pulled Rs 3.51 lakh crore out of Indian equities between 2021 and September 2026, the report noted.

The outflows have accelerated. FPIs sold more than Rs 2.61 lakh crore in 2026 alone, on top of Rs 1.66 lakh crore in 2025. Equity segment outflows of $13 billion in FY27 are testing the rupee.

"The Government must seriously look into anchoring hot money flows into the equity segment by extending the holding period to say 2-3 years and abolishing the long-term capital gains tax for equity," the report stated.

SBI Research's argument is that the Reserve Bank of India's recent measures are necessary but not sufficient. Monetary and foreign exchange interventions alone cannot address the structural challenge facing the rupee.

The report also called for greater clarity on withholding tax applicable to external commercial borrowings. The current range of 5-20 per cent, it said, needs to be simplified to strengthen investor confidence.

On the external balance, the report expects the balance of payments surplus to narrow to around $55-60 billion in FY27. That makes attracting stable capital inflows more important, not less.

Encouragingly, SBI Research noted that India's foreign exchange reserves remain adequate even under stressed scenarios. The problem is one of flows, not stock.

The selling has multiple drivers. A strong dollar, crude above $100 that has pressured the rupee past 95 per dollar, and rotation into cheaper emerging markets have all pulled foreign money away from Indian equities.

Domestic investors have absorbed the outflows. Systematic investment flows through mutual funds continue at near-record monthly levels, which is one reason indices have held up despite sustained foreign selling.

The tax math explains foreign investors' hesitation. India taxes long-term capital gains on equities at 12.5 per cent above the Rs 1.25 lakh annual exemption, a rate that looks steep when exit costs are stacked with transaction levies.

Extending the holding period in exchange for zero LTCG would mimic incentives used in several Asian markets. It rewards patient capital, which is exactly what a narrowing BoP surplus needs.

The report stressed that India's structural story remains intact. "India Play remains structurally attractive and marquee/blue chip names locally promise lucrative returns in a competitive market," it said.

That framing matters. SBI Research is not arguing India has lost its appeal — it is arguing that tax friction is needlessly taxing the flows India should be attracting.

The proposal lands in the middle of a busy policy season. The RBI has spent October supporting the rupee through cash reserve tightening, NDF rule changes and a dollar window for oil companies.

The proposal would sit alongside the securities transaction tax investors already pay on every trade. It also follows the 2024-25 overhaul of the capital gains regime, which unified holding periods but left equity LTCG intact at 12.5 per cent.

Any move carries a fiscal cost. LTCG collections have grown into a meaningful revenue line, and the finance ministry has historically preferred tweaking rates and holding periods to outright abolition.

Whether the finance ministry takes up the suggestion is an open question. Tax policy changes typically arrive with the Budget, and any LTCG abolition would carry a fiscal cost that the government must weigh.

For equity investors, the near-term signal is twofold. Domestic institutions continue to absorb foreign selling, but the persistent outflow is now large enough that the country's largest bank is publicly lobbying for tax relief.

The next checkpoints are the December-quarter FPI flow data and any response from the finance ministry. If outflows continue at the current pace, the LTCG debate will move from research notes to the policy mainstream, with every pre-Budget commentary cycle revisiting the question.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT HAS SBI RESEARCH RECOMMENDED?
IN ITS ECOWRAP REPORT, SBI RESEARCH CALLED FOR ABOLISHING THE LONG-TERM CAPITAL GAINS TAX ON EQUITIES AND EXTENDING THE HOLDING PERIOD TO 2-3 YEARS TO ATTRACT STABLE FOREIGN CAPITAL INFLOWS.
HOW MUCH HAVE FOREIGN PORTFOLIO INVESTORS SOLD?
FPIS HAVE SOLD RS 3.51 LAKH CRORE OF INDIAN EQUITIES BETWEEN 2021 AND SEPTEMBER 2026, INCLUDING OVER RS 2.61 LAKH CRORE IN 2026 ALONE AND RS 1.66 LAKH CRORE IN 2025.
WHY DOES SBI RESEARCH THINK RBI MEASURES ARE NOT ENOUGH?
THE REPORT ARGUES MONETARY AND FOREX INTERVENTIONS ADDRESS SYMPTOMS, NOT THE STRUCTURE. IT WANTS TAX SWEETENERS TO ANCHOR HOT MONEY, PLUS CLARITY ON WITHHOLDING TAX ON ECBS (CURRENTLY 5-20 PER CENT).
WHAT IS THE BALANCE OF PAYMENTS OUTLOOK?
SBI RESEARCH EXPECTS THE BOP SURPLUS TO NARROW TO AROUND USD 55-60 BILLION IN FY27, WHILE NOTING THAT FOREIGN EXCHANGE RESERVES REMAIN ADEQUATE EVEN UNDER STRESSED SCENARIOS.
WHAT IS THE CURRENT LTCG TAX ON EQUITIES?
LONG-TERM CAPITAL GAINS ON EQUITY AND EQUITY FUNDS ARE TAXED AT 12.5 PER CENT ON GAINS ABOVE RS 1.25 LAKH IN A FINANCIAL YEAR, WITH A ONE-YEAR HOLDING PERIOD QUALIFYING AS LONG-TERM. GLOSSARY: LTCG: Long-Term Capital Gains — profit on assets held beyond a set period, taxed at concessional rates in India. FPI: Foreign Portfolio Investor — overseas investors buying Indian securities without taking controlling stakes. Hot money: Short-term capital that moves quickly between markets in search of returns, and out just as quickly. ECB: External Commercial Borrowing — loans raised by Indian companies from foreign lenders, subject to withholding tax. Balance of payments: The record of all economic transactions between a country and the rest of the world. Withholding tax: Tax deducted at source on payments like interest to foreign lenders.
