RBI Bond Sales and FX Intervention Help Halve India's Cash Overhang

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

The Reserve Bank of India's sustained action through bond sales, foreign exchange swaps and rupee defence has helped more than halve the banking system's liquidity surplus, bankers said.

Surplus banking system liquidity fell to Rs 4.92 lakh crore ($51.36 billion) on Monday, down 55% from a record Rs 11.16 lakh crore about two weeks ago. The banking system was flush with cash after lenders raised a much larger-than-expected $133 billion through the RBI's diaspora deposit scheme.

Liquidity Drain Measures

The RBI sold Rs 750 billion worth of bonds over the past week and plans another Rs 250 billion on Monday. Banks have also parked Rs 3.4 lakh crore with the RBI through reverse repos.

Three traders estimated that the RBI conducted FX swaps of about $1 billion per day over the past 10 sessions, with Tuesday's swaps concentrated in the January 2027 maturity. Advance tax and GST payments also contributed to the liquidity drain.

Excess cash threatens to fan inflationary pressures, potentially adding to the impact of elevated oil prices and a weaker rupee.

Rate Hike Expectations

Market expectations for an RBI rate hike have strengthened. ANZ economist Dhiraj Nim said they now expect three consecutive 25 basis point hikes starting from October 2026, taking the policy repo rate to 6.00% by the end of fiscal 2027.

The RBI would need to continue withdrawing excess liquidity and bring it below Rs 3 lakh crore for an October hike to be effective, Nim added. Other foreign lenders including Citi, HSBC and Deutsche Bank have also called for a rate hike in October.

RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra had earlier pointed to bond sales and FX swaps as options to drain the excess liquidity in a media interview.

The benchmark 2036 bond yield settled at about 7.05% in early trading on Tuesday, with retail inflation at 4.82% in August.
