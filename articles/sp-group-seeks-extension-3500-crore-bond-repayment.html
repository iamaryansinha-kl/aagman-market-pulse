SP Group Seeks 3-6 Month Extension on Rs 3,500 Crore Bond Repayment Due This Month

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

The Shapoorji Pallonji Group is seeking more time on a crucial debt obligation. The conglomerate has asked lenders for a three-to-six-month extension on a Rs 3,500 crore bond repayment that falls due by the end of September, people familiar with the matter said.

The extension request marks the latest chapter in one of India's most closely watched corporate debt stories. The Rs 3,500 crore payment was to be funded partly from the group's Rs 21,500 crore refinancing package executed in July, which was primarily intended to settle existing promoter-level debt and is largely secured against SP Group's holding of roughly 18.4% in Tata Sons.

Lenders had earlier warned that a failure to meet the September deadline could be construed as an event of default, putting pressure on the Tata Sons-backed debt structure. The payment matters to creditors beyond the immediate cash involved: meeting it would reduce the loan-to-value ratio on the group's borrowings from about 40% to roughly 32%, strengthening the collateral cushion.

The refinancing history is long. SP Group had initially sought consent to push back repayment of Rs 14,300 crore of zero-coupon bonds through Goswami Infratech, then completed a Rs 21,500 crore fundraising in July by accepting bids worth that amount, in what was billed as one of the largest private-credit style resolutions in the Indian market.

The strategic backdrop is shifting in the group's favour, but slowly. The Reserve Bank of India has reportedly rejected Tata Sons' request for exemption from rules requiring upper-layer NBFCs to list publicly, which raises the probability of a Tata Sons IPO. SP Group's latest financing documents set milestones for a liquidity event involving the Tata Sons stake, and the RBI's listing directive offers lenders a clearer repayment path.

The group's debt is a legacy of its bitter separation from the Tata group, which left the Pallonji family holding a valuable but encumbered stake in Tata Sons. Every extension request tests lender patience, but the prospect of a Tata Sons listing keeps the eventual recovery story intact.

For now, the ask is straightforward: three to six months to assemble the funds. Lenders will weigh the extension against the improving, if uncertain, odds of a Tata Sons liquidity event materialising within that window.
