India Engineering Exports to New Zealand Set to Double to $300M After FTA

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/India's engineering exports to New Zealand hit $140.5M in FY26, up 8% from $129.8M, and are projected to double to $280-300M over five years post-FTA
  • 02/The zero-duty FTA covers all Indian goods across over 8,000 tariff lines, giving MSMEs stable market access in a high-potential but small destination
  • 03/Growth is led by automobiles, dairy machinery and medical instruments; political approval in New Zealand remains uncertain as coalition partner NZ First opposes the deal

India's engineering exports to New Zealand reached $140.5 million in 2025-26. This marks an 8 per cent rise from $129.8 million in the previous fiscal year.

The growth comes ahead of a bilateral free trade agreement that grants zero-duty market access to all Indian goods. The pact covers over 8,000 tariff lines, officials said.

EEPC India Chairman Pankaj Chadha expects exports to double to $280-300 million over the next five years. "With zero-duty access and predictable trade rules, there is a clear opportunity to scale up," he said.

Growth has been led by automobiles, dairy sector machinery and medical instruments. Metal-based products including iron, steel, aluminium and zinc have also contributed meaningfully.

The FTA is particularly significant for MSMEs. Chadha noted it will reduce trade barriers and ensure stable market access in a relatively small but high-potential destination.

India's engineering sector is targeting exports of $250 billion by 2030. FTAs with Australia and the UAE have already opened new avenues, while proposed deals with the EU and US are expected to accelerate growth further.

However, the agreement faces political headwinds in New Zealand. Coalition partner New Zealand First, led by Foreign Minister Winston Peters, has declared it will oppose the enabling legislation. Peters called the deal "neither free nor fair," citing concerns over immigration and India's retained tariff barriers on dairy exports.

Despite this, Indian industry bodies view the pact as aligned with the country's long-term economic vision. The PHD Chamber of Commerce highlighted commitments on investment, regulatory cooperation and student mobility as key strengths.
