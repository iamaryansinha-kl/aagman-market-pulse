Jesse Livermore Investing Rules: When to Book Profits and Hold Winners

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Jesse Livermore made $100 million shorting the 1929 crash, equivalent to $1.5 billion today
  • 02/His core rule: be patient with winners, cut losses quickly, and never sell just because a stock looks expensive
  • 03/Modern investors face the same emotions fear, greed, and impatience that Livermore warned against a century ago

Jesse Livermore was the "Great Bear of Wall Street." He made and lost three fortunes between 1900 and 1940.

His biggest trade came in October 1929. He shorted $300 million worth of shares before Black Tuesday. The market collapsed and he walked away with $100 million in profit.

That single gain equals roughly $1.5 billion in today's money. It remains the most ever made by one individual in a seven-day period.

Livermore's first rule was simple: let winners run. He warned against selling after 10-20% gains. Doing so often prevents investors from capturing multibagger returns.

His second rule was equally blunt: cut losses fast. He never averaged down on losing trades. Small losses were acceptable. Large, portfolio-damaging losses were not.

He also believed in following the trend, not emotions. Fighting market momentum because prices "look too high" often leads to costly mistakes. Price action itself was his most trusted indicator.

Modern markets now run on algorithms and high-frequency trading. Yet investor psychology has barely changed since Livermore's time. Fear, greed, and overconfidence still drive most buy and sell decisions.

The final lesson from Livermore's life is cautionary. Despite his genius, he went bankrupt four times. He died by suicide in 1940 at age 63. Poor risk management eventually erased everything.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHO WAS JESSE LIVERMORE AND WHY IS HE FAMOUS?
JESSE LIVERMORE WAS A LEGENDARY WALL STREET TRADER KNOWN AS THE "GREAT BEAR OF WALL STREET." HE FAMOUSLY MADE $100 MILLION BY SHORTING THE 1929 STOCK MARKET CRASH, WHICH IS EQUIVALENT TO APPROXIMATELY $1.5 BILLION IN TODAY'S MONEY.
WHAT WAS LIVERMORE'S RULE FOR BOOKING PROFITS?
LIVERMORE BELIEVED IN BOOKING PROFITS ONLY WHEN THE ORIGINAL INVESTMENT THESIS CHANGES. HE ADVISED AGAINST SELLING SIMPLY BECAUSE A STOCK HAD RISEN SHARPLY OR APPEARED EXPENSIVE. PATIENCE WITH WINNING TRADES WAS HIS CORE PRINCIPLE.
HOW DID LIVERMORE HANDLE LOSING TRADES?
LIVERMORE ADVOCATED CUTTING LOSSES QUICKLY AND NEVER AVERAGING DOWN ON LOSING POSITIONS. HE VIEWED SMALL LOSSES AS PART OF INVESTING BUT REFUSED TO LET THEM GROW INTO PORTFOLIO-DAMAGING MISTAKES.
WHAT WERE LIVERMORE'S BIGGEST MARKET TRADES?
LIVERMORE MADE $300,000 SHORTING UNION PACIFIC BEFORE THE 1906 SAN FRANCISCO EARTHQUAKE. HE MADE $1 MILLION DURING THE 1907 PANIC. HIS GREATEST TRADE WAS THE 1929 CRASH, WHERE HE EARNED $100 MILLION IN A SINGLE WEEK.
WHY DO LIVERMORE'S PRINCIPLES STILL MATTER TODAY?
DESPITE MODERN ALGORITHMS AND HIGH-FREQUENCY TRADING, INVESTOR PSYCHOLOGY REMAINS UNCHANGED. FEAR, GREED, AND IMPATIENCE STILL DRIVE DECISIONS. LIVERMORE'S FOCUS ON EMOTIONAL DISCIPLINE AND RISK MANAGEMENT REMAINS TIMELESS.
