Q2: India Inc Revenue at 9-Quarter High, Banks IT Pharma Lag

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

Mumbai: The early numbers from India's September-quarter earnings season look strong. They are also telling a more complicated story than the headline suggests. Behind the double-digit aggregates sits a rotation between sectors — industrials and consumption up, finance and technology down — that has implications for which parts of the equity market can justify their valuations in the weeks ahead.

For a common sample of 357 companies that have declared results so far, aggregate revenue grew 22.1% year-on-year, according to data compiled by ET. That is a nine-quarter high.

Net profit expansion came in at 27.5%, a sharp acceleration from the 9.7% advance recorded in the June quarter. For context, the June quarter's near-single-digit profit growth had been one of the reasons analysts began trimming their full-year earnings estimates over the summer, so the Q2 rebound matters for the market's forward math.

Part of the jump is base effect. The year-ago quarter was weak, with revenue growth of just 6.7% and profit growth of 12.4%, making the latest comparison flatter. But the breadth of the recovery is real — it spans old-economy industrials, consumption-linked businesses and exporters alike, which is a different profile from the narrow, bank-driven growth of recent years.

The growth is concentrated in the non-financial economy. Capital goods, metals, consumer durables, chemicals and auto ancillaries have all put in strong showings. These are precisely the cyclical sectors that benefit from an investment upturn — which fits the separate evidence of a record private capex pipeline and rising order books across engineering and construction companies.

The lending sector is the drag. The sample's overall operating margin contracted 150 basis points year-on-year to 20.8%, largely because bank profitability weakened as net interest margins stayed compressed during the quarter. Deposit costs have caught up with lending rates across the system, and the margin relief that bankers had been promising for several quarters has not yet arrived in the reported numbers.

Exclude the lenders, and the picture transforms. Operating margin for the rest of the sample actually improved 20 basis points to 15.1%, while revenue and profit growth jumped to 26.9% and 33.9% respectively. The non-financial corporate sector, in other words, is having its best quarter in years — a detail easily lost in the aggregated numbers.

The lending sector's share of the sample is shrinking accordingly. Banks and finance companies contributed 34.8% of revenue and 54.6% of profit, down from 37.3% and 56.8% in the year-ago quarter. That is a significant one-year shift in the earnings mix of corporate India — and it changes which sectors drive index-level earnings revisions.

Information technology is losing weight too. IT's share of revenue fell to 15.5% from 16.8%, and its share of net profit dropped to 18.1% from 20.4%. The sector's problem is demand, not execution. Clients are taking longer to sign off on projects as they assess the impact of artificial intelligence on their own businesses, stretching execution cycles.

Pharmaceuticals round out the laggards, with year-on-year growth decelerating compared with the June quarter. Pricing pressure in the US generics market and a softer domestic formulation season have both weighed on the sector's numbers this reporting cycle, and the early trend suggests the recovery investors had pencilled in for the second half may take longer to materialise.

At the start of the season, analysts had anticipated double-digit aggregate growth on broad-based momentum. Elara Securities expected 13 of 18 sectors to deliver double-digit sales growth, and the early results suggest that call is holding up. The strength is broad enough that it cannot be dismissed as a base-effect artifact, even if the base has flattered the percentage.

The leaders, in Elara's estimation, are automobiles at around 29%, non-banking financial services above 27%, consumer discretionary over 23%, and industrials above 22%. The pattern is consistent with an economy where investment and premium consumption are running ahead of mass-market demand — a profile that favours capex-linked and premiumisation stocks over pure consumption plays. For investors, the composition of the recovery matters more than its speed. A market priced for bank-led earnings is meeting a quarter where banks lag and industrials lead. Portfolio positioning that worked for the last three years — overweight financials and IT, underweight cyclicals — is being tested by the actual distribution of earnings growth this season.

Whether that rotation holds through the rest of the results season will depend on the big private banks and IT majors still to report — and on whether margin pressure in lending is cyclical or structural. It will also depend on the global backdrop: a hawkish US Federal Reserve and expensive crude are headwinds that no amount of domestic earnings momentum can fully offset in the near term.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
HOW FAST DID INDIA INC GROW IN Q2?
For a sample of 357 companies that have reported, revenue grew 22.1% year-on-year, a nine-quarter high, and net profit rose 27.5%, up sharply from 9.7% growth in the June quarter. A low base in the year-ago period contributed to the acceleration.
WHICH SECTORS ARE LEADING Q2 GROWTH?
Capital goods, metals, consumer durables, chemicals and auto ancillaries are the strongest. Analysts at Elara Securities expect 13 of 18 sectors to post double-digit sales growth, led by automobiles (~29%), NBFCs (>27%), consumer discretionary (>23%) and industrials (>22%).
WHY ARE BANKS AND IT LAGGING?
Bank profitability is under pressure from weak net interest margins, dragging the sample's operating margin down 150bp. IT is shrinking as clients delay projects while assessing AI's impact on their businesses, stretching execution cycles. Pharma growth has also decelerated versus the June quarter.
WHAT HAPPENS IF YOU EXCLUDE BANKS FROM THE SAMPLE?
Operating margin improves 20 basis points to 15.1%, and revenue and profit growth accelerate to 26.9% and 33.9% respectively — showing the non-financial economy is doing considerably better than the headline numbers suggest. GLOSSARY: NET INTEREST MARGIN (NIM): The difference between the interest a bank earns on loans and the interest it pays on deposits. When NIMs compress, bank profitability falls even if loan growth stays strong — the dynamic visible this quarter. OPERATING MARGIN: Operating profit as a percentage of revenue. It measures how much of each revenue rupee a company keeps after direct costs, before interest and taxes. BASE EFFECT: A statistical distortion where weak growth in the year-ago quarter makes current growth look stronger by comparison. Year-ago Q2 revenue grew just 6.7%, flattering this year's 22.1%. COMMON SAMPLE: A fixed set of companies used for year-on-year comparison, so growth rates are not distorted by which companies have or haven't reported yet. AUTO ANCILLARIES: Companies that make components — tyres, batteries, castings, wiring — for automobile manufacturers. They ride vehicle demand but with different margin structures than the OEMs themselves.
