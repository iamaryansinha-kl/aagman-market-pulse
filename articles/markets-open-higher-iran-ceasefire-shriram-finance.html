Markets Open Higher on Iran Ceasefire Hopes; Shriram Finance in Focus

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Indian equity markets opened mildly higher on April 21 as hopes of US-Iran peace talks eased crude oil supply fears
  • 02/Brent crude fell 1% to $94.53 and WTI dropped 1.72% to $88.07 amid expectations of renewed diplomacy
  • 03/Shriram Finance opened at ₹1,040 and traded at ₹1,045.15; investors eye weekly expiry and earnings

Indian equity markets opened mildly higher on Tuesday, April 21, 2026. The uptick follows renewed hopes of diplomatic progress between the United States and Iran.

The Sensex traded at 78,520.30, up 26.76 points or 0.03%. The Nifty 50 stood at 24,364.85, gaining 11.30 points.

Brent crude oil futures fell about 1% to $94.53 per barrel. US West Texas Intermediate crude dropped 1.72% to $88.07 as traders priced in potential supply relief.

The two-week ceasefire between the US and Iran is set to expire later this week. Iran is reportedly considering attending peace talks in Pakistan, brokered by Islamabad to ease Washington's blockade of Iranian ports.

Shriram Finance Ltd (NSE: SHRIRAMFIN) opened at ₹1,040.00 against its previous close of ₹1,036.95. The stock traded at ₹1,045.15 in early deals.

Shriram Finance has gained sharply from its March 27 low of ₹903. It rallied 10.14% to ₹1,025 on April 8 when the initial ceasefire was announced.

The stock's 52-week range is ₹566.50 to ₹1,108.00. The company commands a market capitalisation of ₹1,53,556 crore.

Tuesday's session also marks the weekly derivatives expiry. India VIX, the volatility gauge, hovered around 18.7, indicating elevated option premiums.

Global cues remained mixed. Japan's Nikkei traded higher by over 400 points, while US markets closed lower in the previous session.

Analysts expect markets to remain rangebound until concrete developments emerge from Iran peace negotiations. Any breakdown in talks could trigger renewed selling pressure.
