Copper Rebounds 1.1% to $13,233 as Bargain Hunters Step In After 4% Drop

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/LME three-month copper gained 1.1% to $13,233 per metric ton on June 25, recovering from over 4% losses in the prior two sessions
  • 02/A softer dollar and improved risk appetite from strong tech earnings boosted sentiment, though higher-for-longer US rate expectations remain a drag
  • 03/Other base metals also rose: aluminium +0.8%, tin +1.1%, nickel +0.3%; Indonesia's 2026 nickel quota uncertainty adds supply-side watch

LME benchmark three-month copper rose 1.1% to $13,233 per metric ton by 0915 GMT on June 25. The rebound followed two days of heavy losses that erased over 4% from the metal's price.

Investors seized bargain opportunities after the steep sell-off. A slightly weaker dollar and optimism across broader financial markets provided additional support.

"Copper is recovering after a steep sell-off earlier this week, supported by a softer dollar and improved risk appetite," said Ewa Manthey, commodities strategist at ING.

Globally, stocks surged after strong earnings from chip giants Micron and Qualcomm reignited the AI rally. The dollar index had hit a 13-month peak on Wednesday.

A weaker dollar makes commodities priced in US currency cheaper for buyers using other currencies. US inflation data due could reinforce expectations of at least one rate hike this year.

Sentiment remains cautious. "The broader macro backdrop remains challenging, with higher-for-longer US rate expectations weighing on industrial metals more broadly," Manthey added.

Among other base metals, LME aluminium rose 0.8% to $3,148 a ton. Zinc added 0.3% to $3,432, lead gained 0.4% to $1,921, and tin advanced 1.1% to $50,245.

Nickel gained 0.3% to $16,860 after Indonesia said it had not yet decided its 2026 production quota. Speculation of a quota increase had been pressuring prices.
