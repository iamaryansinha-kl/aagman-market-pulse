HFCL Q2 Profit Jumps 448% on AI Demand; Stock Hits Circuit

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Q2FY27 net profit jumped 448% YoY to Rs 394 crore from Rs 71.92 crore.
  • •- Revenue rose 144% YoY to Rs 2,542 crore, up 33% sequentially.
  • •- EBITDA rose 224% to Rs 658 crore; margin expanded 640 bps YoY to 25.89%.
  • •- The order book exceeds Rs 28,900 crore, giving six to seven years of revenue visibility.
  • •- Management expects FY27 revenue to grow at least 80% over FY26.
  • •- The stock hit its 5% upper circuit at Rs 268.70 and is up nearly 300% this year.

The AI buildout has a supply chain, and on Monday one of its Indian links reported the kind of quarter that explains why. HFCL, the optical fibre and network equipment manufacturer that has repositioned itself as an infrastructure play for artificial intelligence and data centres, posted a Q2FY27 net profit of Rs 394 crore — up 448 per cent from Rs 71.92 crore a year earlier — as revenue surged 144 per cent to Rs 2,542 crore and operating leverage did the rest. EBITDA rose 224 per cent to Rs 658 crore, and the EBITDA margin expanded 640 basis points year-on-year to 25.89 per cent, a 264-basis-point improvement even against the immediately preceding quarter. The market's verdict was mechanical: the stock locked into its 5 per cent upper circuit at Rs 268.70, extending a year-to-date gain that now approaches 300 per cent.

The numbers describe a company riding three converging demand curves, and the second quarter's shape shows which one is now in charge. The first is telecommunications: India's carriers continue to densify 4G and roll out 5G, consuming optical fibre cable at scale, and the government's BharatNet rural broadband buildout adds a public-sector floor under that demand. The second, increasingly the dominant one, is AI infrastructure — hyperscale data centres being built by global cloud providers require exactly the kind of high-capacity fibre and connectivity hardware HFCL makes, and the company said demand from international hyperscalers and telecom operators is increasingly converting from pipeline into firm contracts. The third is defence and aerospace, where HFCL is building a portfolio spanning thermal weapon sights, electronic fuzes, surveillance radars, multi-mode hand grenades and aerostructures, with an acquisition in the sector expected to close in the current quarter — a business whose customers pay in strategic priority as much as in cash, and whose order cycles, once won, run for years.

The order book is the quarter's strategic fact. At more than Rs 28,900 crore, it exceeds six to seven years of current revenue run-rate and provides, in the company's framing, visibility well beyond that. Managing Director Mahendra Nahata called it the company's best-ever quarterly performance and said a strong pipeline of orders at advanced stages sits behind the booked number. The mix of the book matters as much as its size: multi-year orders from hyperscalers and carriers convert volatile spot demand into contracted revenue, while defence contracts add a counter-cyclical layer that does not move with technology spending. On the strength of that visibility, HFCL guided for FY27 revenue growth of at least 80 per cent over FY26 — guidance that implies the second half must sustain nearly the current run-rate, but which the order book coverage makes plausible.

Capacity is being added to meet it. The company plans to invest around Rs 1,800 crore in optical fibre, cable and preform manufacturing, expanding capacity and deepening backward integration into preforms — the highest-value stage of the fibre supply chain and one where India has historically been import-dependent, with domestic demand for fibre running far ahead of local preform capacity. That backward-integration bet is what separates HFCL from pure cable assemblers: in an upcycle it captures the value that would otherwise leak to imports, and it gives the company a negotiating position with hyperscalers who increasingly audit their supply chains for geographic resilience. Defence is targeted at roughly Rs 500 crore of revenue in FY27, small against the consolidated total but strategically significant as a margin-diversifier with sovereign customers. Nahata said fibre and connectivity, telecom, and defence and aerospace will form the three pillars of the growth strategy.

The quarter's cautionary note is the valuation the rally has built. A stock up three hundred per cent in ten months is pricing in not just the FY27 guidance but years of the AI-capex supercycle continuing to flow through Indian suppliers — a thesis that depends on hyperscaler spending plans that have, in recent weeks, faced their own scrutiny on Wall Street. The semiconductor-style rerating of every stock touching the AI supply chain has lifted companies with far thinner order books than HFCL's, which makes its Rs 28,900 crore backlog the strongest single argument in the bull case: even if the cycle's slope flattens, six to seven years of covered revenue give the company time to grow into its multiple. HFCL's margin expansion, order book depth and backward-integration capex are the substantive markers bulls point to; the multiple, compressed against any normalisation of AI spending, is the marker bears will. Monday's print strengthened the substance. What it cannot do is make the cycle it depends on any less cyclical. For investors, then, the quarter offers a clean way to hold the stock's two truths at once: the operating performance is the best in the company's history and is backed by a book of business that would survive a serious downturn in technology spending; and the price already assumes the downturn never arrives. Both things are true simultaneously, and which one matters more will be decided not by HFCL but by the hyperscalers whose invoices fill its order book.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT WERE HFCL'S Q2FY27 RESULTS?
Net profit rose 448% YoY to Rs 394 crore, revenue rose 144% to Rs 2,542 crore, and EBITDA rose 224% to Rs 658 crore with margins at 25.89%.
WHAT IS DRIVING HFCL'S GROWTH?
Demand from AI data centres, hyperscale operators and telecom networks, plus a defence and aerospace business targeting Rs 500 crore revenue in FY27.
HOW LARGE IS HFCL'S ORDER BOOK?
Over Rs 28,900 crore, providing six to seven years of revenue visibility, with more orders at advanced pipeline stages.
WHAT IS MANAGEMENT'S FY27 GUIDANCE?
Revenue growth of at least 80% over FY26, supported by Rs 1,800 crore of planned capacity investment in fibre, cable and preform manufacturing. GLOSSARY: - EBITDA margin: Operating profit as a percentage of revenue, before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation. - Upper circuit: A regulatory price limit that halts trading in a stock after a set daily gain. - Preform: The raw glass cylinder from which optical fibre is drawn; the most valuable step in fibre manufacturing. - Hyperscaler: Operators of very large data centres, typically major cloud providers.
