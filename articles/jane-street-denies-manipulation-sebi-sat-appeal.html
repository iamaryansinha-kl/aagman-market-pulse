Jane Street Denies Manipulation in SEBI Appeal at SAT

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Jane Street's appeal against SEBI's July 2025 market-manipulation order was heard at the Securities Appellate Tribunal on October 6, 2026
  • 02/The firm's lawyer, Darius Khambata, denied manipulating the NSE Nifty Bank Index, saying positions were not designed to influence the index
  • 03/The full 48.4 billion rupees (~$503 million) seized by SEBI sits in an escrow account while the firm has not resumed trading in India

Jane Street on Tuesday appeared at the Securities Appellate Tribunal to challenge a market-manipulation order from SEBI. The firm flatly denied manipulating India's stock index.

The appeal was heard on October 6, 2026, at the SAT in Mumbai. It marks one of the most closely watched enforcement cases involving a global quant fund in India.

SEBI's original order was issued in July 2025. It accused the firm of manipulating India's stock index and seized 48.4 billion rupees of gains it called illegal. That amount equals roughly $503 million.

The order also barred Jane Street from Indian markets. It alleged the firm earned illegal gains through index manipulation.

Representing Jane Street, senior counsel Darius Khambata told the tribunal that the firm did not manipulate the NSE Nifty Bank Index. He said positions taken were not designed to influence the index one way or the other.

Khambata argued that the exchange itself had cleared the trading. He noted that the National Stock Exchange flagged nothing unusual about the transactions at the time.

He further pointed out that SEBI's own surveillance unit initially raised no flags. The defense's position is that both the exchange and the regulator's monitoring found nothing wrong before the investigation committee reached its conclusion.

The case now moves through the appellate process at the tribunal. Tuesday's session was the next scheduled hearing after SEBI issued its order in July 2025.

The seized money remains parked. The entire 48.4 billion rupees sits in an escrow account while the appeal plays out.

An escrow account holds the funds with a neutral third party. Neither Jane Street nor SEBI can access the money until the dispute is resolved.

Jane Street has deposited the money. However, the firm has not resumed trading in India, maintaining the status set by the regulator's bar.

The stakes are high on both sides. For SEBI, the case is a test of its market-manipulation enforcement powers against one of the world's largest quantitative trading firms.

The regulator's July 2025 order framed the seized gains as illegal. That characterization is now the core question before the appellate tribunal.

For Jane Street, the outcome affects not only the frozen funds. It also determines whether the firm's trading model can return to Indian markets, where it remains barred.

The defense's argument rests on what exchanges and surveillance found at the time. If the NSE cleared the trades and SEBI's surveillance unit raised no flags, the firm argues, the manipulation case weakens.

SEBI's side, laid out in its order, alleges the gains came from index manipulation. The tribunal will weigh that against the defense's account of the trading.

The case sits against a broader backdrop of losses in India's derivatives market. Retail investors have lost about $9.6 billion over the past five years in equity derivatives.

That figure has been central to the policy debate over derivatives trading in India. It is part of the environment in which SEBI has tightened market oversight.

Global quant funds have also faced increased scrutiny in recent months. Heightened market volatility has put high-frequency and index-linked trading strategies under the microscope across jurisdictions.

For market participants, the case is a signal on how Indian regulators treat complex index trading. The defense's argument — that exchange surveillance and the regulator's own monitoring saw nothing wrong at the time — will be central to the appeal.

The escrow arrangement means the disputed gains are neither with the firm nor the regulator. They remain frozen until the tribunal decides.

Investors and fund managers worldwide are watching. A ruling either way could shape how quant firms structure index-linked strategies in emerging markets.

GLOSSARY:

SAT (Securities Appellate Tribunal) — The statutory tribunal that hears appeals against orders passed by SEBI and other Indian market regulators.

SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India) — India's capital markets regulator, responsible for securities regulation and market oversight.

NSE Nifty Bank Index — The National Stock Exchange's benchmark index tracking the most liquid and large-cap banking stocks listed in India.

Escrow Account — A neutral account where funds are held by a third party during a dispute, released only when conditions are met or a ruling is made.

Quant Fund — An investment fund that uses mathematical models, algorithms, and high-speed data analysis to make trading decisions.

Market Manipulation — Trading conduct intended to artificially influence the price or value of a security or index, typically prohibited under securities law.

SEBI SEIZED 48.4 BILLION RUPEES, ROUGHLY $503 MILLION, IN GAINS IT CALLED ILLEGAL. THE ENTIRE AMOUNT SITS IN AN ESCROW ACCOUNT PENDING THE APPEAL.

THE DEFENSE ARGUES THE NATIONAL STOCK EXCHANGE CLEARED THE TRADING AND FLAGGED NOTHING UNUSUAL. SEBI'S OWN SURVEILLANCE UNIT ALSO INITIALLY RAISED NO FLAGS, ACCORDING TO THE FIRM'S COUNSEL.
HAS JANE STREET RESUMED TRADING IN INDIA?
NO. THE FIRM HAS DEPOSITED THE 48.4 BILLION RUPEES INTO ESCROW BUT HAS NOT RESUMED TRADING IN INDIA. THE BAR IMPOSED BY SEBI'S JULY 2025 ORDER REMAINS IN EFFECT.
WHAT HAPPENS NEXT IN THE JANE STREET VERSUS SEBI CASE?
THE SECURITIES APPELLATE TRIBUNAL HEARD THE APPEAL ON OCTOBER 6, 2026. ARGUMENTS WILL CONTINUE, AND THE DISPUTED FUNDS WILL REMAIN FROZEN IN ESCROW UNTIL THE TRIBUNAL RULES.
