IMF Lowers 2026 Global Growth Outlook to 3%, Projects 3.4% for 2027

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

The global economy is losing momentum.

The International Monetary Fund has lowered its 2026 global growth outlook to 3.0%, down from earlier projections. The fund expects a modest recovery to 3.4% growth in 2027, but warns that significant downside risks could derail even that modest improvement.

The downgrade reflects multiple converging pressures. Trade policy uncertainty has increased as major economies impose new tariffs and trade restrictions. Geopolitical tensions in the Middle East have pushed oil prices higher, threatening to reignite inflation. And several major economies are experiencing slower-than-expected recoveries.

Advanced economies are projected to grow at just 1.7% in 2026, dragged down by sluggish performance in Europe and policy uncertainty in the United States. Emerging markets are faring better, with growth projected at 4.2%, led by India and parts of Southeast Asia.

The IMF identified several key risks to the outlook. A further escalation of trade wars could disrupt supply chains and raise costs. Persistently high oil prices could reignite inflation, forcing central banks to keep interest rates higher for longer. And financial market volatility could tighten credit conditions unexpectedly.

Inflation remains a concern despite recent progress. While headline inflation has moderated in many countries, core inflation — excluding volatile food and energy prices — has proven stickier. Services inflation in particular has been slow to decline.

The fund called for coordinated policy action. Countries should avoid protectionist measures, maintain central bank credibility, and invest in productivity-enhancing reforms to boost long-term growth potential.
