BHEL Q2 Profit Doubles to Rs 821 Crore, Margin Jumps 330bps

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- BHEL's Q2 net profit more than doubled, rising 119% YoY to Rs 821 crore from Rs 375 crore.
  • •- Consolidated revenue grew 24% to Rs 9,300 crore, with EBITDA margin expanding 330 bps to 11%.
  • •- Gross margin surged 590 bps to 36.5% on better-priced recent orders ramping up.
  • •- Fresh order inflows fell 85% YoY, though the order book stands at Rs 2.6 lakh crore, 7.6 times FY26 revenue.
  • •- Nuvama and JM Financial both maintained Buy ratings with targets of Rs 540 and Rs 535.
  • •- The stock fell 4.3% to Rs 444.05 after rallying nearly 5% in the previous session.

Bharat Heavy Electricals on Thursday showed why India's power-equipment cycle has become one of the stock market's most durable stories: the state-owned manufacturer reported a second-quarter net profit of Rs 821 crore, up 119 per cent from Rs 375 crore a year earlier, as years of cost discipline and better-priced orders finally converted into margin.

Consolidated revenue from operations rose 24 per cent year on year to Rs 9,300 crore, from Rs 7,512 crore, and was up 21 per cent sequentially. The real story was pricing. EBITDA margin expanded 330 basis points year on year to 11 per cent — comfortably ahead of the consensus estimate of about 8.5 per cent — driven by a 590-basis-point surge in gross margin to 36.5 per cent. The mechanics, as Nuvama Institutional Equities described it, are straightforward: newer orders secured at better prices are now ramping up in execution, lifting the blended margin of everything flowing through the factories.

Segment detail sharpened the picture. The power business grew revenue 17 per cent, while the industry segment — defence, transportation, renewables equipment and industrials — grew 45 per cent. EBIT margins in the two segments improved to 13 per cent and 19.5 per cent respectively, confirming that the non-power book, though smaller, is the higher-margin engine. JM Financial noted the quarter beat its estimates across the board: revenue 3 per cent ahead, and net profit a striking 61 per cent above its forecast and 31 per cent above consensus.

The balance sheet is quietly improving too. BHEL's working capital cycle compressed to 141 days from 164 days in FY26, helped by collections rising 33 per cent to Rs 13,200 crore; interest expense fell to Rs 120 crore from Rs 200 crore a year earlier. The company sits on roughly Rs 4,600 crore of net cash, announced Rs 500 crore of capex, and — a landmark for a company that had frozen headcount for a generation — resumed hiring after a gap of nearly 15 years.

The caution flag is the order book's near-term trajectory. Fresh order inflows fell 85 per cent year on year in the quarter, even as the backlog held at Rs 2.6 lakh crore — equivalent to 7.6 times FY26 revenue. That runway is the bull case: execution of the existing book can power revenue for years without a single new tender. But an 85 per cent inflow decline will eventually matter if it persists, which is why both brokerages tied their optimism explicitly to the execution outlook rather than fresh orders. Nuvama expects EBITDA margin to reach 13.8 per cent by FY29 and projects a 53 per cent EPS compound growth rate between FY26 and FY29, while trimming its FY27 EPS estimate by 12 per cent on management's commissioning guidance, which implies 11.9 per cent power-capacity growth in the second half. JM Financial lifted its FY28 and FY29 margin estimates to 13.2 per cent and 15 per cent.

The market's reaction looked paradoxical but was really just sequencing: the stock slid 4.3 per cent to Rs 444.05 on Thursday, a day after rallying nearly 5 per cent on the results themselves. At roughly 39 times Nuvama's FY27 earnings estimate — falling to 33 times FY28 and 29 times FY29 — BHEL is no longer priced as a turnaround; it is priced as a growth franchise, and a growth franchise must keep beating for the multiple to hold. Thursday's dip is the market catching its breath, not repricing the story.

For the wider economy, BHEL's quarter is a data point in a larger argument. India's power demand is growing at a pace that requires sustained thermal capacity additions alongside renewables, and the equipment order books of BHEL and its private-sector rivals have swelled accordingly. The margin inflection now visible in BHEL's numbers is what that capacity cycle looks like when it reaches the income statement — and with a 7.6x revenue backlog, this quarter is unlikely to be the last one that surprises on the upside.

Two details from the quarter reward a second look. The 45 per cent growth in the industry segment — which houses the company's defence, rail transportation, renewables and industrial equipment businesses — matters because it diversifies BHEL away from pure thermal dependence at exactly the moment diversification is becoming a valuation argument. And the resumption of hiring after fifteen years, easy to skim past, signals management's conviction that the current execution load is structural rather than cyclical: you do not rebuild a workforce for a one-quarter blip.

JM Financial, which values the stock at 30 times estimated September 2028 earnings for its Rs 535 target, pointed to the same conclusion from the cash-flow side: collections up 33 per cent to Rs 13,200 crore, working capital down 23 days, and a net cash pile near Rs 4,600 crore give BHEL the balance-sheet room to fund capex and hiring from internal resources — a position the company has not occupied in decades. The thermal pipeline, JM noted, remains promising. The bear case now rests on almost exactly one number: the 85 per cent order-inflow decline. Everything else in the quarter argues the cycle has room to run.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT WERE BHEL'S Q2 NUMBERS?
Net profit rose 119% YoY to Rs 821 crore; revenue grew 24% to Rs 9,300 crore; EBITDA margin expanded 330 bps to 11%.
WHAT DROVE THE MARGIN EXPANSION?
A 590-bps rise in gross margin to 36.5%, reflecting the ramp-up of newer orders booked at better prices.
WHY DID THE STOCK FALL DESPITE THE STRONG RESULTS?
Profit booking after a nearly 5% rally the previous session; fresh order inflows also fell 85% YoY, though the order book stands at Rs 2.6 lakh crore.
WHAT ARE BROKERAGES SAYING?
Nuvama (Buy, target Rs 540) and JM Financial (Buy, target Rs 535) both raised targets, citing execution-led margin expansion and a 7.6x-revenue order book. GLOSSARY: - EBITDA margin: Operating profit as a percentage of revenue; expansion signals better pricing or cost control. - Order book: The value of contracted work not yet executed; a multiple of annual revenue shows revenue visibility. - Working capital cycle: Days between paying suppliers and collecting from customers; fewer days mean healthier cash flow. - Commissioning: Bringing a completed power project into commercial operation. - Net cash: Cash and equivalents minus debt; BHEL holds about Rs 4,600 crore.
