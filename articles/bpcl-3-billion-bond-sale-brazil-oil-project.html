BPCL Plans $3 Billion Bond Sale to Fund Brazil Oil Project

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Bharat Petroleum Corporation Ltd (NSE: BPCL) is preparing an international bond sale of as much as $3 billion, largely to fund its $2.8 billion contribution to Brazil's SEAP-I offshore oil project.
  • 02/Part of the proceeds will refinance debt maturing next year; investor roadshows were held in London, Dubai and Singapore last month to gauge demand.
  • 03/BPCL's unit Bharat PetroResources Ltd owns 40% of SEAP-I, which Petrobras operates; oil production is targeted for 2030, with gas exports expected a year later.

Bharat Petroleum Corporation Ltd (NSE: BPCL) is preparing an international bond sale of as much as $3 billion. The plan was described by people familiar with the matter, who asked not to be named because the discussions are private.

The proceeds would largely fund BPCL's $2.8 billion contribution to SEAP-I, an offshore oil project in Brazil. Part of the money would also be used to refinance debt maturing next year.

A BPCL spokesperson did not immediately respond to a request for comment. The company has not made a formal board announcement on the bond sale.

The plan is to raise the money in several tranches. Investor roadshows were held in London, Dubai and Singapore last month to gauge demand from global investors.

The bond plan underscores India's push to secure overseas energy supplies. Wars in the Middle East and between Russia and Ukraine have disrupted traditional sources of crude.

India imports almost 90% of its crude and about half of its natural gas. State-run companies including BPCL and ONGC are preparing to boost investments abroad as the country seeks to diversify supply.

Brazil is already gaining ground as a supplier. India's crude imports from Brazil have more than doubled from 2025 levels so far this year, according to Kpler data.

That surge has made Brazil the country's fifth-biggest oil supplier. For Indian refiners, the South American producer offers an alternative source outside the traditional Middle East supply base.

SEAP-I sits in the BM-SEAL-11 area off Brazil's coast. Petrobras operates the area and holds the largest stake in the project.

BPCL holds its interest through unit Bharat PetroResources Ltd, which owns 40% of SEAP-I. The remaining structure puts Petrobras in the lead role as operator.

When fully operational, SEAP-I will have capacity to produce 120,000 barrels of oil a day. It will also process 10 million cubic meters of natural gas daily, according to project plans.

Oil production is targeted for 2030. Gas exports are expected a year later, per Petrobras.

The road to this point has been long. Discoveries in the area were declared commercially viable in 2021.

A development plan was submitted to Brazil's oil regulator the following year. BPCL then approved the project's final investment decision earlier this year.

That approval came after delays in securing a floating production, storage and offloading vessel for the project. The vessel is central to developing the offshore field.

The scale of the planned bond sale reflects the size of the funding commitment. A $2.8 billion project contribution, plus debt refinancing, points to substantial cash needs in the coming years.

Details such as pricing, currency mix and final timing have not been disclosed. The company plans to raise the money in several tranches following last month's roadshows.

BPCL's Brazil bet also fits a broader industry shift. Global energy companies are returning to oil and gas investment.

South America is emerging as a key destination for that capital. Exxon Mobil and TotalEnergies are among the companies planning investments in the region.

For India, the logic is supply security. For BPCL, the SEAP-I stake secures a direct position in a producing offshore basin years before first oil is expected.

Execution risk remains. First production is targeted for 2030, and the project has already experienced delays in securing key equipment.

Investors will be watching for the formal launch of the bond sale. The structure and size of the first tranche will show how the company plans to split its funding needs across markets.
