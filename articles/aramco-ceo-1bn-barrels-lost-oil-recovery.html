Aramco CEO: 1 Billion Barrels Lost, Oil Recovery Will Take Months

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Aramco CEO Amin Nasser states the world has lost approximately 1 billion barrels of oil over the past two months due to the Strait of Hormuz blockade.
  • 02/Aramco Q1 2026 adjusted net income jumped 25% to $33.6 billion; East-West Pipeline reached peak capacity of 7.0 million barrels per day.
  • 03/Brent crude trades near $113 per barrel; markets face a 3.7 mb/d supply deficit in Q2 2026 even if shipping routes reopen.

Saudi Arabian Oil Company (Saudi Aramco) CEO Amin Nasser warned that global energy markets have lost roughly 1 billion barrels of oil over the past two months. The disruption stems from Iran's blockade of the Strait of Hormuz following the U.S.-Israeli war.

Nasser said reopening shipping routes will not immediately normalize markets deprived of one billion barrels. Years of underinvestment have compounded strain on already-low global inventories.

Aramco reported a 25% surge in first-quarter net profit on May 10, 2026. Adjusted net income reached $33.6 billion, up from $26.6 billion in Q1 2025.

The company ramped up its East-West Pipeline to maximum capacity of 7.0 million barrels per day. Nasser called the pipeline a "critical lifeline" bypassing Hormuz to the Red Sea.

Global oil supply crashed by 10.1 million barrels per day in March, per the World Bank. Brent crude peaked at $114.44 and trades near $113 as of early May.

The oil market faces a projected 3.7 mb/d deficit in Q2 2026. Global output is expected to fall 6.9 mb/d year-on-year, the largest drop since the COVID-19 pandemic.

Aramco declared a Q1 base dividend of $21.9 billion, up 3.5% year-on-year. Free cash flow came in at $18.6 billion despite a $15.8 billion working capital build.

Nasser reiterated that Asia remains central to global demand and a key priority for Aramco. The World Bank forecasts Brent averaging $86 per barrel in 2026.

"Our objective is simple: keep energy flowing, even when the system is under strain," Nasser told Reuters.
