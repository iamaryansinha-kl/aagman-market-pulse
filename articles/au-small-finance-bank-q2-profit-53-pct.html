AU Small Finance Bank Q2 Profit Jumps 53% to Rs 858 Crore

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Net profit rose 53% year-on-year to Rs 858 crore in Q2 FY27, from Rs 561 crore a year earlier.
  • •- Net interest income grew 32% to Rs 2,820 crore and net interest margin expanded to 5.8% from 5.5%, while cost of funds fell 43 basis points to 6.41%.
  • •- Asset quality strengthened: gross NPA improved 42 basis points to 1.99% and net NPA to 0.81%, allowing provisions to drop 25% to Rs 359 crore.
  • •- Deposits grew 29% to Rs 1.70 lakh crore and advances 25% to Rs 1.49 lakh crore; return on assets improved to 1.7%.

Small finance banks live and die on two numbers: the cost of their money and the cleanliness of their loan book. AU Small Finance Bank delivered its best version of both together on Saturday, reporting a 53% jump in second-quarter net profit to Rs 858 crore, from Rs 561 crore a year earlier — a print that lands in the middle of results season as perhaps the cleanest demonstration yet that the small-lender model can scale without losing discipline. Every line that matters moved the right way at the same time, which in banking rarely happens by accident.

The earnings engine was net interest income, up 32% year-on-year to Rs 2,820 crore, powered by a net interest margin that expanded to 5.8% from 5.5% a year ago. Margin is where small finance banks are supposed to be vulnerable — they lend to customers mainstream banks ignore, fund themselves more expensively than the big private lenders, and get squeezed from both ends whenever the rate cycle turns. AU's answer this quarter was to attack its funding cost instead: cost of funds fell 43 basis points to 6.41%, a decline that flowed almost directly into margin. Core pre-provision operating profit rose 31% to Rs 1,480 crore, confirming the improvement is operational rather than cosmetic. Return on assets improved to 1.7%, a ratio many full-service banks would accept gladly.

Growth stayed aggressive without turning reckless. Deposits expanded 29% to Rs 1.70 lakh crore and advances grew 25% to Rs 1.49 lakh crore — deposit growth outpacing loan growth is exactly the sequence regulators want to see from a lender scaling up, because it means the funding side is keeping pace with the asset side rather than being stretched to fund it. It also matters in the current environment, where the RBI's tightening stance has made deposit competition fiercer and the market is punishing any lender whose liability franchise looks fragile. AU's advance-deposit math gives it room to keep growing while others slow.

The asset quality numbers are the part worth dwelling on. Gross non-performing assets fell 42 basis points to 1.99%, net NPA improved to 0.81%, and annualised credit cost dropped 45 basis points to 0.8% from 1.2% a year earlier. That improvement funded a 25% decline in provisions to Rs 359 crore — and lower provisions, not lower lending standards, are what turned a good operating quarter into a 53% profit jump. The distinction matters: in the small finance lending space, where borrowers are economically fragile and collections are operationally intensive, a falling GNPA ratio during rapid growth is evidence that underwriting and recovery machinery are working, not merely that the economy is kind. A 1.99% gross NPA ratio, in particular, sits comfortably below the levels at which regulators start asking hard questions, and it does so while the loan book is growing at 25% — the hardest combination to hold. This is the same beat that larger rival Shriram Finance hit in its own results a day earlier, and the two together suggest the organised retail-lending sector is entering this tightening cycle with far cleaner books than the last one.

The H1 picture extends the story. For the first half of FY27, the bank compounded the same drivers — interest income growing faster than interest expense, credit cost grinding lower — and the first-half trajectory suggests the second half of the fiscal year can sustain the profit pace even if the RBI's calibrated tightening lifts funding costs for the entire system. AU's own cost-of-funds decline this quarter shows it is not merely riding the cycle down; it is actively repricing its deposit base faster than the market, which is a structural advantage that survives a harder rate environment.

The read-across for shareholders is straightforward, and it extends beyond AU itself. The bank has spent years proving its deposit franchise could fund its ambitions without the costly wholesale-money crutch that has damaged other small lenders; this quarter shows what the model looks like when margins, asset quality and growth all align at once. For the broader small-finance sector, AU's print is the second strong result in as many days — Shriram Finance's 59% profit jump on Friday made the same point at a larger scale — and together they suggest organised retail lending is entering this tightening cycle with meaningfully cleaner books than the last one. For the RBI, watching from its calibrated-tightening perch, results like this are the soft data that justifies treating the sector as resilient rather than fragile. The macro backdrop — ten weeks of foreign selling, crude near six-month highs, a hawkish central bank — has crushed the valuations of exactly this kind of stock, which means the market is currently paying very little for a 53% profit print with improving returns. Results like this do not reverse a macro-driven selloff by themselves. But they establish something more durable: when the tide turns, the lenders that compounded through it will be the ones left standing with these numbers.

GLOSSARY: SMALL FINANCE BANK — A niche lender focused on underserved retail borrowers — small businesses, low-income households — granted a banking licence with priority-sector obligations. NIM — Net interest margin, the spread a bank earns between loan yields and funding costs; the core profit engine of any lender. CREDIT COST — Provisions for loan losses expressed as a percentage of advances; a falling figure means improving loan-book health. COST OF FUNDS — The average interest rate a bank pays on its deposits and borrowings; AU's 43-basis-point drop here drove its margin expansion.
