India Government Bonds Post Fifth Straight Weekly Loss as Fed Hike and RBI Bond Sales Bite

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

Indian government bonds have now fallen for five consecutive weeks, the longest losing streak in months, as global and domestic forces aligned against the market.

The benchmark 6.94% 2036 bond yield closed at 7.0686% on Friday, up from 7.0463% on Thursday, taking the weekly rise to 4.5 basis points. The market weathered a triple jolt: a global debt rout that pushed yields to multi-decade peaks, a Federal Reserve rate hike that recast the outlook for RBI tightening, and the central bank's own bond sales adding supply in a demand-light market.

The Fed's widely expected 25-basis-point hike on Wednesday fortified its inflation-fighting resolve and, crucially for India, raised the chances of an RBI rate hike in October. The decision will add to calls for the RBI to hike at its October meeting to maintain India's interest rate differential with the US and mitigate the risk of large foreign fund outflows, said Ajit Banerjee, president and chief investment officer at Shriram Life Insurance.

Global yields are the anchor problem. The US 10-year Treasury yield probed 5.03% this week, its highest level since the 2008 financial crisis, spurring foreign investors to retreat from risk, including by unloading Indian government bonds that are part of global indexes.

At home, the RBI turned seller. Through its first open market operation bond sale in nine years, the central bank drained liquidity equivalent to nearly 0.2% of total bank deposits on Thursday, and traders expect it to step up these operations. A large liquidity surplus in the banking system can blunt monetary policy transmission by curbing banks' need to borrow at the policy rate, which is why the RBI is absorbing it even as it signals tolerance for higher yields.

Rate expectations are repricing across the curve. Overnight indexed swaps rose on firmer domestic rate-hike wagers: the one-year rate jumped 4 basis points to 6.0725%, the two-year rose 4 basis points to 6.28%, and the five-year added nearly 2 basis points to 6.5875%.

For borrowers and the government, the direction is clear. Higher global yields reduce the scope for the RBI to keep policy accommodative while defending the rupee, and each weekly rise in bond yields feeds directly into the cost of funding a large borrowing programme.
