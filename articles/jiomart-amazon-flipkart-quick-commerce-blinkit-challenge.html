JioMart, Amazon, Flipkart Scale Up Quick Commerce Challenge to Blinkit

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/JioMart clocks 2 million daily orders in Q4 FY26, up 300% YoY, as Amazon Now targets 100 cities and Flipkart scales to 1,000 dark stores
  • 02/Blinkit reports 95.4% NOV growth and Rs 37 crore adjusted EBITDA, but guides for 60% annual growth vs 104% CAGR historically
  • 03/Blinkit CEO Albinder Dhindsa warns aggressive discounting is driving poor-quality growth; next phase hinges on speed, cost and network depth

India's quick commerce sector is intensifying as Reliance Retail, Amazon, and Flipkart accelerate expansion. JioMart's hyperlocal business reached 2 million average daily orders in Q4 FY26. That marks 29% sequential growth and over 300% year-on-year.

Amazon Now plans to expand into 100 cities with more than 1,000 micro-fulfilment centres. Flipkart is targeting 1,000 dark stores, up from roughly 800 currently. Both are leveraging deep balance sheets to subsidise aggressive customer acquisition.

The incumbents are not standing still. Blinkit reported 95.4% year-on-year growth in net order value during Q4 FY26. It added 216 dark stores, taking its network to 2,243 locations across the country.

Profitability is improving at the market leader. Blinkit posted adjusted EBITDA of Rs 37 crore in Q4 FY26, against Rs 4 crore in the previous quarter and a loss of Rs 178 crore a year earlier. Mature markets are nearing steady-state margins.

Growth rates are normalising across the sector. Blinkit management guided for over 60% annual growth in the coming years, down from a 104% CAGR between FY23 and FY26. Average monthly transacting users nearly doubled to 27.2 million, even as average order value fell to Rs 525 from Rs 665.

The competitive pressure is reshaping the market. Blinkit CEO Albinder Dhindsa noted that aggressive discounting is driving poor-quality growth skewed toward low-margin categories. He expects the next phase to be defined by a balance between speed, cost efficiency and network depth.

Analysts see metro markets as the profit engine for now. Karan Taurani, executive vice president at Elara Capital, said higher throughput in big cities supports better returns. Non-metro expansion remains viable but stores need six to twelve months to mature.
