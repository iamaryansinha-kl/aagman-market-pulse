GST Collections September 2026: GST Up 14.7% to ₹2.04 Lakh Crore

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/India's gross GST collections rose 14.7% year-on-year to ₹2.04 lakh crore in September 2026, crossing the ₹2 lakh crore mark for the month
  • 02/Import GST surged 25.9% to ₹65,525 crore while domestic GST grew 10.1% to ₹1,37,996 crore — a divergence experts say needs watching
  • 03/Net GST revenue rose 18.1% to ₹1.77 lakh crore; a 13.5% decline in domestic refunds was also flagged for monitoring

India's gross GST collections rose 14.7% year-on-year to ₹2.04 lakh crore in September 2026. The figure crossed the ₹2 lakh crore mark for the month, according to government data released on Thursday.

The headline number masks a sharp split in where the growth came from. Domestic GST revenue grew 10.1% to ₹1,37,996 crore. Revenue from imports jumped 25.9% to ₹65,525 crore.

Net GST revenue rose 18.1% to ₹1.77 lakh crore in September. Gross collections count all taxes deposited, while the net figure reflects settlements and adjustments after inter-government transfers.

GST is India's largest indirect tax, covering most goods and services across manufacturing, trade and consumption. Monthly collections are tracked closely as a near real-time proxy for domestic economic activity.

The divergence between import-linked and domestic collections is the story tax experts zeroed in on. GST on imports growing nearly 26% against roughly 10% domestic growth suggests trade flows, not just consumption, are doing heavy lifting.

Import-linked GST collections typically track the value of goods brought into the country. When import duties-linked collections consistently outpace domestic ones, it can signal that external trade is growing faster than home-grown demand.

"GST collections remain strong, but it's interesting to see that domestic revenues grew 10.1% compared with almost 26% growth in import GST. This suggests a meaningful part of the headline buoyancy is coming from imports." - Pratik Jain, Partner at Price Waterhouse & Co LLP

Jain also pointed to the rise in cumulative refunds as a positive sign for business liquidity. He said sustained growth in domestic revenues remains important to watch in the coming months.

Saurabh Agarwal, Tax Partner at EY India, said crossing the ₹2 lakh crore mark with growth of close to 15% shows domestic demand continues to do well. He noted that GST on imports growing much faster than domestic collections is worth watching.

Agarwal went further, saying the trend could warrant a reassessment of some production-linked incentive schemes. The objective, he indicated, would be to strengthen domestic manufacturing.

Production-linked incentive schemes offer companies financial rewards linked to incremental output. They were designed in part to cut import dependence and build domestic supply chains across key sectors.

Vivek Jalan, Partner at Tax Connect Advisory Services LLP, agreed September was a strong month overall. He flagged a 13.5% decline in domestic refunds during September.

Jalan said the refund decline "warrants close monitoring to ensure liquidity for businesses." Refunds matter because they return blocked input tax credit to firms.

GST refunds release input tax credit back to businesses, particularly exporters and capital-intensive companies. Delays or declines in refunds can squeeze working capital even when headline collections look robust.

The overall takeaway from tax experts is one of continued resilience in GST revenues. But the faster pace of import-linked collections and the refund trend are the two indicators they will track most closely in the coming months.
