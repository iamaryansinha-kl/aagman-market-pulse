T-Bill Yields Hit 18-Month High; Repo Premium at 4-Year Peak

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- The government sold 182-day and 364-day treasury bills at cutoff yields of 6.29% and 6.49%, their highest levels in about 18 months.
  • •- The premium over the RBI's 5.5% repo rate jumped to 79 bps and 99 bps, the highest since November 2022.
  • •- Yields on both maturities rose more than 20 basis points compared with a week ago.
  • •- The RBI raised rates for the first time in nearly four years last week and signalled more increases.
  • •- Governor Sanjay Malhotra said on Tuesday that monetary policy cannot be seen as separate from financial stability.
  • •- The bond yield curve is flattening as short-term rates rise faster than long-term ones.

India's ultra-short-term government borrowing costs jumped at a treasury bill auction on Wednesday, with the premium over the benchmark policy rate climbing to a four-year high as investors priced in a more aggressive cycle of monetary tightening than the central bank has delivered so far.

New Delhi sold 182-day and 364-day treasury bills at cutoff yields of 6.29 per cent and 6.49 per cent respectively, their highest levels in about 18 months. Yields for both maturities rose more than 20 basis points in a single week — a violent move for the front end of the curve, where rates usually move in single digits.

The premium, or spread over the RBI's 5.5 per cent repo rate, jumped to 79 basis points for six-month paper and 99 basis points for the one-year note, levels last seen in November 2022. That spread is the market's real-time verdict on where policy is heading: when the premium widens this fast, bond desks are saying they expect the repo rate to rise towards, and possibly through, the bill yields now on offer.

The trigger is the RBI's changed posture. The central bank hiked rates for the first time in nearly four years last week and shifted its stance, signalling more increases. Governor Sanjay Malhotra added fuel on Tuesday, saying monetary policy cannot be seen as separate from financial stability — a comment traders read as linking the rate path to the same defensive campaign the RBI is running in the currency market.

"A clearly hawkish policy guidance and latest commentary has raised terminal rate expectations on the back of resilient growth and higher inflation," said VRC Reddy, treasury head at Karur Vysya Bank. In plain terms: the market now believes the RBI will keep hiking until growth or inflation breaks, and it is not yet clear which will break first.

The curve is telling the same story from a different angle. India's bond yield curve is flattening: the one-year rate has risen 31 basis points in the last two weeks, while the five- and ten-year yields have risen 26 and 20 basis points over the same period. Short rates rising faster than long rates is the signature of an expected tightening cycle — markets front-load policy expectations into the front end first.

How far could it go? Mandar Pitale, head of treasury at SBM Bank (India), said he sees another 50 basis points of rate hikes this fiscal year, and warned that if risks from higher oil prices materialise, persistently elevated inflation could compel the RBI to keep hiking even in FY28. Bond yields across the three tenors could converge at the peak of the tightening cycle, he added — meaning the curve would flatten all the way to inversion territory before the cycle ends.

For borrowers, the transmission is immediate and uneven. The government is the most visible casualty — Wednesday's auction locks in higher costs for its own borrowing programme — but the pain travels quickly to corporates rolling over commercial paper, banks funding through certificates of deposit, and mutual funds holding duration in liquid schemes. Every 20 basis points at the front end eventually surfaces as higher working-capital costs across the economy.

For the RBI, the auction is a double-edged signal. A market that prices tightening ahead of policy is doing the central bank's work for it — financial conditions are tightening without a single additional hike. But a four-year-high premium also means the market has committed to a path; if the RBI under-delivers against those expectations, the reversal in yields could be just as violent, unwinding the tightening financial conditions the governor has been carefully constructing.

Wednesday's auction numbers, in that sense, are less about treasury bills than about credibility. The market has staked a position: several more hikes are coming. The RBI's next few statements will either confirm that bet — or force a costly repricing in the most rate-sensitive corner of the world's fifth-largest economy.

The timing is no accident. Wednesday morning's wholesale price data — 11.39 per cent inflation in September, a full point above consensus — landed hours before the auction, and bidders priced the two together. A central bank fighting imported energy inflation with rate hikes while the currency war consumes its other toolkit has left the bond market as the only honest price-maker in the room.

There is a silver lining for the RBI's other battle. Higher short-term yields widen India's rate differential with developed markets, improving the carry on rupee assets for foreign investors — one reason the currency stabilised even as FIIs kept selling equities into Tuesday's rally. The auction, in effect, is doing quiet work for the forex market even as it raises the government's borrowing costs.

But the government's own finances feel the squeeze first. Weekly treasury bill auctions fund the state's day-to-day cash management, and a sustained 20-basis-point step-up at the front end compounds quickly across the year's borrowing programme. Every auction from now until the Budget will be read not just as a funding event, but as a referendum on how much tightening the economy can absorb before growth starts paying the bill.

GLOSSARY: - Treasury bill: Short-term government debt sold at auction, maturing in under a year. - Repo rate: The RBI's benchmark lending rate, currently 5.5%. - Cutoff yield: The highest yield accepted at a bond auction, setting the rate for the entire issue. - Yield curve: The relationship between interest rates and maturity dates across government bonds. - Flattening curve: When short-term yields rise faster than long-term yields, often signalling expected rate hikes.
