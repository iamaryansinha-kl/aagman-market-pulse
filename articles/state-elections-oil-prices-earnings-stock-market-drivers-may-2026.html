State Elections, Oil Prices, Q4 Earnings to Drive Markets This Week

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Vote counting for five state assembly elections begins Monday, May 4, with West Bengal outcome seen as the most market-moving result
  • 02/Brent crude oil surged toward USD 120 per barrel recently and remains elevated amid US-Iran tensions over the Strait of Hormuz
  • 03/Q4 earnings season continues with Ambuja Cements, BHEL, Hero MotoCorp, Mahindra & Mahindra, and Bajaj Auto reporting this week

Stock markets are likely to be guided this week by the outcome of state elections and high crude oil prices amid the West Asia conflict, say analysts.

Counting of votes for the five assembly elections in West Bengal, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Assam, and Puducherry will begin on Monday, May 4.

"The most immediate catalyst will be the outcome of key state elections, with investors closely watching whether the ruling party at the Centre can wrest West Bengal from Trinamool Congress," Hariprasad K, Research Analyst and Founder, Livelong Wealth, said.

Crude oil remains the single most critical macro variable. Brent prices are sustaining elevated levels amid ongoing tensions around the Strait of Hormuz.

"For an import-dependent economy like India, persistently high crude prices exert pressure on the rupee—currently near record lows—and weigh on corporate margins as well as fiscal dynamics," Hariprasad added.

"Crude oil price trends will remain the primary external variable, with the ongoing US–Iran standoff and closure of the Strait of Hormuz likely to keep volatility elevated," Ajit Mishra, SVP, Research, Religare Broking Ltd, said.

Key macroeconomic data releases include the HSBC Manufacturing PMI (May 4), Services and Composite PMI (May 6), and foreign exchange reserves data (May 8).

Major corporate earnings announcements this week are from Ambuja Cements, BHEL, Hero MotoCorp, Mahindra & Mahindra and Bajaj Auto.

Santosh Meena, Head of Research at Swastika Investmart Ltd, said, "This week, markets are expected to initially react to the outcome of state elections, particularly in West Bengal, which could trigger 1-2 days of volatility."

In a holiday-shortened last week, the BSE benchmark Sensex climbed 249.29 points or 0.32 per cent, and the NSE Nifty went up by 99.6 points or 0.41 per cent.
