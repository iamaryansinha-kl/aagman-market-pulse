Polycab To Exit NSE F&O From December 30 After Insolvency Order Shock

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/NSE will exclude Polycab India from the equity derivatives segment from December 30; the stock fell over 5% to Rs 7,955.
  • 02/The trigger was an October 7 NCLT order admitting insolvency proceedings over an alleged Rs 2.79 crore operational debt — though NCLAT has stayed the order.
  • 03/Existing October, November and December contracts continue, a 100% margin kicks in from October 14, and IBC-style surveillance begins January 1, 2027.

Polycab India shares tumbled more than 5 per cent on Tuesday morning. The National Stock Exchange announced the cables-and-wires maker will be excluded from its equity derivatives segment from December 30.

The stock dropped to Rs 7,955 apiece on the news. For a company with a market capitalisation of around Rs 1.23 lakh crore, the derivatives exit is a serious market-structure event.

NSE laid out the mechanics. No contracts for new expiry months will be issued after the expiry of existing contract months.

Existing contracts expiring in October, November and December will continue to trade as usual. NSE will also introduce new strikes in these contract months before the discontinuation.

Margin requirements jump immediately. A 100 per cent margin will be levied on all existing derivative contracts from October 14, covering both open positions as of October 13 and new positions.

After the last contracts expire, stricter surveillance under the insolvency framework kicks in. Price bands and gross settlement rules will apply from January 1, 2027.

The reason for the exclusion is an insolvency proceeding. On October 7, the National Company Law Tribunal admitted a corporate insolvency resolution process against Polycab.

The petition was filed by Asier Metals Private Limited under Section 9 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code. The alleged operational debt is just Rs 2.79 crore.

That is a striking mismatch — a Rs 1.23 lakh crore company facing derivatives expulsion over a sub-Rs 3 crore claim. Polycab, however, has informed exchanges that the NCLAT has stayed the NCLT order and kept it in abeyance.

The appellate stay is the company's central defence. But under NSE's framework, admission into the IBC framework itself triggers restrictive trading categories, regardless of the debt size or the stay.

Stocks are excluded from the F&O segment when they fail Sebi's criteria or when they are admitted to insolvency proceedings. Once under the IBC framework, a stock moves to restrictive trading and becomes ineligible for derivatives.

The market has been punishing Polycab for weeks. The shares have fallen around 17 per cent in three months, hit by UltraTech Cement's entry into the cables and wires segment and now the insolvency shock.

Over one month the stock is down about 2 per cent. Longer-term holders have still done well — shares are up around 6 per cent in a year, more than 52 per cent in three years and nearly 220 per cent in five years.

Brokerages have stuck with the story. Jefferies has previously flagged upside even through the recent selloff, arguing the competitive threat from new entrants is manageable.

The longer arc depends on the courts and the market.

Polycab is India's largest wires and cables manufacturer, a position built over decades before the company expanded into consumer electricals and EPC projects. Its size makes the derivatives expulsion all the more unusual.

The competitive backdrop has toughened. UltraTech Cement's entry into the cables and wires segment triggered a sharp derating, with investors pricing in a long price war in the category Polycab dominates.

The 100 per cent margin effectively makes leveraged positions uneconomic, discouraging fresh speculation while existing expiries run off. Futures volumes typically compress quickly even before the formal exit date.

The episode also tests how exchanges apply IBC-linked rules. The framework is mechanical: admission to CIRP, not the final legal outcome, drives the exclusion — leaving large companies exposed to small-ticket petitions.

The F&O exit changes the trading dynamics regardless of fundamentals. Arbitrage and hedging flows that keep cash-futures prices aligned will unwind by December 30, typically adding volatility to the underlying.

The resolution path now runs through the courts. If NCLAT's stay holds and the insolvency petition is dismissed, Polycab could in principle seek re-entry into the derivatives segment, though that process has its own timelines.

For shareholders, the episode is a reminder that market-structure risk can arrive from unexpected directions. A disputed Rs 2.79 crore claim has, for now, cost the stock its derivatives status and about 5 per cent of its market value on the day.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHY IS POLYCAB BEING REMOVED FROM THE F&O SEGMENT?
NSE ANNOUNCED EXCLUSION FROM DECEMBER 30 AFTER AN NCLT ORDER ADMITTED INSOLVENCY PROCEEDINGS AGAINST THE COMPANY. STOCKS ADMITTED TO THE IBC FRAMEWORK MOVE TO RESTRICTIVE TRADING CATEGORIES, MAKING THEM INELIGIBLE FOR DERIVATIVES.
WHAT TRIGGERED THE INSOLVENCY PROCEEDING?
A SECTION 9 PETITION BY ASIER METALS PRIVATE LIMITED OVER AN ALLEGED OPERATIONAL DEBT OF RS 2.79 CRORE. THE NCLT ADMITTED THE CORPORATE INSOLVENCY RESOLUTION PROCESS ON OCTOBER 7.
HAS THE ORDER BEEN STAYED?
YES. POLYCAB HAS INFORMED EXCHANGES THAT THE NCLAT HAS STAYED AND KEPT IN ABEYANCE THE OCTOBER 7 NCLT ORDER.
WHAT HAPPENS TO EXISTING F&O CONTRACTS?
CONTRACTS EXPIRING IN OCTOBER, NOVEMBER AND DECEMBER CONTINUE TO TRADE, WITH NEW STRIKES ADDED. A 100 PER CENT MARGIN APPLIES FROM OCTOBER 14. FROM JANUARY 1, 2027, IBC-FRAMEWORK SURVEILLANCE SUCH AS PRICE BANDS APPLIES.
HOW HAS THE STOCK PERFORMED?
SHARES FELL OVER 5 PER CENT TO RS 7,955 ON TUESDAY. THE STOCK IS DOWN ABOUT 17 PER CENT IN THREE MONTHS BUT UP ROUGHLY 6 PER CENT IN A YEAR, WITH A MARKET CAPITALISATION OF ABOUT RS 1.23 LAKH CRORE. GLOSSARY: F&O segment: The futures and options segment of an exchange where derivatives on stocks and indices trade. CIRP: Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process — the insolvency proceedings under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code. Section 9 petition: An insolvency application filed by an operational creditor, such as a supplier, for unpaid dues. Operational debt: A claim arising from goods or services supplied, as opposed to financial borrowing. NCLAT: National Company Law Appellate Tribunal — the appeals body above the NCLT that has stayed the Polycab order. Gross settlement: A surveillance mechanism under the IBC framework where trades settle trade-by-trade rather than netted.
