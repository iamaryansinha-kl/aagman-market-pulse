India Food Grains Target 450 Million Tonnes by 2047: FAI Director General

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Fertiliser Association of India DG SK Chaudhari says India must produce over 400 million tonnes of food grain by 2030 and close to 450 million tonnes by 2047
  • 02/He called intelligent technology use in agriculture and the fertiliser sector a national imperative, not an option
  • 03/Blockchain and AI are highlighted as key digital tools to transform fertiliser logistics, governance, and agricultural productivity

India must increase food grains production to 450 million tonnes by 2047. Fertiliser Association of India Director General SK Chaudhari announced the target on Wednesday in Shimla.

Speaking at the inauguration of FAI's four-day national training programme, Chaudhari set an interim milestone. India will need to produce more than 400 million tonnes of food grain by 2030.

The programme on ICT for smart fertiliser management is being held in Kufri, Shimla. It runs for four days and covers the full spectrum of digital applications in agriculture.

Chaudhari stressed that making intelligent use of technology in agriculture was no longer optional. He called it a national priority given the scale of production required.

He said the industry's target should be the plant root, not the farmer. Delivering the right nutrient, in the right quantity, at the right location and time opens the door to precision nutrition and sensor-based delivery.

India's deep agrarian knowledge base dating back to the Vedic period positions it uniquely. Natural farming, organic farming, conservation agriculture and regenerative agriculture are the frameworks.

Blockchain technology can reshape fertiliser sector logistics and governance. It enables traceability and transparency from port-of-entry to the farmer's farm gate.

ICT applications span production planning, risk management, supply chain optimisation, remote sensing, GIS-based soil mapping, satellite imagery and AI-driven advisory systems. These tools drive gains in energy efficiency, policy compliance, and agricultural productivity.
