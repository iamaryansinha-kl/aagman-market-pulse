NHB May Double Refinance Funds For Large HFCs This Fiscal

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/National Housing Bank is likely to roughly double refinance and affordable-housing funding available to large housing finance companies this fiscal year
  • 02/Regular NHB refinance costs about 7.65% and the affordable-housing window about 4.25%, both cheaper than comparable bank funding
  • 03/NHB's share of borrowing by some HFCs could rise to 30-35% this year from 20-25% last year; LIC Housing, Bajaj Housing, PNB Housing among likely beneficiaries

National Housing Bank is likely to roughly double the refinance and affordable-housing funds available to large housing finance companies this fiscal year. The plan follows multiple company requests for cheaper long-term funding, people familiar with the discussions said.

The likely beneficiaries include LIC Housing Finance, Bajaj Housing Finance, PNB Housing Finance, Aadhar Housing Finance and TruHome Finance. These are among the largest non-bank lenders in the home-loan segment.

Housing finance companies approached NHB and the finance ministry seeking more funding. Their stated aim was to diversify borrowings and reduce reliance on banks for growth capital.

The constraint is structural. Banks operate internal exposure limits for lending to NBFCs and HFCs. Once a bank hits its limit, fresh loans become difficult even when home-loan demand remains strong.

That is where NHB pricing becomes attractive. The rate on regular NHB refinance is about 7.65%. The affordable-housing fund window is priced near 4.25%.

Both rates are cheaper than comparable bank funding for most HFCs. For lenders managing tight net interest margins, the gap is meaningful at scale.

One official estimate suggests NHB could meet 30-35% of the borrowing raised by some HFCs this year. That compares with 20-25% of their borrowing in the last fiscal.

The doubling would mark a step-change in NHB's role as a funding backstop. The institution disbursed Rs 32,308 crore in refinance in the one-year period ended June 2025.

That was marginally higher than the Rs 32,085 crore disbursed in the preceding year. Disbursements from the affordable-housing fund, at Rs 5,791 crore, were included in that overall figure.

The demand backdrop supports the case for a bigger pool. RBI's June Financial Stability Report shows HFCs had gross payables of Rs 7.35 lakh crore to the financial system in March 2026.

Their gross receivables stood at just Rs 0.19 lakh crore in the same month. The mismatch underlines how dependent HFCs remain on wholesale and bank funding.

NHB data shows HFCs held Rs 7.44 lakh crore in outstanding individual housing loans at end-July 2026. That is 18.6% of the Rs 40.07 lakh-crore home-loan market spanning HFCs, public and private banks, and regional rural banks.

The HFC share has edged up from 18.3% a year earlier. Cheaper refinance could help them defend and extend that share against bank competition.

There is an additional pricing lever for priority segments. Targeted funding for areas like green housing and homes in underserved regions can run about 30 basis points cheaper than regular NHB refinance.

NHB is a state-owned development financial institution with an AAA rating. It provides refinance against eligible housing loans and works with development finance agencies on affordable and green housing programmes.

For HFCs squeezed by bank exposure limits, the plan offers a growth lever that does not strain existing banking relationships. What to watch: the final size of the doubled pool, disbursement run-rates through the year, and which of the named lenders draw the most.

GLOSSARY: Refinance: Fresh funding provided by a specialised institution such as NHB against a lender's existing eligible loans, replacing or supplementing original funding at a lower cost. HFC (Housing Finance Company): A non-bank financial company whose primary business is providing housing loans, funded through borrowings rather than deposits. Affordable Housing Fund: A dedicated low-cost funding window, priced at about 4.25% at NHB, aimed at home loans in qualifying affordable segments. Development Financial Institution: A state-owned institution, such as NHB, created to provide long-term funding for priority sectors; NHB carries an AAA rating. Exposure Limit: An internal ceiling a bank sets on lending to a borrower group or sector, such as NBFCs and HFCs, which blocks fresh loans once reached.
