India External Debt Rises to $778.2 Billion in June Quarter: RBI

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/India's external debt rose $15.4 billion QoQ to $778.2 billion at end-June 2026, per RBI data released September 30, 2026
  • 02/Debt-to-GDP ratio moderated to 20.8% from 20.9%; long-term debt rose $11.2 billion to $624.7 billion
  • 03/Watch item: short-term debt-to-forex-reserves ratio climbed to 23.0% from 21.6%, though debt service held at 5.6% of current receipts

India's external debt stood at $778.2 billion at end-June 2026. The Reserve Bank of India released the quarterly data on September 30, 2026.

The debt stock rose by $15.4 billion from end-March 2026. Excluding valuation effects, the increase would have been $16.4 billion.

Valuation gains of $0.9 billion masked part of the rise. These gains came from US dollar appreciation against the yen and the euro.

External debt refers to money owed to non-residents, repayable in foreign currency, goods or services. It includes government borrowing, corporate loans, trade credit and deposits.

A rising debt stock is not automatically a stress signal. It partly reflects borrowing to fund trade, investment and normal balance-of-payments flows.

The key comfort metric moved the other way. The external debt-to-GDP ratio moderated to 20.8% at end-June 2026, from 20.9% at end-March 2026.

That moderation suggests the debt stock grew slower than the economy. It keeps India well below the vulnerability thresholds that rating agencies typically flag.

By maturity, long-term debt — original maturity above one year — rose $11.2 billion to $624.7 billion. It forms the bulk of the total stock.

Short-term debt, with maturity up to one year, edged up to 19.7% of total external debt. It stood at 19.6% in the preceding quarter.

Short-term debt matters more than its size suggests. It must be rolled over or repaid within a year, making it sensitive to global funding conditions.

The short-term debt-to-forex-reserves ratio rose to 23.0% at end-June 2026, from 21.6% at end-March 2026. That is the one mild watch item in this release.

Even at 23.0%, reserves cover short-term debt more than four times over. Analysts generally treat sustained readings above 100% as a stress threshold.

Debt service — principal repayments plus interest — stood at 5.6% of current receipts. That ratio is unchanged from end-March 2026.

On currency composition, US dollar-denominated debt remains the largest at 54.8%. It is followed by the Indian rupee at 29.8%, the yen at 6.9%, SDR at 4.1% and the euro at 3.5%.

The 29.8% rupee share limits valuation swings from dollar moves. However, dollar obligations still form more than half the stock.

By instrument, loans form the largest share at 34.3%. Currency and deposits account for 22.2%, trade credit and advances 19.1%, and debt securities 16.5%.

Outstanding debt of both government and non-government sectors increased during the quarter. The RBI publishes this data quarterly, with a one-quarter lag.

Overall, the June-quarter numbers point to a stable external position. A moderating debt-to-GDP ratio and steady debt service ratio offset the small uptick in short-term obligations.

On the sovereign side, the government's outstanding external debt increased during the quarter. The non-government sector's debt also rose.

Both components moving up together points to broad-based external borrowing. It is not concentrated in one part of the economy.

The debt service ratio measures the burden of repayments against the money flowing in from trade and transfers. At 5.6% of current receipts, it is low by historical standards.

Valuation effects cut both ways across quarters. If the dollar weakens against the yen and euro, the same debt stock would show a valuation increase.

The next data point arrives with the RBI's end-September 2026 release. Watchers will track whether the short-term debt-to-reserves ratio stabilizes near 23%.
