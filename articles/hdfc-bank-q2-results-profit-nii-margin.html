HDFC Bank Q2 Profit Rises 5% to Rs 19,510 Crore, NII Up 10%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Net profit rose 4.6% YoY to Rs 19,510 crore from Rs 18,641 crore.
  • •- Net interest income grew 9.7% to Rs 34,620 crore; NIM was 3.16% on total assets.
  • •- Gross NPAs eased to 1.11% from 1.17%; net NPAs fell to 0.37%.
  • •- Average deposits grew 16.8% to Rs 31.66 lakh crore; CASA rose 10.7%.
  • •- Advances under management grew 14.1% YoY to Rs 31.87 lakh crore.
  • •- The balance sheet reached Rs 45.78 lakh crore from Rs 40.03 lakh crore a year earlier.

India's largest private lender has reported the kind of quarter that reads modest until you see the scale it was achieved at. HDFC Bank on Saturday posted net profit of Rs 19,510 crore for Q2FY27, up 4.6 per cent from Rs 18,641 crore a year earlier, with net interest income growing 9.7 per cent to Rs 34,620 crore. The numbers confirm the post-merger normalisation narrative: growth is steady rather than spectacular, margins are stabilising rather than recovering, and the franchise's sheer size — a balance sheet of Rs 45.78 lakh crore, up from Rs 40.03 lakh crore a year ago — makes percentage optics secondary to absolute trajectory.

The margin line remains the market's fixation. Net interest margin came in at 3.16 per cent on total assets and 3.32 per cent on interest-earning assets — thinner than ICICI's 4.18 per cent or Axis's 3.36 per cent, and the lasting legacy of the HDFC Ltd merger, which imported a large, low-yielding mortgage book into the banking balance sheet. The question investors have asked for two years is whether that margin grinds back toward 3.5 per cent as the loan mix normalises; this quarter's answer is incremental rather than decisive. What the bank can show is deposit momentum: average deposits grew 16.8 per cent year-on-year to Rs 31.66 lakh crore, and sequentially by 5.1 per cent — a pace that, if sustained, resolves the post-merger funding concern that had shadowed the stock.

Asset quality, the other post-merger watch item, improved again. Gross NPAs eased to 1.11 per cent of advances from 1.17 per cent in June — in absolute terms Rs 35,810 crore, marginally below the previous quarter's Rs 35,846 crore — while net NPAs fell to 0.37 per cent from 0.41 per cent, or Rs 11,851 crore from Rs 12,357 crore. The mortgage-heavy book behaves differently from a corporate book: defaults arrive slowly and recoveries are high, which is why the ratio glide matters more than any single quarter's slippage print. On that glide, HDFC Bank remains on the right side of the line.

Advances under management grew 14.1 per cent year-on-year and 4.9 per cent sequentially to Rs 31.87 lakh crore, including inter-bank participation certificates, bills rediscounted and securitisation — the bank's preferred measure of true lending momentum. That is a respectable pace for the industry's largest book, though it underscores the divergence now visible across the private-bank league: Axis is growing at 23 per cent, ICICI at 30 per cent, and HDFC Bank at 14. The strategic read is that HDFC Bank is optimising for margin-quality and deposit depth rather than share — a rational choice for a franchise whose mortgage book reprices slowly, but one that keeps the stock's valuation tethered to the recovery story rather than the growth one.

The day's three results together map the sector precisely. ICICI is the risk-adjusted compounder — 20 per cent profit growth on fortress buffers. Axis is the accelerant — 38 per cent profit growth on 23 per cent advances and the biggest FCNR haul in the system. HDFC Bank is the ballast — a Rs 45.78 lakh crore balance sheet grinding out single-digit profit growth while its funding and asset-quality mechanics quietly repair. For an index where HDFC Bank remains among the heaviest weights, the quarter does what it needed to do: no surprises on the downside, continued evidence that the merger digestion is progressing, and a deposit trajectory that finally looks worthy of the franchise. The re-rating case — whenever margins inflect — remains intact, and the market will now look to the December quarter for the first real evidence of it.

Worth noting is what did not move: there was no repeat of the treasury-driven shock that hit some PSU banks this season, and no evidence that the bond market's nine-week slide has impaired the franchise. HDFC Bank holds one of the largest government-securities books in the system by absolute size, and the mortgage book's slow repricing means the margin question and the treasury question are, for this bank, the same question. The quarter's modest 4.6 per cent profit growth should be read with that exposure in mind — steadiness in this rate environment is itself an achievement, and the absence of negative surprises is what keeps the recovery narrative fundable.

For depositors, borrowers and the broader system, the three-way result describes a healthy competitive geometry: one bank chasing share at speed, one compounding quietly at the top of the risk curve, and the largest grinding through a structural repair while still funding a fifth of the system's credit. The RBI, which spent the week draining liquidity and defending the currency, will read the triptych the same way the market does — private-sector balance sheets strong enough to keep credit flowing are the one tailwind the macro battle does not have to manufacture.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT WERE HDFC BANK'S Q2 HEADLINES?
Net profit rose 4.6% to Rs 19,510 crore, NII grew 9.7% to Rs 34,620 crore, and the balance sheet reached Rs 45.78 lakh crore.
HOW DID MARGINS LOOK?
NIM was 3.16% on total assets and 3.32% on interest-earning assets, still reflecting the low-yielding mortgage book inherited in the HDFC Ltd merger.
WHAT HAPPENED TO ASSET QUALITY?
Gross NPAs eased to 1.11% from 1.17% and net NPAs to 0.37% from 0.41%, with gross NPAs at Rs 35,810 crore.
HOW FAST ARE DEPOSITS GROWING?
Average deposits rose 16.8% YoY to Rs 31.66 lakh crore, with CASA deposits up 10.7% to Rs 9.71 lakh crore. GLOSSARY: - NIM on total assets: Net interest income as a share of the entire balance sheet — a stricter measure than interest-earning-asset NIM. - Advances under management: Total loans including those routed through participation certificates and securitisation — the bank's preferred lending gauge. - CASA: Current and savings account deposits — the cheapest, stickiest funding a bank holds. - Post-merger normalisation: The multi-year process of a bank's ratios settling after absorbing a large parent company's balance sheet.
