IREF Cuts Basmati Freight To Iraq From $350 To $90 Per Tonne

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/IREF has launched a bulk-shipping initiative with Singapore-based Eximwala Solutions to cut Basmati freight to Iraq from USD 350 to USD 90 per tonne, a reduction of nearly 74%
  • 02/The plan covers bulk shipments of 1,02,210 metric tonnes of Basmati rice to Iraq across three vessels, generating gross freight savings of roughly USD 26.6 million
  • 03/Iraq is a Rs 5,583 crore market for Indian Basmati; IREF plans to develop similar freight solutions for other Gulf destinations

The Indian Rice Exporters' Federation (IREF) has launched a bulk-shipping initiative targeting a sharp cut in freight costs for Basmati rice exports to Iraq. The goal: bring the cost down from USD 350 to USD 90 per tonne.

That works out to a reduction of nearly 74% on the Iraq route. The estimated saving is USD 260 per tonne.

The initiative is being executed in collaboration with Singapore-based Eximwala Solutions Pte. Ltd. It covers bulk shipments of 1,02,210 metric tonnes of Basmati rice to Iraq, moved through three vessels.

Across the full three-vessel volume, gross freight savings are estimated at roughly USD 26.6 million. That is a direct margin benefit for exporters and, potentially, for overseas buyers.

The timing matters. Rising freight costs, container shortages and geopolitical disruptions have been squeezing India's rice exports to the Middle East. Freight has become the single biggest competitiveness lever in the region.

The stakes are large. The Middle East accounts for nearly 70% of India's total Basmati export volume. In FY 2025-26, India exported 45.64 lakh metric tonnes of Basmati rice worth Rs 34,284 crore to the region.

Including non-Basmati rice, total rice exports to the Middle East stood at 56.48 lakh tonnes valued at Rs 39,657 crore in FY26. Saudi Arabia, Iran, Iraq and the UAE are the largest individual destinations.

Iraq alone imported about 7.64 lakh tonnes of Indian Basmati in FY26, worth Rs 5,583 crore. For context, Saudi Arabia accounted for Rs 8,324 crore, Iran Rs 6,970 crore and the UAE Rs 4,132 crore.

"The Middle East accounts for nearly 70% of India's Basmati exports, and rising shipping costs directly affect our competitiveness. By targeting a reduction in freight from US$350 to US$90 per tonne, we are demonstrating how better logistics can benefit both Indian exporters and overseas buyers." - Dev Garg, National Vice President, IREF

Garg added that the objective is to develop similar solutions for other Gulf destinations. The aim is to strengthen exports and improve returns across the value chain.

The initiative is part of IREF's broader "One Nation, One Trade, One Policy" vision. It also lands deliberately ahead of the Bharat International Rice Conference (BIRC) 2026, scheduled for October 23-25 at Bharat Mandapam, New Delhi.

BIRC 2026 will feature a dedicated session on shipping costs, bulk and container logistics, cargo insurance, port delays and maritime disputes. Those are precisely the friction points this Iraq pilot is designed to address.

The initiative addresses what exporters say has become their single biggest competitive problem. Container freight quotations for Iraq have climbed to roughly $350 per metric tonne amid container shortages and geopolitical disruption, a burden that lands directly on the landed price of Indian rice in Gulf markets. By switching to bulk vessels — which carry rice unpackaged rather than in containers — the federation and its Singapore-based partner Eximwala Solutions are betting that scale and dedicated tonnage can reset the economics.

The stakes are visible in the destination split. Saudi Arabia remained the largest individual buyer of Indian Basmati in FY26 at Rs 8,324 crore, followed by Iran at Rs 6,970 crore, Iraq at Rs 5,583 crore and the UAE at Rs 4,132 crore. Iraq alone absorbed about 7.64 lakh tonnes of Indian Basmati — volume that was increasingly priced out of contention as freight quotes tripled from pre-disruption levels.

For the wider trade, the timing is deliberate. The Bharat International Rice Conference 2026 convenes at Bharat Mandapam in New Delhi from October 23 to 25, with a dedicated session on shipping costs, bulk and container logistics, cargo insurance, port delays and maritime disputes. The federation is effectively road-testing its bulk-shipping answer weeks before the industry gathers to debate the problem.

The ambition does not stop at Iraq. The initiative is framed under IREF's "One Nation, One Trade, One Policy" umbrella, and the federation says it intends to replicate commercially viable shipping solutions across other Gulf destinations. If the three-vessel pilot performs, the model could become a template for how Indian commodity exporters respond to a world of expensive containers and contested sea lanes.
