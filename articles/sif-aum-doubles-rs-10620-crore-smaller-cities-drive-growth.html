SIF AUM Doubles to Rs 10,620 Crore as Smaller Cities Drive 50% of New Inflows

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/SIF assets under management more than doubled to Rs 10,620 crore by March 2026, up from Rs 4,892 crore in December 2025.
  • 02/Investors from beyond India's top 30 cities account for 30% of SIF assets and 50% of participation, outperforming regular mutual funds' 20% tier-2 penetration.
  • 03/Fund houses target Rs 1 lakh crore AUM within three years, but distribution bottlenecks persist with only 6,000 NISM-certified distributors.

Specialised Investment Funds (SIF), SEBI's newest regulated category for sophisticated investors, has crossed Rs 10,620 crore in assets under management by March 2026. The figure represents a 117% jump from Rs 4,892 crore just three months prior in December 2025.

The category, operational since April 1, 2025, is attracting significant interest from beyond India's metropolitan centres. Madhu Nair, Chief Executive Officer at Union Mutual Fund, confirmed that 50% of participation comes from smaller cities. These tier-2 and tier-3 locations now hold 30% of total SIF assets, compared to just 20% in conventional mutual fund schemes.

SBI Mutual Fund's Magnum SIF leads the pack with Rs 2,994 crore in AUM as of March 2026, surging from Rs 1,181 crore in the December quarter. The fund has accumulated 10,152 investor folios, the highest in the category.

Edelweiss Mutual Fund's Altiva SIF ranks second at Rs 2,366 crore, up sharply from Rs 508 crore in December. ICICI Prudential's iSIF, the latest major entrant, has already gathered Rs 1,036 crore despite launching in early 2026.

Other notable players include Quant MF's qSIF at Rs 867 crore, Tata MF's Titanium SIF at Rs 404 crore, and ITI MF's Diviniti SIF at Rs 355 crore. The total number of SIF offerings doubled to 14 schemes quarter-on-quarter as of March-end, per AMFI data.

Hybrid SIF strategies dominate the landscape, commanding 76.1% of total category assets at approximately Rs 7,389 crore. These funds combine equity, debt, and limited derivatives exposure, offering a familiar structure for high-net-worth investors migrating from balanced advantage funds.

February 2026 set a monthly inflow record at Rs 3,127 crore, marking an 80% increase from January's Rs 1,729 crore. This acceleration coincided with broader market volatility, suggesting investors sought downside-protected strategies.

The minimum investment threshold stands at Rs 10 lakh per investor across all SIF strategies from a single fund house. This barrier, while restrictive, enables access to sophisticated strategies including long-short positions using derivatives, previously available only through Portfolio Management Services requiring Rs 50 lakh minimums.

Anand Vardarajan, Chief Business Officer at Tata Mutual Fund, stated the category addresses three key pain points: taxation efficiency, accessible ticket size, and talent deployment. "At Rs 10 lakh, investors receive a superior product capable of participating across bull, bear, or sideways markets," he noted.

Distribution remains the primary constraint. Only 6,000 distributors have cleared NISM's 150-mark certification examination covering derivatives, trading strategies, and risk management. The test includes negative marking and currency derivatives sections that industry bodies want removed.

Average ticket sizes vary significantly across fund houses. Bandhan Mutual Fund reports Rs 78.4 lakh per investor, indicating ultra-HNI concentration. Quant Mutual Fund shows a more democratised Rs 12.5 lakh average, closer to the regulatory minimum.

Union Mutual Fund's Nair projects the category will reach Rs 1 lakh crore AUM within three years if current momentum persists. This would represent a near-10x expansion from current levels.

Performance data presents a mixed picture. All five live equity-oriented SIF strategies traded below their issue prices as of late March 2026, according to INDmoney analysis. The two Ex-Top-100 funds, concentrating on mid and small-cap stocks ranked below the top 100 by market capitalisation, have been hardest hit by recent corrections.
