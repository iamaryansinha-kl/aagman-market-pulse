Union Bank Q2 Profit Up 30% to Rs 5,537 Crore as Provisions Halve

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Net profit rose 30% YoY to Rs 5,537 crore from Rs 4,249 crore.
  • •- Net interest income grew 20% to Rs 10,582 crore; NIM improved to 2.9% from 2.7%.
  • •- Provisions nearly halved to Rs 701 crore from Rs 1,397 crore a year ago.
  • •- Total advances grew 19% to Rs 11.6 lakh crore; corporate advances surged 24% to Rs 5.2 lakh crore.
  • •- The bank holds a Rs 1 lakh crore corporate loan pipeline and raised $2.3 billion via FCNR(B) deposits.
  • •- Net NPAs improved to 0.4% from 0.6%; provision coverage stays above 95%.

Union Bank of India delivered on Monday the kind of quarter that explains why PSU bank stocks have been the trade of the earnings season. Net profit for the July-September period rose 30 per cent year-on-year to Rs 5,537 crore, from Rs 4,249 crore a year ago, powered by a 20 per cent rise in net interest income to Rs 10,582 crore and a near-halving of provisions to Rs 701 crore from Rs 1,397 crore. Three engines fired at once — margins up, credit costs down, balance sheet growing — and the result is a continuation of the PSU bank renaissance that this week's PNB numbers had already sketched. It is also the cleanest demonstration yet of the rate-cycle arithmetic now running in PSU banks' favour: deposit-heavy funding costs reprice with a lag, loan yields reprice immediately, and every quarter of that asymmetry drops straight into the margin line. For a lender that spent years in the market's penalty box over legacy bad loans, the transformation in how its quarterly prints are received — from suspicion to rerating — is as valuable as the profit growth itself.

The margin line is the one chief executive Asheesh Pandey wants investors to watch. Net interest margin improved to 2.9 per cent from 2.7 per cent a year ago, and Pandey has set a public target of pushing it beyond 3 per cent by next fiscal year. His arithmetic leans on the rate cycle turning in the bank's favour: "We have started building up from when rates were on the way down. Now that the trajectory has changed and the repo rate has been hiked by 25 basis points, we expect to further improve margins." In a rising-rate environment, a liability-heavy PSU balance sheet with sticky low-cost deposits reprices income faster than cost — the mirror image of the margin squeeze these same banks endured during the easing cycle. Each twenty-five basis points of policy tightening is, for a deposit franchise this size, worth hundreds of crores of annual net interest income before competition claws any of it back.

Growth is broad but corporate-led. Total advances rose 19 per cent year-on-year to Rs 11.6 lakh crore, with domestic advances up 17 per cent and corporate advances up a striking 24 per cent to Rs 5.2 lakh crore. The bank says it is sitting on a corporate loan pipeline of Rs 1 lakh crore — demand from companies that are finally borrowing for capacity after years of deleveraged caution, spanning infrastructure, manufacturing and working-capital needs across the economy. That mix flatters near-term margins, since corporate yields reprice quickest, though it also carries the familiar PSU-bank caveat: the wholesale book is where slippages historically emerge, and this cycle's credit quality will be judged two years from now, not this quarter.

Asset quality, for now, is the cleanest part of the story. Net non-performing assets improved to 0.4 per cent from 0.6 per cent a year ago, the provision coverage ratio stands above 95 per cent, and the bank still chose to add another Rs 200 crore to its provision kitty this quarter. The near-95-plus coverage is the number sophisticated credit investors focus on: it means the visible stock of bad loans is fully provided, so future surprises would have to come from new slippages rather than old skeletons. That combination — low NNPA plus high coverage plus voluntary top-ups — is the balance-sheet equivalent of running up the score, and it is exactly what investors have been paying for in PSU banks: not just recovery, but the credibility that the recovery is fully provisioned and therefore real. The provision buffer also means the near-halving of quarterly provisions to Rs 701 crore has room to keep flattering profit even if credit costs tick up from here.

Two quieter data points round out the quarter and hint at where the next leg of the story could come from. Union Bank collected $2.3 billion through FCNR(B) deposits, of which about 70 per cent came via loans offered to customers — a funding-cost play that lets the bank arbitrage offshore dollar appetite while deepening customer relationships. It is also a signal that NRI and offshore confidence in the bank's paper remains intact, a funding channel several Indian lenders lost during previous stress cycles. And the margin roadmap to 3 per cent, if delivered, would place Union Bank's profitability metrics closer to its private-sector aspirational peers than the PSU pack it has historically anchored. The stock market has already noticed: PSU banks have rallied hard through this results season while private lenders sold off, a rotation that says less about the private franchises' quality than about how far expectations had drifted apart. At 30 per cent profit growth with improving margins and a visible pipeline, Union Bank is giving that rotation its fundamental justification — one quarter at a time.

WHAT WERE UNION BANK'S Q2 NUMBERS?
Net profit rose 30% YoY to Rs 5,537 crore, NII grew 20% to Rs 10,582 crore, and NIM improved to 2.9% from 2.7% a year ago.
HOW DID ASSET QUALITY PERFORM?
Net NPAs improved to 0.4% from 0.6%, provisions nearly halved to Rs 701 crore, and provision coverage remains above 95%.
WHAT IS THE GROWTH PROFILE?
Total advances rose 19% to Rs 11.6 lakh crore, with corporate advances up 24% to Rs 5.2 lakh crore and a Rs 1 lakh crore corporate pipeline.
WHAT IS THE MARGIN OUTLOOK?
The CEO targets NIM above 3% by next fiscal year, helped by the 25 basis-point repo rate hike repricing loan yields faster than deposit costs. GLOSSARY: - Net interest margin (NIM): The difference between what a bank earns on loans and pays on deposits, as a share of assets. - FCNR(B) deposits: Foreign-currency deposits raised from non-resident Indians under a special RBI window. - Provision coverage ratio: The share of bad loans a bank has already set aside money against. - Corporate loan pipeline: Loans sanctioned or in advanced stages of approval but not yet disbursed.
