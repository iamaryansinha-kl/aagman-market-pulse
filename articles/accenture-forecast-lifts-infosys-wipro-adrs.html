Accenture Forecast Lifts Infosys, Wipro ADRs Up To 8% In Premarket

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Accenture forecast full-year revenue growth of 3-6%, ahead of analyst estimates; its shares rose more than 7% in US premarket trade on Thursday.
  • 02/Infosys ADRs gained about 8% and Wipro ADRs rose 3% in US premarket, tracking the positive read-through for Indian IT exporters.
  • 03/Watch the September quarter: brokerages expect one of the weakest quarterly performances for top Indian IT firms in three years.

Accenture shares rose more than 7% in US premarket trading on Thursday. The trigger was a full-year revenue growth forecast of 3-6%, which came in stronger than analyst estimates.

The Dublin-based company reported fourth-quarter revenue of $18.68 billion. That was comfortably ahead of the $18.03 billion that analysts had expected.

The optimism spread quickly to Indian IT majors trading in US markets. Infosys ADRs gained about 8% in premarket trade, while Wipro ADRs rose 3%.

ADRs, or American Depositary Receipts, let US investors trade shares of foreign companies. Moves in Infosys and Wipro ADRs often set the tone for how these stocks open once domestic trading resumes in India.

Accenture's commentary carries weight because it is seen as a gauge of global demand for technology services. A stronger outlook from the company directly improves sentiment for Indian IT exporters such as Infosys, Wipro, TCS, HCLTech and Tech Mahindra.

The reason is simple: these companies depend heavily on spending from clients in the US and Europe. When Accenture signals steady demand, it suggests the budgets that drive Indian IT revenue are holding up.

The forecast pointed to steady demand across consulting and managed services. AI-related work also helped sentiment, coming at a time when investors have worried about slowing discretionary technology spending.

The read-across is not just theoretical. Earlier on Thursday, Indian IT shares advanced even as broader domestic indices fell sharply.

Domestic IT bucked a weak market, helped by softer-than-expected US inflation data. That data reduced expectations of an immediate rate hike, easing one source of pressure on rate-sensitive growth stocks.

Mphasis rose up to 5% in domestic trade. Coforge gained about 5%, and Infosys rose about 3%.

Persistent Systems, TCS, HCL Technologies, Tech Mahindra and Wipro also advanced, rising up to 2% each during the session.

The backdrop makes the Accenture signal more significant. Indian IT stocks have had a difficult year as clients delayed discretionary spending.

Investors have also wrestled with a bigger question: whether AI could reduce the need for traditional software services work. That concern has weighed on the sector through much of the year.

The near-term test is the September quarter. Tech firms are expected to report weak results, with brokerages flagging pressure from AI-led pricing changes and cautious client spending.

According to the report, the top Indian IT firms could see one of their weakest quarterly performances in three years. Those results are still expectations at this stage, not reported numbers.

For now, the Accenture forecast offers a counterpoint to that gloom. It suggests global technology services demand has stabilised at a level better than the market feared.

The balance of risks, however, has not disappeared. Weak September-quarter numbers, AI-led pricing pressure and slow discretionary spending could still cap the upside for Indian IT stocks.

Investors will be watching two things in the coming weeks. The first is September-quarter earnings from the major Indian IT exporters; the second is any fresh commentary on AI-driven pricing and client budgets.
