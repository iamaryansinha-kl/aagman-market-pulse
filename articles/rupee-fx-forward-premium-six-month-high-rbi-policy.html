Rupee FX Forward Premium Jumps to Six-Month High After RBI Policy

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/The implied 1-year dollar/rupee forward premium climbed 20 basis points on Wednesday, October 7, to 3.66%, its highest level in six months
  • 02/RBI announced no CRR hike or fresh liquidity-absorption measure at its October 7 policy, reinforcing trader expectations of more sell/buy swaps to manage the liquidity overhang
  • 03/Spot USD/INR rose 0.28% to 96.70, within striking distance of its all-time high of 96.96, and the rising spot amplified the premium's upward move

India's one-year dollar/rupee forward premium climbed 20 basis points on Wednesday, October 7, 2026. The implied premium reached 3.66%, its highest level in six months, as currency traders reacted to the Reserve Bank of India's monetary policy outcome.

The central bank announced no CRR hike and no fresh liquidity-absorption measure at the October 7 review. Within minutes of the statement, traders began positioning for more sell/buy swaps — the forward market delivered its verdict before the press conference ended.

The premium had already jumped 14 basis points on Tuesday, a move attributed to likely RBI sell/buy swaps in the market. The two-day rise now totals 34 basis points, signalling a decisive shift in how the street expects the RBI to manage surplus rupee liquidity.

The mechanic is straightforward. In a sell/buy swap, the RBI sells dollars to banks in the spot market and agrees to buy them back at a future date. The spot leg drains rupee liquidity immediately; the forward leg adds to future dollar demand and pushes the premium higher.

These swaps have been the RBI's key tool for draining the rupee liquidity created by its dollar mobilisation schemes. Each operation converts surplus rupees into a temporary dollar outflow, sterilising the liquidity impact without touching the CRR.

RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra flagged FX swaps last month as among the options to manage surplus liquidity. At Wednesday's policy, he said the central bank would use an "appropriate" mix of liquidity management tools, reinforcing the swap narrative.

"The moment that the policy had no new liquidity measure in the policy, traders started positioning for more sell/buy swaps," said a currency trader at a private sector bank. The spot market added fuel: USD/INR rose 0.28% to 96.70, within striking distance of its all-time high of 96.96.

A weakening spot amplifies the premium's upward push, as forward points get repriced higher alongside it. For exporters, richer premiums lift rates on forward dollar sales but raise roll-over costs; importers face more expensive cover for payables, lifting hedging bills across corporate India.

The RBI's own arithmetic also changes. Higher premiums increase the central bank's sterilisation cost, and the bill grows with every swap it rolls. Watch the swap calendar from here: stepped-up volumes keep premiums elevated, while a surprise CRR move could pull them back down.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT HAPPENED TO THE RUPEE FORWARD PREMIUM AFTER THE RBI POLICY ON OCTOBER 7, 2026?
THE IMPLIED 1-YEAR DOLLAR/RUPEE FORWARD PREMIUM CLIMBED 20 BASIS POINTS ON WEDNESDAY TO 3.66%, ITS HIGHEST LEVEL IN SIX MONTHS. THE PREMIUM HAD ALREADY JUMPED 14 BASIS POINTS ON TUESDAY AMID LIKELY RBI SELL/BUY SWAPS, TAKING THE TWO-DAY RISE TO 34 BASIS POINTS.
WHY DID THE PREMIUM RISE WHEN THE RBI DID NOT ANNOUNCE ANY NEW LIQUIDITY MEASURE?
THE RBI ANNOUNCED NO CRR HIKE AND NO FRESH LIQUIDITY-ABSORPTION TOOL AT ITS OCTOBER 7 POLICY. TRADERS READ THE SILENCE AS CONFIRMATION THAT THE CENTRAL BANK WILL RELY MORE HEAVILY ON SELL/BUY SWAPS TO MANAGE THE SURPLUS RUPEE LIQUIDITY SITTING IN THE SYSTEM.
HOW DOES AN RBI SELL/BUY SWAP WORK?
IN A SELL/BUY SWAP, THE RBI SELLS DOLLARS TO BANKS IN THE SPOT MARKET AND AGREES TO BUY THEM BACK AT A LATER DATE AT A PRICE SET BY THE FORWARD PREMIUM. THE SPOT SALE DRAINS RUPEE LIQUIDITY IMMEDIATELY, WHILE THE FORWARD LEG ADDS TO FUTURE DOLLAR DEMAND AND PUSHES FORWARD PREMIUMS HIGHER.
WHAT HAS THE RBI SAID ABOUT USING FX SWAPS TO MANAGE LIQUIDITY?
RBI GOVERNOR SANJAY MALHOTRA FLAGGED FX SWAPS LAST MONTH AS AMONG THE TOOLS AVAILABLE TO MANAGE SURPLUS LIQUIDITY. AT WEDNESDAY'S POLICY, HE SAID THE CENTRAL BANK WOULD USE AN "APPROPRIATE" MIX OF LIQUIDITY MANAGEMENT TOOLS, WHICH TRADERS TOOK AS A GREEN LIGHT FOR MORE SWAP OPERATIONS.
WHAT DOES A HIGHER 1-YEAR FORWARD PREMIUM MEAN FOR COMPANIES AND FOR THE RBI?
EXPORTERS GET RICHER RATES ON FORWARD DOLLAR SALES BUT FACE HIGHER ROLL-OVER COSTS, WHILE IMPORTERS PAY MORE FOR FORWARD COVER ON PAYABLES, RAISING HEDGING BILLS ACROSS CORPORATE INDIA. FOR THE RBI, ELEVATED PREMIUMS INCREASE THE STERILISATION BILL AS IT DRAINS LIQUIDITY CREATED BY ITS DOLLAR MOBILISATION SCHEMES. GLOSSARY: Forward Premium: The annualised difference between the forward exchange rate and the spot rate — effectively the interest-rate differential implied by the currency market. A higher premium makes hedging costlier for importers but rewarding for exporters. Sell/Buy Swap: The RBI sells dollars spot and buys them back forward, draining rupee liquidity created by its dollar-raising schemes without permanently spending reserves. Implied Yield: The return embedded in a forward contract derived from spot and forward prices — here, 3.66% annualised for a one-year dollar/rupee forward. Sterilisation: Offsetting the domestic money-supply impact of reserve operations — when the RBI buys or sells dollars, it uses swaps and other tools so bank liquidity stays in its target zone. CRR (Cash Reserve Ratio): The share of deposits banks must park with the RBI — a blunt liquidity tool the central bank chose NOT to use at this policy.
