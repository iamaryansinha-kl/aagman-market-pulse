RBI Minutes: Growth Up to 7.1%, But Inflation Spreads

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- The RBI's October 5-7 MPC minutes show the growth forecast for 2026-27 raised to 7.1% from 6.7% in August.
  • •- All six members, including Governor Sanjay Malhotra, voted to raise the repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.5%.
  • •- It was the first rate hike since February 2023, ending nearly four years of cuts and pauses.
  • •- 37% of items in the CPI basket had inflation above 4% in August, up from 20% in March — a sign of inflation broadening.
  • •- The RBI flagged that the global rate environment is narrowing India's room to keep policy loose.

The Reserve Bank of India's October policy minutes, released on Wednesday, read like a central bank that has stopped hoping oil will behave. Governor Sanjay Malhotra's assessment in the minutes of the October 5-7 meeting upgrades India's growth forecast for 2026-27 to 7.1% — 40 basis points above the 6.7% projected in August — while warning that "inflationary pressures are building up." All six members of the Monetary Policy Committee voted to raise the repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.5%, the first increase in nearly four years and a formal reversal of the easing cycle that had dominated Indian monetary policy since 2023.

The upgrade itself is striking. Central banks do not lift a full-year growth projection by 40 basis points in the same meeting where they hike rates unless the incoming data has genuinely surprised them — and on the minutes' own account, the surprise is that India's growth "has held up better than expected" even as inflation risks rebuild and the global interest-rate environment narrows the room for Indian policy to stay loose. In effect, the RBI concluded the economy no longer needs the accommodation it has been carrying, and that the risk balance has tilted from defending growth toward defending price stability.

The most consequential sentence in the minutes is a statistic. The governor noted that 37% of the commodities in the consumer price inflation basket had inflation above 4% in August, against merely 20% in March — what central bankers call generalisation, the point at which price pressure stops being a fuel-and-food story and starts infecting the core of the basket. Generalised inflation is qualitatively different from the imported kind: it survives base effects, it embeds in wages and expectations, and it demands a policy response rather than patience. That 37% figure is the minutes' quiet justification for why the committee moved when it did, and it directly validates the swaps market's pricing of roughly 100 basis points of hikes over the coming year — including bets on a larger move by December.

The growth upgrade and the hike together describe a central bank choosing its battle. The MPC's judgment, as recorded, is that the economy can absorb some tightening without significant damage to growth — a luxury conferred by the very resilience the minutes celebrate. India's output has held up better than expected, and Wednesday's core-sector data — 7.9% growth in September — supports that confidence. But the minutes also acknowledge the constraint that made the decision urgent: the global interest-rate environment is narrowing the gap between Indian policy rates and the rest of the world, making an overly loose stance harder to defend as foreign money exits. The committee's framing suggests it would rather hike into strength than be forced to hike into weakness later.

The minutes also matter for what they reveal about the committee's collective posture — unanimity is itself information. When all six members, including the external appointees, back a hike with no dissenting pause vote, the internal debate has clearly shifted from whether to tighten to how fast. That posture has consequences across the financial system: banks' loan pricing will re-anchor higher within weeks, the housing-loan and corporate-credit pipelines that drove credit growth through the easing cycle will face their first headwind in four years, and the government's borrowing programme — which has leaned on a captive domestic buyer base — will meet a market that now prices a steeper path. None of that argues against the decision; it simply means the minutes' real audience is not today's trader but next quarter's borrower, who will meet a meaningfully different rate environment than the one that financed the last eighteen months.

There is also a quiet irony in the timing. The minutes landed on a day when the equity market's losses were led by exactly the rate-sensitive names a tightening cycle pressures hardest — Bajaj Finance fell about 4% — while the day's one strong sector, IT, was the one whose earnings are earned in foreign currency and priced against a domestic rate backdrop. Both moves are local symptoms of the same global condition the committee described: capital now demands to be paid for. India's appeal in that world rests on the growth premium the RBI just underlined; the cost of defending it, the committee has now made explicit, is the end of easy money at home.

For markets, the minutes deliver exactly the signal the bond market had pre-empted. Yields on the benchmark 2036 bond hit their highest since March 2023 on Wednesday morning as traders front-ran the minutes' release, and the document's inflation language gives that repricing official sanction; equity markets, which surrendered a 1% rally to close deep in the red, now face the reality that the "calibrated tightening" flagged this month comes with a growth-confident, inflation-anxious committee behind it. The open question the minutes leave is pace: 37% generalisation argues for speed, the 7.1% growth forecast argues for calibration, and the committee's word of choice — calibrated — suggests it believes it can walk that line. The next MPC meeting, and October's inflation print that economists expect to breach the tolerance band, will be the tests.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT DID THE RBI MINUTES REVEAL?
The growth forecast for FY27 was raised to 7.1% from 6.7%, all six MPC members voted for a 25-basis-point repo rate hike to 5.5%, and the governor flagged broadening inflation pressure.
WHEN WAS THE LAST RATE HIKE BEFORE THIS?
February 2023, when the repo rate stood at 6.6%; the October 2026 hike ends nearly four years of cuts and holds.
WHAT IS INFLATION GENERALISATION?
When price rises spread beyond food and fuel into the broader basket; the share of CPI items with inflation above 4% rose to 37% in August from 20% in March.
WHAT HAPPENS NEXT?
The next inflation print is expected to test the upper tolerance band, and markets price further hikes — a possible 50-basis-point move by December. GLOSSARY: - MPC: The Monetary Policy Committee, the six-member body that sets the RBI's repo rate. - Repo rate: The rate at which the RBI lends to banks, the benchmark for economy-wide interest rates. - Generalisation of inflation: The spread of price pressures from volatile food and fuel into the broader consumer basket. - Basis points: One hundredth of a percentage point; 25 basis points equals 0.25%.
