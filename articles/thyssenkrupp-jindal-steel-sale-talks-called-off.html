Thyssenkrupp, Jindal Call Off Steel Sale Talks After Months of Negotiations

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Thyssenkrupp and Jindal Steel International have mutually ended months of negotiations for the sale of Thyssenkrupp Steel Europe
  • 02/Talks that began in September faced hurdles over pension liabilities, investments, and energy costs
  • 03/Thyssenkrupp will now pursue restructuring TKSE as an independent entity with a minority stake retained

Thyssenkrupp has paused talks with Jindal Steel International on selling its steel unit. The decision was announced on Saturday, May 3, 2026.

The negotiations began in September after Jindal made an indicative bid for Thyssenkrupp Steel Europe. TKSE ranks as Europe's second-biggest steelmaker.

"The original assumptions and prerequisites for a potential sale have significantly changed," Thyssenkrupp stated. The decision to halt talks was mutual.

Reuters reported in March that differences over pension liabilities, investments and energy costs could derail the deal. Four people familiar with the matter confirmed the disputes.

CEO Miguel Lopez said steel business prospects are "better than they have been in a long time." EU safeguard measures against cheap Asian imports have boosted the sector.

Higher steel prices and protective tariffs have improved conditions across the continent. Analysts say Q1 2026 could mark an inflection point for European steel.

Narendra Misra, Jindal's director of European operations, said the groups "remain connected in friendship." He cited a shared goal of building low-carbon steel production in Europe.

Thyssenkrupp will continue TKSE restructuring with a medium-term goal of independence. The company plans to eventually retain a minority stake in the business.

Juergen Kerner of IG Metall called on management to start talks over an independent set-up for TKSE. The labour union deputy chief sits on Thyssenkrupp's supervisory board.
