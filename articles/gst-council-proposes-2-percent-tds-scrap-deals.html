GST Council Proposes 2% TDS on Scrap, RCM on Unregistered Sellers

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/The 57th GST Council has proposed a 2% TDS on specified waste and scrap transactions between registered businesses, extending the metal-scrap compliance model.
  • 02/Registered buyers must also pay GST under reverse charge on purchases from unregistered sellers, even those below the GST registration threshold.
  • 03/The levy covers plastic waste, discarded tyres, e-waste and used cooking oil; the compliance cost on small dealers is the key risk to watch.

The 57th GST Council has proposed a 2% tax deducted at source on specified waste and scrap transactions between registered businesses. The recommendations were detailed in an FAQ issued by the finance ministry on Friday.

Registered buyers will deduct the 2% TDS when purchasing scrap from registered suppliers. On purchases from unregistered sellers, the same buyers must pay GST under the reverse charge mechanism.

The reverse charge obligation applies even when the unregistered seller falls below the GST registration threshold. That is the sharpest compliance squeeze in the package.

Even transactions with sellers who were never required to register will now carry a GST cost for the buyer. That erodes the price advantage of sourcing from the unregistered informal segment.

The proposed levy covers plastic waste, discarded tyres, electrical and electronic scrap, and used cooking oil. These streams were largely outside systematic tax tracking until now.

The recommendations follow the compliance framework introduced for metal scrap after the 54th GST Council meeting. The new measures extend a similar tax-monitoring mechanism to the specified waste streams.

India's recycling chain runs heavily through informal, unregistered dealers. The reverse charge rule pulls purchases from those dealers into the GST net even where the seller was never registered.

The policy logic is a tax trail in the waste stream. As recycling scales under the circular-economy push, waste is being treated as a tracked commodity rather than an untracked by-product.

For the recycling industry, the change formalises the supply chain tier by tier. Compliance is no longer avoidable at any point where a registered buyer enters the chain.

The trade-off is compliance burden versus formalisation. Small dealers face higher documentation and working-capital costs, while larger processors gain a cleaner input tax trail.

The Council also settled several classification disputes. Seaweed-extract biostimulants registered under the Fertiliser Control Order, 1985 will be classified as fertilisers under HSN heading 3101.

Products containing plant growth regulators will not qualify for that concession. Sublimation paper has been classified under heading 4809.

Second-hand car dealers can use the concessional GST margin scheme while claiming input tax credit on eligible business inputs. This covers repair and maintenance services and spare parts.

However, input tax credit on the purchase of the second-hand vehicles themselves is not permitted if the margin scheme is used. Dealers must choose between the margin scheme and full credit.

The Council also recommended aligning the GST treatment of retreaded tractor tyres with that of new tractor tyres. Toy-related rate entries now cover other products under Customs Tariff heading 9503, including dolls and puzzles.

A separate nil-rate entry has been created for psyllium seeds, irrespective of whether they are fresh, chilled, frozen or dried. This ends rate uncertainty for the psyllium trade.

Past disputes involving eligible seaweed-based biostimulants and sublimation paper are proposed to be regularised on an as-is-where-is basis. That closes a long-running source of litigation for importers and manufacturers.

The mechanics deserve a closer look, because both legs of the proposal bite differently. The 2% TDS applies between registered businesses — a small cash-flow cost on every covered transaction, deducted at the point of payment.

The reverse charge leg is the bigger shift. Normally a seller collects GST from the buyer; under reverse charge, the buyer must pay the tax directly to the government, on purchases from unregistered suppliers.

That second rule applies even when the seller falls below the GST registration threshold. In effect, the threshold exemption stops shielding the recycling chain's smallest dealers once a registered business buys from them.

India's waste trade runs largely through informal collectors. Plastic waste, old tyres and electronic scrap change hands multiple times before reaching a processor, and much of that chain has no GST registration.

The proposal pushes the tax trail to the first organised touchpoint. A registered recycler buying from an unregistered kabadi network can no longer treat that purchase as invisible — the tax must be paid, and the transaction surfaces in returns.

Used cooking oil is the quiet inclusion with industrial implications. It is a regulated feedstock for biodiesel, and a TDS trail makes its aggregation more visible to both tax officers and fuel-blending buyers.

The metal-scrap precedent shows the template. After the 54th Council meeting, a similar framework went live for that stream; Friday's FAQ extends the same monitoring logic to four more waste categories.

Whether the formalisation push helps or hurts depends on scale. Large recyclers gain cleaner input trails and fewer disputes; small dealers face paperwork they previously avoided, or see margins negotiated away.

The classification clarifications are the Council's quieter workload. Seaweed biostimulants get a fertiliser home under HSN 3101, sublimation paper settles at 4809, and past disputes on both are proposed for as-is-where-is regularisation.

Second-hand car dealers get certainty of a different kind. The margin scheme stays, and they can claim input credit on repairs and spares — but not on the vehicles themselves.

Retreaded tractor tyres get aligned with new-tyre treatment, toys under heading 9503 are covered by the toy rate entries, and psyllium seeds get a nil-rate entry in every form.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT HAS THE 57TH GST COUNCIL PROPOSED ON WASTE AND SCRAP TRANSACTIONS?
THE COUNCIL HAS PROPOSED A 2% TAX DEDUCTED AT SOURCE ON SPECIFIED WASTE AND SCRAP TRANSACTIONS BETWEEN REGISTERED BUSINESSES. IT HAS ALSO PROPOSED REVERSE CHARGE MECHANISM ON PURCHASES FROM UNREGISTERED SUPPLIERS.
WHICH WASTE STREAMS ARE COVERED UNDER THE PROPOSED 2% TDS?
THE LEVY COVERS PLASTIC WASTE, DISCARDED TYRES, ELECTRICAL AND ELECTRONIC SCRAP, AND USED COOKING OIL.
DOES THE REVERSE CHARGE APPLY TO SELLERS BELOW THE GST REGISTRATION THRESHOLD?
YES. REGISTERED BUYERS MUST PAY GST UNDER REVERSE CHARGE ON PURCHASES FROM UNREGISTERED SELLERS EVEN WHEN THE SELLER FALLS BELOW THE GST REGISTRATION THRESHOLD.
WHAT IS THE BACKGROUND OF THIS PROPOSAL?
THE RECOMMENDATIONS FOLLOW THE COMPLIANCE FRAMEWORK INTRODUCED FOR METAL SCRAP AFTER THE 54TH GST COUNCIL MEETING. THE CHANGES EXTEND A SIMILAR TAX-MONITORING MECHANISM TO SPECIFIED WASTE STREAMS.
HOW ARE SECOND-HAND CAR DEALERS AFFECTED BY THE NEW CLARIFICATIONS?
SECOND-HAND CAR DEALERS CAN AVAIL THE CONCESSIONAL GST MARGIN SCHEME WHILE CLAIMING INPUT TAX CREDIT ON ELIGIBLE BUSINESS INPUTS SUCH AS REPAIR AND MAINTENANCE SERVICES AND SPARE PARTS. HOWEVER, ITC ON THE PURCHASE OF THE SECOND-HAND VEHICLES THEMSELVES IS NOT PERMITTED IF THE MARGIN SCHEME IS USED. GLOSSARY: Tax deducted at source (TDS): Tax withheld by the buyer from the seller's payment and deposited directly with the government. Reverse charge mechanism (RCM): A GST rule under which the buyer — not the seller — pays the tax on a purchase. HSN code: The Harmonized System of Nomenclature number used to classify goods under GST. Margin scheme: A concessional GST scheme for used goods where tax applies only to the dealer's margin, not the full sale price. Input tax credit (ITC): GST paid on business inputs that a registered business can offset against its output tax. As-is-where-is regularisation: Settling past tax disputes on the basis of the position actually prevailing, without re-opening liability.
