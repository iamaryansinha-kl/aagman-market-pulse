Super El Niño Threatens India Monsoon, 56% GDP at Risk as Nifty Stays Flat

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Cumulative rainfall as of June 26, 2026 stands 42% below long-term average, marking weakest monsoon start in a decade
  • 02/56% of India's GDP tied to consumption is threatened as El Niño risks exceed 2019 (-40%) and 2023 (-36%) deficits
  • 03/Nifty 50 has returned virtually nothing over past two years; analysts expect markets to remain range-bound

Brent crude has tumbled more than 40% from its 2026 peak above $120 per barrel. But Indian equity investors are looking at the wrong indicator for a market breakout.

A rapidly developing "Super El Niño" has triggered the weakest start to the monsoon in ten years. Cumulative rainfall as of June 26, 2026 stood at 42% below the long-term average.

The shortfall covers approximately 72% of the country. Central India is down 57%, East and North East India down 43%, the Southern Peninsula down 30%, and North and West India face a 24% deficit.

The rainfall deficit has exceeded El Niño years of 2019 (-40%) and 2023 (-36%) for the same period. The Indian Meteorological Department has lowered its forecast to 90% of the long-period average, the weakest in 11 years.

The IMD attaches a 60% probability to deficient rainfall. Kharif harvest accounts for roughly 50% of India's grain production, and agriculture employs 46% of the workforce.

"In 2026, Indian equities have been like a top—spinning all round yet range-bound," says Prateek Parekh of Nuvama Institutional Equities. "Can they now break out as the oil shock fades? We doubt it."

Brokerages are already downgrading consumer sectors. PL Capital has gone underweight on the consumer sector by 40 basis points. It cut Mahindra & Mahindra weight by 50 basis points, warning of tepid tractor demand.

"While markets have largely discounted the recent improvement in macro conditions, we believe any meaningful re-rating will require additional triggers," says Amit Khurana, analyst at Dolat Capital. "Investor focus is likely to shift towards the progress of the monsoon season."

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT IS THE CURRENT MONSOON RAINFALL DEFICIT IN INDIA?
AS OF JUNE 26, 2026, CUMULATIVE RAINFALL STANDS AT 42% BELOW THE LONG-TERM AVERAGE. THIS IS THE WEAKEST START TO THE MONSOON SEASON IN TEN YEARS, WITH THE SHORTFALL AFFECTING APPROXIMATELY 72% OF THE COUNTRY.
HOW DOES THE 2026 EL NIÑO COMPARE TO PREVIOUS YEARS?
THE 2026 RAINFALL DEFICIT HAS EXCEEDED THOSE SEEN IN EL NIÑO YEARS OF 2019 (-40%) AND 2023 (-36%) FOR THE SAME PERIOD. THE IMD HAS LOWERED ITS FORECAST TO 90% OF THE LONG-PERIOD AVERAGE, THE WEAKEST FORECAST IN 11 YEARS.
WHAT PERCENTAGE OF INDIA'S GDP IS AT RISK FROM THE MONSOON FAILURE?
56% OF INDIA'S GDP IS TIED TO CONSUMPTION, WHICH IS DIRECTLY THREATENED BY THE SUPER EL NIÑO. AGRICULTURE EMPLOYS 46% OF THE WORKFORCE AND KHARIF HARVEST ACCOUNTS FOR 50% OF GRAIN PRODUCTION.
HOW HAVE BROKERAGES RESPONDED TO THE EL NIÑO THREAT?
PL CAPITAL HAS GONE UNDERWEIGHT ON THE CONSUMER SECTOR BY 40 BASIS POINTS. IT CUT MAHINDRA & MAHINDRA WEIGHT BY 50 BASIS POINTS, WARNING THAT EL NIÑO COULD RESULT IN TEPID TRACTOR DEMAND GROWTH ON A HIGH BASE.
WHAT IS THE OUTLOOK FOR INDIAN EQUITY MARKETS?
THE NIFTY 50 HAS RETURNED VIRTUALLY NOTHING OVER THE PAST TWO YEARS. ANALYSTS EXPECT MARKETS TO REMAIN RANGE-BOUND AS RISKS SHIFT FROM SUPPLY TO DEMAND. "INVESTOR FOCUS IS LIKELY TO SHIFT TOWARDS THE PROGRESS OF THE MONSOON SEASON," SAID AMIT KHURANA OF DOLAT CAPITAL.
