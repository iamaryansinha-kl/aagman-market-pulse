Gold Safe-Haven Status Under Scrutiny: Morgan Stanley Says Iran Conflict Forces Rethink

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Gold plunged 14.5% in March 2026, the first month of the Iran conflict, underperforming equities and US Treasuries
  • 02/Morgan Stanley says gold's sensitivity to monetary policy has overtaken its safe-haven appeal as the key price driver
  • 03/Investment bank forecasts gold recovery to $5,200 per ounce in H2 2026 as central banks and ETFs resume buying

Gold's reputation as a crisis hedge is facing fresh scrutiny after prices declined during the Iran conflict, according to a Morgan Stanley report. The precious metal fell 14.5% in March 2026, the first month of hostilities, even as traditional risk assets also sold off.

The decline marks a sharp reversal for gold, which had posted consistent annual gains since 2021. The metal surged 55% in 2025 alone, reaching elevated levels before the conflict began.

By April, global equities had recovered to near pre-conflict levels. Gold, however, remained roughly 10% below its pre-war price, recouping only about one-third of its March losses.

Amy Gower, Metals & Mining Commodity Strategist at Morgan Stanley Research, said monetary policy sensitivity has overtaken geopolitical risk as gold's primary driver. "Gold's sensitivity to monetary policy has taken over as the key price driver," she noted. "This has overshadowed its safe-haven status."

The conflict triggered an energy supply shock that reduced expectations for US interest rate cuts. Higher real interest rates make non-yielding assets like gold less attractive to investors.

Additional pressure came from central bank selling. Turkey's central bank sold 52 tons of gold between late February and late March, while ETFs liquidated nearly 90 tons of holdings accumulated earlier in the year.

Morgan Stanley maintains a constructive long-term view. The bank forecasts gold could rise to approximately $5,200 per ounce in the second half of 2026. This outlook assumes central banks and ETFs resume purchases and markets begin pricing in future Federal Reserve rate cuts.

Morgan Stanley economists expect the Fed to cut rates by 25 basis points each in January and March 2027. However, risks remain if prolonged conflict sustains elevated oil prices and delays monetary easing.
