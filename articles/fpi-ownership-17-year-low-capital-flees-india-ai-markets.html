FPI Ownership Hits 17-Year Low as Capital Flees India for AI Markets

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/FPI ownership of NSE-listed companies dropped to 15.8% by March 31, 2026, the lowest level in seventeen years
  • 02/Foreign investors sold a record $19.6 billion of Indian equities in FY26, with 72% of outflows concentrated in the March quarter alone
  • 03/Capital is rotating to Taiwan and South Korea, where investors gain exposure to AI and semiconductor growth

Foreign Portfolio Investor ownership in NSE-listed companies has fallen to 15.8% as of March 31, 2026. This marks a seventeen-year low. FPI ownership dropped 166 basis points during FY26 alone.

Total FPI net outflows for FY26 reached a record $19.6 billion. Nearly 72% of these outflows, or $14.2 billion, occurred during the March quarter alone.

FPI holdings in NSE-listed companies declined 17.9% quarter-on-quarter to Rs 64.6 lakh crore by March end. Secondary market selling dominated, with FPIs unloading Rs 2.28 lakh crore through this channel in 2026.

The money is not leaving emerging markets. It is relocating to Taiwan and South Korea, where FIIs allocated over $1.5 billion and roughly $1 billion respectively in April 2026. These markets offer direct exposure to artificial intelligence and semiconductor growth that India lacks.

Domestic institutional investors have absorbed much of the foreign selling. DIIs held 18.3% of NSE-listed equities by value in Q2 FY26, surpassing foreign ownership at 16.7% for the first time in over two decades.

"With Brent crude around $100, the market bulls are on the defensive," said VK Vijayakumar, Chief Investment Strategist at Geojit Investments. The rupee has weakened to 96.14 per US dollar amid sustained selling.

Goldman Sachs strategists noted that while FPI selling may be nearing exhaustion, a meaningful return of foreign capital could take time. Expensive valuations and weak earnings visibility remain key concerns for investors.
