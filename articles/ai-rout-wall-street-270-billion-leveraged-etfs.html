AI Rout Exposes Wall Street's $270 Billion Leveraged ETF Machine

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Global leveraged ETFs oversee $270 billion, with US holding $200 billion and Asia exceeding $45 billion
  • 02/Korean retail AI funds lost over 20% in a week; SpaceX leveraged funds tumbled 40% from debut
  • 03/S&P 500 fell nearly 2% and Nasdaq 100 dropped over 4% as speculation machinery worked in reverse

This week's tech rout did more than puncture a hot trade. It exposed how modern speculation machinery amplifies both rallies and reversals.

Leveraged ETFs now oversee more than $270 billion globally. The US accounts for over $200 billion and Asia exceeds $45 billion, according to Bloomberg data.

In South Korea, retail investors piled into leveraged AI chip funds. As enthusiasm cooled, several trading vehicles lost more than 20% during the week.

Wall Street's newest obsession, SpaceX, provided another example. Leveraged funds tied to the rocket company attracted nearly $1 billion since launching earlier this month. They have since tumbled about 40% from their debut.

The benchmark indexes suffered losses. The S&P 500 fell nearly 2% while the Nasdaq 100 tumbled more than 4%.

Barclays estimates rebalancing by US leveraged ETFs has surged to several times its long-term average. This creates mechanical buying and selling flows large enough to influence broader market trading.

"These products bring a casino element into the market," said James St. Aubin, chief investment officer at Ocean Park Asset Management. "Anyone using leverage should understand they are playing with fire."

Strategy Inc.'s leveraged ETFs tell an even starker story. Bullish and bearish products launched in 2024 have lost more than 90% since inception despite attracting billions in investor capital.
