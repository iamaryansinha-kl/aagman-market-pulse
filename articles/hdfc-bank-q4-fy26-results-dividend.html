HDFC Bank Q4 FY26 Results: Net Profit Up 9% To ₹19,221 Cr; ₹13 Dividend Declared

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/HDFC Bank reports standalone net profit of ₹19,221 crore for Q4 FY26, marking 9.1% YoY growth from ₹17,616 crore in Q4 FY25
  • 02/Net interest income rises 3.2% to ₹33,082 crore; NIM stands at 3.38% on total assets and 3.53% on interest-earning assets
  • 03/Board recommends final dividend of ₹13 per share; total FY26 payout at ₹15.50 including earlier interim dividend

HDFC Bank Ltd (NSE: HDFCBANK) reported a 9.1% year-on-year rise in standalone net profit to ₹19,221 crore for the quarter ended March 31, 2026. The figure compares with ₹17,616 crore posted in the same quarter last fiscal.

On a consolidated basis, profit after tax grew 8.04% to ₹20,350.76 crore. The country's largest private lender declared a final dividend of ₹13 per equity share of face value Re 1.

The bank's net interest income (NII) for Q4 FY26 came in at ₹33,082 crore, up 3.2% from ₹32,006 crore in Q4 FY25. Net interest margin was recorded at 3.38% on total assets and 3.53% on interest-earning assets.

Operating profit increased 4.8% YoY to ₹27,802 crore from ₹26,537 crore in the year-ago period. Provisions declined 18% to ₹2,610 crore versus ₹3,193 crore in Q4 FY25.

Asset quality showed marked improvement during the quarter. Gross non-performing assets (GNPA) ratio moderated to 1.15% of gross advances as of March 31, 2026. This improved from 1.24% at the end of December 2025 and 1.33% in March 2025.

Net NPA ratio shrank to 0.38% from 0.42% in the previous quarter. The absolute GNPA amount declined to ₹34,061.2 crore from ₹35,179 crore in Q3 FY26.

The bank's total deposits grew 14.4% YoY to approximately ₹31,05,300 crore. Gross advances jumped 12% to ₹29,60,000 crore as of March 31, 2026.

The total dividend for FY26 stands at ₹15.50 per share. This includes a special interim dividend of ₹2.50 per share paid on August 11, 2025. The record date for the final dividend is fixed at Friday, June 19, 2026.

Capital adequacy ratio under Basel III guidelines stood at a healthy 19.7%. This compares favourably against the regulatory requirement of 11.9%.
