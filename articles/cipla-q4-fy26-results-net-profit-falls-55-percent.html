Cipla Q4 FY26 Results: Net Profit Crashes 55% To ₹555 Crore, Misses Estimates

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Cipla Ltd reports Q4 FY26 consolidated net profit of ₹555 crore, a 55% YoY decline and well below analyst estimates of ₹716 crore
  • 02/Revenue dips 3% to ₹6,541 crore while EBITDA margin crashes to 15.2% from 22.8% on weak US sales and nil gRevlimid contribution
  • 03/Board recommends final dividend of ₹13 per share with record date June 5; stock trades 4% higher at ₹1,344.9 despite the earnings miss

Cipla Limited (NSE: CIPLA) reported a sharp 55% year-on-year drop in consolidated net profit for Q4 FY26. The pharma major posted PAT of ₹555 crore against ₹1,222 crore in the year-ago quarter, falling well short of analyst estimates of ₹716 crore.

Revenue from operations declined 3% YoY to ₹6,541 crore. The topline also missed consensus expectations, which had pegged revenue at approximately ₹6,700 crore.

EBITDA for the quarter fell 35% to ₹997 crore from ₹1,538 crore in Q4 FY25. The EBITDA margin contracted sharply to 15.2%, down from 22.8% a year earlier, reflecting a weaker product mix and rising cost pressures.

The North America business remained the primary drag. Quarterly revenue from the region plummeted 26% YoY to ₹1,414 crore. The decline was attributed to nil contribution from gRevlimid, supply disruptions in gLanreotide, and broader pricing pressures in US generics.

On a brighter note, Cipla’s India business delivered robust growth. The One India segment, covering prescription, trade generics and consumer health, rose 15% YoY to ₹3,007 crore. Branded therapies in respiratory, urology, anti-diabetes and cardiac segments posted strong traction.

The Africa business also shone. One Africa revenue jumped 21% YoY to ₹1,236 crore, with South Africa alone surging 33% to ₹984 crore. This offset some of the weakness in developed markets.

For the full year FY26, Cipla reported its highest-ever annual revenue of ₹28,163 crore, up 2% YoY. However, annual PAT declined 26% to ₹3,879 crore from ₹5,273 crore in FY25. EBITDA for the year fell 17% to ₹5,925 crore, with margins narrowing to 21% from 25.9%.

The Board recommended a final dividend of ₹13 per share for FY26. The record date for payment is fixed at June 5, 2026. An interim dividend was already paid during the year.

"Cipla recorded its highest-ever annual revenue during FY26 despite near-term challenges in certain markets," said Achin Gupta, MD and Global CEO. He added the company would continue focusing on flagship brands, pipeline investments and regulatory resolutions.

On the bourses, Cipla shares defied the weak earnings print. The stock traded 4% higher at ₹1,344.9 on the NSE following the results announcement, suggesting investors may have priced in the bad news.

The results underscore the twin challenges facing Indian pharma majors: fading exclusivity windfalls in the US and margin compression in a hyper-competitive generics landscape. Cipla’s FY27 trajectory will hinge on its ability to stabilise North America revenues and sustain India-Africa momentum.
