SP Group Taps $125 Million Bond Issue, Plans Rs 3,250 Crore Rupee Sale

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Shapoorji Pallonji Group plans to raise $125 million via a tap issue of its existing dollar bonds, alongside a Rs 3,250 crore rupee bond issue
  • 02/The fundraising follows a Rs 21,500 crore (~$2.25 billion) dual-currency refinancing in July 2026 that carried yields of up to 18.95%
  • 03/Proceeds will be used to buy rupee securities issued by another group entity, extending the group's dollar-rupee debt route; Rs 13,500 crore falls due within 24 months

Shapoorji Pallonji Group plans to raise $125 million through a tap issue of its existing dollar bonds. Simultaneously, the conglomerate is targeting Rs 3,250 crore through rupee bonds.

VCCircle first reported the plan on September 28, 2026. The Economic Times confirmed the twin-track fundraising on September 30.

The group intends to raise dollar-denominated debt and deploy the proceeds to buy rupee-based securities issued by another group entity. This is a repeat of the dollar-rupee debt route the group has used before.

CONTEXT: A tap issue re-opens an existing bond line rather than launching a new one. The issuer sells additional notes under the same terms and ISIN, which keeps the bond's liquidity concentrated in a single large issue. Pricing typically settles at prevailing market yields, meaning the $125 million tranche will price off where SP Group's existing dollar paper is trading.

The July 2026 refinancing sets the benchmark. SP Group accepted bids worth Rs 21,500 crore (~$2.25 billion) in a dual-currency deal arranged by Deutsche Bank, closing a long-delayed fundraising effort.

The rupee portion was roughly Rs 15,100 crore (~$1.6 billion), raised as a zero-coupon bond yielding about 18.95% annually. The dollar tranche was $650 million at a yield of roughly 14.5%.

That three-year dollar bond was issued through group entity Mercury Finance. The notes are backed by shares of Afcons Infrastructure and the group's approximately 18.4% stake in Tata Sons.

The investor list underscored the private-credit stampede into Indian promoter paper. Cerberus Capital Management, Farallon Capital Management and Davidson Kempner Capital Management each committed $175-200 million.

Domestic investors included InCred Capital, DSP Finance, IIFL Capital Services, ASK Asset & Wealth Management and Nuvama Wealth Management.

IMPACT: The structure cuts both ways for creditors. The Tata Sons stake is the collateral that makes these yields palatable to distressed-debt funds, but it also concentrates risk in a single, illiquid asset that SP Group has already explored monetising. Any stress at the group level now transmits partly through the value of that holding.

The borrowing-cost gap is stark. AAA-rated Indian corporates raise funds at single-digit yields, while stressed groups like SP pay 14-19%. Private credit has filled the gap banks and public bond markets leave open, but at prices that embed heavy default risk.

The July deal carries a Rs 13,500 crore repayment obligation within 24 months under the bond agreement. The group plans to raise Rs 25,500 crore overall and had intended to secure another ~$350 million over the following six months.

Group debt has been reported around Rs 60,000 crore. Part of that burden traces back to the group's legacy 18.4% stake in Tata Sons, which it has considered selling down to cut leverage.

The new $125 million tap and Rs 3,250 crore rupee issue suggest the refinancing treadmill is accelerating rather than slowing. Each new tranche buys time but adds to the stack that must be rolled.

Afcons Infrastructure remains central to the collateral story. The flagship infrastructure and EPC company listed after an IPO that raised Rs 1,250 crore from primary issuance, and SP Group holds 50.17% in the company, per a CRISIL rating report dated August 19, 2026.

The bigger picture is the 2026 Indian private-credit boom. Global distressed funds are deploying billions into leveraged Indian promoters at near-equity returns, chasing yield in a market where public credit stays closed to all but the strongest names.

What to watch: the pricing on the tap issue, which will signal how secondary market yields on SP Group dollar paper have moved since July. Investors will also track whether the Rs 3,250 crore rupee tranche repeats the zero-coupon structure and what security package attaches to it.

WHAT IS A TAP ISSUE?
A TAP ISSUE RE-OPENS AN EXISTING BOND LINE INSTEAD OF ISSUING A NEW ONE. ADDITIONAL NOTES ARE SOLD UNDER THE SAME TERMS AND ISIN, WHICH KEEPS TRADING LIQUIDITY CONCENTRATED IN ONE ISSUE. THE NEW TRANCHE IS PRICED AT PREVAILING MARKET YIELDS.
HOW DOES THE DOLLAR-RUPEE DEBT ROUTE WORK?
THE GROUP RAISES DOLLAR-DENOMINATED DEBT AND USES THE PROCEEDS TO BUY RUPEE-BASED SECURITIES ISSUED BY ANOTHER GROUP ENTITY. THIS CONVERTS OFFSHORE FUNDING INTO ON-SHORE RUPEES WHILE KEEPING THE LIABILITY IN THE INTERNATIONAL MARKET.
