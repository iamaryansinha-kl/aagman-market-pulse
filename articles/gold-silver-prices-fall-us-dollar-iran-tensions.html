Gold, Silver Prices Fall 2-6% on US Dollar Strength, Iran Tensions Weigh

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/MCX gold futures for August fell ₹3,041 (2.06%) to ₹1.44 lakh per 10 grams during the week
  • 02/Silver for September contract plunged ₹15,269 (6.4%) to ₹2.23 lakh per kilogram on weak industrial demand
  • 03/Investors await US manufacturing PMIs, Eurozone inflation, and nonfarm payrolls for Fed policy cues

Gold and silver prices are facing sustained downward pressure as the week begins. Investors are weighing escalating US-Iran tensions, crude oil volatility, and a raft of key economic data releases.

On the Multi Commodity Exchange, gold futures for August delivery fell ₹3,041, or 2.06 per cent, during the week. The contract settled at ₹1,44,000 per 10 grams.

Silver for the September contract plunged ₹15,269, or 6.4 per cent, to ₹2,23,000 per kilogram. Weak industrial metals demand and a stronger US dollar weighed heavily on the white metal.

"For gold and silver prices, the momentum still remains down and corrective," said Pranav Mer, Vice President, EBG - Commodity & Currency Research, JM Financial Services Ltd.

"Gold remained under significant selling pressure last week, ending lower by 2 per cent, extending its week-on-week decline as persistent US dollar strength continued to weigh on precious metals," said Jateen Trivedi, VP Research Analyst, Commodity and Currency, LKP Securities.

Crude oil prices corrected sharply by nearly 10 per cent, easing inflation concerns. This reduced gold's appeal as an inflation hedge and kept investors favouring the US dollar over bullion.

In overseas markets, Comex gold futures fell $149.6, or 3.5 per cent, during the week to close at $4,096.3 per ounce. Silver slumped $7.13, or 10.7 per cent, to $59.67 per ounce in New York.

Gold recovered modestly on Friday on bargain buying. US Personal Consumption Expenditures data showed inflation rose at a slower pace than the previous month.

Continued gold purchases by China's central bank, following fresh US-Iran strikes, and President Donald Trump's threat of 100 per cent tariffs on the European Union also lent some support to prices.

However, higher US Treasury yields capped gains. Silver remained under pressure due to weak industrial metals, a stronger dollar, and subdued demand.

Analysts said bullion prices will largely hinge on the upcoming US economic data. Manufacturing and services PMI from major economies, Eurozone inflation data, and US nonfarm payrolls and unemployment figures will shape the Federal Reserve's next moves.
