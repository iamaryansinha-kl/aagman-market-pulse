Weakest Monsoon Since 2015: ICRA Cuts Farm Growth Forecast

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/ICRA has cut its FY27 agriculture, forestry and fishing GVA growth forecast to about 1 per cent from 1.3 per cent after the 2026 southwest monsoon fell short.
  • 02/Monsoon rainfall came in at 87 per cent of the long-period average in 2026, the weakest since 2015, with El Nino conditions developing during the season.
  • 03/Reservoir storage stood at 71 per cent of full levels as of September 24, raising the risk to rabi sowing of rice and wheat and to food inflation in Q3 FY27.

Ratings agency ICRA has cut its growth forecast for agriculture, forestry and fishing gross value added in FY27 to about 1 per cent. The earlier estimate was 1.3 per cent, and actual growth in FY26 came in at 3.3 per cent.

The downgrade follows the 2026 southwest monsoon, which delivered rainfall at just 87 per cent of the long-period average. It was the weakest monsoon since 2015.

The season started badly and never fully recovered. June rainfall was just 60 per cent of the long-period average, followed by 103 per cent in July, 84 per cent in August and 93 per cent in September.

El Nino conditions developed during the season, adding to the stress on rainfall distribution.

The shortfall was not evenly spread. East and Northeast India received 74 per cent of the long-period average and the South Peninsula 76 per cent, both classified as deficient.

Northwest India received 94 per cent and Central India 97 per cent. Central India was the only region to record normal rainfall.

Kharif sowing felt the impact, running 1.2 per cent below year-earlier levels by end-September. Kharif acreage stood at 110.7 million hectares, broadly in line with the historical average.

The composition shifted. Rice acreage fell 3.6 per cent and cotton acreage fell 1 per cent, partly offset by increases in pulses and coarse cereals.

The state-level divergence was sharp. Karnataka's kharif acreage fell 14.8 per cent from a year earlier, Andhra Pradesh 6.1 per cent, Telangana 4.9 per cent and Maharashtra 4 per cent.

"ICRA remains circumspect around the potential impact of the weak monsoon on crop output and farm incomes," the agency said.

Weaker farm incomes could dampen rural demand in the second half of FY27. They also create upside risks to food inflation.

The bigger worry is what comes next. Reservoir storage stood at 71 per cent of full reservoir level as of September 24, versus 90 per cent a year earlier and about 80 per cent of the historical average.

Southern India is the epicentre of the stress. Storage there was 36 percentage points below year-earlier levels and 24 points below the historical average.

Northern reservoirs were 29 percentage points below last year and 17 points below average. That combination threatens rabi sowing of irrigation-intensive crops such as rice and wheat.

The geography matters. Southern states account for about 98 per cent of India's rabi rice acreage and roughly 33 per cent of coarse cereal acreage.

Punjab and Rajasthan together account for about 31 per cent of wheat acreage, and both sit in the region with depleted northern reservoirs.

There is one meaningful offset. Uttar Pradesh, which holds about 28 per cent of India's wheat acreage, had reservoir storage 14 percentage points above its historical average at end-September. Its rainfall ran at 108 per cent of the long-period average.

Not all of the farm economy is equally exposed. Non-crop segments such as livestock, forestry and fishing have been less monsoon-dependent and have historically grown faster than the crop sector, which could cushion the overall impact.

The rural economy was already showing strain before the full effect of the monsoon shortfall. Rural unemployment fell to 4.1 per cent in August from 4.5 per cent in July, but nominal rural wage growth slowed to 3.3 per cent in July, a 59-month low.

Real rural wages contracted 1.6 per cent year-on-year. Rural consumer sentiment deteriorated sharply in the July 2026 RBI survey, though spending intentions stayed relatively resilient.

Buffer stocks offer some comfort on the supply side. Government rice stocks stood at 39.1 million tonnes on September 1, 5.8 per cent above a year earlier and well above prescribed stocking norms.

The paddy procurement target for the 2026-27 marketing season is 70.86 million tonnes, below actual procurement of 72.84 million tonnes last year.

Wheat stocks were 48 million tonnes on September 1, up 44.1 per cent year-on-year and about 75 per cent above stocking norms. The wheat outlook, however, depends on rabi sowing and reservoir conditions.

The fiscal math is also shifting. ICRA expects the fertiliser subsidy requirement to exceed the FY27 budget estimate of Rs 1.7 trillion by about Rs 900 billion, citing higher raw-material prices amid renewed tensions in West Asia.

ICRA expects rural inflation pressures to increase in the third quarter of FY27. Higher food prices, West Asia-related cost pressures and a low base will all feed through.

The weak monsoon therefore lands on an economy where rural demand is already fragile. How rabi sowing holds up between October and December will determine whether the 1 per cent forecast holds or slips further.
