Tata Steel UK Profitability Target Pushed to FY29 Amid EAF Delays

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Tata Steel Chairman N. Chandrasekaran pushes UK business EBITDA- and PAT-positive target to FY29 from FY26 at the company's annual general meeting
  • 02/The delay is driven by slippage in the Port Talbot EAF project commissioning to FY29 due to UK National Grid power connection delays
  • 03/India remains the primary cash engine with domestic EBITDA rising from ₹10,000 crore to ₹30,000 crore over the past decade

Tata Steel Ltd has pushed back its UK business profitability target by three years. Chairman N. Chandrasekaran now expects the unit to turn EBITDA- and PAT-positive by FY29 instead of FY26.

The revised target was announced at Tata Steel's annual general meeting on Thursday. Chandrasekaran told shareholders the EAF project at Port Talbot will be completed in FY29.

"So, we hope to stem the losses, and this project will be completed in FY29. At that time, the company should not just be EBITDA positive, it should also be PAT positive. That is the target that we have for the UK," Chandrasekaran said.

The EAF commissioning has slipped from FY28 to FY29. Chandrasekaran blamed delays in securing a power connection from the UK's National Grid.

"The national grid is continuing to delay. It is causing us anxiety. Overall, that is why the project is not FY28; it is FY29," he said. Both blast furnaces at Port Talbot have already been shut down.

Tata Steel UK currently operates only downstream facilities. It is processing imported steel slabs while awaiting the EAF commissioning under an agreement with the UK government.

The UK business accounts for almost 10% of Tata Steel's consolidated revenue. The unit faces sustained pressure from low-priced imports and subdued pricing in the domestic market.

Tata Steel UK CEO Rajesh Nair said in June that import quotas do not reflect UK market conditions. He noted that quota volumes allow significant import penetration into strategically important steel markets.

India has become the company's "primary cash engine". Domestic EBITDA increased from just over ₹10,000 crore to around ₹30,000 crore over the past decade.

Tata Steel's consolidated revenue rose 6% to ₹2.32 trillion in FY26. Consolidated net profit attributable to owners jumped threefold to ₹10,793.87 crore.

On Netherlands operations, Chandrasekaran said the business continued to face a difficult regulatory environment. The company is in constant discussions with Dutch authorities to resolve outstanding issues.

THE DELAY IS PRIMARILY DRIVEN BY THE SLIPPAGE IN COMMISSIONING THE ELECTRIC ARC FURNACE (EAF) PROJECT AT PORT TALBOT. THE PROJECT HAS BEEN PUSHED FROM FY28 TO FY29 DUE TO DELAYS IN SECURING A POWER CONNECTION FROM THE UK'S NATIONAL GRID.
WHAT IS THE CURRENT STATUS OF TATA STEEL'S UK OPERATIONS?
BOTH BLAST FURNACES AT PORT TALBOT HAVE BEEN SHUT DOWN. TATA STEEL UK IS CURRENTLY OPERATING ONLY DOWNSTREAM FACILITIES BY PROCESSING IMPORTED STEEL SLABS. THE EAF PROJECT IS BEING IMPLEMENTED UNDER AN AGREEMENT WITH THE UK GOVERNMENT WHICH IS PROVIDING A SUBSTANTIAL GRANT.
HOW SIGNIFICANT IS THE UK BUSINESS TO TATA STEEL'S OVERALL REVENUE?
TATA STEEL UK ACCOUNTS FOR ALMOST 10% OF THE STEELMAKER'S TOTAL CONSOLIDATED REVENUE FROM OPERATIONS. THE CONSOLIDATED REVENUE STOOD AT ₹2.32 TRILLION IN FY26, WITH CONSOLIDATED NET PROFIT JUMPING THREEFOLD TO ₹10,793.87 CRORE.
WHAT IS THE ROLE OF INDIA IN TATA STEEL'S FINANCIAL PERFORMANCE?
INDIA HAS BECOME THE COMPANY'S "PRIMARY CASH ENGINE". DOMESTIC EBITDA INCREASED FROM JUST OVER ₹10,000 CRORE TO AROUND ₹30,000 CRORE OVER THE PAST DECADE. THIS CASH GENERATION SUPPORTS THE INTERNATIONAL RESTRUCTURING EFFORTS INCLUDING THE UK AND NETHERLANDS OPERATIONS.
