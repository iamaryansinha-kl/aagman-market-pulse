CBDT Removes Arrest and Imprisonment as a Route for Tax Recovery, Shifts to Property-Based Collection

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

Tax defaulters will no longer face arrest and detention as a prescribed route for recovering outstanding dues. The Central Board of Direct Taxes has amended Rule 225, deleting the provision that empowered tax recovery authorities to use arrest and imprisonment for recovery.

The September 17 notification also removed the corresponding reference to arrest from the police-assistance provision and deleted several other Rule 225 clauses.

The change marks a clear shift in the prescribed recovery toolkit, away from personal detention and towards property-based recovery. Attachment and sale of movable and immovable property, and the appointment of a receiver, remain available to recovery officers.

The amendment, however, does not by itself extinguish any separate arrest power that may exist elsewhere under the Income-tax Act. Taxpayers facing prosecution for offences such as evasion or false statements remain exposed to those provisions.

In the same notification, the department significantly tightened gatekeeping for valuers. Under the revised Form 169, applicants must disclose their qualifications, professional history and valuation work, including details of assets valued or works executed during the preceding three years.

Registration is now sought separately for each asset class. The form covers categories ranging from land, buildings and plantations to mines, securities, business assets, machinery, jewellery and works of art.

The tax department has also given valuers and authorised income-tax practitioners six more months, until March 31, 2027, to complete their transition to the registration framework under the new Income-tax Act.

The twin moves fit a broader pattern under the new Act: softer recovery and compliance mechanics for routine dues, paired with sharper scrutiny of the professionals, valuers and intermediaries who operate inside the system. Removing arrest from the standard recovery route reduces coercive discretion at the field level, while the Form 169 disclosures create an audit trail for who values what.

For businesses and individuals with tax dues, the practical effect is that recovery will run through property and bank accounts rather than the threat of detention. For valuers, the deadline is now a hard one: register asset-class by asset-class under the new framework, or lose eligibility to practice.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
HAS THE CBDT BANNED ARREST FOR TAX DEFAULTERS?
It has removed arrest and imprisonment as a prescribed recovery route under Rule 225. Separate arrest powers elsewhere in the Income-tax Act, such as for prosecution offences, remain untouched.
WHAT RECOVERY OPTIONS REMAIN AVAILABLE?
Tax authorities can still attach and sell movable and immovable property and appoint receivers to recover outstanding dues.
WHAT IS CHANGING FOR VALUERS?
Revised Form 169 requires detailed disclosures of qualifications and three years of valuation work, with separate registration per asset class. Valuers and authorised practitioners have until March 31, 2027 to transition to the new Income-tax Act framework.
