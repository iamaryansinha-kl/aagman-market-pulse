Toyota to Build 100,000-Unit SUV Plant in Maharashtra by 2029

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Toyota Kirloskar Motor will build a new 100,000-units-per-year factory in Maharashtra's Bidkin Industrial Area, with production starting H1 2029
  • 02/The plant will manufacture a new SUV model and employ approximately 2,800 people; TKM sold 388,801 units in 2025, up 19% YoY
  • 03/This is the first of three planned Maharashtra plants as part of a ¥300 billion ($1.9 billion) investment push to triple India capacity to one million units by the 2030s

Toyota Kirloskar Motor has announced a new vehicle manufacturing plant in Maharashtra's Bidkin Industrial Area. The facility will have an annual production capacity of 100,000 units and is scheduled to begin operations in the first half of 2029.

The plant will manufacture a new SUV model. Toyota has not yet disclosed the model name or pricing details for the vehicle.

The facility will employ approximately 2,800 people at the start of production. It will include stamping, welding, painting, and full assembly operations.

This marks Toyota's first manufacturing facility in Maharashtra. TKM currently operates two plants in Bidadi, Karnataka, with a combined installed capacity of 342,000 units per year.

The new plant is part of a broader plan to build three facilities in Maharashtra. Total investment for all three plants is estimated at ¥300 billion, or approximately $1.9 billion.

When all three plants are operational, Toyota aims to triple its India production capacity to one million units annually by the 2030s. India is expected to become Toyota's fourth-largest manufacturing base globally.

The Maharashtra plants will serve as export hubs for the Middle East and Africa. Toyota's 2025 sales in these regions grew 3% to ¥4,521.2 billion, with operating income up 27.4%.

India surpassed Japan in 2025 to become the world's third-largest automobile market. Light vehicle sales exceeded five million units for the first time, reaching 5.19 million units.
