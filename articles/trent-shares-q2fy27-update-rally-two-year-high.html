Trent Shares Jump 13% to Two-Year High After Strong Q2FY27 Update

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Trent Ltd shares surged nearly 13% on October 6, hitting a two-year high and posting their biggest single-day gain since August 2024
  • 02/Q2 FY27 standalone revenue rose 23% YoY to Rs 5,788 crore, the fastest growth in six quarters, as sales per store decline narrowed to 1.7%
  • 03/Four brokerages raised targets, but Citi kept a Sell rating at Rs 2,950; full Q2 results come on October 29

Shares of Trent Ltd (NSE: TRENT) surged nearly 13% on Tuesday, October 6, racing to a two-year high. It was the stock's biggest single-day gain since August 2024.

The rally followed the Tata Group retailer's Q2 FY27 business update, released a day earlier. Standalone revenue from operations rose 23% year-on-year to Rs 5,788 crore.

Intraday, the stock hit its daily permissible upper circuit of 10% at Rs 2,832.50 on the BSE. It later extended gains to Rs 2,909 before paring some of the move.

Trent opened at Rs 2,800 against the previous close of Rs 2,575. It was still up about 11% at Rs 2,865 by mid-session, making it the top gainer in the Nifty 50, Nifty 200 and Nifty 500.

For context, the stock had been through a rough patch. Concerns over steep valuations, uneven discretionary demand, and slowing store productivity had capped the shares well below earlier peaks.

Through FY26 and into H1 FY27, revenue growth had cooled from the 30%-plus prints investors grew used to. Every update was scrutinised for signs the model was saturating.

Tuesday's move changed that narrative in a single session. The 23% revenue print was Trent's fastest growth in six quarters.

It was only the second time in at least six quarters that revenue growth crossed 20%. In the same quarter last year, Q2 FY26 revenue stood at Rs 4,724 crore.

Revenue from the sale of merchandise, excluding other operating income, also grew 23% in the quarter. That signals the growth was volume-led rather than driven by ancillary income.

For the first half of FY27, standalone revenue rose 21% YoY to Rs 11,454 crore. The print edges Trent closer to management's long-term 25% CAGR guidance.

The store rollout continued in parallel. Trent crossed a milestone during the quarter, opening its 1,000th Zudio store.

It added 17 Zudio and 10 Westside stores in Q2. Total store count stood at 1,342 as of September 30, versus 1,101 a year earlier.

The pace of additions was slower than the year-ago quarter, when Trent added 40 Zudio and 13 Westside stores. Around 250 stores were added across FY26, and 27 more in Q2 FY27.

Zudio remains the engine, contributing roughly two-thirds of Trent's revenue. Its sub-Rs 500 price points have made it the most aggressive value-fashion play in the country.

Westside, the older premium format, is the cash cow funding that expansion. Together the two brands anchor a portfolio of 1,342 stores across Westside, Zudio and other lifestyle formats.

What really moved the stock, though, was evidence that the productivity slide is stabilising. Sales per store declined just 1.7% in Q2, Goldman Sachs noted, against a 5.6% decline in Q1.

Motilal Oswal, which has a Buy rating, saw the same trend. Revenue per store fell only about 1% YoY versus a 5% drop in Q1, which it read as an easing of cannibalisation.

The brokerage said growth was driven primarily by new store additions, with the 23% print coming in ahead of its 18% estimate.

Goldman Sachs raised its target to Rs 3,010 from Rs 2,960, saying revenue growth exceeded estimates and sales productivity improved sequentially.

Morgan Stanley retained its Overweight rating and lifted its target to Rs 3,406. Bernstein stayed at Outperform with a Rs 3,500 target, calling the quarter a strong positive surprise versus its 18.5% estimate.

Macquarie expects momentum in same-store sales growth to improve from Q1 levels. Citi agreed the quarter was strong, but stopped well short of a bullish call.

The US bank noted revenue topped its expectations and that the decline in revenue per square foot was the slowest in five quarters.

Even so, Citi retained its Sell rating with a Rs 2,950 target. It flagged weak sales productivity, rising input costs, and intensifying competition.

That makes the split four bulls against one bear. The gap between Bernstein's Rs 3,500 target and Citi's Rs 2,950 target is wide, and it frames the "priced for perfection" debate neatly.

Trent has historically traded at a steep premium to the broader market, leaving little room for a soft quarter. Miss once, and the stock pays for it twice.

The bears argue store expansion is masking weakening per-store economics. Open too many stores too fast, and each new outlet slices revenue from the ones around it.

The bulls counter that a 1.7% per-store decline, narrowing from 5.6%, shows the worst of the cannibalisation is passing. Competition is not standing still either, with value-fashion rivals expanding aggressively across the same high-street locations.

Three things changed this quarter. Growth re-accelerated, the productivity decline narrowed sharply, and the network crossed the 1,000-Zudio threshold.

That combination is what the market had been waiting for. Proof that per-store economics are healing turned a good print into a 13% rally.

Tuesday's price action suggests the market currently sides with the bulls. LIC, which holds a stake in Trent, saw the value of its holding rise more than Rs 531 crore in a single day to nearly Rs 4,700 crore.

The rally landed on a strong day for the market, with the Sensex closing 685 points higher. Trent's move still dwarfed the index and did the heavy lifting for retail-sector sentiment.

The next checkpoint is October 29, when Trent's board meets to approve full Q2 and H1 results. In Q1 FY27, the company reported revenue of Rs 5,666.3 crore, up 19% YoY, with net profit rising 26% to Rs 531.77 crore.

Investors will be watching margins, not just the top line. Key metrics from here: same-store sales growth, revenue per square foot, the pace of store additions, and whether input costs start eating into profitability.

For now, the Q2 update has reset the conversation around Trent. Growth is back above 20%, productivity is stabilising, and the market has responded in kind.

GLOSSARY: Upper Circuit: The maximum price a stock is allowed to move up in a single trading session on an exchange, set as a percentage band to curb extreme volatility. YoY (Year-on-Year): A comparison of a financial metric against the same period in the previous year, used to strip out seasonal effects. Same-Store Sales Growth (SSSG): Revenue growth from stores that have been open for at least a year, showing whether existing outlets are selling more, not just expanding. Revenue Per Square Foot: Total store revenue divided by selling area, a standard measure of how efficiently a retail space generates sales. Cannibalisation: When a company's new store takes sales away from its existing nearby stores instead of adding fully incremental revenue. CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate): The smoothed annual growth rate of a metric over a multi-year period. Broker Rating (Overweight/Outperform/Sell): An analyst's stance on a stock relative to its expected return, with Overweight and Outperform meaning the broker expects it to beat the market, and Sell meaning it is expected to underperform.
