SBI Research Sees Sept CPI at 5.65%, October Above 6.5%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/SBI Research forecasts CPI inflation at around 5.65% in September 2026, rising above 6.5% in October and November.
  • 02/The research arm of State Bank of India expects the RBI to raise the repo rate by at least 25 basis points from 5.25% at the October 5-7 MPC meeting.
  • 03/Inflation pressures have broadened from 22 to 51 commodities, while a weak rupee and a deficient monsoon add to the risks.

SBI Research expects India's retail inflation to climb to around 5.65 per cent in September 2026. The forecast was published in a pre-Monetary Policy Committee report on Friday, October 2.

The research arm of State Bank of India sees consumer price index inflation crossing 6.5 per cent in October and November. Price pressures are expected to ease below 6 per cent only in early 2027.

Alongside the inflation call, SBI Research said the Reserve Bank of India may raise the policy repo rate by at least 25 basis points at its October 5-7 monetary policy meeting. The current repo rate stands at 5.25 per cent.

"We believe the balance of risks has tilted decisively towards a 25-bps rate hike at this juncture," the report said.

The assessment cites rising inflation, worsening global macroeconomic conditions and pressure on the rupee. It also points to changing liquidity conditions and a renewed repricing of risks in global markets.

A 25-basis-point hike would mark a shift from the RBI's previous policy stance. At its August 3-5 meeting, the Monetary Policy Committee unanimously kept the repo rate unchanged at 5.25 per cent and retained a neutral stance.

In August, the RBI said it wanted greater clarity on the direction and composition of inflation before taking further policy action. The inflation data released since then has moved further away from comfort levels.

Retail inflation rose to 4.82 per cent in August from 4.45 per cent in July. More importantly, the pressures are spreading across a wider range of items.

In January, 22 commodities accounted for 90 per cent of the weighted contribution to CPI inflation. By August, that number had increased to 51, indicating that inflationary pressures have become far more broad-based.

SBI Research also highlighted weakness in the rupee as a key risk. The pressure stems from a strong US dollar, foreign currency demand from oil marketing companies and corporates, and continued selling by foreign portfolio investors.

On the domestic front, the 2026 southwest monsoon stood at 87 per cent of the long-period average. As many as 43 per cent of districts received deficient rainfall during the season.

Strong El Nino conditions and below-normal rainfall in October could pose risks to Rabi crops. That would add to food inflation pressures in the coming months.

The RBI currently projects FY27 GDP growth at 6.7 per cent and CPI inflation at 5 per cent. SBI Research expects the central bank to raise its growth forecast by 30 basis points and its inflation projection by 20 basis points at the October policy review.

The repo rate is the rate at which the RBI lends to commercial banks. Changes in the rate flow through to lending and deposit rates across the economy.

The October 5-7 meeting will be closely watched for the MPC's reading on inflation momentum. SBI Research's baseline suggests price pressures will stay elevated through October and November before moderating in early 2027.
