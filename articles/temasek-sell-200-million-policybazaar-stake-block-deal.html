Temasek to Sell $200 Million Policybazaar Stake Via Block Deal at 4.6% Discount

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Temasek to sell 2.6% stake in PB Fintech via ₹1,909 crore ($200 million) block deal
  • 02/Floor price ₹1,604/share, 4.6% discount to Thursday close of ₹1,682.10
  • 03/Macritchie Investments offering 11.9 million shares; 60-day lock-up on remaining 3.8%

Singapore's sovereign investment firm Temasek Holdings is likely to sell a 2.6% stake in PB Fintech Ltd. The sale will be through a ₹1,909 crore, or $200 million, block deal on Friday. This is according to deal terms reviewed by Mint.

Temasek, through its subsidiary Macritchie Investments Pte, is offering up to 11.9 million shares. The floor price is ₹1,604 apiece. This represents a 4.6% discount to PB Fintech's closing price of ₹1,682.10 on the National Stock Exchange on Thursday.

Before the transaction, Macritchie Investments held a 6.47% stake in the online insurance aggregator. After the sale, it will retain a 3.8% stake, subject to a 60-day lock-up.

Citigroup Global Markets India is the sole placement agent for the deal. The books are scheduled to close by around 7:30 am on Friday. The trade is expected to be executed later in the day.

The proposed transaction comes amid continued churn in PB Fintech's shareholder base. In May, co-founders Yashish Dahiya and Alok Bansal sold 3.8 million shares, or about 0.8% of the company. This was through a ₹654 crore block deal.

Chinese technology major Tencent exited its remaining 1.05% stake via a separate ₹805 crore block transaction in May. This was also reported by Mint.

Temasek's stake sale also comes as PB Fintech expands overseas. On June 30, the company's board approved setting up two step-down subsidiaries in Dubai. These will offer financial advisory and reinsurance services, strengthening its presence in the UAE.

PB Fintech ended FY25 on a strong note. Consolidated revenue from operations climbed 37% year-on-year to ₹2,061 crore for the March quarter. This was supported by growth in its core insurance marketplace and steady renewal income.

Total insurance premiums collected via the platform during the March quarter rose 46% year-on-year to ₹9,217 crore. This showed sustained traction in the insurance distribution vertical.

The block deal reflects a broader trend in India's secondary markets. With high interest rates, valuation gaps, and global upheavals weighing on traditional M&A, promoters and institutional investors are turning to the secondary market for liquidity.

This drove block and bulk deals to a multi-year high in January-April despite bearish sentiment. As India's primary markets remain largely muted, secondary markets are giving institutional investors some much-needed liquidity events.

In June, Lenskart saw SoftBank and the Abu Dhabi Investment Authority sell stakes worth up to ₹2,873 crore and ₹1,944 crore respectively. GQG Partners sold GMR Airports stake worth ₹1,906 crore through open market transactions.

June also saw investors sell equity in SoftBank-backed Meesho, jewellery retailer Bluestone, Groww parent Billionbrains Garage Ventures, Capital Small Finance Bank, and logistics platform Delhivery.
