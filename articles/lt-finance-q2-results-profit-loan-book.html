L&T Finance Q2 Profit Jumps 33% to Rs 975 Crore, Loan Book Up 28%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Consolidated net profit rose 33% YoY to Rs 975 crore from Rs 735 crore a year ago.
  • •- The loan book expanded 28% year-on-year to Rs 1.36 lakh crore.
  • •- Net interest margin improved to 10.48% from 10.22%; management guides to a 10-10.50% band for Q3.
  • •- Gross non-performing loans declined to 2.7% from 3.2% a year earlier.
  • •- The average cost of borrowing fell to 6.95% from 7.32%, cushioned by front-loaded borrowing in Q2.

L&T Finance delivered the kind of September-quarter print that non-bank lenders dream of: consolidated net profit up 33 per cent year on year to Rs 975 crore, the loan book up 28 per cent to Rs 1.36 lakh crore, margins widening and asset quality improving at the same time — a clean sweep across every metric that matters, announced late on Thursday.

The income statement shows the growth was earned, not bought. Total income rose 26 per cent year on year to Rs 5,484 crore, with interest income up 28 per cent at Rs 5,158 crore and fee and commission income rising to Rs 326 crore from Rs 289 crore in the same period last year. Profit before tax climbed 33 per cent to Rs 1,319 crore from Rs 989 crore a year earlier, matching the bottom-line growth and confirming the profit surge is operating performance rather than a tax or one-off effect.

Net interest margin — the spread between what the lender earns and what it pays for funds — improved to 10.48 per cent from 10.22 per cent a year ago, an unusually wide band for any lender and a function of L&T Finance's retail-heavy, high-yield lending mix. Managing director and chief executive Sudipta Roy expects AUM growth momentum of around 20 per cent to continue into the third quarter, and guided to holding NIM within the 10-10.50 per cent band — guidance that implies confidence in pricing power even as system interest rates drift upward. He also indicated the company does not expect any meaningful change in the lending rates banks offer it, an assumption rooted in the liability-side work done over the past year.

That rate environment is the quarter's quiet subplot. The RBI's repo rate increase earlier this month has begun raising funding costs across the financial system, but Roy indicated L&T Finance is insulated: the company's average cost of borrowing has actually declined to 6.95 per cent from 7.32 per cent a year ago, helped by a deliberate decision to front-load a substantial portion of its borrowings in the second quarter. Even if system rates rise further, he said, the impact on the company's cost of funds is unlikely to be meaningful — a funding strategy that effectively locked in cheap money ahead of the tightening cycle.

Asset quality rounded out the quarter's trifecta. Gross non-performing loans fell to 2.7 per cent from 3.2 per cent a year ago — an improvement achieved while the loan book was growing at 28 per cent, which is the harder trick. Rapid growth usually strains underwriting; L&T Finance appears to have grown into its risk frameworks rather than out of them.

The lender's franchise gives it a structural advantage worth noting. As part of the wider L&T group, it inherits a distribution footprint and brand credibility in semi-urban and rural India that pure-play NBFCs spend years building, and its loan book tilts toward the retail and rural segments where yields are high and competition from banks is thinnest. That mix explains the double-digit NIM that would be impossible in corporate lending, and it also explains the granularity: tens of thousands of small-ticket borrowers whose defaults are statistically predictable rather than cyclically catastrophic.

The print positions L&T Finance squarely in the market's sweet spot for NBFCs: retail-focused, granular lending with improving credit costs, a liability franchise cheap enough to fund growth without equity dilution, and management guiding with confidence into a rising-rate environment that has worried peers. With the festive season landing in the third quarter — historically the strongest stretch for retail disbursements — the 20 per cent AUM guidance looks conservative rather than ambitious.

The watch-items are the ones that apply to every fast-growing lender rather than anything company-specific. Rural and semi-urban borrowers are more exposed to monsoon variability and informal-income shocks than salaried urban customers; collections efficiency in the October-December quarter will test whether the 2.7 per cent gross NPA number holds through the festive lending surge. Funding competition is the second variable: if banks begin chasing the same retail customers with cheaper secured products, yield pressure could compress the NIM band management has guided to. Neither risk is visible in Thursday's numbers — but both are the kind that arrive two or three quarters after the growth that causes them.

For investors, the quarter extends a run that has made the stock one of the cleaner expressions of India's retail credit boom. Profit compounding at a third, book growth at 28 per cent, margins stable near double digits and credit costs falling is a combination that rarely survives long without the market re-rating it — the only question on Friday morning will be how much of that story is already in the price, and whether the festive-quarter sequel justifies paying up for the next leg of the run.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT WERE L&T FINANCE'S Q2 NUMBERS?
Net profit rose 33% YoY to Rs 975 crore, the loan book grew 28% to Rs 1.36 lakh crore, and total income increased 26% to Rs 5,484 crore.
HOW DID MARGINS AND ASSET QUALITY PERFORM?
Net interest margin improved to 10.48% from 10.22%, and gross NPAs fell to 2.7% from 3.2% a year ago.
WHAT IS THE MANAGEMENT'S OUTLOOK?
MD & CEO Sudipta Roy expects around 20% AUM growth to continue in Q3 and NIM to hold in the 10-10.50% band.
HOW IS THE COMPANY POSITIONED FOR HIGHER RATES?
Its average cost of borrowing fell to 6.95% from 7.32% after front-loading borrowings in Q2, limiting the impact of the RBI's repo rate increase. GLOSSARY: - Net interest margin (NIM): The difference between interest earned on loans and interest paid on deposits and borrowings, expressed as a percentage of assets. - AUM: Assets under management — the total value of loans or investments a financial firm manages; here, the loan book. - Gross NPA ratio: The share of a lender's loans classified as non-performing, before accounting for provisions. - Front-loading: Raising borrowings or deploying capital early in a period to lock in rates or capture demand ahead of time.
