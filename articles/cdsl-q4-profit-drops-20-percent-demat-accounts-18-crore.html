CDSL Q4 Profit Drops 20% to ₹80 Cr, Demat Accounts Hit 18 Cr Milestone

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/CDSL Q4 FY26 consolidated net profit dropped 20% YoY to ₹80 crore from ₹100 crore in the year-ago quarter.
  • 02/Despite profit compression, total income grew 4.7% to ₹268 crore, and demat accounts crossed 18 crore with ~80% market share.
  • 03/Watch FY27 account opening momentum and margin trajectory as technology costs rise and retail participation remains below 10% of India's population.

Central Depository Services (India) Ltd reported consolidated net profit of ₹80 crore for Q4 FY26 ended March 31, 2026. This marks a 20% decline from ₹100 crore posted in Q4 FY25. Profit compression reflects rising technology investments and higher employee costs.

Total income during the quarter rose to ₹268 crore against ₹256 crore in the year-ago period, a 4.7% increase. Revenue growth remains modest despite the expanding account base. The top-line expansion is not fully flowing to the bottom line.

For the full fiscal year 2025-26, net profit stood at ₹455 crore versus ₹526 crore in FY25, down 13.5%. Total consolidated income climbed to ₹1,239 crore from ₹1,199 crore in the previous year.

CDSL became the first depository in India to register over 18 crore demat accounts as of March 31, 2026. The count rose from 15.30 crore a year earlier, adding roughly 2.7 crore accounts in twelve months.

Nehal Vora, Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer, called FY26 a defining year for empowering India's investors. He noted that under 10% of India's population participates in markets today, leaving substantial runway for growth.

The company commands approximately 80% market share in demat accounts against rival NSDL's 20%. Technology spending has doubled from 7% to 14% of revenue over recent quarters to support regulatory demands and scale.

The Board recommended a final dividend of ₹12.75 per equity share of face value ₹10 for FY26. The payout signals confidence in cash generation despite the annual profit decline.

Analysts will monitor Q1 FY27 account additions and margin recovery. With India's demat penetration at roughly 13.4%, sustained retail participation remains the key long-term earnings driver.
