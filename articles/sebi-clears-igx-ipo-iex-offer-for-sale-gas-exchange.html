Sebi Clears IGX IPO: IEX To Sell 1.67 Crore Shares In Gas Exchange

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Sebi gave its final observation on the IGX draft prospectus on October 12, clearing the path for the IPO of India's first delivery-based gas trading platform.
  • 02/The issue is a pure offer for sale of up to 1.67 crore shares by promoter IEX, which must cut its stake from over 47 per cent to 25 per cent; IGX gets no proceeds.
  • 03/IGX revenue grew to Rs 61 crore in FY26 from Rs 48.8 crore in FY25, and MD Rajesh Kumar Mediratta has targeted a listing by December 2026.

Indian Gas Exchange Ltd, the country's first online delivery-based trading platform for natural gas, on Monday secured approval from markets regulator Sebi to launch its initial public offering, according to an update on the regulator's website.

The update showed Sebi's final observation on the draft red herring prospectus. In the IPO process, this observation is the crucial regulatory clearance that allows a company to move ahead with the final preparations for taking its shares to the public market, subject to other applicable requirements.

The public issue is structured entirely as an offer for sale of up to 1.67 crore equity shares. The seller is promoter Indian Energy Exchange Ltd, the country's largest power exchange, which set out the terms of the issue in its draft red herring prospectus.

Because the issue is a pure offer for sale, IGX will not receive any proceeds from the IPO. The entire amount raised will flow to the selling shareholder, IEX, which currently holds more than 47 per cent of the gas exchange and is looking to monetise part of that holding.

There is also a regulatory compulsion at work. Under listing norms, IEX is required to bring its stake in IGX down to 25 per cent. The offer for sale is the route through which the parent will dilute its controlling position while staying on as promoter of the exchange.

IGX filed its preliminary IPO papers in July this year. Sebi reviewed the draft prospectus over the following months and delivered its final observation on October 12, putting the exchange within reach of a listing it has publicly discussed for most of 2026.

The regulator's observations mark an important milestone in the IPO process. They allow the company to proceed with preparations for the public issue, though the launch itself remains subject to market conditions and other applicable regulatory requirements before the offer can open to investors.

In the draft papers, IGX stated that the primary objective of the IPO is to achieve the benefits of listing its equity shares on a stock exchange. A listing, it said, would enhance the company's visibility, strengthen its brand image and provide a public market for its shares.

A public listing also brings a practical benefit that a wholly-owned subsidiary cannot offer: a transparent market price for the shares and a ready exit route for investors. For IEX, a successful offer also unlocks value from an incubated business without giving up control.

The approval comes amid strong fundraising momentum in India's primary market. Companies have raised more than Rs 1.1 lakh crore through 96 public issues so far in 2026, making it one of the busiest years for IPO activity the country has seen.

Several more IPOs are lined up for launch this month alone, signalling continued activity in the primary market as issuers and bankers look to tap investor appetite before the year ends. IGX now joins that queue with its Sebi clearance in hand, offering investors a rare chance to own part of the country's first online delivery-based natural gas trading platform.

IGX was incorporated in November 2019 as a wholly-owned subsidiary of IEX. Its electronic gas trading platform was inaugurated in June 2020, and it received authorisation from the Petroleum and Natural Gas Regulatory Board, or PNGRB, to operate as a gas exchange in December 2020.

The company operates a technology-enabled electronic marketplace for trading natural gas through standardised physical delivery-based contracts. These contracts are executed across multiple delivery hubs that are connected to India's national gas pipeline network.

Unlike several international gas exchanges, where a significant share of contracts are financially settled, IGX operates a physical delivery-based market. Buyers and sellers transact with the intention of actual gas changing hands, and the exchange also offers forward contracts with tenures of up to six months.

The financial record is modest but consistently improving. For the financial year ended March 2026, IGX reported revenue from operations of Rs 61 crore, compared with Rs 48.8 crore in FY25 and Rs 34.8 crore in FY24.

That works out to growth of roughly 25 per cent in FY26 and close to 40 per cent in FY25, a steady climb for an exchange still in the early stages of building trading volumes and deepening participation on its platform.

The listing timeline has been public knowledge for months. In January, IGX's Managing Director and CEO Rajesh Kumar Mediratta stated that the country's first online delivery-based natural gas trading platform was targeting an IPO by December 2026, a goal the Sebi clearance now brings well within reach, barring any last-minute delays in the final offer formalities.

Axis Capital and Motilal Oswal Investment Advisors are the book-running lead managers to the issue, while KFin Technologies is acting as the registrar to the offer. The company is yet to announce the final size, price band and timeline for the share sale.

GLOSSARY: IPO: Initial Public Offering — the process by which a private company offers its shares to the public for the first time and gets listed on a stock exchange. Offer for Sale (OFS): A type of IPO where existing shareholders sell their stakes rather than the company issuing new shares, so proceeds go to the sellers, not the company. DRHP: Draft Red Herring Prospectus — the preliminary document filed with Sebi that details a company's business, financials and the proposed terms of an IPO. Sebi: Securities and Exchange Board of India — the statutory regulator that oversees India's securities markets and must approve all public issues. PNGRB: Petroleum and Natural Gas Regulatory Board — the sector regulator that authorises entities to operate as gas exchanges in India. Gas exchange: An electronic marketplace where natural gas is bought and sold through standardised contracts, similar to how power and commodity exchanges function. Delivery-based contract: A trade contract where the underlying commodity is physically delivered to the buyer, as opposed to being settled purely in cash.
