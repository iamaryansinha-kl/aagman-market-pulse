DIIs Raise Stakes in 8 Midcap Stocks in September Quarter

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Eight NSE midcap stocks saw DII shareholding rise over 200 basis points quarter-on-quarter in Q2FY27.
  • •- Swiggy saw the largest increase, to 34.49% from 27.38% — a jump of over 700 basis points.
  • •- Paytm rose to 29.52%, Meesho to 13.65%, and Cochin Shipyard to 11.24%.
  • •- The buying confirms domestic institutions as the market's dominant marginal buyer while FPIs sell.
  • •- The list tilts heavily toward new-age consumer tech: four of eight are recently listed platforms.

The shareholding disclosures trickling in for the September 2026 quarter are starting to show where India's domestic institutions placed their bets while foreign investors headed for the exits. Based on disclosures available so far, eight NSE-listed midcap stocks recorded an increase of more than 200 basis points in domestic institutional investor shareholding quarter-on-quarter in Q2FY27, according to a StockEdge shareholding scan compiled by ETMarkets. The increases are not marginal tinkering; several are conviction-sized reallocations, and the names on the list sketch the outline of a domestic-money worldview that differs sharply from the foreign one.

The largest move belongs to Swiggy. DII ownership of the food-delivery and quick-commerce platform rose to 34.49 per cent in the September quarter from 27.38 per cent in June — an addition of more than seven percentage points in a single quarter, a pace that implies sustained accumulation through the period rather than a single block trade. One97 Communications, the Paytm parent, followed with a rise to 29.52 per cent from 24.86 per cent, and Meesho — the newest of the three listings — saw domestic ownership jump to 13.65 per cent from 9.14 per cent, a near-50 per cent proportional increase in holdings in ninety days. Cochin Shipyard, the defence-and-shipbuilding outlier, rose to 11.24 per cent from 6.95 per cent.

The rest of the list filled in more measured steps. Lenskart Solutions rose to 26.81 per cent from 23.57 per cent, Piramal Finance to 21.75 per cent from 18.63 per cent, APL Apollo Tubes to 20.83 per cent from 18.6 per cent, and SBI Cards and Payment Services to 20.2 per cent from 18.14 per cent. Each crossed the 200-basis-point threshold that the scan uses to define significant accumulation; each, notably, now carries domestic institutional ownership above 20 per cent, the level at which DIIs begin to exert real influence over a stock's trading character and governance conversations.

The composition is the story. Four of the eight names — Swiggy, Paytm, Meesho and Lenskart — are new-age consumer-technology platforms, most of them listed within the last few years at prices well above where several now trade. Domestic institutions stepping into these names at scale signals a judgement that the platforms' path to profitability and their valuations have finally met, and that the discount at which new-age tech trades relative to the broad market overcompensates for the execution risk. Foreign investors built much of the early positioning in these stocks and have been among the sellers; the Q2 disclosures suggest the other side of that trade, increasingly, is Indian money.

That pattern generalises beyond this list, and it is the macro point. October's FPI outflows crossed Rs 52,100 crore even as benchmarks rallied — a divergence made possible by domestic institutional buying, of which these shareholding shifts are the stock-level evidence. The 200-basis-plus accumulation in midcaps is a particularly telling detail: it is easier for large domestic flows to hide in index heavyweights, and choosing instead to build positions in mid-cap new-age platforms and niche financials argues that the buying is active stock selection, not passive index replication. When the marginal buyer changes identity, the market's valuation logic changes with it, and the Q2 register is a document of that handover.

There are caveats worth keeping. Shareholding disclosures capture quarter-end positions, not the path — a fund could have accumulated through July and sold into September strength, though the size of several increases argues against round-tripping. The list is also drawn from disclosures available so far, and will lengthen as more companies report. And rising DII ownership is a sentiment indicator, not a guarantee: domestic institutions have their own redemption pressures, and a quarter of accumulation says nothing about the quarter now underway. But as a photograph of where India's own money moved while the world's left, it is unusually clear — and its subject is a domestic bid that no longer waits for foreign permission to buy India.

The longer arc deserves a final frame. Five years ago, the standard worry about Indian equities was concentration — that foreign investors owned the market and could take it down with them. The Q2 register inverts that fear: the stocks foreigners built and then abandoned are precisely the ones domestic institutions are now absorbing at scale, at prices the sellers no longer found acceptable. Whether that transfer proves wise depends on the platforms' earnings over the next four quarters, which begin reporting in earnest this month. But the ownership transfer itself is already a fact, and it rewrites the risk map of the Indian midcap market — from a tape governed by global risk appetite to one increasingly governed by domestic conviction, with all the liquidity consequences, good and ill, that such a shift implies. For investors, the practical takeaway is double-edged: domestic sponsorship can cushion falls that would once have become crashes, but it can also extend rallies beyond what global money would have paid — and both effects will be on display in the earnings season that starts now.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHICH STOCKS SAW THE BIGGEST DII STAKE RISES?
Swiggy (27.38% to 34.49%), One97 Communications (24.86% to 29.52%), Meesho (9.14% to 13.65%) and Cochin Shipyard (6.95% to 11.24%).
WHAT THRESHOLD WAS USED?
The scan covers NSE midcap stocks where DII shareholding rose more than 200 basis points quarter-on-quarter in Q2FY27.
WHAT DOES THIS SIGNAL?
Domestic institutions are actively accumulating midcaps, especially new-age tech platforms, even as foreign investors sell — evidence of the changing identity of the market's marginal buyer.
DOES HIGH DII OWNERSHIP GUARANTEE STOCK PERFORMANCE?
No. It indicates institutional conviction at quarter-end, but holdings can change quickly and past accumulation does not ensure future returns. GLOSSARY: - DII: Domestic institutional investor — Indian mutual funds, insurance companies, pension and provident funds. - Basis point: One-hundredth of a percentage point; 200 basis points equal two percentage points. - Shareholding pattern: The quarterly disclosure of a listed company's ownership by category of investor. - Marginal buyer: The market participant whose buying or selling most influences prices at the margin.
