Rupee Hits Record Low Near 96/USD; Bonds Fall on Iran War, Oil at $110

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Rupee slipped past 96-per-dollar to hit a record low, falling 5% since the Iran war began on February 28
  • 02/Brent crude climbed to nearly $110 per barrel, straining India's current account as it imports 90% of its oil
  • 03/India's 10-year bond yield rose 8 bps to 7.06%; traders see range of 7.00%-7.14% with RBI intervention key

The Indian rupee slipped past 96 per dollar last week to hit a fresh record low. The currency has fallen 5% since the Iran war began on February 28.

Brent crude oil prices climbed to nearly $110 per barrel. The Strait of Hormuz, which handles about a fifth of global oil flows, remains effectively shut.

Rising oil prices have sent global bond yields higher. Markets now expect central banks to tighten policy to rein in inflation.

India's 10-year benchmark bond yield ended at 7.0644% on Friday. It rose 8 basis points over the week, marking a third climb in four weeks.

India imports nearly 90% of its crude requirements. Higher oil prices widen the current account deficit and complicate fiscal calculations.

Policymakers imposed curbs on precious metal imports to ease rupee pressure. Most silver imports were restricted over the weekend.

The Reserve Bank of India has intervened in currency markets. It has also limited the size of banks' net open positions.

"Given escalating geopolitical risks, we expect the 10-year bond yield to touch 7.25% in fiscal first half in anticipation of 50 basis points rate hike in second half," said Arun Srinivasan, chief of fixed income at ICICI Prudential Life Insurance.
