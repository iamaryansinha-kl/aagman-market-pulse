India's 350 Million Tonnes of Farm Waste Could Generate 18,000 MW of Renewable Power

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/India produces nearly 350 million tonnes of agricultural residue each year, with a large share burned in fields or left unused
  • 02/MNRE estimates this biomass can generate over 18,000 MW of power; 979 biogas plants now operational across half of India's districts
  • 03/GOBARdhan scheme and National Bioenergy Programme are driving the shift from stubble burning to resource recovery

India generates nearly 350 million tonnes of agricultural residue every year. This includes crop residues, husk, straw, and food processing by-products.

A significant share of this biomass is either burned in open fields or left to decay. Stubble burning remains a major source of air pollution across Delhi-NCR, Punjab, Haryana, and Madhya Pradesh.

According to the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy, agricultural waste holds the potential to generate over 18,000 megawatts of power. This makes it one of India's largest untapped renewable energy sources.

As of January 2026, nearly 979 biogas plants are operational across the country. These plants cover over half of India's districts under the GOBARdhan scheme and the National Bioenergy Programme.

The conversion of biomass into energy also produces organic fertilisers. This reduces dependence on chemical inputs and improves soil quality over time.

Beyond energy output, biomass programmes create rural employment. They reduce fossil fuel dependence and support circular economy practices at the village level.

Efficient collection and logistics remain the biggest barriers. Agricultural residue is spread across rural areas, making aggregation a costly and fragmented challenge.

"The shift from stubble burning to resource recovery reflects a broader move toward circular economy practices that convert waste into sustainable value." - Vishal Khalde, Founder & CEO, Blue Planet Biofuels
