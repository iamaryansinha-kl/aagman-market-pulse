IT Stocks Fall as OFSS Tumbles 7% on Oracle Debt Pressure; Trump Extends H-1B Fee

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

IT stocks came under pressure on Monday despite positive broader market sentiment. Oracle Financial Services Software (OFSS) led the decline, falling more than 7% to Rs 11,061. That puts the stock on track for its sharpest single-day fall since late July.

OFSS is the top loser on the Nifty IT index, which itself was down over half a percent.

Why is OFSS falling? The trigger is a Financial Times report that around $18 billion in loans tied to an Oracle-leased data centre in New Mexico has come under pressure. Syndicate banks including Santander and Jefferies are quoting the loans at 89 to 91 cents on the dollar.

The loans are connected to Oracle’s 1,400-acre “Project Jupiter” campus in Dona Ana County. Local opposition over concerns about water supply and air quality could derail Oracle’s massive AI infrastructure build-out. OFSS is the Indian subsidiary of Oracle.

Trump extends H-1B visa fee Other IT stocks came under pressure after US President Donald Trump extended the $100,000 (around Rs 83 lakh) fee on employers for H-1B visas by another year. The fee was originally imposed in 2025.

Trump, in a presidential proclamation issued Friday, said the 2025 decision led to a 92% decrease in H-1B registrations by large IT outsourcing firms. Indians remain the single largest beneficiary group of the H-1B visa programme.

Infosys, Wipro, and Coforge all fell up to 2% on Monday. Nifty IT was down over half a percent.

Goldman Sachs cautious on IT services Goldman Sachs issued a cautious note on the sector, stating that IT services demand remains weak despite broadly robust tech spending. AI-led deflation has become more broad-based and could continue for another one to two years.

The brokerage noted that IT services firms can help clients customise AI solutions. However, GCCs (Global Capability Centres) continue to grow faster than IT services companies, bringing more work in-house. Competition remains elevated across both new deals and renewals.

IT stocks have seen sharp swings this year. Earlier this month, Indian IT stocks rallied after OpenAI and Anthropic leaders called for a slowdown in AI development to manage risks. HSBC said India can serve as an “anti-AI” diversifier.
