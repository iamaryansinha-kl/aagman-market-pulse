Crude Oil Futures Drop to ₹9,417 as UAE Announces OPEC Exit

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/MCX crude oil for May delivery fell ₹68 or 0.72% to ₹9,417 per barrel on April 29 after UAE announced OPEC exit
  • 02/Brent crude eased 0.34% to $104.04 and WTI fell 0.68% to $99.25 amid US-Iran conflict uncertainty
  • 03/ING THINK expects limited immediate impact from UAE's departure but warns of medium-term supply increases

Crude oil futures fell nearly 1 per cent to ₹9,417 per barrel on Wednesday. On the Multi Commodity Exchange, crude oil for May delivery dropped ₹68, or 0.72 per cent.

The decline followed the United Arab Emirates announcing its exit from OPEC and the wider OPEC+ alliance. The departure becomes effective on May 1, 2026.

The UAE has been an OPEC member since 1967 through Abu Dhabi. It continued membership after the country was formally established in 1971.

In global markets, Brent crude for June delivery fell 0.34 per cent to $104.04 per barrel. West Texas Intermediate declined 0.68 per cent to $99.25 per barrel in New York.

Analysts at ING THINK said the UAE's departure will likely have limited immediate impact. However, it may raise supply prospects over the medium-to-longer term.

"Had this been announced at any other time, we would likely have seen more downward pressure on oil prices," ING THINK analysts said in a note.

Analysts said uncertainty over the US-Iran conflict and continued disruption in the Strait of Hormuz have capped losses. Near-term prices will follow Persian Gulf developments.

ING revised its Brent forecast to $104 per barrel for the second quarter and $92 for the fourth quarter. No imminent resumption of Hormuz oil flows is expected.
