India IIP Growth Likely Slowed to 2% in March on Energy Weakness: UBI

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/India's IIP growth likely moderated to 2% YoY in March 2026 from 5.2% in February and 3.9% in March 2025, per Union Bank of India research
  • 02/Core sector contracted 0.4% in March, its lowest in 19 months, with coal, crude oil, fertilisers and electricity declining
  • 03/Manufacturing PMI fell to 53.9 from 56.9; trade deficit narrowed to $20.67 billion as exports fell 7.4%

India's Index of Industrial Production (IIP) growth is expected to moderate to 2% year-on-year in March 2026. The figure is down sharply from 5.2% in February and 3.9% in March 2025, per a Union Bank of India research report.

The slowdown reflects broad-based weakness in manufacturing and energy sectors. Higher input costs and supply disruptions weighed on production margins and demand.

The core sector, which contributes nearly 40% to IIP, contracted by 0.4% in March. This marks its lowest level in 19 months, compared to revised 2.8% growth in February and 4.5% in March 2025.

Natural gas, refinery products, steel and cement recorded positive growth. However, coal, crude oil, fertilisers and electricity declined. Fertiliser production fell sharply by 25.9% on a month-on-month basis.

The manufacturing Purchasing Managers' Index fell to 53.9 in March from 56.9 in February. This is its lowest reading since June 2022, as new orders and output growth slowed.

Merchandise trade data showed the deficit narrowing to $20.67 billion in March from $27.10 billion in February. Both exports and imports contracted, falling 7.4% and 6.5% respectively.

E-way bill generation grew 12.9% in March, slower than 18.8% in February. GST revenue rose to 8.8% from 8.1%, reflecting sustained consumption and improved compliance.

Union Bank noted that persistently high crude prices, potential gas shortages and sluggish export demand could raise input costs. Faster infrastructure execution and capital goods demand remain key upside drivers for industrial momentum.
