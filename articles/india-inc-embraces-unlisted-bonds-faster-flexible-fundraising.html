India Inc Embraces Unlisted Bonds for Faster, Flexible Fundraising

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

Indian companies are finding a new route to capital.

More firms are embracing unlisted bonds through private placements to raise funds quickly and flexibly. The trend reflects a maturing corporate debt market and growing investor appetite for credit exposure outside traditional bank loans.

Unlisted bonds are issued through private placements to a select group of investors. Unlike public bond issues, they do not require extensive regulatory filings, roadshows, or ratings. This makes them faster to execute and more adaptable to the issuer's specific needs.

The market has grown substantially. Companies across sectors — infrastructure, real estate, manufacturing, and financial services — are using private placements to raise working capital, refinance debt, and fund expansion. Insurance companies, mutual funds, and pension funds are the primary buyers.

Lower costs are a key driver. Public bond issues involve underwriting fees, rating agency charges, and compliance costs. Private placements eliminate many of these expenses, making them attractive for smaller issuances or companies that need funds quickly.

Flexibility is another advantage. Issuers can negotiate terms directly with investors, including covenants, security packages, and repayment schedules. This is particularly valuable for companies with complex capital structures or unique financing needs.

The trend is supported by regulatory encouragement. The Securities and Exchange Board of India has streamlined rules for private placements, and the RBI has allowed banks to invest in certain categories of unlisted bonds. Market infrastructure has also improved with platforms for trading and settlement.
