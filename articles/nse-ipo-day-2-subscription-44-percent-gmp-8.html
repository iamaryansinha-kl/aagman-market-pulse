NSE IPO Day 2: Subscription Reaches 44%, Grey Market Premium Holds at 8%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

The National Stock Exchange's maiden public offer has entered its second day with steady, if unspectacular, demand. By Friday morning, the Rs 22,569 crore IPO was subscribed about 44% of the 8.86 crore shares on offer, exchange data showed.

The issue opened on Thursday and had a measured first day. Investors bid for 3.70 crore shares against the 8.86 crore on offer, a subscription of 0.43 times by 7 pm. The non-institutional investor category led the way at 72%, retail came in at 44%, and qualified institutional buyers booked 19% of their quota on day one.

The grey market is pricing in a modest gain. The GMP stands around Rs 142 per share, or roughly 8% over the upper price band of Rs 1,785, which implies an expected listing price near Rs 1,927.

The IPO is entirely an offer for sale of 12.64 crore shares by ten existing shareholders, so NSE itself will not receive any proceeds. The price band is Rs 1,700 to Rs 1,785, with a lot size of eight shares and a minimum retail application of Rs 14,280. At the cap, the post-issue market capitalisation works out to about Rs 4.42 lakh crore. The issue closes on September 21 and the stock is expected to list on rival BSE on September 24.

Brokerage houses are broadly positive. LKP Securities has a Subscribe rating, and YES Securities argues NSE is available at a 21% discount to BSE on price-to-earnings, with BSE trading at 54.3 times FY26 diluted earnings against NSE's 42.9 times at the cap price. Angel One pegs the post-issue P/E at 35.4 times and also recommends subscribing, calling it a rare chance to own India's dominant market infrastructure company.

The long-term case rests on NSE's entrenched position. Its market share as of June 2026 stood at about 93% in the cash market, 99.7% in equity futures and 68.5% in equity options by premium turnover, with 132.4 million registered investors and 3,005 listed companies.

The risk is concentration. Transaction charges contributed 78.7% of FY26 revenue, and options alone accounted for 60.2% of revenue from operations. NSE's equity options market share has already slipped from 96.86% in FY24 to 68.48% in the June 2026 quarter. FY26 revenue fell 3.1% to Rs 16,601 crore and profit after tax dropped to Rs 10,302 crore from Rs 12,188 crore.

The bottom line for applicants: the 8% GMP signals positive but not euphoric demand, the valuation is full but cheaper than BSE, and the regulatory trajectory of the options business is the swing factor.
