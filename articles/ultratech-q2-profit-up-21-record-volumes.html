UltraTech Q2 Profit Up 21% to Rs 1,494 Crore on 14% Volume Growth

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Consolidated net profit rose 21% YoY to Rs 1,494 crore from Rs 1,232 crore.
  • •- Revenue rose 19% YoY to Rs 23,295 crore; net sales grew 19% to Rs 23,024 crore.
  • •- Domestic grey cement volumes rose 14% to 35.94 million tonnes; capacity utilisation hit 71% from 66%.
  • •- Profit fell 43% sequentially from Rs 2,599 crore as margins normalised from Q1's 21% to 16%.
  • •- The company flagged monsoon disruption and the West Asia crisis as quarter-specific headwinds.
  • •- Its new wires and cables business UltraVolt began operations from a Jhagadia, Gujarat plant.

The country's largest cement maker delivered on Monday the quarter the monsoon usually denies. UltraTech Cement reported consolidated net profit of Rs 1,494 crore for the July-September period, up 21 per cent from Rs 1,232 crore a year earlier, on revenue that rose 19 per cent to Rs 23,295 crore. The sequential comparison is the honest one for a business this seasonal: profit fell 43 per cent from the June quarter's Rs 2,599 crore, and operating margins compressed from 21 per cent to 16 per cent as monsoon rains and the West Asia crisis weighed. The company itself named both headwinds in its exchange filing — an unusually direct acknowledgment that this quarter's margins carried a geopolitical freight cost.

The volume number is the quarter's structural fact. Domestic grey cement sales rose 14 per cent year-on-year to 35.94 million tonnes, and capacity utilisation jumped to 71 per cent from 66 per cent a year ago. In an industry that spent two years in a demand trough, a double-digit volume print from the market leader is evidence that the construction cycle — housing completion, infrastructure execution and pre-election government spending — is finally converting into dispatches. Costs moved the other way, as they have all year: total expenses rose 18 per cent to Rs 21,339 crore, leaving net margin at 7 per cent against 6 per cent a year earlier but well below the 11 per cent of the seasonally stronger June quarter. Net worth stood at Rs 77,702 crore, down about 7 per cent sequentially, and the company attributed the sequential margin compression squarely to the quarter's twin headwinds rather than to any deterioration in the underlying cost curve.

The strategic story sits beside the P&L. During the quarter UltraTech formally launched UltraVolt, its entry into the wires and cables business — a diversification that takes India's largest cement franchise into the electricals aisle of the same construction site it already supplies. A manufacturing facility has been commissioned at Jhagadia in Gujarat, and the planned capacity — consuming 78,000 tonnes of copper and 24,000 tonnes of aluminium — already positions the business as the country's second-largest player in housing wires by capacity. Management said early market response from retailers, electricians and distributors has been strong; the strategic logic is that the wires business monetises UltraTech's unmatched dealer network while hedging the core franchise against the cement cycle's long winters. It is also a bet on the same electrification supercycle that is driving the broader market's enthusiasm for power and grid-exposed stocks — every new home wired is, in effect, cement demand converted into copper demand, and UltraTech now sells both sides of that trade.

The market's reaction was a shrug with a reason. The stock traded about 2 per cent lower at Rs 10,690 after the numbers — not a verdict on the quarter, which beat the year-ago base cleanly, but a reflection of a stock already down more than 10 per cent in 2026 and 14 per cent over the past year, caught in the market's current preference for anything that isn't a rate-sensitive industrial. The read-through from results season has not helped: a market that punishes private banks for good numbers and rewards PSU banks for better ones is not allocating on quality, and cement — leveraged to energy costs, infrastructure budgets and the housing cycle all at once — requires a conviction about the rate path that few managers currently hold. That longer chart flatters the patient: three-year returns exceed 25 per cent and five-year returns approach 49 per cent, and the franchise's advantages — lowest-cost capacity, national distribution, and now a second product line — compound regardless of which way the multiple moves in a given quarter.

What the quarter establishes is that India's cement demand has crossed into a volume-led phase where growth no longer depends on price. The industry's capacity additions of the last three years — much of it commissioned into a weak market — are now being absorbed, and the leaders with the lowest cost curves are taking the largest share of the recovery. With capacity utilisation at 71 per cent and still climbing, pricing discipline — the variable that determines whether volumes convert into margins — becomes the number to watch through the second half; even modest realisation recovery on this volume base would compound meaningfully into EBITDA. The headwinds UltraTech named are also notably external: the monsoon is calendar, and the West Asia crisis, while it inflates energy costs, is the same shock that has pushed government capital spending toward energy security and logistics — both cement-intensive programmes. UltraVolt, meanwhile, gives the company a second engine whose margins behave nothing like clinker. If the construction cycle holds and energy costs stabilise, the June quarter's 21 per cent margins are the destination; Monday's print was the fare for the ride.
