SEBI Proposes Overhaul of Securitisation Norms to Align With RBI Rules

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/SEBI proposed sweeping changes to securitisation norms on May 4, 2026, aiming to bring its framework in line with RBI regulations and boost the listed market.
  • 02/The proposal allows RBI-regulated entities to conduct single-asset securitisation and exempts them from the current 25% cap on exposure to a single asset.
  • 03/SEBI is seeking public comments on the proposal as it looks to reduce regulatory overlap and shift India's securitisation market away from private placements.

SEBI unveiled a proposal on May 4, 2026, to overhaul its securitisation framework. The move is designed to bring the regulator's norms in closer alignment with those of the RBI.

The proposed changes would allow RBI-regulated entities to undertake single-asset securitisation. Currently, such transactions are restricted, limiting flexibility for banks and financial institutions.

Under the existing framework, originators face a 25% cap on exposure to any single asset within a securitisation pool. SEBI's proposal would exempt RBI-regulated entities from this cap entirely.

The draft amendments also include revisions to rules governing Securitised Debt Instruments, or SDIs. These instruments are critical to how packaged loans and receivables trade in the secondary market.

India's securitisation market has long been dominated by private placements. SEBI wants to change that by creating conditions that encourage listed securitisation offerings.

A more vibrant listed market could attract broader investor participation, including from mutual funds and foreign portfolio investors. It would also improve price discovery and transparency.

Regulatory overlap between SEBI and RBI has created friction for market participants in the past. Harmonising the two frameworks is expected to reduce compliance complexity.

SEBI has invited public comments on the draft proposal. Stakeholders now have a window to weigh in before the changes are finalised and implemented.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT DID SEBI PROPOSE ON MAY 4, 2026?
SEBI PROPOSED CHANGES TO SECURITISATION NORMS TO ALIGN ITS FRAMEWORK WITH RBI REGULATIONS, INCLUDING ALLOWING SINGLE-ASSET SECURITISATION AND REMOVING THE 25% CAP ON EXPOSURE FOR RBI-REGULATED ENTITIES.
WHICH ENTITIES WOULD BENEFIT FROM THE SINGLE-ASSET SECURITISATION PROVISION?
RBI-REGULATED ENTITIES SUCH AS BANKS AND NON-BANKING FINANCIAL COMPANIES WOULD BE ALLOWED TO CONDUCT SINGLE-ASSET SECURITISATION UNDER THE PROPOSED RULES.
WHAT IS THE CURRENT 25% CAP AND WHO WOULD BE EXEMPTED?
ORIGINATORS CURRENTLY CANNOT HAVE MORE THAN 25% EXPOSURE TO A SINGLE ASSET IN A SECURITISATION POOL. RBI-REGULATED ENTITIES WOULD BE EXEMPTED FROM THIS CAP UNDER THE NEW PROPOSAL.
WHY DOES SEBI WANT TO BOOST THE LISTED SECURITISATION MARKET?
INDIA'S SECURITISATION MARKET IS CURRENTLY DOMINATED BY PRIVATE PLACEMENTS. A LISTED MARKET WOULD ATTRACT MORE INVESTORS, IMPROVE PRICE DISCOVERY, AND INCREASE TRANSPARENCY.
WHAT IS THE NEXT STEP IN THE PROCESS?
SEBI HAS ISSUED THE PROPOSAL FOR PUBLIC COMMENT. STAKEHOLDERS CAN SUBMIT FEEDBACK BEFORE THE REGULATOR FINALISES AND IMPLEMENTS THE AMENDED NORMS.
