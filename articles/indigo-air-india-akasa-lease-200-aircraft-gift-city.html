IndiGo, Air India, Akasa Pledge 200+ Aircraft Leases via GIFT City IFSC

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Four Indian airlines pledge to lease over 200 aircraft through GIFT City IFSC, nearly tripling current leased fleet of 101 planes
  • 02/IndiGo plans to grow from 78 to 150 aircraft by March 2027; Air India targets 75 aircraft in FY27; Akasa commits to 60 over five years
  • 03/The push comes as Indian carriers hold orders for 1,631 aircraft, driving demand for domestic aviation financing infrastructure

Four Indian airlines have pledged to lease over 200 aircraft through Gujarat's GIFT City International Financial Services Centre. IndiGo, Air India, Akasa Air, and Star Air announced the commitments at the Aircraft Leasing and Financing Summit 2.0 on Friday.

IndiGo's leasing arm, InterGlobe Aviation Financial Services IFSC, plans to nearly double its portfolio. It will grow from 78 aircraft as of March 2026 to 150 aircraft by March 2027. The additional aircraft are estimated at $3.5 billion to $4 billion.

Air India subsidiary AI Fleet Services aims to expand from 16 aircraft to as many as 75 during FY27. The assets involved are worth approximately $2.5 billion to $3 billion.

Akasa Air received approval on Thursday to set up Akasa Air Leasing IFSC Pvt Ltd. It plans to lease 60 aircraft over the next five years through GIFT City structures. Star Air subsidiary Ghodawat Aviation IFSC will lease six to eight regional aircraft.

Only 101 commercial aircraft have been leased through GIFT IFSC so far. Of these, 78 are from IndiGo and 16 from Air India. The new pledges would nearly triple this number.

K Rajaraman, chairperson of the International Financial Services Centres Authority, said 40 aircraft lessors now operate in GIFT IFSC. The centre currently has 203 aircraft and 84 engines leased, with total aviation assets exceeding 373. International banking units have funded $855 million in aviation debt.

Indian carriers hold combined orders for 1,631 aircraft. This includes 1,245 Airbus planes and 386 Boeing aircraft. IndiGo alone has ordered 901 aircraft, while the Air India Group has 541 on order. Akasa Air holds orders for 189 Boeing 737 Max jets.

Civil Aviation Secretary Samir Kumar Sinha said the sector must shift from leasing to large-scale ownership. He noted that aircraft have economic lives exceeding 20 years and require long-tenure domestic capital. The Ministry is exploring financing from institutions such as NaBFID, NIIF, and pension funds.
