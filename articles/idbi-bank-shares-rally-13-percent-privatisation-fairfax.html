IDBI Bank Shares Rally 13% as Market Value Swells Rs 11,460 Crore

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- IDBI Bank shares jumped nearly 13% to Rs 93.60 on the NSE, adding over Rs 11,460 crore to its market capitalisation.
  • •- The bank's market value crossed the Rs 1 lakh crore mark, hitting the highest level in nearly two weeks.
  • •- Fairfax has emerged as the frontrunner to acquire a combined 61% stake from the government and LIC, at an implied valuation of about Rs 53,000 crore.
  • •- The deal would top Emirates NBD's $2.75 billion purchase of RBL Bank as the biggest foreign investment in an Indian bank.
  • •- IDBI's Q2FY27 provisional business grew 18% year-on-year to Rs 6.27 lakh crore, with deposits and advances both up 17-18%.

Shares of IDBI Bank rallied nearly 13 per cent on Wednesday, adding more than Rs 11,460 crore to its market capitalisation and bucking an otherwise muted market, as the long-running privatisation of the state-run lender moved closer to its conclusion.

The stock jumped to Rs 93.60 apiece on the NSE, its highest level in nearly two weeks, and the bank's market capitalisation crossed the Rs 1 lakh crore mark on the surge.

The proximate driver is progress in the stake sale. Fairfax has emerged as the frontrunner to acquire a combined stake of nearly 61 per cent being sold by the government and Life Insurance Corporation: the government plans to sell its 30.48 per cent, while LIC is looking to offload another 30.24 per cent. Prem Watsa-owned Fairfax Holdings raised its offer to around Rs 81 per share earlier this year, valuing the stake at roughly Rs 53,000 crore, or about $5.5 billion.

The numbers involved would set a record. Emirates NBD's purchase of a 60 per cent stake in RBL Bank for $2.75 billion in 2025 currently stands as the biggest foreign investment in an Indian bank; the IDBI transaction, at nearly double that size, would decisively top it, marking a watershed for overseas capital in the country's financial sector. The successful bidder will have to make an open offer to public shareholders and clear a final 'fit and proper' assessment by the Reserve Bank of India, along with approvals from statutory authorities including the Competition Commission of India.

There is also a fresh fundamental tailwind. Earlier this month, IDBI Bank released its provisional business update for the July-September quarter, reporting an 18 per cent year-on-year increase in total business to Rs 6.27 lakh crore. Total deposits rose 17 per cent to Rs 3.56 lakh crore, current and savings account deposits grew 12 per cent to Rs 1.56 lakh crore, and net advances rose 18 per cent to Rs 2.72 lakh crore.

The rally, then, is really three stories arriving at once: a privatisation process that has been running since 2021 and finally has a frontrunner, a quarterly business update confirming double-digit growth, and a banking sector entering earnings season with loan books expanding at their fastest pace in years.

That broader context comes from JM Financial, which noted that provisional business updates for 30 banks show loan growth accelerating sharply to 19 per cent year-on-year and deposit growth to nearly 16 per cent, aided meaningfully by the FCNR-B mobilisation drive since June 2026. The brokerage expects margins to contract 5-20 basis points quarter-on-quarter, mainly for private banks dealing with FCNR-B inflows and muted CASA growth, while treasury income comes under pressure from rising yields. On the positive side, operating expense growth of 7 per cent should continue to trail balance sheet growth of 20 per cent, and credit costs remain benign at around 50 basis points.

For IDBI specifically, the privatisation thesis has always been about what a cleaned-up, professionally run franchise is ultimately worth outside government ownership. The bank's return to the Rs 1 lakh crore market capitalisation club on Wednesday is a marker of how far sentiment has travelled: investors are no longer pricing the stock as a perennially stalled disinvestment story, but as an imminent change-of-control event with a named buyer.

The risks are equally clear. The Fairfax offer of around Rs 81 sits below the current market price, and Wednesday's surge widens that gap further — the market is now pricing in either a sweetened bid or scarcity value around the open offer. If the process stalls again on regulatory clearances, the premium embedded in the stock can unwind just as quickly.

Still, the direction of travel has not looked this certain in four long years of stop-start effort. With the RBI's fit-and-proper assessment and CCI clearance the remaining gates, and a Q2 update showing 18 per cent business growth to underline the asset being sold, IDBI Bank's privatisation has moved from perpetual promise to visible finish line.

The wider significance is what the deal would say about India's openness to foreign control of its banks. Every previous foreign acquisition in Indian banking has been a minority or small-stake affair; a Fairfax-controlled IDBI would be the first time a global investor takes outright control of a major domestic lender. The RBI's final assessment will therefore be watched far beyond this one stock, as a template — or a warning — for the next decade of bank M&A in the country.

For now, the market is voting with its wallet. A 13 per cent single-day move in a Rs 1 lakh crore bank is not retail froth; it is institutional repositioning around an event the market now considers probable rather than possible. The coming weeks of regulatory filings will show whether that confidence is justified.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHY DID IDBI BANK SHARES SURGE?
Shares rose nearly 13% to Rs 93.60 as the privatisation process moved closer to conclusion, with Fairfax the frontrunner, alongside a strong Q2 business update showing 18% growth.
WHAT IS FAIRFAX BUYING?
Fairfax is the frontrunner for a combined 61% stake — the government's 30.48% and LIC's 30.24% — at around Rs 81 per share, valuing the stake at roughly Rs 53,000 crore or $5.5 billion.
HOW DID IDBI'S Q2 BUSINESS PERFORM?
Total business grew 18% year-on-year to Rs 6.27 lakh crore, with deposits up 17% to Rs 3.56 lakh crore, CASA up 12% to Rs 1.56 lakh crore and advances up 18% to Rs 2.72 lakh crore.
WHAT APPROVALS ARE STILL NEEDED?
Fairfax must make an open offer to public shareholders, clear the RBI's 'fit and proper' assessment, and obtain approvals from statutory authorities including the Competition Commission of India. GLOSSARY: - Privatisation: The sale of government ownership in a state-run company to private investors. - FCNR-B: Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank) deposits, a tool banks use to raise dollar funding from the diaspora. - CASA: Current account and savings account deposits, a bank's cheapest source of funding. - Fit and proper assessment: The RBI's review of a prospective bank owner's credentials, integrity and financial standing. - Open offer: A mandatory additional share purchase offer to public shareholders triggered by a change of control.
