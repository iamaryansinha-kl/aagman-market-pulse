Qualcomm AI Strategy 2026: $40B Revenue Target, Data Center Chip Push

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Qualcomm doubles its non-handset revenue target to $40 billion by fiscal 2029, up from a prior $22 billion goal
  • 02/Data center chip sales are projected to exceed $15 billion by 2029, with Meta committed to using the new Dragonfly C1000 CPU
  • 03/Bank of America raised its price target to $220 from $195 but maintained an Underperform rating on valuation concerns

Qualcomm Inc. has unveiled its most ambitious diversification plan yet. The company doubled its non-handset revenue target to $40 billion by fiscal 2029 at its June 24 Investor Day.

The data center business alone is expected to contribute over $15 billion. This marks a decisive pivot away from the cyclical smartphone market.

Qualcomm revealed the Dragonfly C1000, a data-center CPU designed for agentic AI workloads. Meta Platforms has committed to using the chip when production begins in 2028.

The company also acquired Modular Inc., an AI software infrastructure firm, for approximately $3.9 billion in an all-stock deal on June 21, 2026. This strengthens Qualcomm's software stack for AI developers.

In fiscal Q2 2026, Qualcomm reported non-GAAP EPS of $2.65, beating the $2.56 consensus. Revenue reached $10.60 billion, slightly above the $10.59 billion estimate.

Automotive revenue surged 38% year-over-year to $1.3 billion. IoT revenue grew 9% to $1.7 billion. The automotive design-win pipeline now stands at $65 billion.

Qualcomm shares closed at $197.32 on June 24, down 3.33%. The stock surged 13% in after-hours trading to $222.97 following the announcement.

Bank of America analyst raised the price target to $220 from $195. However, the Underperform rating signals that AI optimism may already be priced in.

Competition remains intense. Nvidia and AMD dominate AI semiconductors. Qualcomm's success depends on executing its data center strategy faster than rivals.
