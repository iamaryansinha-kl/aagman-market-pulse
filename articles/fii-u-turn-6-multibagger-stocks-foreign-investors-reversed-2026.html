FII U-Turn: 6 Multibagger Stocks Where Foreign Investors Reversed Course in 2026

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Six stocks — Bajaj Consumer Care, Acutaas Chemicals, SML Mahindra, Dee Development, United Foodbrands, and RateGain — surged 102% to 265% after FIIs reversed selling
  • 02/FII data from ACE Equity shows foreign investors raised stakes in March 2026 quarter after cutting holdings in September and December 2025
  • 03/Analysts at Nuvama, JM Financial, and Tata Mutual Fund now favor large caps, pharma, and exporters amid macro uncertainty

Foreign institutional investors (FIIs) have been relentless sellers of Indian equities. But buried within that outflow is a sharper story.

In six select stocks, FIIs quietly changed their minds. They reversed course and started accumulating even as they continued exiting positions elsewhere. The result: multibagger returns.

Bajaj Consumer Care led the pack. The stock surged 265% from Rs 169.8 to Rs 619.7 in one year. FIIs had slashed their stake from 10.95% in June 2025 to 9.7% by December 2025. In March 2026, they aggressively bought back, raising the holding to 16.59%.

Acutaas Chemicals followed with a 187% gain. FII shareholding rose from 16.94% in June 2025 to 19.48% in March 2026. The stock climbed from Rs 1,130.75 to Rs 3,248.45.

SML Mahindra delivered a 125% return. The reversal was dramatic — FIIs had cut stake from 15.73% in June 2025 to just 0.61% by December 2025. The March 2026 quarter saw a modest rebound to 0.77%, yet the stock more than doubled.

Dee Development Engineers gained 119%. FII holding inched from 0.81% to 0.99% — a marginal shift in percentage terms, but enough to send the stock from Rs 309.8 to Rs 677.65.

United Foodbrands rose 112%. FII accumulation was measured, from a trough of 9.66% back to 10.14%. The stock moved from Rs 316.7 to Rs 672.

RateGain Travel Technologies rounded out the list with a 102% gain. FII ownership rose from 5.51% in June 2025 to 5.35% in March 2026, after dipping to 4.97% in December. The stock climbed from Rs 431.9 to Rs 873.25.

Nuvama's strategy team favors high dividend yield sectors, less cyclical names, and exporters if the US-Iran deal goes through. Overweights include consumer, cement, chemicals, IT, private banks, and pharma.

JM Financial has repositioned toward pharma, healthcare, oil and gas, and metals. It is cautious on banks and automobiles where earnings growth is constrained.

Tata Mutual Fund believes large caps should outperform given cheaper valuations and earnings stability. Bandhan AMC CIO Manish Gunwani sees small caps as the most attractive for a 3 to 5-year horizon, with earnings growth for FY27 projected at around 15%.
