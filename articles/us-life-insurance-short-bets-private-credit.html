US Life Insurance Short Bets Hit $5.3 Billion on Private Credit Fears

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Short bets against US life insurers doubled to $5.3 billion in past year on private credit exposure fears
  • 02/US life insurers hold 35% of balance sheets in private lending; holdings doubled over last decade
  • 03/S&P 500 insurance index fell 5% YTD versus 4.7% broader market rise; Barclays sees 7% EPS drop

Short sellers' bets against US life insurance stocks more than doubled to over $5 billion in the past year. Traders added nearly $3 billion in short positions on 10 top US life insurers, bringing total bets to approximately $5.3 billion according to ORTEX data analyzed by Reuters.

The surge reflects growing concerns about exposure to the opaque private credit sector. Private credit holdings among US life and annuity insurers have more than doubled over the last decade, AM Best data shows.

US life insurers have roughly 35 per cent of their balance sheets tied up in private lending, the IMF reported citing Moody's data. This alternative credit offers higher yields but carries significant transparency risks.

The S&P 500 US insurance index has fallen almost 5 per cent this year. The broader S&P index rose 4.7 per cent during the same period.

Barclays analysts estimate collective earnings per share for 15 US life insurers will drop nearly 7 per cent this year. They believe markets are pricing in a fairly severe outcome.

Short positions in Principal Financial Group soared over 80 per cent to peak above 4 per cent in March. Brighthouse Financial hit a record high above 13 per cent on March 9.

"Concerns are not about a single blow-up, but about potential structural vulnerabilities with much less regulation than traditional banking," said Daniel Loughney, head of fixed income at Mediolanum International Funds.

Global short bets against insurance firms grew over 60 per cent to $31 billion in the year to April 15. Former insurance examiner Tom Gober calculates insurers made $1.54 trillion in transactions into opaque captive insurance subsidiaries.
