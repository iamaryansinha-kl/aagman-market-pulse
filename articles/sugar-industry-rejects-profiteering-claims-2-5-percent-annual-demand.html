Sugar Industry Rejects Profiteering Claims, Says Mills Sold Less Than 2.5% of Annual Demand at August Peak

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

The sugar industry has rejected claims of profiteering, stating that mills across India sold just over 7 lakh metric tonnes of sugar at elevated prices during a brief period in August. This accounted for less than 2.5% of the country's annual domestic consumption.

According to a joint statement by the Indian Sugar & Bio-energy Manufacturers Association (ISMA) and the National Federation of Cooperative Sugar Factories (NFCSF), sugar mills sold 7.22 lakh metric tonnes between August 17 and August 31, 2026, at an average ex-mill price of Rs 4,996.98 per quintal.

With India's annual domestic sugar consumption estimated at around 285 lakh metric tonnes, the volume sold during the elevated price period represented less than 2.5% of annual consumption. More than 97.5% of the country's annual requirement was marketed at normal or subdued price levels.

Prices Have Corrected Sharply

Retail sugar prices have fallen 11-12% to around Rs 57.5 per kg. State-wise retail prices recorded on September 21 were lower in several major markets. Sugar was priced at Rs 55.09 per kg in Uttar Pradesh, Rs 55 per kg in Delhi, Rs 56.74 per kg in Maharashtra, Rs 56.19 per kg in Karnataka and Rs 57.45 per kg in Tamil Nadu.

At the ex-mill level, average sugar prices across India are currently around Rs 4,450 per quintal, nearly 30% below their peak.

Season Average Below Cost

For the entire Sugar Season 2025-26 up to mid-September, the weighted pan-India average ex-mill realisation stood at around Rs 4,100 per quintal, or Rs 41 per kg. ISMA and NFCSF said this seasonal average remained significantly below operational costs, including statutory sugarcane prices and conversion costs.

Farmer Payments

During the current sugar season, mills have paid approximately Rs 1.12 lakh crore to farmers, representing around 97.5% of total cane payments due. For the 2024-25 sugar season, more than 99.5% of cane dues have been cleared.

ISMA Director General Deepak Ballani said the recent price correction needed to be viewed in the context of actual sales volumes during the brief period of elevated prices. He reiterated that the industry's priority remains uninterrupted and affordable supplies for consumers alongside timely payments to sugarcane farmers.
