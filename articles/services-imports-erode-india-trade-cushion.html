Faster Services Imports Erode India's Trade Cushion: ICICI Report

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Net services exports fell 10% year-on-year to $16.9 billion in September 2026, an ICICI Bank report said.
  • •- Gross services exports slipped 0.9% to about $36 billion while services imports jumped 8.9% to $19 billion.
  • •- The services surplus is the cushion that offsets India's merchandise trade deficit; a narrowing surplus weakens that defence.
  • •- Merchandise exports grew a strong 23% in September — the two sides of trade are moving in opposite directions.
  • •- For April-September, cumulative net services exports still rose 4% to $103 billion, so the September dip is not yet a trend.
  • •- Whether it becomes one depends on services export earnings keeping pace with the growing import bill.

The quiet half of India's trade account is making noise. India's services trade surplus — the steady foreign-exchange earner that has for years offset the country's large merchandise trade deficit — narrowed sharply in September, with net services exports falling 10 per cent year-on-year to $16.9 billion, according to an ICICI Bank research report. The driver was not a collapse in earnings but a divergence: gross services exports slipped 0.9 per cent to roughly $36 billion, while India's purchases of services from overseas grew 8.9 per cent to about $19 billion. Imports are now growing ten times faster than exports, and the surplus between them — the cushion — is thinning.

The significance of that arithmetic lies in what the cushion does. India runs a structural deficit in goods: the country imports far more merchandise than it exports, and the gap is financed in large part by the surplus earned selling services to the world — information technology, financial, business and professional services. As long as services exports comfortably exceed services imports, the current account stays manageable, the rupee's external funding need stays covered, and episodes like this year's oil shock remain absorbable. When services imports outgrow services exports, that protection erodes at exactly the margin that matters, because the services surplus is the last line of defence after portfolio flows — which, as October's Rs 52,100 crore of foreign selling demonstrates, cannot be relied upon in a pinch.

September's numbers land in an awkward juxtaposition. Merchandise exports grew 23 per cent year-on-year in the same month — a genuinely strong print, aided by the tariff-relief window for goods shipped to the United States — while the services side, the traditional powerhouse, went flat. The two engines of India's external account, which have reliably taken turns carrying it, are now both running below their service ceiling: goods because global demand and tariff regimes dictate the pace, services because the great post-pandemic outsourcing boom is maturing and because India's own companies and consumers are buying more services from abroad.

What they are buying matters. Rising services imports are not a distress signal in the way rising oil imports are; they partly reflect Indian corporates purchasing cloud, data, research and professional services from global providers — the flip side of digitalisation, and in part the cost of the AI buildout that has dominated this earnings season's commentary. An economy that imports the compute and consulting it needs to modernise is making a choice, not suffering a shock. But choices have balance-sheet consequences, and a services import bill compounding at high single digits while exports flatline will, within a few years, compress the net surplus by an amount large enough to matter to the current account even if nothing else goes wrong.

The report itself counsels against over-reading one month. For April-September 2026, cumulative net services exports still rose 4 per cent year-on-year to $103 billion, and the average monthly surplus in the first half of FY27 was $17.1 billion against $16.5 billion a year earlier. September could be a seasonal wobble, a data-revision artefact — the figures are provisional — or the first print of a genuine moderation. The honest answer is that one month cannot tell the difference, and the report is careful to say so. What it establishes is where to watch: not the headline surplus, but the growth differential between the two legs, because that is where the cushion's thickness is decided.

For the rupee, already defended through a year of record portfolio outflows, the stakes are direct. India's external funding has three pillars — the services surplus, remittances, and capital flows — and capital flows are negative while remittances are stable but slow-growing. That leaves the services surplus doing the work, at a moment when Brent above $100 is enlarging the merchandise deficit it must cover. A cushion that shrinks by 10 per cent in a single month does not break the account; it raises the price of defending it, in reserves and in interest rates. The September print deserves its asterisk. It does not deserve to be ignored.

There is a second-order irony in the timing. The same quarter that saw domestic institutions defend equities with record buying, and the same month that saw goods exports surge on the back of the US tariff waiver, also produced a services print that quietly weakened — three legs of the external account moving in three directions. Economies rarely get to choose which vulnerability surfaces; they get to choose which ones they watch. The services balance has been the least-watched line in India's external account for a decade, precisely because it was always strong. September's number is a small reminder that a cushion ignored is a cushion unmanaged, and the half-year data still offers time to manage this one before a wobble hardens into a structure.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
HOW MUCH DID INDIA'S NET SERVICES EXPORTS FALL?
They declined 10% year-on-year to $16.9 billion in September 2026, per an ICICI Bank research report.
WHY DID THE SURPLUS SHRINK?
Services imports grew 8.9% to about $19 billion while services exports fell 0.9% to $36 billion — imports outpaced exports tenfold.
IS THIS A SUSTAINED DECLINE?
Not yet. Cumulative net services exports for April-September rose 4% to $103 billion, and the report calls September's dip provisional.
WHY DOES THE SERVICES SURPLUS MATTER?
It offsets India's merchandise trade deficit, funding the current account when foreign capital flows turn negative. GLOSSARY: - Net services exports: The value of services sold to foreign buyers minus services purchased from foreign providers. - Merchandise trade deficit: The gap when a country's goods imports exceed its goods exports. - Current account: The broadest measure of a country's external transactions, including trade, services and transfers. - Provisional data: Early official estimates subject to revision in later releases.
