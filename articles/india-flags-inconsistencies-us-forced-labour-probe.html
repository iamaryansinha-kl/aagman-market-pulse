India Flags Inconsistencies in US Forced Labour Probe, Seeks Resolution

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

India is pushing back against US labour allegations.

The government has flagged inconsistencies in a US forced labour investigation and sought resolution through diplomatic channels. The dispute centres on allegations that Indian supply chains use forced labour, which could trigger trade restrictions under US law.

The US Customs and Border Protection agency has been investigating several Indian industries, including cotton, textiles, and seafood. If forced labour is confirmed, US authorities can issue withhold release orders that block imports of the affected products.

India contends that the US investigation has procedural flaws. The government argues that the methodology used to identify forced labour is inconsistent and that some of the allegations are based on outdated information or unreliable sources.

The stakes are high for Indian exporters. The United States is one of India's largest export markets, and a withhold release order could disrupt billions of dollars in trade. Textiles and apparel alone account for a significant share of Indian exports to the US.

The Indian government has engaged at multiple levels. Diplomatic channels, trade officials, and labour ministry representatives have all been involved in seeking a resolution. India has also highlighted its own labour law reforms and enforcement mechanisms.

The dispute reflects broader tensions in the trade relationship. The US has been increasingly aggressive in using labour and environmental standards as trade barriers, while India argues that developed countries should not impose unilateral standards on developing nations.
