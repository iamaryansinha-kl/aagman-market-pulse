India Services PMI Final 55.2: 3-Month High, Quarter Lowest

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/HSBC India Services PMI rose to 55.2 in September 2026 from 54.1 in August, a three-month high for services activity
  • 02/The final print fell short of the 55.8 flash estimate, and the Q2 FY27 quarterly average was the weakest since the March 2022 quarter
  • 03/Domestic demand drove the rebound while export orders grew at their weakest pace in nearly three years

India's services sector expanded at its fastest pace in three months in September 2026. The final HSBC India Services Purchasing Managers' Index rose to 55.2 from 54.1 in August, according to the survey released on Tuesday, October 6. A reading above 50 signals expansion.

The September reading marked a clear step up from August. It was the strongest monthly print for services activity since June 2026.

Even so, the final number came in below the preliminary flash estimate of 55.8 published in late September. That gap matters for how the month is read.

Flash estimates are built from early survey responses. The final print captures the full month, and the downward revision suggests momentum was slightly softer than early responses had indicated.

The bigger story sits at the quarter level. September's rebound was not enough to prevent the July-September period — the second quarter of fiscal 2026-27, or Q2 FY27 — from recording its weakest average since the quarter ended March 2022.

That makes Q2 FY27 the softest quarter for India's services sector in more than four years. The monthly improvement arrived, but not in time to lift the quarter as a whole.

Demand drove the September gain. The new business index, the survey's gauge of demand, rose at its quickest pace since June, pointing to firmer order flows across services firms.

The strength was home-grown. Domestic demand was firmer, even as international demand turned less buoyant.

Export orders were the weak link. Growth in international orders slowed to its weakest pace in nearly three years, marking a clear divergence between local and overseas demand.

That split defines the current phase of the cycle. Domestic demand is carrying services activity, while the export engine is losing steam.

Hiring cooled alongside the quarter's softness. Employment growth across services firms eased from August's pace.

Cost pressures stayed muted. Input cost inflation fell to its lowest level in 10 months, easing the squeeze on firms' margins.

Pricing power remained limited. Firms raised fees and charged prices at their slowest pace since June, even as their own input costs rose more slowly.

Business confidence improved but stayed subdued. Optimism rose to a three-month high in September, yet it remained weak by historical standards. Only around 16% of firms expected activity to increase over the coming year.

Manufacturing picked up some of the slack. Factory activity accelerated to its fastest pace since February.

Taken together, the two sectors pushed the composite PMI to a three-month high of 55.9 in September, up from 54.3 in August. The composite quarterly average was also the weakest since the January-March 2022 quarter.

The September survey therefore cuts both ways. Activity and new orders rebounded, confirming that the services sector still has domestic momentum. But the weakest quarter since early 2022, softer hiring, fading export orders and historically muted confidence show that momentum is narrower than the headline suggests.

GLOSSARY: Purchasing Managers' Index (PMI): A survey-based diffusion index tracking activity in the services or manufacturing sector. A reading above 50 indicates expansion compared with the previous month; below 50 indicates contraction. Flash vs final estimate: The flash estimate is a preliminary PMI reading published late in the month based on early survey responses. The final estimate, released at the start of the following month, incorporates the complete survey and can differ from the flash. Composite PMI: A weighted average of the manufacturing and services PMIs. It measures private sector activity across the economy as a whole. New business index: A PMI sub-index tracking the inflow of new orders. It is used as the survey's gauge of demand for services or manufactured goods. Input costs: The prices firms pay for raw materials, labour, fuel and other inputs. The input prices sub-index tracks inflation in these costs and is watched as an early signal of margin pressure and pricing trends.
