China's April Growth Slows Sharply: Industrial Output at 4.1%, Retail Sales at 0.2%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/China's April industrial output slowed to 4.1% YoY from 5.7% in March, weakest pace since July 2023 and below 5.9% forecast
  • 02/Retail sales growth collapsed to 0.2% from 1.7%, weakest since December 2022; fixed-asset investment contracted 1.6% in Jan-April
  • 03/Higher energy costs from Iran conflict and subdued domestic demand driving slowdown; exports offer partial offset

China's economy lost momentum in April as key growth engines weakened. Data from the National Bureau of Statistics showed industrial output, retail sales and investment all missing forecasts.

Industrial output rose 4.1% year-on-year in April. This slowed sharply from March's 5.7% growth and marked the weakest expansion since July 2023.

The figure fell short of market expectations for 5.9% growth. Higher energy costs linked to the Iran conflict squeezed factory margins across sectors.

Retail sales rose only 0.2% in April, slowing from 1.7% growth in March. This was the weakest increase since December 2022 and well below the 2% forecast.

Fixed-asset investment contracted 1.6% in the January-April period. This reversed the 1.7% growth recorded in the first quarter of 2026.

Domestic car sales fell 21.6% year-on-year in April. The decline extended a seven-month slump as consumers avoided large purchases tied to housing.

Exports accelerated in April as manufacturers rushed to meet AI-related demand. Overseas buyers also stockpiled components amid fears of further supply chain disruptions.

China's economy had expanded 5.0% in the first quarter. The slowdown raises questions about whether Beijing can meet its 4.5% to 5.0% full-year target.

THE SLOWDOWN WAS LINKED TO HIGHER ENERGY COSTS DRIVEN BY THE IRAN CONFLICT AND CONTINUED SOFTNESS IN DOMESTIC DEMAND. WEAK HOUSEHOLD SPENDING AND THE ONGOING PROPERTY MARKET DOWNTURN ALSO WEIGHED ON GROWTH.

FIXED-ASSET INVESTMENT CONTRACTED 1.6% IN THE JANUARY-APRIL PERIOD. THIS REVERSED THE 1.7% GROWTH RECORDED IN THE FIRST QUARTER AND MISSED FORECASTS FOR A 1.6% INCREASE.
