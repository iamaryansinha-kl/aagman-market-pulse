EPFO Wage Ceiling Hiked to Rs 25,000 From Rs 15,000 After 12 Years; Up to 1 Crore Workers to Gain Cover From September 17

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/The EPFO wage ceiling has been raised from Rs 15,000 to Rs 25,000 per month, the first revision since September 2014.
  • 02/Union Labour Minister Mansukh Mandaviya said 50 lakh to 1 crore new beneficiaries could come under mandatory social security coverage as a result.
  • 03/The revised ceiling takes effect from Thursday, September 17, which coincides with Vishwakarma Jayanti.
  • 04/Employees earning Rs 15,000-25,000 will now be covered under EPF, the Employees' Pension Scheme and the EDLI insurance scheme.
  • 05/Employer and employee contribution amounts rise for the affected bracket; EY India said some workers may see lower take-home pay from higher employee PF contributions.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
What is the new EPFO wage ceiling and when does it take effect?
A/01/ The EPFO wage ceiling has been raised from Rs 15,000 to Rs 25,000 per month, effective Thursday, September 17, 2026. It is the first revision since September 2014.
How many workers will benefit from the EPFO wage ceiling hike?
A/02/ Union Labour Minister Mansukh Mandaviya estimated 50 lakh to 1 crore new beneficiaries. The cabinet's official estimate put the number at 51 lakh workers gaining mandatory social security coverage.
What benefits do newly covered employees get?
A/03/ Employees earning Rs 15,000-25,000 per month will now be covered under the Employees' Provident Fund, pension benefits under the Employees' Pension Scheme and insurance protection under the Employees' Deposit Linked Insurance Scheme, subject to applicable provisions.
Does the EPFO wage ceiling hike reduce take-home pay?
A/04/ For employees in the Rs 15,000-25,000 bracket whose contributions were restricted to the old statutory ceiling, mandatory employee PF contributions now apply on wages up to Rs 25,000. EY India said this will likely reduce take-home pay, while enhancing retirement savings. Employers face higher PF, pension and EDLI contributions for the same bracket.
