Yes Bank Unlikely to Pay AT1 Coupons Despite Court Ruling

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Yes Bank is unlikely to pay AT1 bond coupons due this year, despite the Supreme Court overturning the 2020 write-off.
  • •- The bank is preparing to seek a review of the ruling, while bondholders press for payment of coupons unpaid since the write-off six years ago.
  • •- Retail investors say they did not receive a coupon payment due October 18, three days after the ruling.
  • •- The bank carries a Rs 5,279 crore debit balance in its profit and loss account; losses from FY20-21 are expected to be fully offset only by FY2028.

Six years after Yes Bank wrote off Rs 8,400 crore of additional tier-1 bonds in a rescue that saved the lender and vaporised its bondholders, the money is back in legal play — and the bank still is not paying. People familiar with the matter told Reuters that Yes Bank is unlikely to pay coupons due this year on its AT1 bonds, despite the Supreme Court last week upholding a 2023 Bombay High Court ruling that set the write-off aside. The bank is preparing to seek a review of the ruling, one of the sources said. The announcement of potential non-payment this fiscal year has already been made, and the bank maintains that the final call on coupons rests with it.

The immediate flashpoint is practical. Retail investors holding the bonds said on Thursday they had not received coupon payments due on October 18 — three days after the Supreme Court ruling. In a letter to Axis Trustee Services, the debenture trustee, reviewed by Reuters, investors sought clarity on the status and urged the trustee to intervene. "We call upon the Trustee to obtain immediate written confirmation from Yes Bank as to whether this coupon has been paid, cancelled or remains outstanding," the letter said. The bondholders cited the Supreme Court's own order: payment of annual coupons "shall remain unaffected by the outcome of these appeals and must be made in terms of the Information Memoranda."

The bank's counter-argument rests on the bond documentation and timing. The sources said the documentation gives Yes Bank discretion over coupon payments, and the issue can only be considered once the AT1 bonds are reinstated on the bank's balance sheet — which has not yet happened. Reinstatement itself is a non-trivial exercise: the bonds must be restored as liabilities before any coupon logic runs, and the bank has signalled it will contest that through a review petition first. In short, Yes Bank is treating the ruling as a step in a longer legal process, not a final instruction to pay.

The financial constraint is real. Yes Bank carries a debit balance of Rs 5,279 crore in its profit and loss account, according to its latest annual report, and the losses accumulated in fiscal 2020 and 2021 are expected to be fully offset only by fiscal 2028, a third source said. AT1 coupons are paid out of distributable profits; with the P&L still in deficit, paying coupons would require regulatory comfort and a capital decision the bank may prefer not to make while the matter is still legally contested. It would also set a precedent: if the bank pays one coupon while disputing the underlying reinstatement, it implicitly concedes the bonds are live liabilities again, with consequences for capital ratios it has spent six years rebuilding. That arithmetic, not just legal strategy, is what could weigh on the coupon decision for years.

The backdrop matters. The AT1 write-off was the most controversial piece of Yes Bank's 2020 rescue, executed at the direction of the RBI's reconstruction scheme: institutional and retail bondholders absorbed a total loss while equity holders were diluted but survived. The instrument was designed to absorb losses precisely this way — contingent convertible bonds that convert to equity or are written down when a bank's capital buffers breach thresholds. What the courts have now questioned is whether the specific mechanics of that write-off followed the information memoranda and the Banking Regulation Act. The Supreme Court's ruling last week upheld the Bombay High Court's 2023 decision setting the write-off aside, in what was a setback not just to the bank but to the regulator that had defended the move.

For investors, the case is now a two-front contest. The legal front runs through the review petition and whatever reinstatement mechanics follow; the financial front runs through the bank's accumulated-loss position, which will not fully clear until FY2028. Bondholders are organised enough to write to the trustee and cite the court order; the bank is positioned enough to decline to pay while it litigates. Yes Bank did not respond to an email seeking comment, and the sources declined to be named as they were not authorised to speak on the matter. The stock closed Thursday at Rs 16.05, down 2.2%, a relatively mild reaction for a ruling that put Rs 8,400 crore of contingent liabilities back in play — a sign the market, like the bank, expects this to take years, not weeks.

For the bondholders, the fight is about more than one coupon date. Restoring the bonds to the balance sheet would reopen a Rs 8,400 crore claim, and every coupon left unpaid in the interim compounds their argument that the bank is enjoying the benefit of money the courts have said should never have been written off. For Yes Bank, the sequencing question is equally stark: reinstate first and the capital hit lands immediately; refuse and the legal fight continues while the P&L heals. The review petition the bank is now preparing is the next battleground — and until it is heard, the coupons due now will almost certainly stay unpaid.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT DID THE SUPREME COURT RULE?
Last week it upheld a 2023 Bombay High Court ruling that set aside Yes Bank's 2020 write-off of Rs 8,400 crore of AT1 bonds during the bank's rescue.
WHY HASN'T THE OCTOBER 18 COUPON BEEN PAID?
Bondholders say it remains unpaid three days after the ruling. Yes Bank's position, per sources, is that coupons are discretionary and can only be considered once the bonds are reinstated on its balance sheet.
WHAT IS YES BANK'S FINANCIAL POSITION?
It carries a Rs 5,279 crore debit balance in its P&L, with FY20-21 losses expected to be fully offset only by fiscal 2028. GLOSSARY: AT1 BONDS — Additional tier-1 capital instruments issued by banks that can absorb losses by converting to equity or being written off when capital buffers are breached. DEBIT BALANCE — An accumulated-loss position in a company's profit and loss account that must be offset by future profits before dividends or certain coupons can be paid. DEBENTURE TRUSTEE — An intermediary appointed to protect bondholders' interests and act on their behalf with the issuer.
