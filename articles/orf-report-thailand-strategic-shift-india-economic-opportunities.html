ORF Report: Thailand's Strategic Shift Opens Economic Doors for India

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/ORF report says Thailand is diversifying strategic partnerships, positioning India as a "third axis" alongside the US and China
  • 02/Five sectors identified as early-harvest opportunities: semiconductors, AI, automotives, MSMEs, and tourism
  • 03/Only 1.4 lakh Thai tourists visited India in 2025 despite strong civilisational ties; infrastructure gaps remain

An Observer Research Foundation (ORF) report says Thailand is recalibrating its strategic partnerships. India is emerging as a potential "third axis" for Bangkok alongside the US and China.

The report, authored by Jaibal Naduvath and titled "India and Thailand: The Compelling Case for a Deeper Compact", was published on May 7, 2026.

Thailand views the US as increasingly unpredictable. Washington's response to the 2014 political developments and tariff policies under President Donald Trump have strained trust.

China has expanded its footprint through investments and cultural engagement. But Bangkok remains concerned over Beijing's aggressive maritime posture and coercive geo-economic practices.

India's strategic autonomy and absence of "strategic baggage" make it an attractive long-term partner. The two nations share over two millennia of civilisational ties.

The report identifies five early-harvest sectors: semiconductors, artificial intelligence, automotives, MSMEs, and tourism. These could anchor a stronger bilateral relationship.

On semiconductors, Thailand dominates assembly, packaging and testing. India contributes around 20% of the global integrated circuit design workforce. A co-development model could link design and manufacturing.

In artificial intelligence, Thailand relies on foreign hyperscalers and seeks sovereign AI capabilities. India's pool of over one million AI professionals can help bridge this gap.

Thailand is the world's tenth-largest vehicle producer. India hosts around 50% of the world's automotive global capability centres (GCCs). An India-Thailand-Japan trilateral framework is proposed in this sector.

In tourism, only around 1.4 lakh Thai tourists visited India in 2025. Destinations such as Bodh Gaya, Sarnath and Kushinagar hold deep spiritual significance for Thailand.
