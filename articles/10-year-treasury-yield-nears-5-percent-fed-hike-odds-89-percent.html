10-Year Treasury Yield Nears 5% as Fed Rate Hike Odds Hit 89% on Hot Inflation Data

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Benchmark 10-year Treasury yield climbed to 4.983%, within striking distance of the psychologically critical 5% level
  • 02/Fed funds futures traders assign an 89% probability to a rate hike at the conclusion of the Fed's two-day meeting on Wednesday
  • 03/August consumer price data came in hotter than expected, reviving inflation concerns after months of cooling
  • 04/Employers added 162,000 jobs last month, reinforcing the case for tighter policy
  • 05/The Treasury will test demand with a $13 billion 20-year bond auction on Tuesday and $19 billion in 10-year TIPS on Thursday

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
What is the current 10-year Treasury yield?
A/01/ The benchmark 10-year US Treasury yield closed Monday at 4.983%, up 0.84 basis points, just below the key 5% psychological level.
Will the Fed raise rates this week?
A/02/ Fed funds futures traders assign an 89% probability to a rate hike at the conclusion of the two-day meeting on Wednesday.
Why are Treasury yields rising?
A/03/ Hotter-than-expected August inflation data, 162,000 jobs added last month, surging oil prices and heavy government debt supply have all pushed rate expectations higher.
Why does 5% matter?
A/04/ The 10-year yield underpins mortgages and other loans. A sustained move above 5% raises borrowing costs across the economy and tests whether equity markets can handle higher rates.
