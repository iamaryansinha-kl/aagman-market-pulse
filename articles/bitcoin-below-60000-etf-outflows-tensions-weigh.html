Bitcoin Below $60,000: ETF Outflows Hit $4 Billion, Tensions Weigh

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Bitcoin trades at $59,915 after falling below $60,000 on June 29, with June losses nearing 19%
  • 02/Spot Bitcoin ETFs saw over $4 billion in net outflows this month, the largest monthly withdrawal since their launch
  • 03/Investors remain cautious as Middle East tensions escalate and the Fed signals higher interest rates for longer

Bitcoin slipped below the $60,000 mark on Monday, trading at $59,915. The decline was driven by significant ETF outflows and escalating geopolitical tensions in the Middle East.

The cryptocurrency is on track to end June down nearly 19%. This marks its weakest monthly performance since mid-2022, according to Akshat Siddhant, Lead quant analyst at Mudrex.

Spot Bitcoin ETFs have recorded more than $4 billion in net outflows this month. This is the largest monthly withdrawal since the ETFs launched.

From a technical standpoint, a large cluster of short positions sits above the current price. This makes $60,000 a crucial breakout level for any near-term recovery, Siddhant added.

The global crypto market capitalisation edged down 0.2% to $2.06 trillion. Nearly 50,000 BTC have been moved to exchanges at a loss in the past day, according to CoinDCX Research Team.

"Bitcoin is trading around $59,500 after briefly reclaiming $60,000, as markets react to persistent ETF outflows and macroeconomic uncertainty," said Avinash Shekhar, Co-Founder and CEO at Pi42.

Vikram Subburaj, CEO at Giottus, said the fall was mainly driven by continued selling through U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs. Investors remain cautious as expectations grew that the U.S. Federal Reserve could keep interest rates higher for longer.

Piyush Walke, Derivatives Research Analyst at Delta Exchange, noted this is only the third time Bitcoin has started the year with two straight losing quarters. Q2 has historically been one of Bitcoin's strongest periods.
