Adani Ports Gets Creditors' Nod for ₹625 Crore Karanja Terminal Acquisition

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Adani Ports receives 100% creditor approval to acquire Karanja Terminal under IBC with ₹625.49 crore recovery plan
  • 02/London-listed MPL's one-time settlement proposal was rejected; company alleges process abuse and has NCLT hearing on July 1
  • 03/Acquisition would strengthen APSEZ's western coast footprint if NCLT clears the resolution plan

Adani Ports and Special Economic Zone Ltd (NSE: ADANIPORTS) has secured unanimous approval from the Committee of Creditors to acquire Karanja Terminal & Logistics Pvt Ltd. The resolution plan covers total unpaid dues of approximately ₹625.49 crore.

The creditors voted 100% in favour of Adani Ports' bid under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code framework. The decision came after rejecting a one-time settlement offer from existing promoter Mercantile Ports and Logistics Ltd.

Karanja Terminal is a port and logistics asset located near Mumbai on India's western coast. The facility has been under insolvency proceedings as lenders sought to recover outstanding dues through a structured resolution process.

The approval marks a key milestone, but the deal still requires final clearance from the National Company Law Tribunal. An NCLT hearing is scheduled for July 1, 2026, at the Mumbai bench.

MPL, the London-listed parent of Karanja Terminal, is mounting a legal challenge. The company claims its Section 12A settlement proposal was improperly rejected and alleges the process was "stage managed" to favour a pre-determined buyer.

MPL Managing Director Pavan Bakhshi said the company had deposited approximately ₹43 crore under a sanctioned settlement and was within the agreed payment timeline. He termed the outcome "nothing short of brazen."

The competing bid came from Prudent ARC, which submitted a ₹520 crore offer to the consortium of lenders. MPL claims this offer was being considered even before its own settlement was annulled.

If completed, the acquisition would add to Adani Ports' portfolio of strategic infrastructure assets. APSEZ already operates multiple port assets along India's western coast and has been actively pursuing distressed assets through insolvency proceedings.

MPL maintains that Karanja Terminal has never been operationally insolvent and remains revenue generating. The company recently entered into its largest contract with a state-owned oil and gas enterprise.
