Rupee Closes Flat at 96.2925 on Likely RBI Support

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/The Indian rupee ended Monday's session at 96.2925 per dollar, virtually unchanged from the previous close of 96.3150
  • 02/A firm dollar near a 17-month high and crude oil near $103 a barrel kept the rupee under pressure, likely RBI intervention kept it moored
  • 03/All eyes are on the RBI's monetary policy decision on Wednesday, where markets widely expect a 25 basis point rate increase

The Indian rupee closed at 96.2925 per dollar on Monday, October 5. The currency ended the session virtually unchanged from its previous close of 96.3150.

The rupee spent the day wedged in a thin trading range. Pressure from a broadly firmer dollar and higher crude oil prices was countered by likely intervention from the Reserve Bank of India, according to traders cited in the report.

The flat close masks a tug-of-war beneath the surface. On one side, global factors were pushing the rupee toward weaker levels. On the other, reported central bank presence in the market kept the currency from sliding.

The dollar hovered near a 17-month high on Monday. Euro weakness, driven by worries over France's fiscal situation, boosted the greenback against most peers.

Asian currencies reflected the dollar's broad strength. Regional units fell between 0.1% and 0.5% during the session, showing the rupee's flat close was relatively resilient in comparison.

Crude oil added to the strain on the rupee, nudging up to nearly $103 per barrel. Middle Eastern exports have increased, but concerns about ongoing war-related disruption have kept prices elevated.

That matters because India imports the bulk of the energy it consumes. Elevated crude prices raise the country's import bill and deepen demand for dollars, which weighs on the rupee over time.

Those global strains have left the rupee reliant on central bank action. Intervention, reported as likely rather than confirmed, has been needed to avert a test of the record low levels the currency hit in May.

In other words, the rupee's stability on Monday was purchased, not earned. Without official support, market participants believe the currency would face renewed downside pressure.

Hedging data illustrates why the pressure keeps building. Clearing house data for January through September shows importers booked $576.6 billion in forward hedges, far exceeding the $305.6 billion booked by exporters over the same period.

This persistently high importer hedging, combined with exporter reticence, has weighed on the currency. The asymmetry means steady dollar demand in the forward market even on days when the spot rate appears calm.

On the trade front, Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman said on Monday that India-US trade talks have reached a plateau. She indicated that further concessions might be difficult from here.

A stalemate in trade negotiations removes a potential source of relief for the currency. Progress on the trade front could have improved the outlook for capital flows and the rupee.

Attention now shifts to the RBI's monetary policy decision on Wednesday. Market participants widely expect the central bank to raise benchmark rates by 25 basis points.

"While the inflation shock is largely supply-driven, we expect the RBI to raise rates to prevent inflation expectations from de-anchoring," said Deepali Bhargava, regional head of research for Asia-Pacific at ING, in a note. "Markets will be focused on the RBI's guidance on the extent and duration of the tightening cycle."
