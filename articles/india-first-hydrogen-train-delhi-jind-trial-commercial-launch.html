India's First Hydrogen Train Clears Delhi-Jind Trial, Nears Commercial Launch

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Indian Railways' first hydrogen-powered train completes successful trial run between Delhi and Jind, testing emergency braking and oscillation parameters
  • 02/The 10-coach trainset delivers 2,400kW output and was built by ICF Chennai at a project cost of approximately ₹112 crore including infrastructure
  • 03/Hydrogen storage and refuelling facility at Jind has received PESO licence; pilot operations on the 90-km Jind-Sonipat route expected to begin soon

Indian Railways has completed a successful trial run of its first hydrogen-powered train between Delhi and Jind. The test evaluated critical safety parameters including emergency braking distance and train oscillation.

The 10-coach hydrogen fuel cell trainset is designed for the Jind–Sonipat section in Haryana. The Ministry of Railways confirmed that pilot operations are expected to begin shortly.

The hydrogen storage and refuelling facility at Jind railway station has received a licence from the Petroleum and Explosives Safety Organisation (PESO). This clears a major regulatory hurdle for commercial operations.

The trainset comprises eight passenger coaches and two Driving Power Cars with a combined output of 2,400 kW. It was manufactured by the Integral Coach Factory (ICF) in Chennai.

The train is designed for speeds up to 150 km/h. However, the pilot service will run at a conservative 110 km/h on the 90-km route, cutting the journey to about one hour compared to two hours for diesel services.

The project cost for the trainset and ground infrastructure runs to approximately ₹112 crore. A green hydrogen plant at Jind has been built at a cost of ₹120 crore over a 2,000-square-metre site.

The train emits only water vapour and steam during operation. It can carry around 2,500 passengers with fares expected to range between ₹5 and ₹25.

India is set to become the fifth country globally to operate a hydrogen-powered train, joining Germany, Sweden, Japan, and China. Indian Railways has a wider plan to deploy 35 such trains at an estimated cost of ₹2,800 crore.
