Gold Slips as Oil Prices Stoke Inflation Fears Ahead of Fed Meeting

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Spot gold fell 0.3% to $4,579.34 per ounce as rising oil prices stoked inflation fears ahead of Fed Chair Jerome Powell's remarks
  • 02/US gold futures for June delivery dropped 0.4% to $4,592.60, hitting the lowest level since April 2 in the previous session
  • 03/Analyst Zain Vawda warns gold remains sensitive to rate environment; if US-Iran deal reached, prices could hit $5,300-$5,500/oz by year-end

Spot gold prices slipped 0.3% to $4,579.34 per ounce on Wednesday. The decline came as rising oil prices fueled concerns over persistent inflation.

US gold futures for June delivery fell 0.4% to $4,592.60. The contract had dropped to its lowest level since April 2 in the previous trading session.

Markets are focused on the Federal Reserve's two-day meeting concluding today. The central bank is widely expected to hold interest rates steady, with Chair Jerome Powell's remarks watched for clues on rate hike risks.

High interest rates reduce gold's appeal as a non-yielding asset. The precious metal offers no income, making it less attractive when yields rise.

"Market sentiment has shifted toward skepticism regarding a potential US-Iran agreement," said Zain Vawda, analyst at MarketPulse by OANDA. He noted the "higher-for-longer" rate narrative is gaining traction.

Oil prices extended gains on reports the US will extend its blockade of Iranian ports. This would prolong supply disruptions from the key Middle East producing region.

Global gold demand rose 2% year-on-year in the first quarter of 2026. The World Gold Council reported strong bar and coin purchases, with central bank buying offsetting a 23% decline in jewellery demand.

"If the US and Iran can reach a swift deal, bulls could return," Vawda said. He projected gold could finish the year between $5,300 and $5,500 per ounce.
