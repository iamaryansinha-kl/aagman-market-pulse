JSW Steel Q2 Profit Soars 225% to Rs 5,279 Crore on Record Output

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Consolidated net profit rose 225% year-on-year to Rs 5,279 crore, from Rs 1,623 crore a year earlier.
  • •- Revenue from operations climbed 21% to Rs 54,486 crore; EBITDA rose 39% to Rs 9,941 crore, with margins at 18.3%.
  • •- Crude steel production hit a record 7.27 million tonnes and saleable steel sales a record 7.46 million tonnes.
  • •- Shares still fell around 3% to Rs 1,182 — a falling market does not pause to applaud results.

On any other Friday, this would have been the market's headline. JSW Steel reported a consolidated net profit of Rs 5,279 crore for the July-September quarter of FY27, a 225% surge from the Rs 1,623 crore it earned in the same period last year, as revenue from operations climbed nearly 21% to Rs 54,486 crore. EBITDA excluding forex gains and losses rose 39% to Rs 9,941 crore, and the adjusted EBITDA margin expanded to 18.3% from 17.2% a year earlier — driven by higher volumes, partly offset by higher coking coal prices and lower realisations. Total expenses rose a controlled 12% to Rs 48,168 crore in the quarter. On a day when the Sensex was busy erasing Rs 10 lakh crore of market value, however, even a triple-digit profit growth print could not hold the stock: JSW Steel fell around 3% to Rs 1,182 on the NSE after the numbers hit.

The operational numbers are the real story, and they are all records. JSW Steel produced a highest-ever 7.27 million tonnes of crude steel and sold a highest-ever 7.46 million tonnes of saleable steel in the quarter — up 13% year-on-year and 19% sequentially. Within that, sales of flats rose 16% and value-added and special products 11%, both record figures. Domestic sales hit a record 6.29 million tonnes, up 12% from a year ago and 18% from the June quarter, while institutional sales rose 15% to 4.15 million tonnes. Most strikingly, sales to the automotive sector were the highest ever, up 36% year-on-year — evidence that the auto-cycle strength visible across this earnings season runs through steel order books too. Exports contributed 9.8 lakh tonnes, or 13% of sales from India operations, giving the company a release valve when domestic realisations soften.

The management commentary doubled as a macro tour. JSW Steel noted that global economic activity stayed resilient despite geopolitical uncertainty, citing the IMF's recent upgrade of its 2026 global growth forecast by 10 basis points to 3.1%, with 2027 held at 3.4%. It flagged the Middle East conflict as the key wildcard, feeding energy markets and commodity prices, while "inflation and fiscal concerns have kept bond yields elevated and prompted major central banks to hike rates" — a line that reads as a direct description of the market rout unfolding outside its results. On the home front, the company leaned in: India remains a bright spot, it said, pointing to the RBI's upgrade of the FY27 GDP forecast to 7.1% from 6.7%, resilient engineering exports and strong automotive momentum. It also noted that AI-led investments, improving manufacturing indicators and resilient consumer demand are supporting global growth — an observation that places the company, perhaps unintentionally, in the small camp of Indian corporates publicly crediting the AI capex cycle for part of today's demand backdrop.

There is a quiet irony in the timing. Steel is precisely the kind of cyclical whose earnings should thrive in the environment JSW describes — record domestic demand, an investment-cycle upswing, a weak rupee flattering export realisations — and the quarter's numbers validate that thesis completely. Yet the same macro forces driving the profit surge (a commodity cycle strong enough to push coking coal costs up, an economy hot enough to invite rate hikes) are what's crushing the equity market around it. The 3% drop in the stock is less a verdict on JSW than on a tape where even Rs 3,656 crore of quarterly profit improvement cannot compete with a 55% crash in silver and an oil spike for investors' attention.

The quarter also fits a pattern visible across this earnings season: India's heavy-industry and manufacturing companies are posting volume records while their share prices deflate on global macro fears. For JSW Steel specifically, the composition of the sales record argues the strength is broad rather than narrow — domestic and institutional channels at records, exports at 13% of India volumes, and the auto book, often the first to crack when demand wavers, up 36% to its own all-time high. That breadth is what distinguishes a cyclical peak from a cyclical plateau, and the company clearly believes it is the latter.

For the full year, the volume trajectory matters more than any single quarter's margin mix: record production and sales with domestic demand at record levels gives JSW Steel operating leverage if steel prices merely hold, and the 36% auto-sales surge suggests the product mix is quietly upgrading. The exports lever — 9.8 lakh tonnes this quarter — provides a hedge against domestic softness, though it exposes the company to the same global rate-and-dollar headwinds it described in its own results commentary. But the market has stopped paying for that optionality this week — a reminder that on days like Friday, India's largest steelmaker and a falling knife are, briefly, the same chart.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT WERE JSW STEEL'S Q2 NUMBERS?
Net profit of Rs 5,279 crore, up 225% from Rs 1,623 crore a year earlier. Revenue rose 21% to Rs 54,486 crore and EBITDA climbed 39% to Rs 9,941 crore, with the adjusted margin at 18.3% versus 17.2% last year.
HOW BIG WERE THE VOLUME NUMBERS?
Record-sized. Crude steel production reached 7.27 million tonnes and saleable steel sales 7.46 million tonnes, up 13% year-on-year and 19% from the June quarter. Domestic sales were a record 6.29 million tonnes, flats and VASP sales were records, and auto-sector sales hit an all-time high, up 36%.
WHY DID THE STOCK FALL DESPITE THE PROFIT SURGE?
The broader market was in a severe selloff — the Sensex lost over 600 points and broader indices fell up to 4% on Friday — and even strong results were caught in the risk-off tide. The stock fell around 3% to Rs 1,182.
WHAT DROVE THE RECORD QUARTER?
Volumes, with a margin kicker. Crude steel production hit 7.27 million tonnes and saleable steel sales 7.46 million tonnes, both records, with domestic sales at a record 6.29 million tonnes and auto-sector sales up 36%. Margins gained on higher volumes, offset partly by higher coking coal costs and softer realisations — hence the EBITDA margin of 18.3%, only about 110 basis points above last year despite profit tripling. GLOSSARY: REALISATIONS — The average selling price a steelmaker actually achieves, as distinct from listed benchmark prices; falling realisations mean discounts are widening. VASP (VALUE-ADDED AND SPECIAL PRODUCTS) — Higher-margin steel products such as coated, galvanised and specialised grades, as opposed to basic commodity steel. PLANT LOAD FACTOR (PLF) — The average output of a power plant divided by its maximum possible output; India's coal-plant PLF rose to 68% in Q2 FY27 from 62%. COKING COAL — The key raw material for steelmaking; rising coking coal prices squeeze steelmakers' margins when they cannot pass costs through.
