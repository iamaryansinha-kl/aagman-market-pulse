Small Exporters Get Relief: Government Scraps RCMC Requirement for Shipments Up to Rs 3 Lakh

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/The government has removed the RCMC or Certificate of Registration requirement for export consignments with an FOB value of up to Rs 3 lakh.
  • 02/The DGFT amended Para 2.57 of the Foreign Trade Policy 2023 to provide the exemption wherever the certificate is otherwise required.
  • 03/Consignments up to $3,000 accounted for 43% of shipping bills but only 0.86% of India's merchandise export value between 2021-22 and 2025-26.
  • 04/The move targets MSMEs, first-time exporters, artisans and small businesses, letting them test overseas markets without upfront registration.
  • 05/Consignments above Rs 3 lakh will continue to require the RCMC wherever applicable; larger exporters' framework is unchanged.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
What is the new RCMC exemption for exporters?
A/01/ Export consignments with a Free-on-Board value of up to Rs 3 lakh no longer need a Registration-cum-Membership Certificate or Certificate of Registration. The DGFT amended Para 2.57 of the Foreign Trade Policy 2023 to provide the exemption wherever the certificate is otherwise required.
Why did the government scrap the RCMC requirement for small shipments?
A/02/ Consignments up to $3,000 made up 43% of shipping bills but only 0.86% of India's merchandise export value between 2021-22 and 2025-26. The exemption cuts compliance costs for MSMEs, first-time exporters and artisans, letting them test overseas markets without upfront registration with an Export Promotion Council or Commodity Board.
Do large exporters still need an RCMC?
A/03/ Yes. Consignments above Rs 3 lakh will continue to require the RCMC wherever applicable under the Foreign Trade Policy. The framework for larger exports is unchanged, and exporters crossing the threshold can then obtain membership of the relevant Export Promotion Councils or Commodity Boards.
Which export channels benefit most from the RCMC exemption?
A/04/ The ministry expects the exemption to support e-commerce, postal, courier and other emerging export channels that move low-value consignments, alongside small manufacturers, artisans and first-time exporters building an export track record.
