Wipro ADRs Crash 17% on NYSE, India Shares Up 1% Amid AI Disruption Fears

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Wipro ADRs crashed 16.89% to $1.870 on NYSE overnight as JPMorgan downgraded the stock to Underweight with a price target cut to $1.70
  • 02/Indian shares defied the ADR fall, rising 1% to Rs 172.74 on NSE on July 2, 2026, even as the stock hit a fresh 52-week low of Rs 169.25 on July 1
  • 03/JPMorgan cited generative AI pricing pressure and weak demand as key headwinds, while consensus earnings estimates may still be overly optimistic for FY27

Wipro Ltd (NSE: WIPRO) ADRs plunged 16.89% to $1.870 apiece on the NYSE overnight. The crash followed a JPMorgan downgrade to Underweight from Neutral.

JPMorgan slashed its price target to $1.70 from $2.20. The brokerage cited mounting pricing pressure from generative AI and weak demand as key headwinds.

Despite the ADR collapse, Wipro shares rose as much as 1% to Rs 172.74 on the NSE in July 2 trading. The stock opened at Rs 169.94 against the previous close of Rs 170.39.

The stock had hit a fresh 52-week low of Rs 169.25 on July 1, 2026. It is now down 36.47% year-to-date and 36% over the past year.

Wipro's 52-week high stands at Rs 273.10, touched on December 22, 2025. The stock has fallen 3% in a week and 19% in a month.

The company reported Q4 FY26 IT services revenue of $2,651 million, up just 2.1% year-on-year. Net profit declined 1.9% YoY to Rs 3,521.6 crore.

JPMorgan warned that consensus earnings estimates for Wipro could still be overly optimistic. Structural shifts from accelerating AI adoption are prompting enterprises to automate operations.

Peers told a different story. Infosys ADRs rose 1.53% to $10.65, while Cognizant gained 6.04% and Accenture surged 5.38% on the same session.

Wipro announced its largest-ever buyback of Rs 15,000 crore at Rs 250 per share. The record date was June 8, 2026, but the stock has fallen 17% since then.

The IT giant faces a fundamental challenge: reshaping its business model as AI reduces dependence on conventional outsourcing and exerts pressure on billing rates.

Wipro's FY26 large deal bookings of $16.4 billion is the highest ever. This provides a potential recovery catalyst, but execution risk remains high.
