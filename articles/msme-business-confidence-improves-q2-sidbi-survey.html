MSME Business Confidence Improves as Manufacturing Picks Up

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- The SIDBI MSME Business Confidence Index rose to 60.4 in Q2 FY27 from 59.3, staying above the 50 mark that separates expansion from contraction.
  • •- Manufacturing led the recovery at 61.0, up from 59.5, with services close behind at 61.3 and trading lagging at 57.5.
  • •- Firms expect conditions to stay expansionary: the forward-looking Business Expectations Index reads 61.7 for October-December 2026 and 62.2 for July-September 2027.
  • •- The sore spot is margins — between 20% and 30% of firms across sectors reported a decline, reflecting input costs that refuse to fall.

India's small enterprises spent most of this year hedged and cautious, caught between expensive credit, uneven demand and input bills that would not settle. The latest SIDBI MSME Outlook Survey suggests the mood is finally thawing. The Business Confidence Index rose to 60.4 in the July-September quarter from 59.3 in the April-June period, holding comfortably above the neutral mark of 50 that divides expansionary sentiment from contractionary. The quarterly survey canvasses around 1,200 micro, small and medium enterprises, roughly half of them manufacturers, and its latest read is the clearest signal yet that the small-economy slowdown of recent quarters is bottoming out.

The composition of the recovery is what makes it credible. Manufacturing confidence climbed to 61.0 from 59.5, trading improved to 57.5 from 56.5, and services edged up to 61.3 from 60.4 — a broad-based advance rather than a one-sector bounce. The sales detail supports the headline: 43% of manufacturing firms reported positive sales growth while the share expressing pessimism fell to 27%, and in services 45% were optimistic with pessimists down to 23%. Trading was the relative laggard — 38% positive on sales, but the pessimist share rose to 22% from 15% — which fits everything else we know about discretionary consumption at the wholesale and distribution level this year. Broad-based advances are what distinguish a genuine cyclical turn from a statistical wobble, and the fact that two of three sectors improved while the third only softened at the margin puts this one in the former camp.

Forward-looking expectations carry the usual survey optimism, but the levels are worth noting anyway. The Business Expectations Index stands at 61.7 for October-December 2026 and 62.2 for the year-out quarter — implying firms see conditions staying expansionary, not merely stabilising. Manufacturing's expectations index of 63.3 for the current quarter is the strongest sub-reading in the survey, and the share of manufacturers expecting better access to skilled labour is projected to rise from 25% to 40% over the coming year. For a sector whose biggest unquantified constraint has long been people rather than capital, that is a materially better pipeline than a year ago.

Margins are where the survey refuses to flatter. Between 48% and 58% of firms across the three sectors reported no significant change in profitability, while 20% to 30% reported a decline — the arithmetic of input costs that are still rising faster than most small firms can pass through. Trading firms reported the greatest margin strain. The partial offset: between 31% and 35% of firms expect margins to improve over the coming year, and the ones most confident on pricing power skew toward manufacturing. Anyone looking for the transmission of this year's commodity and energy cycle into Indian consumer prices will find the mechanism here — a small-enterprise sector absorbing cost pressure it cannot fully pass on.

Access to finance, the perennial MSME complaint, shows incremental rather than dramatic improvement. In manufacturing, 46% of firms reported better access to working capital, with the share reporting deterioration expected to decline from 14% to around 11% over the coming year. Services came in at 38% improved, trending toward 40%. Trading firms struggled most, with 31% reporting a decline in working capital financing in the September quarter — consistent with lenders tightening unsecured credit and distributors feeling it first. On overall finance availability, 47% of manufacturing and 44% of services MSMEs reported improvement. The direction is right, the pace is glacial, and it remains the single highest-leverage policy lever for converting this confidence bump into actual investment — every basis point of easier working-capital access shows up in MSME order books within a quarter.

The macro read-across is quietly important. Small enterprises are the economy's employment engine and its most honest demand sensor, and a confidence index climbing while margins compress tells you the demand side is recovering before the cost side has loosened. That is the early phase of a capex-and-hiring cycle, not yet the payoff phase. It also rhymes with what the banking results this weekend showed from the other end of the telescope: Shriram Finance's gold loans growing 68%, AU Small Finance Bank's advances up 25%, micro and small borrowers transacting and borrowing again. Credit and confidence are moving together, which is how recoveries in this economy actually start.

The survey also carries a policy footnote that policymakers will note. Roughly half of respondents are manufacturers — the segment most exposed to import competition and, lately, to the tariff volatility that has dominated India's trade news for months now — and their optimism is holding anyway. Trading firms, the channel through which imported goods reach domestic shelves, are the ones registering strain. If the government wants one clean read on where trade friction is biting hardest, the SIDBI data points squarely at the distribution layer rather than the factory floor.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT DID THE SIDBI MSME SURVEY SHOW?
The MSME Business Confidence Index rose to 60.4 in Q2 FY27 from 59.3, with manufacturing at 61.0, services at 61.3 and trading at 57.5. Expectations indices of 61.7 and 62.2 point to continued expansion.
WHICH SECTOR IS MOST CONFIDENT?
Manufacturing leads on both current confidence (61.0) and forward expectations (63.3 for October-December 2026), with services close behind. Trading is the laggard.
WHAT IS THE MAIN CONCERN?
Margins. Between 20% and 30% of firms reported declining profitability on persistent input-cost pressure, and trading firms reported the greatest strain on working capital financing. GLOSSARY: M-BCI — The MSME Business Confidence Index from SIDBI's quarterly outlook survey; readings above 50 signal expansionary sentiment. M-BEI — The MSME Business Expectations Index, the survey's forward-looking measure for upcoming quarters. SIDBI — Small Industries Development Bank of India, the principal development finance institution for the MSME sector and the survey's publisher. WORKING CAPITAL FINANCING — Short-term credit that funds inventory and receivables; the first casualty when lenders tighten.
