Fastener Traders Urge Govt to Roll Back QCO as Costs Surge, Supply Choked

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Fastener traders urge government to withdraw 2023 QCO amid rising costs, supply disruptions and manufacturing delays
  • 02/India's fastener imports stand at $1.13 billion; BIS certification has forced some firms to cut turnover by 50%
  • 03/DPIIT issued the QCO in 2023; traders say one-product-one-licence rule is unworkable for small-batch, high-variety production

Fastener traders have urged the government to withdraw the 2023 Quality Control Order on nuts, bolts and fasteners. The order mandates BIS certification before import.

The QCO imposes a rigid "one-product-one-licence" system. Traders say this creates duplication, delays and uncertainty for an industry that produces small batches in huge variety.

Shaunak Rungta, Central Executive Committee Member at FISME, said his firm's turnover has dropped by around 50 per cent. He warned that continued delays risk closure and urged the government to withdraw the QCO.

The problem is compounded by port delays from HS code confusion. This increases uncertainty and transaction costs for importers already facing certification bottlenecks.

Think tank GTRI said India imports $1.13 billion worth of fasteners annually. China supplies 24.6 per cent, followed by Japan at 13.3 per cent, South Korea at 9.9 per cent and Germany at 9.8 per cent.

GTRI Founder Ajay Srivastava said fasteners account for less than 1 per cent of final product cost. Their absence can halt entire production lines across automobiles, infrastructure and electronics.

Srivastava said restricting trade in such a low-value, high-utility input raises production costs. It undermines manufacturing competitiveness at a time when India is pushing for industrial growth.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT IS THE QUALITY CONTROL ORDER THAT FASTENER TRADERS WANT ROLLED BACK?
THE DEPARTMENT FOR PROMOTION OF INDUSTRY AND INTERNAL TRADE ISSUED A QUALITY CONTROL ORDER IN 2023 FOR NUTS, BOLTS AND FASTENERS. IT REQUIRES IMPORTERS TO OBTAIN BIS CERTIFICATION BEFORE BRINGING THESE PRODUCTS INTO INDIA.
WHY ARE FASTENER TRADERS DEMANDING A ROLLBACK OF THE QCO?
TRADERS SAY THE QCO IMPOSES A RIGID "ONE-PRODUCT-ONE-LICENCE" SYSTEM THAT CREATES DUPLICATION, DELAYS AND UNCERTAINTY. FASTENERS ARE PRODUCED IN SMALL BATCHES BUT IN HUGE VARIETY, OFTEN ON THE SAME MACHINE, MAKING THE LICENSING SYSTEM UNWORKABLE.
WHAT IMPACT HAS THE QCO HAD ON FASTENER BUSINESSES?
SHAUNAK RUNGTA, CENTRAL EXECUTIVE COMMITTEE MEMBER OF FISME, SAID HIS FIRM'S TURNOVER HAS DROPPED BY AROUND 50 PER CENT. HE WARNED THAT CONTINUED DELAYS RISK BUSINESS CLOSURE AND URGED THE GOVERNMENT TO WITHDRAW THE ORDER.
HOW MUCH DOES INDIA IMPORT IN FASTENERS AND FROM WHERE?
INDIA IMPORTS $1.13 BILLION WORTH OF FASTENERS ANNUALLY. CHINA ACCOUNTS FOR 24.6 PER CENT, JAPAN FOR 13.3 PER CENT, SOUTH KOREA FOR 9.9 PER CENT AND GERMANY FOR 9.8 PER CENT OF THESE IMPORTS.
WHAT DO EXPERTS SAY ABOUT THE IMPORTANCE OF FASTENER IMPORTS?
GTRI FOUNDER AJAY SRIVASTAVA SAID FASTENERS ACCOUNT FOR LESS THAN 1 PER CENT OF FINAL PRODUCT COST BUT THEIR ABSENCE CAN HALT ENTIRE PRODUCTION LINES. RESTRICTING TRADE IN THIS LOW-VALUE, HIGH-UTILITY INPUT RAISES OVERALL PRODUCTION COSTS AND UNDERMINES MANUFACTURING COMPETITIVENESS.
