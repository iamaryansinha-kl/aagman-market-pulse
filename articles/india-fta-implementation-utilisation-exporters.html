India FTA Gap: Experts Urge Shift From Signing To Implementation

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/India's FTA utilisation rate stands at roughly 25%, far below the 70-80% seen in developed economies
  • 02/Experts say the gap between negotiated market access and actual export claims is India's weakest trade policy link
  • 03/Immediate priority must shift from FTA signing to practical implementation, MSME awareness, and certification infrastructure

India's next trade policy priority must shift from signing Free Trade Agreements to ensuring exporters actually use them. The country's FTA utilisation rate has historically hovered around 25%, compared with 70-80% in developed economies.

Gulzar Didwania, Partner at Deloitte India, said India's weakest link is the gap between negotiated market access and actual claims at the border. The policy narrative must move decisively from FTA signing to FTA utilisation.

India has implemented pacts with Singapore, Japan, Korea, the UAE, Australia, ASEAN, and the EFTA bloc. It has also finalised agreements with Oman, New Zealand, the European Union, and the UK.

Under the India-UK CETA, India secured duty-free access for 99% of its exports. The India-EU FTA covers market access for over 99% of India's exports by trade value.

Rudra Kumar Pandey, Partner at Shardul Amarchand Mangaldas & Co, noted that Australia ECTA utilisation has reached 84%. However, awareness among MSMEs remains low and certification infrastructure is underdeveloped across most other agreements.

Pandey identified textiles, leather, engineering goods, pharmaceuticals, and marine products as sectors with the deepest tariff concessions. These need targeted support to build production capacity and quality standards required to exploit that access fully.

India's export basket is shifting toward higher-value manufacturing. Electronics has risen from 3.3% to 7.9% over the last decade, machinery from 3.8% to 6.9%, and smartphones are now India's top exported commodity.

But this shift carries structural vulnerabilities. High-value manufacturing remains heavily dependent on imported inputs from China and East Asia, including electronic components, APIs, chemicals, and non-ferrous metals. Any supply disruption directly threatens India's ability to produce and export finished goods.

Didwania said FTAs can cushion the impact of geopolitical uncertainties by creating alternative trade routes. India's best approach is to diversify export markets, deepen global value chain integration, and strengthen domestic manufacturing competitiveness.

Pandey added that competing in the EU, UK, and US markets requires meeting quality, traceability, and sustainability standards. India must accelerate standards alignment, develop domestic certification systems, and build carbon accounting frameworks that the European market will increasingly require.

"Every firm with export potential must understand the preferential access available to it and have the documentation and compliance support to use it," Pandey said.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT IS INDIA'S CURRENT FTA UTILISATION RATE?
INDIA'S FTA UTILISATION HAS HISTORICALLY HOVERED AROUND 25 PER CENT, WHICH IS SIGNIFICANTLY LOWER THAN THE 70-80 PER CENT UTILISATION RATES SEEN IN DEVELOPED ECONOMIES.
WHICH FREE TRADE AGREEMENTS HAS INDIA ALREADY IMPLEMENTED?
INDIA HAS IMPLEMENTED TRADE PACTS WITH SINGAPORE, JAPAN, KOREA, THE UAE, AUSTRALIA, ASEAN, AND THE EFTA BLOC. IT HAS ALSO FINALISED AGREEMENTS WITH OMAN, NEW ZEALAND, THE EUROPEAN UNION, AND THE UK.
WHAT MARKET ACCESS DID INDIA SECURE UNDER THE INDIA-UK AND INDIA-EU FTAS?
UNDER THE INDIA-UK CETA, INDIA SECURED DUTY-FREE ACCESS FOR 99 PER CENT OF ITS EXPORTS. UNDER THE INDIA-EU FTA, MARKET ACCESS WAS SECURED FOR MORE THAN 99 PER CENT OF INDIA'S EXPORTS BY TRADE VALUE.
WHY IS FTA UTILISATION LOW DESPITE NEGOTIATED MARKET ACCESS?
AWARENESS AMONG MSMEs REMAINS LOW AND CERTIFICATION INFRASTRUCTURE IS UNDERDEVELOPED ACROSS MOST AGREEMENTS. MANY FIRMS LACK THE DOCUMENTATION AND COMPLIANCE SUPPORT NEEDED TO CLAIM PREFERENTIAL ACCESS AT THE BORDER.
WHICH SECTORS SHOULD INDIA PRIORITISE TO MAXIMISE FTA BENEFITS?
TEXTILES, LEATHER, ENGINEERING GOODS, PHARMACEUTICALS, AND MARINE PRODUCTS HAVE THE DEEPEST TARIFF CONCESSIONS. THESE SECTORS NEED TARGETED SUPPORT TO BUILD PRODUCTION CAPACITY AND QUALITY STANDARDS REQUIRED TO FULLY EXPLOIT MARKET ACCESS.
