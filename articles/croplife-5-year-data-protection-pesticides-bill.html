CropLife Demands 5-Year Data Protection in Draft Pesticides Bill

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/CropLife India, representing 70% of the domestic pesticide industry, demands 5-year regulatory data protection in the Draft Pesticides Management Bill
  • 02/Crop losses from pests and diseases are estimated at 10-35%, worth Rs 2 lakh crore per year; China offers 6 years, EU/US offer 10 years
  • 03/Agriculture Minister Shivraj Singh Chouhan says the Bill is ready and could be introduced in the next session of Parliament

CropLife India has demanded a five-year regulatory data protection provision in the Draft Pesticides Management Bill. The industry body, representing nearly 70 per cent of the domestic pesticide market, submitted formal recommendations to the Ministry of Agriculture in February 2026.

Union Agriculture Minister Shivraj Singh Chouhan said the Bill is ready and could be introduced in the next session of Parliament. The legislation had missed its previous deadline of the Budget session.

CropLife has proposed a time-bound framework of five years from first registration. The proposal would cover both patented new molecules and off-patent ones where a company has independently generated fresh registration data.

"Farmers in turn continue to depend on older and generic molecules, many of which are more hazardous and less effective against evolving pest-resistance and challenges. Regulatory data protection is thus a key public policy tool to encourage the availability of newer, safer and more effective products for farmers," said Ankur Aggarwal, chairman of CropLife India.

Crop losses in India are between 10 and 35 per cent due to pests and diseases. The annual damage is estimated to be worth Rs 2 lakh crore per year.

Out of 338 molecules registered in India, a large number were introduced four decades ago. Newer chemistries are more targeted, require lower doses, and are better suited to evolving pest resistance.

The industry body also flagged gaps around e-commerce platforms selling insecticides without any license or approval. It suggested that only nominated responsible persons be held liable for violations, similar to the Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006.

CropLife cautioned that provisions in the draft bill could be misused to convert temporary measures into de facto permanent bans. This would be a departure from the 60-90 days ceiling under the current Insecticides Act, 1968.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT DATA PROTECTION IS CROPLIFE INDIA DEMANDING IN THE DRAFT PESTICIDES MANAGEMENT BILL?
CROPLIFE INDIA HAS DEMANDED A FIVE-YEAR REGULATORY DATA PROTECTION PROVISION FROM THE DATE OF FIRST REGISTRATION. THE PROPOSAL WOULD COVER BOTH PATENTED NEW MOLECULES AND OFF-PATENT ONES WHERE A COMPANY HAS INDEPENDENTLY GENERATED FRESH REGISTRATION DATA.
WHAT PERCENTAGE OF THE DOMESTIC PESTICIDE INDUSTRY DOES CROPLIFE INDIA REPRESENT?
CROPLIFE INDIA REPRESENTS NEARLY 70 PER CENT OF THE DOMESTIC PESTICIDE INDUSTRY.
HOW DO INDIA'S PROPOSED DATA PROTECTION TERMS COMPARE WITH OTHER COUNTRIES?
CHINA OFFERS 6 YEARS OF DATA PROTECTION AFTER FIRST REGISTRATION, WHILE THE EU, BRAZIL AND THE UNITED STATES EACH PROVIDE 10 YEARS. CROPLIFE HAS PROPOSED 5 YEARS FOR INDIA.
WHAT IS THE ESTIMATED ANNUAL CROP LOSS DUE TO PESTS AND DISEASES IN INDIA?
CROP LOSSES IN INDIA ARE BETWEEN 10 AND 35 PER CENT DUE TO PESTS AND DISEASES, ESTIMATED TO BE WORTH RS 2 LAKH CRORE PER YEAR.
WHAT OTHER CONCERNS HAS CROPLIFE RAISED ABOUT THE DRAFT BILL?
CROPLIFE HAS FLAGGED THE ABSENCE OF E-COMMERCE REGULATIONS FOR PESTICIDE SALES, INDISCRIMINATE PROSECUTION OF DIRECTORS UNDER DECRIMINALISATION PROVISIONS, AND THE RISK OF MISUSING TEMPORARY BAN PROVISIONS INTO DE FACTO PERMANENT BANS WITHOUT SCIENTIFIC FINDINGS.
