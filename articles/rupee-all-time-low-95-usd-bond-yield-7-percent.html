Rupee Hits All-Time Low of 95.25/USD; 10-Year Bond Yield Surges Past 7%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Rupee opens at 95.01 per USD, hits all-time low of 95.2475 on April 30, 2026
  • 02/10-year benchmark G-Sec yield surges past 7% to 7.6% amid rising oil prices
  • 03/Fed holds rates at 3.5-3.75%; analysts warn RBI options to stabilise INR remain limited

The Indian rupee opened 17 paise weaker at 95.01 per US dollar on April 30, 2026. It quickly hit an all-time low of 95.2475 as the currency faced fresh selling pressure.

The US Federal Reserve held interest rates unchanged at 3.50-3.75 per cent. The hawkish stance added to the Reserve Bank of India's policy constraints, according to Emkay Global's Chief Economist Madhavi Arora.

India's 10-year benchmark government security yield hardened sharply past the 7 per cent mark. It is currently trading at 7.6 per cent, up from the previous close of 7 per cent.

The bond yield surge reflects rising global crude oil prices amid the ongoing West Asia conflict. The Strait of Hormuz blockade and UAE's exit from OPEC are adding fresh supply-side uncertainty.

"The current rupee depreciation is not in line with India's macro fundamentals," said Soumya Ghosh, Group Chief Economic Advisor at State Bank of India. He warned that exchange rate depreciation is feeding higher imported inflation.

Amit Pabari, MD at CR Forex Advisors, said the rupee is reacting to three dominant forces. These are oil prices, capital flows, and central bank policies.

SBI's economic research department has called for urgent balance of payments support measures. These include soliciting diaspora funds, better tax treatment for G-Sec investments, and temporarily reducing the Liberalised Remittance Scheme limit in FY27.

Analysts at Emkay Global expect foreign portfolio flows to remain weak as long as Middle East tensions stay elevated. Without structural solutions, the rupee could face further pressure.
