India, South Korea Expand Shipbuilding Ties, Target ₹2.2 Lakh Crore Maritime Push

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/India and South Korea sign a comprehensive framework for partnership in shipbuilding, shipping and maritime logistics on April 20, 2026
  • 02/India plans to procure over 400 vessels valued at ₹2.2 lakh crore (~USD 25 billion), channeling demand into bilateral industrial partnerships
  • 03/Korean firms gain access to India's $13.3 billion port modernisation pipeline, including Vadhvan port and terminals in Odisha and Gujarat

Prime Minister Narendra Modi and South Korean President Lee Jae Myung signed a comprehensive framework for shipbuilding and maritime cooperation on April 20, 2026. The agreement was formalised during bilateral talks at Hyderabad House in New Delhi.

The framework targets India's plan to procure over 400 vessels with an estimated value of ₹2.2 lakh crore. This demand will be channeled into bilateral partnerships to build a sustainable shipbuilding ecosystem.

Both nations agreed to upgrade Indian shipyards through brownfield expansion. This includes support for block fabrication units and new dry docks for constructing large and specialised vessels.

Korean companies will expand into India's shipbuilding components market. The Korea Marine Equipment Association has opened a Mumbai branch, and the Korea Marine Equipment Research Institute is pursuing cooperation.

India and Korea will jointly run skill training programmes in shipbuilding. The Korea International Cooperation Agency will partner with India's Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways to build workforce capacity.

Korean shipowners are encouraged to use India's GIFT City to flag vessels. India's pool of over 320,000 seafarers offers a strong workforce base for global maritime operations.

Multiple MoUs were signed on port development and infrastructure. Korean firms can participate in India's port modernisation pipeline, estimated at USD 13.3 billion over the next five years.

The two countries will jointly design and manufacture next-generation maritime and port cranes in India. Academic ties will also deepen between the Indian Maritime University and Korea Maritime and Ocean University.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT DID INDIA AND SOUTH KOREA AGREE TO ON APRIL 20, 2026?
PRIME MINISTER NARENDRA MODI AND SOUTH KOREAN PRESIDENT LEE JAE MYUNG SIGNED A COMPREHENSIVE FRAMEWORK FOR PARTNERSHIP IN SHIPBUILDING, SHIPPING AND MARITIME LOGISTICS. THE AGREEMENT FOCUSES ON JOINT INDUSTRIAL PARTNERSHIPS, INFRASTRUCTURE DEVELOPMENT AND SKILL BUILDING.
WHAT IS THE VALUE OF INDIA'S PLANNED VESSEL PROCUREMENT?
INDIA PLANS TO PROCURE OVER 400 VESSELS WITH AN ESTIMATED VALUE OF ₹2.2 LAKH CRORE, APPROXIMATELY USD 25 BILLION. THIS DEMAND WILL BE CHANNELED INTO BILATERAL PARTNERSHIPS WITH SOUTH KOREA.
HOW WILL KOREAN COMPANIES BENEFIT FROM THIS AGREEMENT?
KOREAN COMPANIES GAIN ACCESS TO INDIA'S SHIPBUILDING COMPONENTS MARKET AND PORT MODERNISATION PIPELINE. THE KOREA MARINE EQUIPMENT ASSOCIATION HAS OPENED A MUMBAI BRANCH. KOREAN FIRMS CAN BID FOR PROJECTS INCLUDING THE VADHVAN PORT AND TERMINALS IN ODISHA AND GUJARAT.
WHAT SKILL DEVELOPMENT INITIATIVES WERE ANNOUNCED?
INDIA AND KOREA AGREED TO COOPERATE ON SHIPBUILDING SECTOR TRAINING THROUGH A PROJECT LED BY THE KOREA INTERNATIONAL COOPERATION AGENCY IN PARTNERSHIP WITH INDIA'S MINISTRY OF PORTS, SHIPPING AND WATERWAYS. THE INITIATIVE WILL ENHANCE WORKFORCE CAPACITY THROUGH DEVELOPMENT COOPERATION AND PUBLIC-PRIVATE PARTNERSHIPS.
WHAT IS THE SCALE OF INDIA'S PORT MODERNISATION PLANS?
INDIA'S PORT MODERNISATION PIPELINE IS ESTIMATED AT USD 13.3 BILLION OVER THE NEXT FIVE YEARS. MAJOR PROJECTS INCLUDE THE VADHVAN CONTAINER PORT IN MAHARASHTRA AND TERMINALS IN ODISHA AND GUJARAT. THE SAGARMALA PROGRAMME ENVISAGES 839 PROJECTS WITH A TOTAL INVESTMENT OF ABOUT ₹5.79 LAKH CRORE BY 2035.
