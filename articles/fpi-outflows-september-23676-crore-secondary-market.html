FPIs Pull Out Rs 23,676 Crore From Indian Equities in September, Snapping Two-Month Buying Streak

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

Foreign investors are selling India again. NSDL data shows FPIs have pulled out Rs 23,676 crore from Indian equities through the exchanges up to September 19, snapping a two-month buying streak that had briefly revived hopes of a durable foreign return.

The contrast is sharp. FIIs bought Rs 11,045 crore in July and Rs 10,231 crore in August, the first back-to-back monthly inflows after a brutal first half. Between March and June they had sold Rs 1.15 lakh crore, Rs 71,203 crore, Rs 50,188 crore and Rs 35,174 crore respectively, on top of Rs 34,152 crore of selling in January.

The reasons are external. Analysts point to higher crude prices, elevated US bond yields, with the US 10-year at 5%, geopolitical risk from the Iran-US conflict and currency concerns. Higher crude is particularly negative for India, widening the current account deficit, feeding inflation pressure and weakening the rupee.

The selling has been persistent. FPIs sold Rs 3,106 crore, Rs 588 crore and Rs 3,164 crore in the cash market between September 15 and 17, and a Rs 600 crore purchase on Friday was not enough to change the picture. For the week, FPIs were net sellers by Rs 6,258 crore on provisional numbers, making it five straight weeks of foreign selling. Month-to-date, FIIs have sold Rs 7,041 crore.

The striking exception is the primary market. Foreign investors continue to pour money into IPOs even as they shun listed stocks. FPI investment through the primary market stood at Rs 2,703 crore up to September 19, taking the total for this year to Rs 48,550 crore. That explains why India's IPO boom, from the Rs 22,569 crore NSE issue to a packed calendar of listings, continues even as the secondary market struggles.

Domestic institutions have been the cushion. DIIs bought Rs 11,232 crore last week, extending a buying streak, and Rs 36,219 crore month-to-date. Their support helped the Nifty recover from mid-week lows, though the index is still down 3% from its August-end close of 24,080.

Analysts expect volatility to persist while crude and US yields stay elevated. The near-term triggers are the Iran-US conflict, rupee movement, Brent crude and PMI data from the US and India. The underlying support remains intact, with a resilient economy and improving earnings growth, but for now foreign investors are voting with their feet, one IPO at a time.

IPOs. FPIs have invested Rs 48,550 crore in the primary market this year, including Rs 2,703 crore in September, even as they sell listed equities.
