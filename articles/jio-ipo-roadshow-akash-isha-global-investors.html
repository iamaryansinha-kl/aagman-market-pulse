Jio IPO: Akash, Isha Ambani Pitch Rs 30,000 Crore Issue Globally

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Akash and Isha Ambani have completed international roadshows across the Americas, Europe and Southeast Asia for Jio Platforms' planned Rs 30,000 crore IPO
  • 02/Every marquee backer — Meta, Google, Saudi Arabia's PIF, KKR among them — plans to hold or increase stakes; the issue is all-fresh with no OFS
  • 03/Anchor book opens October 19, public subscription October 21-23; at up to Rs 10.3 lakh crore valuation it would top Hyundai's Rs 27,800 crore record

Akash Ambani and Isha Ambani have personally led the pitch for Jio Platforms' planned Rs 30,000 crore IPO. The siblings met investors across the Americas, Europe and Southeast Asia, travelling regularly for the roadshows, according to people familiar with the matter.

The international leg is now complete. Jio has moved to domestic roadshows, where major mutual funds and institutional investors are seeking a share of the offering.

The deal could become India's largest-ever IPO. Pricing, per an earlier Bloomberg report, is set at a band of Rs 1,065 to Rs 1,119 per share. At the upper end, Jio would be valued at up to Rs 10.3 lakh crore and raise about Rs 30,200 crore.

That would surpass Hyundai Motor India's Rs 27,800 crore IPO of October 2024, currently the country's largest share sale. It would also be Reliance Industries' first public offering in three decades, and make Jio the first top-three company to list on Indian stock exchanges.

The investor response carries an unusual feature: nobody is selling. In most mega IPOs, existing backers use the listing to cash out. Here, every marquee investor plans to hold — and some are seeking to increase their exposure.

The register reads like a global who's who of capital: Meta, Google, Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, KKR, Vista Equity Partners, Silver Lake, Mubadala, General Atlantic, Abu Dhabi Investment Authority and TPG. Meta affiliate Jaadhu Holdings is the largest external investor with a 9.98% stake, followed by Google International with 7.73%.

The IPO is an entirely fresh issue with no offer-for-sale component. All proceeds go to the company, and Reliance Industries will continue to hold about 66.4% of Jio Platforms post-listing. The June draft prospectus provided for up to 270 million new shares, roughly 2.93% of post-issue equity.

Jio is being positioned as a technology company, not merely a telecom business. That framing matters to how investors value it: tech platforms typically command richer multiples than commoditised telecom operators.

The valuation still came down from earlier ambitions. Jio had previously been poised to seek about Rs 11 lakh crore, but a selloff in Indian stocks has tempered pricing expectations for several IPOs. Motilal Oswal Financial Services had estimated Jio's value at Rs 11.2 lakh crore.

Motilal Oswal said the listing could create a case for a holding company discount on Reliance's stake. It believes Reliance's current share price already factors in an estimated 18-36% discount on its Jio Platforms holding. The brokerage reiterated buy ratings on both Reliance Industries and Bharti Airtel, calling the risk-reward compelling.

The brokerage expects a smartphone tariff increase of about 15% in December 2026, following the Jio IPO and Vodafone Idea's planned fundraise. Such a hike could support stronger earnings and a multiple re-rating for both Bharti Airtel and Jio Platforms.

Concerns over a partial shift in telecom allocations from Bharti to Jio are "overblown", Motilal Oswal said, because Jio would initially have limited free float.

The company is focusing on retail investors and plans to price the issue to leave some value on the table. Nineteen banks are on the mandate, including Kotak Mahindra Capital, Morgan Stanley, Bank of America, Axis Capital, BNP Paribas and Citigroup.

The anchor book opens October 19, with public subscription on October 21-23 and trading expected to begin October 28. Details could still change.

The scale of the banker bench tells its own story. Nineteen banks are working the share sale — among them Kotak Mahindra Capital, Morgan Stanley, Bank of America, Axis Capital, BNP Paribas and Citigroup — a syndicate sized for a once-a-decade listing.

For Reliance Industries shareholders, the listing changes how the stock is priced. Motilal Oswal's point is that direct exposure to Jio will no longer require holding the conglomerate — energy, retail, new energy and data centres bundled together.

That cuts both ways. Once Jio trades separately, the market may apply a holding-company discount to Reliance's residual stake — the brokerage's estimate of an existing 18-36% discount on the Jio holding suggests much of that repricing is already in the price.

The retail pitch is explicit. The company plans to price the issue to leave value on the table, and the anchor book opens October 19 ahead of the October 21-23 subscription window.

The fresh-issue structure matters for the money trail. With no offer for sale, every rupee raised — up to about Rs 30,200 crore at the top of the band — stays inside the company.

The timing tells reveal a cooled market. Jio had earlier been poised to seek roughly Rs 11 lakh crore; the eight-week slide in Indian equities trimmed those ambitions to Rs 10.3 lakh crore even for the country's most anticipated listing.

The float will still be small. Up to 270 million new shares is about 2.93% of post-issue equity — limited free float that, per Motilal Oswal, makes fears of telecom allocations rotating from Bharti Airtel "overblown."

The December wildcard is tariffs. The brokerage expects a roughly 15% smartphone tariff increase after the IPO and Vodafone Idea's fundraise — a potential earnings and multiple catalyst for both listed telecom names.

What the roadshow has already delivered is a headline inversion. India's largest-ever IPO is also a no-exit event for its pre-IPO investors — a rarity at this scale.

THE BROKERAGE HAS BUY RATINGS ON BOTH RELIANCE AND BHARTI AIRTEL. GLOSSARY: Anchor book: The portion of an IPO allocated to institutional investors a day or two before the public offer opens. Fresh issue: New shares created by the company; proceeds go to the company rather than to selling shareholders. Offer for sale (OFS): A secondary sale where existing investors sell their shares; the company receives nothing. Free float: The portion of a listed company's shares available for public trading. Holding company discount: A market valuation of a parent's stake below the market value of the underlying holdings. EV/EBITDA: Enterprise value relative to earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation — a common valuation multiple. Tariff hike: An increase in the prices telecom operators charge subscribers.
