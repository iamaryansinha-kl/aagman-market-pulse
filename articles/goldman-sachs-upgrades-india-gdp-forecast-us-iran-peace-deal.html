Goldman Sachs Upgrades India GDP Forecast To 6.8% After US-Iran Peace Deal

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Goldman Sachs raises India's 2026 real GDP growth forecast by 30 bps to 6.8%, citing lower oil prices post US-Iran peace deal
  • 02/FY27 GDP upgraded to 6.5% from 6.1%; inflation and current account deficit forecasts also cut
  • 03/Bank still expects 50 bps RBI rate hikes in 2026 but warns lower oil prices could delay tightening

Goldman Sachs has upgraded India's real GDP growth forecast for calendar year 2026 to 6.8%. The earlier estimate was 6.5%, representing a 30 basis point upward revision.

The investment bank cited the US-Iran peace deal as the key trigger. Lower crude oil prices and easing supply disruptions have improved India's macro outlook significantly.

For fiscal year 2026-27, Goldman Sachs raised its GDP forecast by 40 basis points to 6.5%. The previous estimate stood at 6.1%.

Real GDP growth in Q1 CY26 came in at 7.8% year-on-year. This was approximately 50 basis points above Goldman Sachs' prior forecast.

"The recent US-Iran peace deal should improve India's growth outlook," wrote Goldman economists Santanu Sengupta and Arjun Varma. They noted lower oil prices have "taken out the risk of additional fuel pass-through to consumers."

The bank has cut its headline CPI inflation forecast for CY26 by 20 basis points to 4.4%. For FY27, inflation is now pegged at 4.9%, down 30 basis points from earlier.

Core goods inflation forecasts were trimmed by 30 basis points to 3.2% for CY26 and by 50 basis points to 4.1% for FY27. Overall core inflation is now projected at 4.2% and 4.5% respectively.

On the external front, Goldman Sachs lowered its current account deficit forecast by 20 basis points to 1.1% of GDP for CY26. The oil import bill is now estimated at approximately US$215 billion, or 5.5% of GDP.

Remittances are projected stronger at US$140 billion, up from an earlier US$138 billion estimate. The balance of payments surplus is now expected at 0.7% of GDP versus 0.6% previously.

Despite the disinflationary impulse, Goldman Sachs retains its base case of 50 basis points of RBI rate hikes in 2026. It expects 25 basis points each in the October and December policy meetings, taking the repo rate to 5.75%.

However, the economists cautioned that if petrochemical prices remain lower for longer, "there is a risk that the RBI may defer the policy tightening cycle." Gross fixed capital formation rose to a six-quarter high of 10.8% YoY in Q1 CY26.
