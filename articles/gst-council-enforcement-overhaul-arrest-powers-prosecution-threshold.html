GST Council Weighs Ending Arrest Powers, ₹5 Crore Bar

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/The GST Council is likely to consider scrapping GST officers' power to arrest taxpayers at its October 7 meeting; any arrest would instead require a court order
  • 02/The criminal prosecution threshold is proposed to rise from ₹1 crore to ₹5 crore, and nine of the 24 existing offences could be removed entirely
  • 03/Nothing is approved yet — the Council's decisions on October 7 will determine the final shape, and amendments to the GST law would be required

The GST Council is likely to consider a sweeping overhaul of tax enforcement at its October 7 meeting. The proposals include scrapping the power of GST officers to arrest taxpayers and requiring a court order for any arrest.

The Council is also likely to weigh raising the threshold for launching criminal prosecution from ₹1 crore to ₹5 crore. The prosecution provisions would be narrowed so that routine disputes stay outside the criminal process.

Routine disagreements between taxpayers and authorities over GST classification, valuation or input tax credit eligibility would be kept outside prosecution under the proposed framework. People familiar with the matter told PTI the higher threshold would reserve the criminal process for cases whose scale warrants it.

The proposals follow the major GST rate rationalisation implemented in September 2025. They are designed to make enforcement more proportionate, with greater emphasis on tax recovery, interest and penalties rather than arrest.

At the centre of the proposed changes is Section 69 of the Central GST Act. The section currently allows the Commissioner, subject to statutory conditions and reasons to believe specified offences have been committed, to authorise an officer to arrest a person.

The reform would remove this arrest power from GST officials entirely. Any arrest would instead require judicial authorisation, shifting the coercive element of enforcement out of the tax department's hands.

Of the 24 offences currently covered, nine could be removed entirely while 11 would remain unchanged. The minimum sentence provision is proposed to be removed, giving courts greater discretion including the option of a fine. The maximum sentence in the middle band could be reduced from three years to two.

The changes would not take away the government's powers to recover tax or impose financial penalties. Taxpayers found to have short-paid tax or wrongly claimed input tax credit would continue to face recovery proceedings and other statutory consequences.

GST's technology architecture has evolved considerably since 2017. Invoice-level information now enables closer matching of what sellers report with what buyers claim, helping identify potentially fraudulent input tax credit earlier in the transaction chain.

That data capability is central to the proposed shift. Enforcement would move towards data-led detection and financial recovery rather than arrest as a primary deterrent.

"With arrest removed and prosecution reserved for more serious cases, the focus is increasingly on using GSTN's data capabilities to detect fraud rather than relying on coercive powers," said Rajat Mohan, Managing Partner at AMRG Global.

The wider reform package before the Council could also include easier GST registration, faster refunds, simpler input tax credit procedures, changes to show-cause notices and penalties, and relief on late fees for small taxpayers.

The approach builds on the government's earlier moves to decriminalise minor economic and regulatory offences, including through the Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Act, 2026. Criminal prosecution would be retained for serious cases involving deliberate evasion or fraud.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHEN WILL THE GST COUNCIL CONSIDER THE ENFORCEMENT OVERHAUL?
THE GST COUNCIL IS LIKELY TO TAKE UP THE PROPOSALS AT ITS MEETING ON OCTOBER 7. NOTHING HAS BEEN APPROVED YET. THE COUNCIL'S DECISIONS WILL DETERMINE THE FINAL SHAPE OF THE CHANGES, AND AMENDMENTS TO THE GST LAW WOULD BE REQUIRED TO GIVE EFFECT TO ANY MEASURES APPROVED. THE WIDER PACKAGE BEFORE THE COUNCIL COULD ALSO INCLUDE EASIER GST REGISTRATION, FASTER REFUNDS, SIMPLER INPUT TAX CREDIT PROCEDURES, CHANGES TO SHOW-CAUSE NOTICES AND PENALTIES, AND RELIEF ON LATE FEES FOR SMALL TAXPAYERS.
WHAT WOULD HAPPEN TO GST OFFICERS' POWER TO ARREST TAXPAYERS?
THE PROPOSALS WOULD REMOVE THE POWER OF GST OFFICERS TO ARREST TAXPAYERS UNDER SECTION 69 OF THE CENTRAL GST ACT. UNDER THE CURRENT LAW, THE COMMISSIONER CAN, SUBJECT TO STATUTORY CONDITIONS AND REASONS TO BELIEVE SPECIFIED OFFENCES HAVE BEEN COMMITTED, AUTHORISE AN OFFICER TO ARREST A PERSON. UNDER THE PROPOSED FRAMEWORK, ANY ARREST WOULD REQUIRE JUDICIAL AUTHORISATION THROUGH A COURT ORDER.
HOW WOULD THE CRIMINAL PROSECUTION THRESHOLD CHANGE?
THE THRESHOLD FOR LAUNCHING CRIMINAL PROSECUTION IS PROPOSED TO RISE FROM ₹1 CRORE TO ₹5 CRORE. PEOPLE FAMILIAR WITH THE MATTER TOLD PTI THE HIGHER THRESHOLD WOULD RESERVE THE CRIMINAL PROCESS FOR CASES WHOSE SCALE WARRANTS IT. ROUTINE DISAGREEMENTS OVER CLASSIFICATION, VALUATION AND INPUT TAX CREDIT ELIGIBILITY WOULD BE KEPT OUTSIDE PROSECUTION.
HOW MANY GST OFFENCES COULD BE REMOVED, AND WHAT SENTENCING CHANGES ARE PROPOSED?
OF THE 24 OFFENCES CURRENTLY COVERED, NINE COULD BE REMOVED ENTIRELY WHILE 11 WOULD REMAIN UNCHANGED. THE MINIMUM SENTENCE PROVISION IS PROPOSED TO BE REMOVED, ALLOWING COURTS GREATER DISCRETION, INCLUDING THE OPTION OF A FINE. THE MAXIMUM SENTENCE IN THE MIDDLE BAND COULD BE REDUCED FROM THREE YEARS TO TWO.
WILL THE GOVERNMENT STILL BE ABLE TO RECOVER TAX AND IMPOSE PENALTIES?
YES. THE PROPOSED CHANGES WOULD NOT TAKE AWAY THE GOVERNMENT'S POWERS TO RECOVER TAX OR IMPOSE FINANCIAL PENALTIES. TAXPAYERS FOUND TO HAVE SHORT-PAID TAX OR WRONGLY CLAIMED INPUT TAX CREDIT WOULD CONTINUE TO FACE RECOVERY PROCEEDINGS AND OTHER STATUTORY CONSEQUENCES. RAJAT MOHAN, MANAGING PARTNER AT AMRG GLOBAL, SAID THE FOCUS IS INCREASINGLY ON USING GSTN'S DATA CAPABILITIES TO DETECT FRAUD RATHER THAN RELYING ON COERCIVE POWERS.
