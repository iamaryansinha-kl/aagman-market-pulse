PFC Board Clears Next Step for REC Merger, Seeks President's Approval

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •• PFC's board has approved seeking formal approval from the President of India for the proposed merger with REC Limited
  • •• The government currently holds 55.9% in PFC and 52.6% in REC; analysts fear the merged stake could fall below 51%
  • •• The merger is targeted to become effective from April 1, 2027, subject to regulatory and government approvals
  • •• The combined entity would become one of India's largest infrastructure financing institutions

Power Finance Corporation has moved a step closer to its proposed merger with REC Limited. The PFC board has approved seeking formal approval from the President of India for the transaction.

In an exchange filing, PFC said its board approved reserving the proposal for the merger of REC into PFC. The board also authorized Chairman and Managing Director Parminder Chopra to apply for the President's approval.

The merger will be carried out based on a share exchange ratio. Valuers appointed for the transaction will determine this ratio.

PFC clarified that the merged entity would continue to retain its "government company" status. This includes possible issuance of securities or capital infusion by the central government if required.

At present, the government holds a 55.9% stake in PFC and a 52.6% stake in REC. The remaining shares are held by public investors.

Analysts have estimated that the Centre's stake in the merged entity could potentially fall below the critical 51% threshold. This is based on prevailing market prices.

The issue is significant because bonds issued by both companies carry covenants linked to government ownership. A fall below 51% could be interpreted as a change in control.

Analyst estimates suggest the government may need to infuse nearly Rs 25,000 crore. This would be required to maintain majority ownership in the merged entity.

This has raised questions over whether the Centre would commit fresh capital. The concern comes amid fiscal constraints.

Earlier this week, Chopra indicated the merger is targeted to become effective from April 1, 2027. This is subject to regulatory and government approvals.

The proposed merger was originally announced in the Union Budget on February 1. It is part of the government's broader strategy to consolidate state-run financial institutions.

Both PFC and REC had granted in-principle approval to the merger on February 6.

If completed, the merger would create one of India's largest infrastructure financing institutions. The focus would be on power generation, transmission, renewable energy, and infrastructure lending.
