FedEx Completes India's First Intra-City Drone Delivery Trials

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/FedEx and IIT Madras completed India's first intra-city drone delivery flight trials in Bengaluru on April 21, 2026, cutting a 53 km road journey to a 39-42 km aerial route.
  • 02/One-way transit time dropped from over 60 minutes to nearly 21 minutes, demonstrating a potential 65% reduction in mid-mile logistics time.
  • 03/The trials navigated Airport Yellow and Red Zones with DGCA approval, but commercial rollout depends on future regulatory frameworks and scalability testing.

FedEx Corporation (NYSE: FDX) and IIT Madras have completed India's first intra-city drone delivery flight trials in Bengaluru. The announcement was made on April 21, 2026.

The trials were conducted under the FedEx SMART Centre at IIT Madras. This research partnership focuses on air cargo optimisation, electric vehicle integration, and advanced demand forecasting.

The drone route connected Electronic City Phase II to a site near Bangalore International Airport Limited. This corridor represents a critical mid-mile logistics link in one of India's most congested urban environments.

Analysis by the FedEx SMART Centre showed that a 53 km road route — which takes over 60 minutes — can be replaced by an aerial route of approximately 39-42 km. During the trials, this reduced one-way transit time to nearly 21 minutes.

The flights operated at an altitude of 120 metres. Safety systems included an autonomous flight termination system, return-to-home capability, and anti-collision strobe lighting.

The flight path required coordinated navigation through Airport Yellow and Red Zones. All necessary permissions were granted by the Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA), India's aviation regulator.

Amber Wings served as the implementation partner for the trials. The company provided operational support for the high-speed drone operations in complex urban airspace.

"This milestone reflects the FedEx SMART Centre's broader research agenda across air cargo optimisation, electric vehicle integration, and advanced demand forecasting," said Nitin Navneet Tatiwala, vice president of marketing, customer experience, and air network (MEISA) at FedEx. "These efforts are focused on shaping future-ready, resilient and sustainable supply chain ecosystems."

Prof. Satyanarayanan R Chakravarthy, core faculty member at the FedEx SMART Centre, said: "By integrating advanced aerial robotics into urban logistics, we are moving beyond theoretical research to prove the efficacy of high-impact, future-ready solutions."

India's drone logistics sector remains in early stages. Commercial operations require clearances beyond trial permissions, including final-mile delivery regulations and urban airspace management frameworks.

Bengaluru's traffic congestion costs the economy an estimated ₹65,000 crore annually in lost productivity. Drone delivery could offer a partial solution for time-critical logistics in India's tech capital.
