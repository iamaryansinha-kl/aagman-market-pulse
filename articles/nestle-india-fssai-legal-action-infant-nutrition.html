Nestle India Shares Slip Nearly 3% as FSSAI Initiates Legal Action Over Infant Nutrition Products

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

Nestle India came under regulatory heat on Friday. Shares of the FMCG major fell nearly 3% during intraday trading after the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India initiated legal action against the company over alleged non-compliance involving two of its infant nutrition products.

The stock declined 2.75% to hit an intraday low of Rs 1,332.80 on the NSE, and was trading at Rs 1,338.20 at 1:32 PM, down 2.35% from the previous close of Rs 1,370.60. The fall extends a slide that has now taken the stock 14.17% below its 52-week high of Rs 1,553, touched on August 7.

The regulator's action centres on promotional claims. FSSAI said it examined the products and their promotional material on e-commerce platforms, flagging claims relating to 5 HMOs and whey protein on NAN Excella Pro Stage 1, and a claim that the whey protein in Lactogen Pro 1 was easy to digest.

According to FSSAI, both products were found in contravention of Regulation 4(2) of the Food Safety and Standards (Foods for Infant Nutrition) Regulations, 2020, which restricts promotional claims and material intended to increase the saleability of infant foods. The regulator also cited Section 3 of the Infant Milk Substitutes, Feeding Bottles and Infant Foods Act, 1992, which prohibits advertisement and promotion of infant foods.

There is also a laboratory finding. A sample of a follow-up formula manufactured by Nestle India was found sub-standard for its biotin content during analysis. The sample was sent for re-analysis, but the referral laboratory also found it non-conforming with the prescribed biotin requirement under the 2020 regulations.

In view of the alleged violations, FSSAI said three separate adjudication cases have been filed against Nestle India, covering both the promotional claims and the biotin finding. The regulator said it had sought clarifications from the company.

The regulatory risk lands on a stock that has been a relative haven this year, up about 3% in 2026 against a decline of more than 10% in the NSE Nifty 500, with a market capitalisation of Rs 2,57,796 crore. Infant nutrition is a sensitive, high-margin category for Nestle India, and adjudication outcomes could carry penalties as well as reputational costs in a segment where consumer trust is the core asset.

WHAT TRIGGERED FSSAI'S ACTION AGAINST NESTLE INDIA?
The regulator flagged promotional claims on NAN Excella Pro Stage 1 and Lactogen Pro 1 on e-commerce platforms, citing violations of the 2020 infant nutrition regulations and the 1992 IMS Act, and a laboratory finding that a follow-up formula was sub-standard on biotin content.
HOW MANY CASES HAVE BEEN FILED?
Three separate adjudication cases, covering the promotional claims and the biotin finding.
HOW HAS THE STOCK REACTED?
Shares fell 2.75% to an intraday low of Rs 1,332.80, and are down about 14% from the August 7 peak of Rs 1,553.
