10 FMCG Stocks Tumble Up to 31% in 2026: ITC, HUL Among Worst Hit

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/The FMCG sector index has declined 10% year-to-date in 2026, dragged by geopolitical disruptions from the Iran war and inflationary pressures in April and May
  • 02/Procter & Gamble Hygiene and Health Care is the worst hit, plunging 30.62% YTD, followed by ITC tumbling 28.05% and United Breweries falling 18.09%
  • 03/Hindustan Unilever has been relatively resilient with a 6.15% decline, while Colgate-Palmolive and United Spirits are down just over 4% each

The FMCG sector has endured a punishing run in 2026. The sector index has declined 10% on a year-to-date basis as of June 26, 2026.

Geopolitical disruptions stemming from the Iran war and inflationary pressures during April and May have dented investor sentiment across consumer staples. The selling has been broad-based, with nearly every major FMCG name recording negative returns.

Procter & Gamble Hygiene and Health Care Ltd (NSE: PGHH) is the worst performer, plunging 30.62% YTD. The stock has faced heavy selling pressure through the year.

ITC Ltd (NSE: ITC) follows closely, tumbling 28.05% YTD. The conglomerate has struggled to hold investor confidence despite its diversified revenue streams.

United Breweries Ltd (NSE: UBL) has fallen 18.09% YTD, reflecting weak demand conditions in the beverage segment. Godrej Consumer Products Ltd (NSE: GODREJCP) has declined 15.87%, while Dabur India Ltd (NSE: DABUR) is down 15.74% over the same period.

Britannia Industries Ltd (NSE: BRITANNIA) has corrected 13.30% YTD, weighed down by input cost pressures and muted volume growth. Hindustan Unilever Ltd (NSE: HINDUNILVR) has been the most resilient among the large-cap names, slipping just 6.15%.

Tata Consumer Products Ltd (NSE: TATACONSUM) has declined 5.27% YTD. Colgate-Palmolive India Ltd (NSE: COLPAL) and United Spirits Ltd (NSE: MCDOWELL-N) have held up relatively better, falling 4.11% and 4.20% respectively.

The sector-wide weakness has erased significant wealth for investors holding these defensive names. Analysts are watching for signs of rural demand recovery and input cost stabilization to determine if the downturn has bottomed out.
