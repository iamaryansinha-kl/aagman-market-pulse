Swiggy Loss Narrows to Rs 922 Crore on Record Delivery Profit

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Swiggy's consolidated loss narrowed to Rs 922 crore in Q2 FY27 from Rs 1,092 crore a year earlier, though losses widened sequentially from Rs 792 crore in the June quarter.
  • •- Revenue from operations jumped 36% year-on-year to Rs 7,571 crore; adjusted revenue rose 34% to Rs 7,909 crore.
  • •- Food delivery posted its highest-ever quarterly adjusted EBITDA of Rs 341 crore, up 42%, at a 3.4% margin on GOV.
  • •- Instamart's gross order value grew 31% to Rs 9,230 crore, but its adjusted EBITDA loss widened to Rs 895 crore as the company stepped up investment.
  • •- Management said Instamart's losses peaked in the September quarter; Swiggy also agreed to sell Lynks Logistics to Udaan's parent for Rs 500 crore, taking a roughly 3.2% stake in Udaan.

Swiggy's September-quarter results sketched the outline of the company's next chapter: the core food delivery business is now throwing off real profit at record levels, while the quick-commerce machine built to fight Zomato's Blinkit is still burning cash — but, management insists, has now burned through the worst of it. The consolidated loss after tax narrowed to Rs 922 crore in Q2 FY27 from Rs 1,092 crore in the year-ago quarter, with revenue from operations up 36% at Rs 7,571 crore. The sequential picture is less flattering: losses widened from Rs 792 crore in the June quarter, and the consolidated adjusted EBITDA loss deepened to Rs 760 crore from Rs 695 crore a year earlier, as Swiggy poured money into winning the quick-commerce land grab.

The consumer-facing businesses together reported gross order value of Rs 20,793 crore, up 24.6% year-on-year, as demand improved across food delivery, quick commerce and dining-out. Adjusted revenue rose 34% to Rs 7,909 crore.

The profit engine is the original business. Food delivery GOV grew 17.7% year-on-year to Rs 10,054 crore, and the segment delivered its highest quarterly adjusted EBITDA ever at Rs 341 crore — up 42% from Rs 240 crore a year earlier. The adjusted EBITDA margin improved to 3.4% of GOV from 2.8%, helped by advertising revenue, higher order density and better delivery efficiency. Monthly transacting users reached 19.8 million against 17.2 million a year ago. Notably, GOV growth exceeded 20% year-on-year in September alone, and Swiggy retained its full-year growth guidance of 18-20% for the core food delivery business, excluding its low-cost Toing platform, which is now live in more than 50 cities and is drawing two-thirds of its new users either from people new to Swiggy or from long-inactive customers.

Instamart remains the expensive prize. The quick-commerce arm's GOV grew 31% year-on-year to Rs 9,230 crore, and net order value — which strips out discounts — rose 34.8% to Rs 6,594 crore, with sequential growth accelerating sharply to 13.4% from just 2.5% in the June quarter. That acceleration did not come free: the adjusted EBITDA loss widened to Rs 895 crore from Rs 849 crore a year earlier and Rs 778 crore in the June quarter, as Swiggy stepped up customer acquisition spend to lift order frequency. The loss-making headline, however, conceals improving unit economics — the adjusted EBITDA margin improved to negative 9.7% of GOV from negative 12.1% a year earlier, and contribution margin improved to negative 1% from negative 2.6%. The network added 69 net new dark stores during the quarter, reaching 1,240 stores across 133 cities, with 14.8 million monthly transacting users, up 23% year-on-year.

Management's message on the earnings call was pointed: Instamart's adjusted EBITDA losses have peaked in the September quarter, and margins should improve from here as retention and store utilisation build. The company expects another quarter of double-digit sequential growth in Instamart's net order value during the December quarter. If that guidance holds, the September quarter will mark the inflection where Swiggy stops widening the quick-commerce loss to buy growth and starts harvesting the density it has already built — a pivot its shareholders, who have watched the Blinkit duel consume capital for two years, have been waiting for.

Away from the operating lines, Swiggy agreed to sell its Lynks Logistics business to Udaan's parent for a consideration of Rs 500 crore; together with a separate investment of Rs 75 crore, the transaction will give Swiggy a stake of around 3.2% in Udaan, the business-to-business commerce platform. The move trades a non-core logistics asset for equity in a commerce network, and marginally simplifies a business that has accumulated adjacent bets over the years. The out-of-home consumption business, which includes Dineout, reported GOV up 35% year-on-year at Rs 1,508 crore and stayed adjusted EBITDA-positive for a seventh consecutive quarter.

The consolidated cash balance stood at Rs 13,662 crore at the end of September, down from Rs 14,367 crore at the end of June — a drawdown of about Rs 705 crore in three months, which is roughly the size of the quarter's adjusted EBITDA loss once working capital is accounted for. Co-founder and group CEO Sriharsha Majety said the company's businesses grew faster during the quarter on improving customer engagement and operating performance. The next two quarters will test the claim that sits at the centre of this report: that quick-commerce losses peaked in September. If December delivers another double-digit sequential jump in Instamart net order value alongside a narrower loss, the market's math on Swiggy — record food delivery profits funding one last leg of quick-commerce investment — starts looking like a credible path to consolidated profitability rather than a permanent subsidy. If it does not, the Rs 922 crore headline loss will read less like a waypoint and more like a plateau. Either way, the questions the market will put to management next quarter are now fixed: the pace of dark-store additions, the trajectory of contribution margins, and whether the Toing platform's new-user engine keeps compounding without cannibalising the core.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT WERE SWIGGY'S KEY Q2 FY27 NUMBERS?
Consolidated loss narrowed year-on-year to Rs 922 crore from Rs 1,092 crore, while revenue from operations rose 36% to Rs 7,571 crore.
HOW IS INSTAMART PERFORMING?
Instamart's GOV grew 31% to Rs 9,230 crore and net order value rose 34.8%, but its adjusted EBITDA loss widened to Rs 895 crore. Management said the losses peaked in the September quarter.
WHAT HAPPENED IN FOOD DELIVERY?
Food delivery GOV rose 17.7% to Rs 10,054 crore, with record quarterly adjusted EBITDA of Rs 341 crore at a 3.4% margin.
WHAT IS THE UDAAN TRANSACTION?
Swiggy agreed to sell its Lynks Logistics business to Udaan's parent for Rs 500 crore, plus a Rs 75 crore investment, giving Swiggy roughly a 3.2% stake in Udaan. GLOSSARY: - Gross order value (GOV): The total value of all orders placed on a platform before discounts and cancellations are netted out. - Contribution margin: Revenue per order minus the direct costs of fulfilling that order, showing whether individual orders make money. - Dark store: A small warehouse used exclusively for fulfilling online quick-commerce orders.
