India Extends ECGC Export Insurance for West Asia Till March 2027

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/The government has extended enhanced ECGC insurance cover for exports to West Asia until March 31, 2027, announced on September 30, 2026
  • 02/The earlier extension ran only until September 30, 2026, making this a roughly six-month prolongation of the war-risk support umbrella
  • 03/The support runs under the RELIEF scheme with a total outlay of Rs 497 crore, signalling the disruption around the Strait of Hormuz is expected to persist well into 2027

The Government of India has extended enhanced insurance cover for exports to West Asia until March 31, 2027. The extension was announced on September 30, 2026, and was reported by the Economic Times and Rediff.

The earlier round of this cover was set to lapse on September 30, 2026. The fresh notification keeps the support window open for roughly six more months.

The extension covers shipments to ten destinations: the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Israel, Qatar, Oman, Bahrain, Iraq, Iran and Yemen. Both delivery and transshipment through these markets are covered.

The support is delivered through the RELIEF scheme. RELIEF stands for Resilience & Logistics Intervention for Export Facilitation.

The scheme was notified by the Directorate General of Foreign Trade as Notification 65/2025-26, dated March 19, 2026. It operates under the Export Promotion Mission.

Total government support under RELIEF stands at Rs 497 crore. This is drawn from the budgetary allocation of the Export Promotion Mission.

The scheme has three distinct components aimed at different exporter segments. Component I offers enhanced cover of up to 100% of loss for exporters who already hold ECGC insurance.

Component II provides enhanced cover of up to 95% of loss. This is designed to encourage ECGC coverage for upcoming exports that are not yet insured.

Component III is a time-limited reimbursement of extraordinary freight and insurance surcharges. This window is reserved for eligible MSME exporters who do not have ECGC insurance.

ECGC, the Export Credit Guarantee Corporation, is the nodal implementing agency for the scheme. It administers the enhanced cover and processes claims.

One carve-out is worth noting. Energy shipments are excluded from the Component II window. This limits the 95% enhanced cover for upcoming exports to non-energy trade.

The origin of the scheme lies in the geopolitical escalation involving Iran. The security environment around the Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf maritime corridor deteriorated sharply.

Shipping lines and insurers responded with extraordinary levies. These include Additional War Risk Premiums, War Risk Surcharges, Emergency Conflict Surcharges and other extraordinary freight charges.

For exporters, these surcharges pushed up landed costs overnight. Cargo bound for Gulf ports suddenly carried risk premiums that standard insurance did not absorb.

That is precisely the gap RELIEF was built to close. The 100% and 95% enhanced cover windows shift the residual risk to the sovereign balance sheet.

The extension itself is a signal. By pushing the deadline to March 31, 2027, policymakers are indicating they expect West Asia shipping disruption to extend well beyond the current quarter.

For MSMEs, Component III is the most direct relief. The reimbursement of surcharges addresses the cash-flow pain that falls hardest on smaller exporters without insurance cover.

The Rs 497 crore envelope gives a sense of the fiscal scale involved. It is a targeted, time-bound intervention rather than an open-ended subsidy commitment.

Exporters shipping to the covered ten markets should track the scheme guidelines closely. Eligibility under each component differs, and energy shipments face specific exclusions.

The six-month runway also gives exporters a planning window. Contracts priced with war-risk surcharges can now be underwritten against cover that lasts through the end of FY27.

Whether a further extension becomes necessary depends on the trajectory of the conflict and Hormuz corridor security. For now, the government has chosen to keep the umbrella open.
