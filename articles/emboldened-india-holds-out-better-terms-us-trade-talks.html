An Emboldened India Holds Out for Better Terms in US Trade Talks

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

India is negotiating from a position of newfound confidence.

In ongoing trade talks with the United States, New Delhi is holding out for better terms on tariffs, market access, and intellectual property, adopting a firmer stance that reflects the country's growing economic heft and the recognition that American companies need access to India's large and growing consumer market as much as India needs access to US technology and capital.

The shift in India's negotiating posture is significant. For years, India was seen as a defensive negotiator in trade talks, protecting its agriculture sector, generic drug industry, and services exports while making limited concessions on manufacturing and goods. The new approach suggests that India believes it has leverage — particularly in areas where the US wants Indian cooperation, such as countering China, diversifying supply chains, and accessing India's defence market.

The US has its own demands. Washington wants India to lower tariffs on a range of goods, including agricultural products, automobiles, and luxury items. It also wants stronger intellectual property protections, particularly for pharmaceuticals and technology, and greater market access for American financial services and digital companies. The US has used the threat of tariffs and withdrawal of trade preferences to pressure India into concessions.

India is pushing back on several fronts. New Delhi has resisted lowering tariffs on sensitive agricultural products, arguing that millions of small farmers depend on protection from cheap imports. It has also maintained its stance on compulsory licensing for pharmaceuticals, which allows domestic companies to produce generic versions of patented drugs in public health emergencies. And it has resisted US pressure on digital trade rules that would limit India's ability to regulate American tech giants.

The timing works in India's favour. The US is facing a complex geopolitical environment where it needs India as a partner in the Indo-Pacific region. The Biden administration's strategy of building a network of democratic allies to counter China's influence gives India leverage that it did not have in previous trade negotiations. Washington is reluctant to push too hard on trade issues if it risks damaging the broader strategic partnership.

A comprehensive trade agreement may take time. The gap between the two sides on key issues remains wide, and neither country is in a hurry to sign a deal that could be politically costly at home. The negotiations are likely to continue in phases, with easier issues like some tariff reductions and investment facilitation being addressed first, while harder issues like agriculture, intellectual property, and digital trade are deferred.
