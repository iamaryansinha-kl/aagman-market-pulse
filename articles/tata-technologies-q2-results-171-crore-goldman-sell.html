Tata Tech Q2 Profit Up 4% to Rs 171 Crore as Stock Jumps 8%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Tata Technologies reported Q2 net profit of Rs 171 crore, up 4% from Rs 166 crore a year earlier.
  • •- Revenue grew 28% to Rs 1,699 crore, led by a 34% jump in services revenue to Rs 1,356 crore.
  • •- EBITDA rose 32% to Rs 273 crore, with the margin expanding 40 basis points to 16.1%.
  • •- The stock rallied 8% to Rs 808 on the results and a $138 million deal with a luxury European OEM.
  • •- Goldman Sachs kept a Sell rating with a target of Rs 560; JM Financial kept Reduce at Rs 735.
  • •- Management reiterated strong double-digit revenue growth for FY27, with ramp-ups in the second half.

Tata Technologies on Thursday delivered a quarter that the market loved and the brokerages, at least at the big end, declined to celebrate — a split-screen reaction that says as much about valuation as it does about the numbers themselves.

The engineering-services firm reported a 4 per cent year-on-year rise in second-quarter net profit to Rs 171 crore, against Rs 166 crore a year earlier. Operating revenue grew a brisk 28 per cent to Rs 1,699 crore, driven by strong momentum in services and continued demand from automotive and manufacturing clients. Services revenue — the core of the business — rose 34 per cent to Rs 1,356 crore, and grew 4.3 per cent sequentially in constant currency to $142 million. Operating EBITDA rose 32 per cent to Rs 273 crore, with the margin expanding 40 basis points from a year earlier to 16.1 per cent, flat quarter on quarter. Net income margin stood at 10.1 per cent.

The stock surged 8 per cent to Rs 808 on the BSE. Alongside the results, the company disclosed a $138 million engagement with a luxury European original equipment manufacturer, with revenue ramp-up expected to begin in the second half of FY27 — the kind of large, turnkey, full-vehicle programme that management says is an increasing share of the pipeline.

The bulls' case writes itself: 28 per cent revenue growth, 34 per cent services growth, margin expansion, and marquee deal wins from European OEMs. Management reinforced it, reiterating guidance for strong double-digit consolidated revenue growth in FY27, supported by large program ramp-ups in the second half, sustained deal momentum and healthy pipeline conversion. Growth is expected to be broad-based across automotive and non-automotive work, with a growing share of large, turnkey full-vehicle engagements providing revenue visibility.

Yet both major brokerages covering the stock held their bearish ground. Goldman Sachs maintained its Sell rating, raising its target only modestly to Rs 560 from Rs 540. The bank read the quarter as in line — constant currency revenue grew 1.9 per cent sequentially, services 4.3 per cent — decent, but not enough to change a view anchored in valuation. It did acknowledge the deal wins and steady execution by lifting the target slightly, while reiterating its expectation of double-digit organic growth this year.

JM Financial stayed on Reduce with a target of Rs 735, noting that while large deals enhance second-half growth visibility, that visibility is already reflected in the stock price. Its watch-list is specific: automotive demand in Germany, the technology segment, and the margin trajectory — three pressure points that together capture the bear case on European engineering spend and competitive pricing.

The gap between an 8 per cent rally and two bearish targets is the story. At current levels the market is capitalising Tata Technologies as a structural winner of the automotive software transition — EV platforms, software-defined vehicles, outsourced engineering — while the brokerages are pricing it as an auto-linked services vendor exposed to European demand cycles. Both can point to evidence: the deal wins and growth rates support the former; the 4 per cent profit growth against 28 per cent revenue growth — margins at 16.1 per cent remain modest for a company with this growth narrative — supports the latter.

Management itself supplied the honest caveat: sustaining 20 per cent-plus growth becomes increasingly challenging as the business scales, and the key determinants of the next leg are capability development, infrastructure and AI investments rather than demand availability. That is a company telling investors its constraint is no longer finding work, but building the capacity and skills to deliver it — a good problem, but one that compresses margins while the build-out runs.

The operational detail supports that reading. Headcount stood at 12,895, with last-twelve-month attrition at 15.6 per cent — elevated by historical standards for the ER&D industry, and a direct cost in wage inflation and delivery ramp-ups. Growing services revenue 34 per cent while profit inches up 4 per cent is the signature of a company hiring and investing ahead of the revenue it expects to book. That front-loading is a deliberate strategy — capability before contracts — but it means reported profit will lag revenue growth for as long as the expansion phase lasts, and it is precisely that lag the Sell camp is pricing in.

For the second half, then, the market has set its test. The $138 million European OEM deal and other large programmes begin contributing from H2 FY27; management has promised double-digit growth with improving visibility; and the stock at Rs 808 sits far above both broker targets, implying the rally has front-run the proof. If the ramp-ups land on schedule and margins hold near 16 per cent, the Sell ratings will look overly cautious. If European auto demand — JM Financial's first watch item — softens further, they will look prescient. Either way, Thursday's numbers gave each side exactly what it needed.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT WERE TATA TECHNOLOGIES' Q2 NUMBERS?
Net profit rose 4% to Rs 171 crore; revenue grew 28% to Rs 1,699 crore, with services revenue up 34% to Rs 1,356 crore.
WHY DID THE STOCK JUMP 8%?
Strong services growth, a 40-basis-point margin expansion to 16.1%, and a $138 million deal with a luxury European OEM announced alongside results.
WHY ARE GOLDMAN SACHS AND JM FINANCIAL BEARISH?
Goldman (Sell, target Rs 560) sees the quarter as in line and valuation as stretched; JM Financial (Reduce, target Rs 735) says deal-driven growth is already priced in, and flags German auto demand and margin risks.
WHAT IS MANAGEMENT'S FY27 OUTLOOK?
Strong double-digit consolidated revenue growth, driven by large programme ramp-ups in H2 FY27, with pipeline increasingly weighted toward large turnkey full-vehicle engagements. GLOSSARY: - Constant currency: Growth measured excluding the effect of exchange-rate movements. - EBITDA: Earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation — a measure of operating profit. - ER&D: Engineering research and development — outsourced engineering services. - OEM: Original equipment manufacturer, such as a car maker. - Turnkey engagement: A contract to deliver a complete programme, not just specific tasks.
