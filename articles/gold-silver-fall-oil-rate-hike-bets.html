Gold Falls Rs 2,000, Silver Slides as Oil Fuels Rate-Hike Bets

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- MCX December gold futures fell Rs 2,000 to Rs 1,44,196 per 10 grams; silver futures dropped Rs 5,700 to Rs 2,03,520 per kg.
  • •- Spot gold is down 3.5% this week, its worst weekly performance since June, after touching its lowest since July 20.
  • •- The driver: oil above $114 has revived inflation fears, pushing the probability of a US December rate hike to 80%.
  • •- Traders are watching whether spot gold breaks below $4,000 an ounce — a level analysts say would signal a regime shift.

The war premium in oil is being paid for in gold. Precious metals opened sharply lower on the Multi Commodity Exchange on Friday as rising crude prices strengthened expectations that interest rates will stay higher for longer, raising the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding assets. December 2026 gold futures on the MCX declined by Rs 2,000 to Rs 1,44,196 per 10 grams, while December silver futures slid Rs 5,700 to Rs 2,03,520 per kilogram — with intraday losses at points running steeper, at Rs 1,500 per 10 grams for gold and Rs 7,000 per kilogram for silver. All eyes now turn to the US Federal Reserve's October 27-28 policy meeting.

The international picture frames the domestic move. Spot gold fell 0.2% to $4,016.27 an ounce, recovering slightly after touching its lowest level since July 20 earlier in the session, and the metal has lost 3.5% so far this week — on course for its worst weekly performance since June. Spot silver fell 1% to $53.98 an ounce, platinum declined 0.1% to $1,568.58 and palladium held steady at $1,054.50, with all three metals heading for weekly losses. The uniformity matters: this is not a gold-specific story about central-bank buying or ETF flows, but a broad repricing of the rate path hitting every zero-yield asset at once. It is also a quietly historic moment for the metal. Just weeks after being bought as the ultimate hedge against exactly this kind of geopolitical escalation, gold is being sold off by the very inflation that escalation has caused — a inversion of its traditional role that analysts attribute to one structural change: the inflation now comes with a central bank that responds to it. In previous cycles the geopolitical bid and the rate-path hit roughly offset; in this one, with the December hike probability at 80%, they compound. The weekly numbers make the point numerically: gold's 3.5% decline is its steepest since June, and silver, platinum and palladium are all closing out the week lower alongside it — a synchronized withdrawal from the precious-metals complex rather than a rotation within it.

The transmission chain runs straight through crude. Oil climbed about 4% to a five-month high on Thursday after Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia raised the risk of supply disruption, and higher energy prices have reinforced expectations that the Fed will keep rates elevated for longer. The $4,000-an-ounce level is where the debate now sits. "Investors are watching the $4,000-an-ounce level closely, as a sustained break below it could indicate a shift away from expectations of interest rate relief towards concerns over persistent inflation, elevated yields and a stronger dollar," said Monte Safieddine, head of market research at Capital.com. Any further escalation in geopolitical tensions could push oil higher, reviving inflation concerns, lifting bond yields and delaying rate cuts, he added — a sequence in which the geopolitical hedge that gold traditionally provides gets overwhelmed by the rate arithmetic.

The market's focus now shifts to the Fed's October 27-28 meeting and next week's Personal Consumption Expenditures data, the central bank's preferred inflation measure as it targets 2%. Traders largely expect the Fed to leave rates unchanged next week, but the probability of a December rate hike stands at 80% — a pricing that leaves gold caught between two forces pulling in opposite directions. Escalation in the Gulf supports gold through safe-haven demand; the inflation and rate-hike fallout of that same escalation destroys it through yields. Friday's price action says the market currently believes the second force wins.

For Indian investors, the tactical picture from local desks is one of volatility with defined levels. Manoj Kumar Jain of Prithvi Finmart expects gold and silver to remain volatile through Friday's session, driven by swings in crude, the dollar index and geopolitical headlines, with gold support at $4,022-3,984 per ounce and resistance at $4,094-4,120; on the MCX, support sits at Rs 1,42,200-1,40,400 and resistance at Rs 1,44,100-1,45,000, while silver has support at Rs 1,96,000-1,93,300 and resistance at Rs 2,01,400-2,03,800. His advice splits the audience: long-term investors could accumulate through systematic investment plans during the decline, while traders should exercise caution on fresh positions until the US midterm elections pass. The distinction reflects the two different questions being asked of the metal right now — whether the decade's strongest structural driver, central-bank accumulation and de-dollarisation, survives a quarter of hawkish repricing (a question for SIP investors, whose answer is yes on most desks), and whether the next fortnight of Fed and election headlines offers longs any relief (a trader's question, whose answer on Friday morning was plainly no). In the physical markets, 22-carat gold in Delhi traded at Rs 1,04,600 per 8 grams and 24-carat at Rs 1,14,104 — levels that, a month ago, would have been described as all-time highs and are now a five-figure discount to them.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHY ARE GOLD AND SILVER FALLING?
Rising oil prices have revived inflation fears, strengthening expectations that the US Fed will keep rates higher for longer. That raises the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding metals, even as geopolitical risk would normally support them.
HOW MUCH HAVE PRICES MOVED?
MCX December gold futures fell Rs 2,000 to Rs 1,44,196 per 10 grams and silver fell Rs 5,700 to Rs 2,03,520 per kg. Spot gold is down 3.5% this week, its worst since June.
WHAT LEVEL MATTERS MOST FOR GOLD?
Analysts are watching $4,000 an ounce. A sustained break below it would signal a shift from rate-relief expectations toward concerns about persistent inflation, elevated yields and a stronger dollar. GLOSSARY: OPPORTUNITY COST — The return a holder forgoes by owning a non-yielding asset like gold instead of interest-bearing instruments; rising rates increase it. SPOT PRICE — The current market price for immediate delivery of a commodity, as distinct from futures contracts like those traded on the MCX. SIP (SYSTEMATIC INVESTMENT PLAN) — Investing a fixed amount at regular intervals, averaging out entry prices; the accumulation route recommended for long-term precious-metal investors during declines.
