Goldman, BNP Paribas Sell 68 Lakh BSE Shares Worth Rs 2,186 Crore

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Goldman Sachs and BNP Paribas sold a combined 68.33 lakh BSE shares worth Rs 2,186.44 crore through bulk deals on September 29, 2026
  • 02/UTI Mutual Fund and Nippon India Mutual Fund absorbed 52.43 lakh shares worth Rs 1,677.60 crore, both at Rs 3,200 per share
  • 03/BSE enters the Nifty 50 on September 30, replacing Wipro; the stock still rose 3.31% to Rs 3,200 on the sell-off day

Goldman Sachs and BNP Paribas exited a combined 68,33,264 shares of BSE Ltd on Tuesday, September 29, 2026. The sales were executed through bulk deals on the NSE.

The combined divestment was worth approximately Rs 2,186.44 crore. It equals roughly 1.67% of BSE's total equity.

Goldman Sachs Investments Mauritius I Limited sold 23,81,571 shares at Rs 3,200 apiece. BNP Paribas Financial Markets offloaded 44,51,693 shares at a weighted average of Rs 3,199.54.

The timing was deliberate. BSE's entry into the Nifty 50 index takes effect September 30, 2026, replacing Wipro after nearly three decades in the benchmark.

Domestic mutual funds absorbed most of the supply. UTI Mutual Fund bought 27,12,950 shares and Nippon India Mutual Fund bought 25,29,550 shares, both at Rs 3,200 per share.

Together, the two funds purchased 52,42,500 shares worth Rs 1,677.60 crore. That covered more than three-quarters of the shares the two foreign sellers dumped.

Despite the heavy supply, BSE shares ended Tuesday 3.31% higher at Rs 3,200. Demand included passive and index-related buying ahead of the inclusion.

CONTEXT: Index-inclusion trades follow a mechanical logic. Passive funds tracking the Nifty 50 must buy BSE shares at the September 30 reconstitution, on a pro-rata basis, at any price. Large existing holders know this mandated demand is coming, so they plan exits around it, selling into the liquidity the event itself creates. Foreign bank proprietary and ODI desks selling into domestic institutional demand is a familiar pattern in Indian markets around index changes.

The scale of BSE's inclusion explains the size of both the selling and the buying. Nuvama had estimated BSE's free-float market capitalisation in the Rs 1.4 lakh crore range, far above Wipro's roughly Rs 55,930 crore.

Nuvama had also projected roughly $741 million of inflows into BSE from Nifty 50 trackers. It estimated about $246 million of outflows from Wipro on the exit side.

BSE's Nifty 50 entry caps a remarkable re-rating through 2026. The stock has been one of the year's standout performers, driven by a derivatives volume boom and consistent earnings upgrades.

The transaction also shows where conviction sits. Foreign sellers monetised a large block at a predictable liquidity event, while domestic institutions bought a 1.67% stake at market price.

IMPACT: The immediate watchpoint is September 30 itself. Inclusion-day sessions in heavy free-float stocks often see elevated intraday volatility as index funds complete their mandated buying.

Over a longer horizon, the deal expands BSE's effective float and hands a meaningful stake to long-only domestic funds. That mix typically improves liquidity and can reduce share-price swings relative to its thinner-float past.

The signal from the buyer side matters too. UTI and Nippon buying at Rs 3,200 with no discount to the closing price suggests domestic institutions see room in the exchange's earnings trajectory even after the sharp run-up.

One caution: bulk-deal data captures only disclosed transactions, not total session activity. The full picture of who sold and who bought on September 29 may be larger than the Rs 2,186.44 crore on record.
