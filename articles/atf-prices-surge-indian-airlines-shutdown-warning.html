ATF Prices Soar 200%: Indian Airlines Warn of Operational Shutdown Risk

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Federation of Indian Airlines warns government it is "on the verge of closing down" as ATF prices spike to $260 per barrel
  • 02/Fuel costs have ballooned from 30-40% to 55-60% of airline operating expenses amid the West Asia conflict
  • 03/FIA demands reinstatement of crack band pricing, suspension of 11% excise duty, and VAT reduction across major states

India's airline industry has sent a Mayday call to the Union Government. The Federation of Indian Airlines (FIA) warned it is "on the verge of closing down or stopping operations" in a letter dated April 26, 2026.

The FIA represents major carriers including IndiGo, Air India, and SpiceJet. The letter was addressed to the Ministry of Civil Aviation and reviewed by businessline.

Aviation Turbine Fuel (ATF) prices have surged from $87.24 per barrel to a peak of $260.24 per barrel. They are currently trading above $235 per barrel amid the ongoing West Asia conflict.

Brent crude prices have climbed from about $72 per barrel to $118. Yet ATF has risen far more steeply, creating an unprecedented gap.

The crack differential between Brent crude and ATF has exploded. It widened from $11-18 per barrel earlier to over $132 per barrel now.

Fuel costs traditionally accounted for 30-40 per cent of airline expenditure. They have now ballooned to nearly 55-60 per cent of operating costs.

The government capped domestic ATF price increases at ₹15 per litre. International ATF prices, however, rose by ₹73 per litre, sharply widening losses.

Other headwinds include rupee depreciation, airspace closures, and higher supplier costs. These have compounded the financial strain on carriers.

The FIA has demanded three immediate measures. It wants reinstatement of the crack band pricing mechanism from 2022, temporary suspension of the 11 per cent excise duty on ATF, and reduction of VAT on aviation fuel in major states.

The association warned that failure to act could force capacity cuts, network reductions, and flight cancellations. Irrational ATF pricing could lead to aircraft being grounded.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
HOW MUCH HAVE ATF PRICES RISEN?
ATF PRICES HAVE SURGED FROM $87.24 PER BARREL TO A PEAK OF $260.24 PER BARREL AND ARE CURRENTLY TRADING ABOVE $235 PER BARREL. THIS REPRESENTS AN INCREASE OF NEARLY 200% FROM THE BASE LEVEL.
WHAT IS THE CRACK DIFFERENTIAL AND HOW HAS IT CHANGED?
THE CRACK DIFFERENTIAL BETWEEN BRENT CRUDE AND ATF HAS WIDENED SHARPLY FROM $11-18 PER BARREL EARLIER TO OVER $132 PER BARREL NOW. THIS SHOWS ATF IS RISING MUCH FASTER THAN UNDERLYING CRUDE OIL.
WHAT PERCENTAGE OF OPERATING COSTS DO FUEL COSTS NOW REPRESENT?
FUEL COSTS HAVE BALLOONED FROM THE TRADITIONAL 30-40 PER CENT OF AIRLINE EXPENDITURE TO NEARLY 55-60 PER CENT OF OPERATING COSTS.
WHAT IS THE FIA DEMANDING FROM THE GOVERNMENT?
THE FIA HAS URGED THREE MEASURES: REINSTATEMENT OF THE CRACK BAND PRICING MECHANISM INTRODUCED IN 2022, TEMPORARY SUSPENSION OF THE 11 PER CENT EXCISE DUTY ON ATF, AND REDUCTION OF VAT ON AVIATION FUEL IN MAJOR STATES.
WHAT WILL HAPPEN IF THE GOVERNMENT DOES NOT INTERVENE?
THE FIA WARNED THAT AIRLINES MAY BE FORCED TO RATIONALISE CAPACITY, REDUCE NETWORK DEPLOYMENT, GROUND AIRCRAFT, AND INCREASE FLIGHT CANCELLATIONS. THIS WOULD AFFECT PASSENGERS AND REGIONAL CONNECTIVITY.
