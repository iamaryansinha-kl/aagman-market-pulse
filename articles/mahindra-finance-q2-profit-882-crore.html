Mahindra Finance Q2 Profit Surges 55% to Rs 882 Crore on Volumes

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Standalone net profit rose 55% year-on-year to Rs 882 crore, from Rs 569 crore a year earlier.
  • •- Consolidated profit grew 61% to Rs 911 crore; consolidated total assets rose 21% to Rs 1.80 lakh crore.
  • •- Disbursements increased 22% to Rs 16,499 crore, led by gold loans, SME and mortgage lending.
  • •- Non-vehicle disbursements grew 52%; the non-wheel book is now 20% of consolidated lending AUM.
  • •- Credit cost moderated to 1.6% of average assets from 2.2%; gross Stage 3 assets fell to 3.4% from 3.9%.

Mahindra & Mahindra Financial Services closed the September quarter with the kind of print that settles a strategy debate. Standalone net profit rose 55% year-on-year to Rs 882 crore, from Rs 569 crore a year earlier, driven by higher business volumes and sharply lower credit costs; on a consolidated basis, profit rose 61% to Rs 911 crore while consolidated total assets grew 21% to Rs 1.80 lakh crore. The numbers, disclosed on an analyst call by Managing Director and CEO Raul Rebello, show a lender whose deliberate diversification away from pure vehicle finance is now showing up in both the growth and the quality of the book — and doing so at a scale, roughly a fifth of the total portfolio, where the shift is material to results rather than cosmetic.

The top line carried its share of the load. Revenue from operations rose 15% to Rs 5,164 crore, net interest income grew 17% to Rs 2,822 crore, and pre-provisioning operating profit expanded 20% to Rs 1,791 crore. But the quarter's real hero was the credit line: provisions and write-offs declined 16% to Rs 630 crore from Rs 751 crore, and credit cost moderated to 1.6% of average total assets from 2.2% a year earlier. In lending, that swing is worth more than any amount of volume growth — a 60-basis-point improvement in credit cost flows almost directly to profit, and it explains the majority of the 55% bottom-line jump. Net interest margin held unchanged at 7%.

Growth was broad-based across the disbursement book. Overall disbursements rose 22% to Rs 16,499 crore, led by gold loans, SME lending and mortgages — the non-vehicle finance categories, where disbursements grew 52% in aggregate, more than double the pace of the overall book. Within the wheel business, passenger vehicle disbursements rose 28% to Rs 6,826 crore, pre-owned vehicle financing grew 15% to Rs 2,834 crore, commercial vehicle and construction equipment disbursements rose 16% to Rs 2,657 crore, and tractor financing increased 13% to Rs 1,979 crore. Business assets under management grew 15% year-on-year to Rs 1.47 lakh crore. The strategic significance sits in the mix: non-wheel lending now accounts for 20% of consolidated lending AUM, against the passenger-and-commercial-vehicle book's 80% — a diversification target that has moved from slide-deck aspiration to a fifth of the balance sheet.

Asset quality improved on every measure the market watches. Gross Stage 3 assets declined to 3.4% from 3.9% a year earlier, Stage 2 assets fell to 4.7% from 5.8%, collection efficiency improved to 97% from 96%, and the provision coverage ratio on Stage 3 stood at 56.6%. The capital and liquidity position is fortress-grade for a lender of this size: capital adequacy of 18%, including Tier-I capital of 16.2%, and a liquidity buffer above Rs 16,150 crore that Rebello described as an all-time high — adding, pointedly, that the company may not need to raise it further. Management also laid out the profitability road map: a return on assets of 2.2-2.5% by FY31, against 2.2% delivered in the September quarter.

The print lands in the middle of a hard week for rate-sensitive financials, and that context flatters it. Benchmark yields touched multi-year highs on Wednesday after the RBI's October minutes confirmed a tightening committee, and Bajaj Finance — the day's other big NBFC earnings — was punished for a fee-income miss even while beating on profit. Against that backdrop, Mahindra Finance's combination of 22% disbursement growth, falling credit cost and improving collections reads as the cleaner story: a rural-anchored lender whose gold-loan and SME diversification is reducing its dependence on the vehicle cycle at precisely the moment the vehicle cycle faces financing-cost headwinds. The tractor book's 13% disbursement growth and the commercial-vehicle book's 16% suggest the rural and infrastructure-linked customers it finances are still borrowing, which matters because those segments are the first to stall when farm incomes tighten. The ROA path to 2.5% is ambitious but no longer aspirational; at 2.2% with credit cost still normalising, the company has less distance to travel than the FY31 framing suggests. The risk calendar, however, is not idle. A repo rate now at 5.5% with a unanimous MPC behind it means the cost of funds for every NBFC re-prices upward over the coming quarters, and rural borrowers — Mahindra Finance's core customers — carry fewer hedges against EMIs that rise than the urban salaried borrowers its larger competitors serve. The company's unchanged 7% net interest margin shows it has absorbed the early phase of tightening without compression; the test is whether it can hold that margin as funding costs climb and the rural book seasons. Wednesday's numbers say the balance sheet enters that test with room to spare. The stock's verdict, and the street's, will say much about whether the market now rewards rural credit growth over urban fee franchises.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT WERE MAHINDRA FINANCE'S Q2 NUMBERS?
Standalone profit rose 55% to Rs 882 crore; consolidated profit rose 61% to Rs 911 crore; disbursements grew 22% to Rs 16,499 crore.
WHAT DROVE THE PROFIT JUMP?
Higher volumes plus a sharp fall in credit provisions: provisions dropped 16% to Rs 630 crore and credit cost moderated to 1.6% of average assets from 2.2%.
HOW IS THE DIVERSIFICATION STORY PROGRESSING?
Non-vehicle disbursements grew 52%, and non-wheel lending now makes up 20% of consolidated lending AUM, with gold loans, SME and mortgage lending leading.
WHAT DID MANAGEMENT SAY ABOUT LIQUIDITY AND TARGETS?
The liquidity buffer is an all-time high above Rs 16,150 crore, and the company targets a return on assets of 2.2-2.5% by FY31. GLOSSARY: - Credit cost: Provisions and write-offs expressed as a percentage of average assets; a key driver of lender profitability. - Stage 3 assets: Loans in default or with significant credit deterioration under Indian accounting norms. - Disbursements: New loans paid out during the period, a measure of lending growth. - Return on assets: Profit expressed as a percentage of total assets; the standard profitability metric for lenders.
