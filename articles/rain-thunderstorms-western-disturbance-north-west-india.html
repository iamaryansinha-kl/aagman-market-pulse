Rain, Thunderstorms Likely As Western Disturbance Crosses North-West India

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Western disturbance crosses into North-West India on April 30 triggering cyclonic circulation over north Pakistan and adjoining Kashmir
  • 02/Bengaluru recorded 11 cm heavy rainfall accompanied by hail as trough passed directly over the city
  • 03/Australian wet season delivered 680 mm rainfall, 42% above long-term average, ends April 30 supporting timely Indian monsoon onset

The leading edge of a cooler western disturbance crossed into India on Thursday morning, April 30, 2026. It manifested as a cyclonic circulation over north Pakistan and adjoining parts of Kashmir.

A parent upper-air trough passed through Amritsar, Mudki, and Bathinda in Punjab. The disturbance triggered a cyclonic circulation over Punjab with a trough extending southward into south-west Rajasthan.

An additional upper-air cyclonic circulation is active over north-east Rajasthan. The constellation of weather systems will put a lid on rising mercury and usher in pre-monsoon conditions.

The forecast includes rain, thunderstorms, occasional lightning, and possible hail in isolated areas. The India Meteorological Department (IMD) said a successor disturbance will likely affect the hills of North-West India from Saturday.

Meanwhile, a robust anti-cyclonic circulation continued to dominate interior Maharashtra and neighbouring Telangana. This system has been responsible for the scorching summer conditions across the region.

Bengaluru bore the brunt of the wet weather as it lay directly in the path of a trough. The city recorded heavy rainfall accompanied by hail, with 11 cm falling in a short span.

Rain or thundershowers also occurred at most places over Himachal Pradesh, West Uttar Pradesh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Sikkim, Jammu and Kashmir, Ladakh, and East Uttar Pradesh. Many places across South Interior Karnataka, Bihar, Assam, Meghalaya, and north-eastern states also received rainfall.

The Australian wet season is likely drawing to a close around its usual time. This removes a potential obstacle to the timely onset and progression of the south-west monsoon over the Maldives, Sri Lanka, and India.

The Australian monsoon delivered approximately 680 mm of rainfall in northern Australia between October 1 and April 20. This is about 42 per cent above the long-term 1961-1990 average.

The Australian Bureau of Meteorology has forecast relatively little rainfall across most of northern Australia during May 2 to 15. With the transition to dry season now underway, the path is clearing for India's monsoon progression.
