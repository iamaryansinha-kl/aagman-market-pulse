Govt Rules Out Petrol, Diesel Price Hike After State Assembly Polls

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Joint Secretary Sujata Sharma says no proposal to raise petrol, diesel prices despite global crude surging past $114 per barrel
  • 02/State-run fuel retailers losing ₹2,400 crore daily; petrol sold at ₹20/litre loss, diesel at ₹100/litre loss below market rates
  • 03/Panic buying hit Andhra Pradesh with 400+ pumps dry; polls in 5 states end April 29 but government insists no post-election hike

The government has ruled out any immediate increase in petrol and diesel prices. Joint Secretary Sujata Sharma dismissed speculation of a post-poll hike at a news briefing on Tuesday.

Retail petrol and diesel prices have remained frozen since early April 2022. That marks four years without a change, even as global oil markets have swung wildly.

State-run fuel retailers are incurring heavy losses from selling below market rates. The daily loss is estimated at about ₹2,400 crore. Analysts had flagged a possible ₹25-28 per litre hike after elections.

Last week, Sharma stated oil marketing companies are losing about ₹20 per litre on petrol. The loss on diesel is roughly ₹100 per litre. These figures have widened sharply due to the crude surge.

Crude oil averaged over USD 114 per barrel this month. It was around USD 70 per barrel last year. The spike follows US-Israel strikes on Iran and Tehran's retaliation that shut the Strait of Hormuz.

Rumours of an imminent price hike triggered panic buying in Andhra Pradesh. Over 400 petrol pumps ran dry on Sunday. Some outlets saw demand rise by 30-33 per cent, according to the Petroleum Ministry.

Petrol is currently priced at ₹94.77 per litre in Delhi. Diesel costs ₹87.67 per litre. Sharma said India has adequate fuel inventories and all retail outlets are being monitored to prevent dry-outs.

"We have enough supplies of LPG, petrol and diesel. Prices are stable and there has been no increase in rates," Sharma said. "Don't believe in rumours. Avoid panic buying and rely on official sources."
