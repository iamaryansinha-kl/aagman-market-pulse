FPI Outflows Drop to ₹31,823 Cr in June, Lowest Since December 2025

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/FPIs sold ₹31,823 crore in June so far, the lowest monthly outflow since December 2025's ₹31,381 crore
  • 02/US-Iran agreement to reopen Strait of Hormuz on June 15 improved sentiment; foreigners turned buyers in 7 of 8 sessions since
  • 03/DIIs bought ₹76,156 crore in June, marking 35th consecutive month of buying, cushioning the market against foreign selling

Foreign portfolio investors sold shares worth ₹31,823 crore in June so far. This is the lowest monthly outflow since December 2025's ₹31,381 crore, according to StockEdge data.

February was the only month in 2026 when foreign investors were net buyers. They purchased ₹12,950 crore of equities that month.

The sell-off abated after the US and Iran reached an initial agreement on June 15 to reopen the Strait of Hormuz. Foreigners turned buyers in seven out of eight trading sessions since then.

March saw the worst monthly outflow on record at over ₹1.15 lakh crore. The February 28 war outbreak had pushed oil prices sharply higher, triggering panic selling.

So far in 2026, FPIs have been net sellers to the tune of nearly ₹2.90 lakh crore. This exceeds the ₹2.39 lakh crore pulled out during the entire previous year.

Domestic institutional investors bought shares worth ₹76,156 crore in June. This marks the 35th consecutive month of their monthly purchasing spree.

"Easing oil prices on receding geopolitical tensions in West Asia led to the foreign sell-off abating to some extent," said Riddhiman Jain, managing director at Waterfield Advisors.

The Nifty gained 2.2% in June but remains down 8% year-to-date. Analysts say sustained foreign inflows will depend on stronger earnings and economic growth.
