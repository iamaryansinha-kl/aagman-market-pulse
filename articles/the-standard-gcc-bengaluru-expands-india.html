The Standard Opens Second GCC in Bengaluru, Deepens India Technology Play

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/US insurer The Standard inaugurated its second global capability centre in Bengaluru on April 21, 2026, marking a major expansion of its India operations
  • 02/The Embassy Tech Village facility will focus on AI engineering, cloud platforms, data analytics, and digital transformation aligned with the company's global strategy
  • 03/The Standard first entered India in November 2025; the Bengaluru GCC represents a long-term bet on India's talent ecosystem as the firm transitions from vendor-led to in-house engineering

The Standard, a US-based insurance and financial services firm founded in 1906, opened its second global capability centre in Bengaluru on April 21, 2026. Karnataka IT/BT Minister Priyank Kharge inaugurated the facility at Embassy Tech Village.

The company first entered India in November 2025. The new centre marks its permanent Bengaluru office with plans for long-term expansion across software engineering, AI, and enterprise platforms.

The Bengaluru GCC will build capabilities in AI engineering, cloud platform development, data analytics, digital transformation, and insurance operations. The centre is designed as an integrated hub combining enterprise scale with startup agility.

The Standard manages $74.89 billion in assets under administration and employs approximately 5,186 people globally. Its parent company is Meiji Yasuda Life Insurance Company, which acquired The Standard in 2016.

Greg Chandler, Executive Vice President of Information Technology at The Standard, said the India operations play an increasingly critical role in the firm's technology transformation. "The launch of Standard India strengthens our ability to scale technology capabilities, accelerate digital transformation, and deliver solutions faster to customers," he said.

Mohua Sengupta, Senior Vice President and country head, said the GCC structure enables teams to build greenfield capabilities while working closely with global counterparts. The centre will support a gradual shift from vendor-led delivery to in-house engineering.

Bengaluru hosts over 875 global capability centres, contributing more than $22 billion in annual economic value and employing nearly 6 lakh professionals. The city accounts for 35% of India's total GCC workforce.

India's broader GCC ecosystem generates $64.6 billion in export revenue and employs over 1.9 million professionals nationwide. The insurance sector has emerged as a key growth driver, with 15% of GCC companies projecting significant fresher hiring in 2025.
