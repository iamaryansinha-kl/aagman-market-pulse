Infosys Profit Up 6% But Guidance Cut Sends ADRs Down 6%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Consolidated net profit rose 6% year-on-year to Rs 7,823 crore, below Street estimates of around 8% growth.
  • •- Revenue rose 11% to Rs 49,244 crore, in line with expectations; operating margin held at 21%.
  • •- FY27 constant-currency revenue growth guidance was trimmed to 1-2% from 1.5-3% on a challenging demand environment.
  • •- ADRs fell 6% after the announcement, despite $5.2 billion of large-deal bookings and AI revenue jumping 29% sequentially to 10.5% of the total.

The headline number was fine. The forecast was not. Infosys on Friday reported a 6% year-on-year rise in consolidated net profit to Rs 7,823 crore for the second quarter of FY27, with revenue from operations up 11% at Rs 49,244 crore. Revenue was broadly in line with what the Street wanted — an average of seven brokerages had pencilled in an 11% rise — but profit growth fell short of the roughly 8% the same estimates projected, and the company's decision to trim its full-year revenue growth guidance to 1-2% in constant currency, from 1.5-3% earlier, drowned out everything else in the announcement. Infosys cited a challenging demand environment; the market's verdict arrived within hours, with the ADRs crashing 6%.

The guidance cut is the third datapoint in a pattern that now defines the Indian IT sector's FY27: demand is not collapsing, but it is not accelerating either, and every fresh print seems to shave another fraction off the industry's growth ceiling. Sequentially, the picture was flat-to-modest — net profit edged up 0.7% from Rs 7,769 crore in the June quarter and revenue rose 2% from Rs 48,211 crore. In dollar terms, revenue was $5.15 billion, up 1.3% year-on-year and 1.2% sequentially; constant-currency growth of 1.5% year-on-year is the number that feeds directly into the newly cautious full-year band. Operating profit rose 10.4% to Rs 10,330 crore and the margin held at 21%, unchanged from a year earlier and marginally below June's 21.1%; the company retained its 20-22% operating margin guidance, and CFO Jayesh Sanghrajka credited cost discipline for holding the line "amidst heightened volatility."

The counterweight to the caution was the order book — and it was a serious counterweight. Total contract value of large deals signed in the quarter reached $5.2 billion, with new business contributing 62%, and the total included a mega deal valued at over $2 billion. Bookings at that scale, with nearly two-thirds from new logos rather than renewals, tell you clients are still committing to multi-year transformation programmes even while trimming run-the-business spend — a mix that favours the tier-one vendors over the mid-tier. "We had strong large deals of $5.2 billion, including a mega deal of over $2 billion, reinforcing our position as a trusted partner in the marketplace when clients embark on large and complex transformation programs," CEO Salil Parekh said. The AI business, meanwhile, keeps compounding: AI revenue accounted for 10.5% of total revenue in the quarter, up 29% sequentially. "As enterprises move to AI adoption at scale, the strong growth in AI revenues is a recognition of our differentiated market positioning and client confidence," Parekh said — a claim the ADR market, at least on Friday evening, declined to underwrite.

The segment detail shows exactly where the demand challenge lives. Life sciences grew a blistering 32.5% in constant currency, energy and utilities 7.3%, communications 4.1% and financial services 2.1% — but hi-tech plunged 17.3%, retail fell 7.7%, and manufacturing slipped 0.7%. Geographically, Europe grew 4.6% while North America, still 54.6% of revenue, declined 1.5%. Read together, that is a profile of enterprise clients in cost-discipline mode: discretionary transformation budgets cut, but regulatory and AI-led spending intact. The hi-tech decline matters most for the narrative — tech clients were for a decade the industry's growth engine, and their retrenchment is precisely what guidance cuts are made of.

There was comfort elsewhere in the filing for those willing to look past the headline guidance number. Free cash flow came in at Rs 8,898 crore and consolidated cash and investments stood at Rs 45,232 crore at the end of September — a balance sheet that lets the company keep investing through the demand trough rather than retreating from it. Headcount rose by 4,480 to 3,32,542, and voluntary attrition in IT services eased to 12.7% from 13% — signs of a company managing its cost base deliberately rather than bracing for a storm. An interim dividend of Rs 26 per share, with a record date of November 3, 2026, gives shareholders something concrete to hold while the growth debate plays out across the analyst community and in the ADR market.For the first half of FY27, profit rose 9% to Rs 15,592 crore on revenue of Rs 97,454 crore, up 12%.

The read-across to the rest of the sector is the real significance of Friday's print. Infosys was the only Nifty sector in green on a brutal market day, and its results — decent revenue, soft profit, softer guidance — will frame expectations for TCS and the rest of the earnings calendar. The ADR's 6% drop suggests offshore investors see the guidance cut as the start of a sequence rather than a solitary caution; the deal pipeline and the AI ramp suggest the company itself disagrees. One of them will be right by March — and in the meantime, the 1-2% guidance band has effectively reset the sector's earnings math for the year, pulling consensus estimates down with it in the brokerages' weekend notes.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT WERE INFOSYS'S Q2 NUMBERS?
Net profit of Rs 7,823 crore, up 6% from Rs 7,364 crore a year earlier, and below the roughly 8% growth analysts expected. Revenue rose 11% to Rs 49,244 crore, in line with estimates, and the operating margin held at 21%.
WHAT GUIDANCE DID INFOSYS GIVE?
It cut FY27 constant-currency revenue growth guidance to 1-2% from 1.5-3%, citing a challenging demand environment, while retaining its 20-22% operating margin guidance.
HOW DID THE MARKET REACT?
ADRs fell 6% after the announcement. The stock had ended the regular session almost flat at Rs 1,153.80 on a day when the Nifty IT index was the only sectoral gainer.
WHAT ABOUT DEALS AND AI?
Large-deal TCV was $5.2 billion including a mega deal above $2 billion, with 62% from new business. AI services revenue grew 29% sequentially to 10.5% of total revenue. GLOSSARY: CONSTANT CURRENCY — Growth measured with exchange rates held steady, stripping out the effect of currency movements on reported revenue. DEAL BOOKINGS (TCV) — Total contract value of deals signed in a period; a forward indicator of future revenue, as opposed to revenue already earned. ADR (AMERICAN DEPOSITARY RECEIPT) — A US-listed instrument representing shares of a foreign company; Infosys ADRs trade during US hours and often react to results before the Indian listing. ATTRITION — The rate at which employees leave voluntarily; falling attrition signals stabilising demand for IT talent.
