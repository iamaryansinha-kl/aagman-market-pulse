Rupee Slips on Week as Iran Flare-Up Spurs Caution, Merchant Hedging

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

The rupee is feeling the heat from the Middle East.

The Indian currency slipped on the week as the flare-up between Iran and the United States spurred caution among traders and prompted merchant hedging. Higher oil prices and safe-haven demand for the dollar kept the rupee under pressure, though the Reserve Bank of India's intervention prevented a sharper depreciation.

Merchant hedging intensified as oil prices surged. Indian oil importers, concerned about further crude price increases, rushed to hedge their foreign exchange exposure. This created additional dollar demand in the market, putting downward pressure on the rupee. The hedging activity was particularly heavy among state-run oil marketing companies.

The safe-haven dollar bid added to the pressure. When geopolitical tensions rise, global investors typically move toward the US dollar, which is considered the world's safest currency. This flight-to-safety dynamic pushed the dollar index higher, making the rupee and other emerging market currencies weaker by comparison.

The RBI intervened to smooth volatility. The central bank sold dollars and bought rupees to prevent a disorderly depreciation. While the RBI does not target a specific exchange rate level, it aims to prevent excessive volatility that could disrupt trade and investment flows. The intervention was measured but consistent throughout the week.

Despite the weekly slip, the rupee outperformed some peers. The currency remained relatively stable compared to other Asian currencies, which saw sharper declines. India's strong forex reserves, now at $674.19 billion, provide a solid buffer that reassures markets about the RBI's capacity to manage volatility.

The outlook depends on the Middle East. If tensions de-escalate and oil prices retreat, the rupee could recover lost ground. If the conflict escalates further, pushing oil above $90 per barrel, the currency could face renewed pressure despite RBI intervention.
