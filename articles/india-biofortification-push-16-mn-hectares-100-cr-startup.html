India's Biofortification Push: 16 Mn Hectares, ₹100 Cr Startup Bet

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/India has developed 142+ biofortified crop varieties now planted across 16 million hectares, up from 10 million hectares six years ago
  • 02/Hidden hunger from micronutrient deficiencies costs India an estimated 2.4% of GDP, with 58.5% of children anemic per NFHS-4 data
  • 03/Better Nutrition, a Lucknow-based biofortified food startup, targets ₹100 crore revenue by FY27 and works with 20,000+ farmers

Prateek Rastogi, CEO of Better Nutrition, argues India won the calorie war but lost the nutrition battle. The Green Revolution boosted yields while decades of intensive farming stripped soil minerals.

India today produces a record 353.96 million tonnes of food grain. Godowns overflow and exports thrive. Yet millions remain quietly deficient in iron, zinc, and protein.

Nutrition scientists call this "hidden hunger." The NFHS-4 survey found 58.5% of children anemic. A 2020 meta-analysis revealed 54% iron deficiency, 53% vitamin B12 deficiency, and 61% vitamin D deficiency across India.

The economic toll is severe. Hidden hunger from micronutrient deficiencies costs India an estimated 2.4% of GDP annually. It manifests as lower immunity, stunted growth, and reduced cognitive ability generation after generation.

Biofortification offers a deceptively simple fix: breed crops to be inherently richer in micronutrients at the seed stage. No supplements, no powders, no diet overhaul required.

ICAR has released 142+ biofortified varieties including 43 wheat and 10 rice types. Over 16 million hectares are now planted with these crops. Pearl millet zinc content has leapt from 30 mg to 81 mg.

Better Nutrition, founded by IIM Ahmedabad alumnus Prateek Rastogi and Aishwarya Bhatnagar, sells biofortified atta, rice, and pulses. The Lucknow startup targets ₹100 crore revenue by FY27, up from ₹11.5 crore in FY25.

The company scaled monthly revenue from ₹10 lakh to ₹1 crore within six months of consumer launch. It now runs a 150 tonnes per month production rate across Blinkit, Swiggy Instamart, BigBasket, Zepto, Amazon, and Flipkart.

Backed by PV Sindhu and Namita Thapar from Shark Tank India Season 4, Better Nutrition works with over 20,000 farmers. The model pays premium prices for nutrient-dense crops through its Kisan Ki Dukan network of 75 agri-input stores.

"The Green Revolution saved India from hunger. The Smart Nutrition Revolution will save India from deficiency," Rastogi wrote in The Hindu BusinessLine on May 3, 2026. "One was about filling stomachs. The other is about building stronger bodies, sharper minds, healthier futures."

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT IS BIOFORTIFICATION AND HOW DOES IT WORK?
BIOFORTIFICATION IS THE PROCESS OF BREEDING CROPS TO BE NATURALLY RICHER IN ESSENTIAL MICRONUTRIENTS LIKE IRON, ZINC, AND PROTEIN AT THE SEED STAGE. THE CROP ITSELF BECOMES THE NUTRITION SOURCE WITHOUT NEEDING SUPPLEMENTS OR DIETARY CHANGES.
HOW WIDESPREAD IS HIDDEN HUNGER IN INDIA?
NEARLY HALF THE WORLD'S MICRONUTRIENT-DEFICIENT POPULATION LIVES IN INDIA. NFHS-4 DATA SHOWS 58.5% OF CHILDREN ARE ANEMIC, WHILE A 2020 META-ANALYSIS REVEALS 54% IRON DEFICIENCY, 53% VITAMIN B12 DEFICIENCY, AND 61% VITAMIN D DEFICIENCY ACROSS THE POPULATION.
WHAT PROGRESS HAS INDIA MADE ON BIOFORTIFIED CROPS?
ICAR HAS DEVELOPED 142+ BIOFORTIFIED VARIETIES INCLUDING 43 WHEAT AND 10 RICE TYPES. OVER 16 MILLION HECTARES ARE NOW PLANTED WITH BIOFORTIFIED CROPS, UP FROM 10 MILLION HECTARES SIX YEARS AGO.
WHAT IS BETTER NUTRITION AND WHAT DOES IT SELL?
BETTER NUTRITION IS A LUCKNOW-BASED STARTUP FOUNDED BY PRATEEK RASTOGI AND AISHWARYA BHATNAGAR. IT SELLS BIOFORTIFIED ATTA, RICE, PULSES, AND OILS ENRICHED WITH IRON, ZINC, PROTEIN, AND CALCIUM THROUGH QUICK COMMERCE AND E-COMMERCE PLATFORMS.
WHAT ARE BETTER NUTRITION'S GROWTH TARGETS?
THE COMPANY REPORTED ₹11.5 CRORE REVENUE IN FY25 AND TARGETS ₹27 CRORE IN FY26. IT AIMS TO CROSS ₹100 CRORE BY FY27 WITH A MONTHLY PRODUCTION RUN RATE OF 150 TONNES AND A NETWORK OF OVER 20,000 FARMERS.
