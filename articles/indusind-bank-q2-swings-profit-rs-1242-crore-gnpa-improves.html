IndusInd Bank Swings to Q2 Profit of Rs 1,242 Crore, GNPA Improves

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

Private sector lender IndusInd Bank reported a net profit of Rs 1,242 crore for the quarter ended September 2026, compared with a loss of Rs 437 crore in the year-ago period. The result, announced after market hours on Friday, beat the CNBC-TV18 poll estimate of Rs 1,066 crore and marked another step in the bank's long rebuild after its derivatives accounting crisis.

The turnaround was visible across the profit-and-loss statement. Operating profit increased 40.4% year-on-year to Rs 2,875 crore from Rs 2,047 crore, surpassing the poll estimate of Rs 2,687 crore. Pre-provision operating profit rose to Rs 2,874 crore from Rs 2,047 crore a year earlier, as operating expenses declined to Rs 3,943 crore from Rs 4,013 crore.

Net revenue for the quarter increased to Rs 6,817 crore from Rs 6,060 crore in the year-ago period. For the half year ended September 2026, net interest income rose to Rs 9,254 crore from Rs 9,049 crore, and fee income increased to Rs 4,034 crore from Rs 3,808 crore.

The one soft spot was net interest income. NII rose 3.6% year-on-year to Rs 4,569 crore from Rs 4,409 crore but came in below the CNBC-TV18 poll estimate of Rs 4,710 crore. Yield on assets stood at 8.35% for the quarter, down from 8.75% a year earlier, though the cost of funds declined more sharply to 5.11% from 5.43%.

Asset quality improved meaningfully. Gross non-performing assets declined to 2.73% of gross advances as of September 30, 2026, from 3.25% as of June 30 and from 3.60% a year earlier. Net NPAs fell to 0.83% from 0.95% quarter-on-quarter and 1.04% a year ago. Provisions and contingencies, excluding tax, dropped to Rs 1,223 crore from Rs 2,631 crore in the corresponding quarter last year. The provision coverage ratio stood at 70.19%.

The balance sheet continued to expand. Deposits increased to Rs 4,28,832 crore from Rs 3,89,600 crore a year earlier, while advances rose to Rs 3,63,104 crore from Rs 3,25,881 crore. Current account and savings account deposits stood at Rs 1,19,766 crore, accounting for 27.93% of total deposits. Retail deposits as defined under the liquidity coverage ratio grew to Rs 2,01,780 crore from Rs 1,80,475 crore. The bank's total balance sheet stood at Rs 5,80,148 crore compared with Rs 5,27,490 crore a year earlier.

Capital adequacy under Basel III stood at 16.59% as of September 30, 2026, with Tier 1 capital at 15.55%. Risk-weighted assets increased to Rs 4,25,746 crore from Rs 3,98,256 crore.

Despite the profit beat, Jefferies cut its target price on the stock, with the NII miss cited as the reason. The muted net interest income underscores that the bank's core lending profitability is still normalising after the turbulence of the past year, even as fee income, cost discipline and credit costs all move in the right direction.

Rajiv Anand, Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer of IndusInd Bank, framed the quarter as evidence the rebuild is working. He said the results demonstrate that growth has strengthened, profitability has improved, asset quality trends remain encouraging, and the funding profile is becoming more granular and resilient. He also pointed to increasing traction across retail businesses alongside continued improvement in the liability franchise.

The numbers partly back his case. Profit for the first half of FY27 stood at Rs 2,279 crore, compared with just Rs 167 crore in the corresponding period last year. Total income comprising interest and fee income came in at Rs 27,140 crore for H1 FY27 against Rs 27,681 crore a year earlier, meaning the profit improvement has been driven by margins on liabilities, cost control and lower provisions rather than top-line growth.

The bank's distribution network comprised 3,139 branches and banking outlets and 2,773 ATMs as of September 2026, serving a client base of approximately 42 million.

For investors, the Q2 print presents a familiar post-crisis pattern: the turnaround is real and measurable, but the market will keep paying only for quarters where the NII line cooperates. Shares of IndusInd Bank ended 1.76% lower at Rs 835 on Friday ahead of the results, and the stock reaction on Monday will test whether the profit beat or the NII miss frames the narrative. The medium-term question is whether the bank can convert its repaired asset quality and growing retail deposit base into consistent loan-book growth and margin recovery through the remainder of FY27.
