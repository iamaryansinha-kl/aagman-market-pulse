Silver Tumbles Rs 35,000 in 4 Days: Futures Crash 40% From January Peak

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/MCX silver futures crashed Rs 35,300 in four trading sessions after India raised import duty on gold and silver to 15%
  • 02/Prices have corrected nearly 40% from January record high of Rs 4.39 lakh to Rs 2,65,949 per kg
  • 03/Analysts remain cautious near-term; Tata Mutual Fund recommends staggered buying for medium- to long-term investors

MCX silver futures have plummeted Rs 35,300 over the past four trading sessions. The crash followed the Centre's decision to raise import duty on gold and silver to 15% from 9%.

In Monday's session alone, MCX silver futures for July 2026 delivery slumped Rs 5,643, or 2.1%, to settle at Rs 2,65,949 per kg. The white metal opened with a sharp gap-down.

This marks a dramatic reversal from January 2026. Silver had surged to a record high of Rs 4.39 lakh per kg earlier this year.

In just four months, prices have corrected nearly 40%. The selloff reflects demand destruction, macroeconomic worries, and shifting investor sentiment.

Unlike gold, silver derives substantial demand from industrial applications. It is used in solar panels, semiconductors, batteries, electronics, electric vehicles, and AI infrastructure.

"Markets began focusing on the potential impact of prolonged elevated oil prices on global growth momentum. That concern tends to affect industrial metals more heavily than pure defensive assets," said Ponmudi R.

Nirpendra Yadav of Bonanza noted that the duty hike materially raises local prices. Higher costs could dampen jewellery demand and slow industrial imports across segments.

From a technical standpoint, silver is holding above the Rs 2,73,000 mark. Immediate resistance is seen in the Rs 2,76,000-Rs 2,77,000 zone, while a breach below Rs 2,71,000 may drag prices to Rs 2,67,000-Rs 2,68,000.

Tata Mutual Fund said the weakening global economic outlook could restrict silver demand over the medium term. Slower solar installations and unwinding of large long positions have eased supply tightness.

However, Tata Mutual Fund maintained that silver remains a compelling long-term structural story. Rising demand from AI infrastructure, green energy, solar capacity, and electric vehicles supports the bull case.
