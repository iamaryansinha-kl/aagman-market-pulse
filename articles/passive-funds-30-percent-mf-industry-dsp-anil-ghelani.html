Passive Funds To Hit 30% Of MF Industry In 5 Years, Says DSP's Anil Ghelani

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/DSP Mutual Fund's Head of Passive Investments Anil Ghelani predicts passive funds will rise from 17% to 30% of industry AUM within five years
  • 02/In the US, passive funds already exceed 50% of total mutual fund industry; India's 17% share has significant room to grow
  • 03/Passive strategies are becoming core portfolio allocations while active funds shift to satellite roles for alpha opportunities

Anil Ghelani, Head of Passive Investments at DSP Mutual Fund, predicts a seismic shift in India's mutual fund landscape. He expects ETFs and index funds to grow from 17% to 30% of total industry assets within five years.

Passive investing is gaining rapid traction among Indian retail investors. The trend reflects a permanent evolution in how investors build long-term wealth portfolios.

In the United States, passive funds already command over 50% of the total mutual fund industry. India's 17% current share indicates substantial runway for growth ahead.

Ghelani emphasized that the more interesting trend is not just size growth but evolving investor behaviour. Investors are moving from chasing star fund managers to focusing on prudent asset allocation aligned with life goals.

ETFs and index funds are becoming natural beneficiaries of this behavioural shift. Passive strategies are increasingly forming the core of investor portfolios.

Active funds will likely shift to selective satellite allocations for alpha opportunities. Ghelani believes "and" is better than "or" — a blend of passive core and active satellite strategies will emerge.

The case for passive investing is strongest in the large-cap segment where information is real-time and alpha is shrinking. Active management may retain an edge in small-cap and micro-cap segments where bottom-up research opportunities exist.

For long-term goals like child education over 10-15 years, Ghelani recommends a simple index fund with disciplined SIP strategy. Staying invested and maintaining right asset allocation matters more than finding the best-performing fund.
