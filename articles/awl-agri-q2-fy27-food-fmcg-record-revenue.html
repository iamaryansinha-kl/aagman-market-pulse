AWL Agri Q2 FY27: Food & FMCG Posts Record Rs 2,000 Crore

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/AWL Agri Business reported 24% YoY revenue growth and 12% YoY volume growth for Q2 FY27, per an exchange filing.
  • 02/The Food & FMCG segment crossed Rs 2,000 crore of quarterly revenue for the first time, growing 33% YoY.
  • 03/Edible oils grew only 3% by volume while branded exports surged 59% — the FMCG pivot is gaining scale, but margins remain the next test.

AWL Agri Business Ltd (formerly Adani Wilmar) (NSE: AWL) reported a 24% year-on-year jump in revenue for the second quarter of fiscal 2027. Volumes grew 12% YoY in the quarter ended September 30, 2026, according to an exchange filing.

The standout was the Food & FMCG segment. Quarterly revenue crossed Rs 2,000 crore for the first time in the company's history, rising 33% YoY to set a record.

The growth is spreading beyond the core portfolio. Besan, Pulses, Soya Nuggets, Sugar, Sattu and Poha now contribute more than 35% of the Food portfolio and delivered around 50% YoY revenue growth in the quarter. Wheat and rice businesses also posted robust growth.

Fortune Pulses crossed Rs 100 crore in quarterly revenue for the first time. The company is accelerating its distribution across General Trade and Quick Commerce channels.

The legacy edible oil business told a quieter story. Volumes grew just 3% YoY, led by domestic oils — Mustard Oil and Rice Bran Oil.

A regulatory shift is underway in that segment. From September 2026, the government standardized pack sizes for edible oils, phasing out the industry's "grammage play." The company described it as a positive structural development that improves transparency and creates a level playing field.

Industry Essentials, the B2B arm spanning Oleochemicals, Castor and De-oiled Cakes, delivered 25% YoY volume growth. The growth was broad-based across categories.

Alternate channels are scaling quickly. E-commerce, Quick Commerce and Modern Trade all posted double-digit growth; within Food & FMCG, e-commerce and quick commerce together grew 40% YoY.

Branded Exports surged 59% by volume, the fastest growth in the portfolio. HoReCa, the hotel-restaurant-catering channel, grew 21% YoY.

The subsidiaries contributed as well. GD Foods (Tops), the Sauces & Condiments business, grew revenue more than 30% YoY, with Noodles, Vermicelli, Jams and Instant Mixes scaling. Omkar Chemicals posted 16% YoY volume growth.

The company also launched Fortune Yellow Mustard Oil during the quarter, adding to its premium regional oils range.

Investors responded positively to the update. Shares rose nearly 4% on October 6, 2026, to a day's high of Rs 183 on the NSE.

The rally needs that context. The stock is still down 3% in one month, down 24% in the calendar year, down nearly 32% over one year, and down over 48% in three years.

One caution for readers: these are provisional business-update numbers, not audited results. How much of the 24% revenue growth converts into profit will only become clear when the full quarterly results are declared.

The FMCG pivot is now two quarters deep and gaining scale. The record Food & FMCG quarter, the more-than-35% contribution from beyond-core categories, and the 59% branded-export surge suggest the diversification strategy is working. The next test is whether the higher-margin mix shows up in earnings.

GLOSSARY: FMCG — Fast-Moving Consumer Goods: everyday packaged products such as flour, besan, pulses and oils that sell quickly at relatively low prices. HoReCa — Hotel, Restaurant and Catering: a distribution channel that sells to commercial food businesses rather than retail consumers. Quick Commerce — rapid-delivery retail channels that fulfill orders in minutes, an increasingly important route to market for packaged foods. Oleochemicals — chemicals derived from plant and animal oils and fats, used in products ranging from soaps and cosmetics to industrial applications. Grammage Play — an industry practice of adjusting pack weights to mask price changes; standardized edible-oil pack norms effective September 2026 are phasing this out. De-oiled Cakes — protein-rich residue left after oil is extracted from seeds, sold largely as animal feed.
