Gold falls 0.6% to $4,684 as Iran peace talks fail and oil prices spike

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Spot gold fell 0.6% to $4,684.32 per ounce as US-Iran peace talks faltered on May 11, 2026
  • 02/Oil prices jumped on Strait of Hormuz closure, pushing inflation and rate-hike fears higher for investors
  • 03/April US CPI data due this week; Fed warned Iran war tops financial stability risk list

Spot gold fell 0.6% to $4,684.32 per ounce early Monday. US gold futures for June delivery dropped 0.8% to $4,692.70.

President Donald Trump rejected Iran's response to a US peace proposal on Sunday. The 10-week-old conflict has paralyzed the Strait of Hormuz and driven up global energy prices.

"We're essentially seeing an unwinding of hopes for an imminent (peace) deal," said Tim Waterer, chief market analyst at KCM Trade. Gold is feeling the pinch from renewed crude price rises.

The dollar firmed, making dollar-priced bullion costlier for overseas buyers. Rising oil prices risk pushing inflation higher, increasing prospects of elevated interest rates.

While gold is traditionally a hedge against inflation, high rates weigh on the non-yielding asset. The Fed's Friday report cited the Iran war as the top financial stability concern.

China's gold production fell in Q1 2026 from a year earlier. Safety inspections led some smelters to suspend production, the China Gold Association reported on Saturday.

Spot silver rose 0.7% to $80.88 per ounce. Platinum slid 0.6% to $2,042.71, and palladium dropped 0.4% to $1,484.99.

"In the near-to-medium term, the $4,400 to $4,800 range still looks firmly in play," Waterer added. Investors await April's US CPI data due later this week.
