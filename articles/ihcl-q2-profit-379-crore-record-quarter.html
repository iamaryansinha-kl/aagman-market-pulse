IHCL Profit Jumps 19% to Rs 379 Crore, 18th Record Quarter

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Consolidated profit rose 19.35% to Rs 379.85 crore in the September quarter, from Rs 318.26 crore a year ago.
  • •- Revenue from operations grew to Rs 2,484.32 crore from Rs 2,040.89 crore.
  • •- MD and CEO Puneet Chhatwal called it the company's 18th consecutive record quarter.
  • •- Domestic same-store RevPAR rose 25%; management fee income and select-service revenue each grew 25%.
  • •- IHCL upgraded consolidated FY27 revenue growth guidance from 12-14% to 15-17%.

Tata Group-owned Indian Hotels Company delivered its eighteenth consecutive record quarter on Wednesday, and then raised its full-year forecast on the strength of it. Consolidated profit for the July-September period rose 19.35% to Rs 379.85 crore, from Rs 318.26 crore a year earlier, while revenue from operations climbed to Rs 2,484.32 crore from Rs 2,040.89 crore — growth of roughly 22% on the top line. Total expenses rose in step to Rs 2,017.41 crore from Rs 1,671.54 crore, a cost line that includes the running drag from newly opened and recently acquired properties yet to reach maturity. For a business that owns some of the most valuable hotel real estate in the country, the margin question is never whether costs grow — they always do in this sector — but whether revenue grows faster, and for eighteen straight quarters it has.

The quality of the growth is what the management chose to emphasise. "The second quarter of FY27 marks the eighteenth consecutive record quarter," said Managing Director and CEO Puneet Chhatwal, pointing to a 25% increase in RevPAR from domestic same-store hotels, a 25% increase in management fee income led by new-opening momentum, 25% revenue growth in the select-service business, and the contribution from the recent acquisitions of Brij and Atmantan. Eighteen record quarters is a streak that now spans four and a half years — through a pandemic recovery, a rate-tightening cycle and an oil shock — and it positions IHCL as one of the most consistently compounding consumer businesses in the listed Indian market.

The guidance upgrade is the day's market-moving detail. IHCL raised its consolidated revenue growth guidance for FY27 from 12-14% to 15-17%, driven by 17-19% growth in the hotel segment, seasonally strong quarters ahead, high demand from social and MICE events and strong non-like-for-like growth from signings and acquisitions. Chhatwal was explicit about the reasoning: "I see mostly green shoots going forward. That's why we have increased the guidance by 300 basis points. It all depends on how November and January pan out. October is outstanding and if November and January end up being good, we could do even more."

The pipeline numbers support the confidence. IHCL signed 47 hotels in the first half across its brandscape and now operates a portfolio of 670 hotels, with management asserting it will hit its Accelerate 2030 target of 700 properties well ahead of schedule. The asset-light machine — management fees from new openings — is precisely the high-margin revenue that grew 25% in the quarter, which means the growth itself is getting more profitable as it scales, rather than less. Select-service, the mid-market segment that typically carries the thinnest amenities but the strongest per-room economics, matched that pace with 25% revenue growth, giving the company a second compounding engine below the luxury tier where the Taj brand does its pricing work.

The one soft spot is aviation-adjacent. TajSATS, the inflight catering business, was hurt by disruption in air travel, including cancellations of international flights particularly by Air India — a knock-on effect of the West Asia conflict that has otherwise stayed away from IHCL's P&L. Management's read is that the business is beginning to come back, but the episode is a useful reminder of how much of India's services economy remains one regional conflict away from a supply shock: hoteliers can rebook rooms, but a cancelled flight is a meal, a room night and an airport transfer all lost in the same afternoon. Overseas, occupancy is nearly back at two of the company's three Dubai hotels, with the third, The Palm, still at about 70% of pre-crisis levels given its leisure-driven clientele. London and New York are performing well, San Francisco is coming back strongly, Cape Town has been very strong, and the company opened its first lodge — the Taj Bush Lodge — in South Africa's Kruger National Park, with two more in the same area expected operational within six months, by February. Frankfurt, the newest market, is beginning to settle.

The read-across for the travel economy is straightforward: domestic hospitality demand is running ahead of 20% growth by the company's own expectation, social and MICE event demand is filling festive-season calendars, and pricing power — the 25% RevPAR number — remains firmly with hoteliers. In an earnings season where several consumer-facing companies have blamed input costs and cautious spending, IHCL's print is evidence that the top end of Indian consumption is not participating in any slowdown. The company's own framing does the analytical work: management fee income growing 25% means owners of hotel real estate are choosing IHCL's brands over competitors at an accelerating rate, and that income is annuity-like — earned on rooms the company does not have to finance, build or depreciate. The watch item is the one management itself flagged: November and January, the two months that will decide whether the upgraded guidance proves conservative — or merely adequate.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT WERE IHCL'S Q2 NUMBERS?
Consolidated profit rose 19.35% to Rs 379.85 crore from Rs 318.26 crore a year ago; revenue from operations grew to Rs 2,484.32 crore from Rs 2,040.89 crore.
WHAT IS THE SIGNIFICANCE OF THE 18TH RECORD QUARTER?
It extends a streak of record quarters that has run for over four years, underscoring consistent compounding through multiple macro cycles.
WHAT GUIDANCE DID IHCL GIVE FOR FY27?
Consolidated revenue growth of 15-17%, raised from 12-14%, with the hotel segment guided to grow 17-19%.
WHAT WAS THE WEAKEST PART OF THE BUSINESS?
TajSATS inflight catering, hurt by air-travel disruption including Air India cancellations; the Palm hotel in Dubai remains at about 70% of pre-crisis occupancy. GLOSSARY: - RevPAR: Revenue per available room, the hotel industry's standard measure of pricing and occupancy combined. - MICE: Meetings, incentives, conferences and exhibitions — a category of group hotel demand. - Select-service hotels: Mid-market properties with limited food-and-beverage operations, typically higher-margin per room. - Asset-light model: Growing through management contracts rather than owning hotel real estate.
