Navi Mumbai, Panvel to Issue Municipal Bonds for First Time

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Navi Mumbai targets ₹1,000 crore bond issue, Panvel plans smaller maiden municipal bond issuance over next few weeks
  • 02/Navi Mumbai rated AA+ by India Ratings, Panvel rated AA- by Care Ratings; both cities have debt-free balance sheets
  • 03/Government offers 2 percentage point interest subvention and ₹100 crore incentive for municipal bond issuers

Navi Mumbai and Panvel are preparing to issue municipal bonds for the first time. Three merchant bankers said the cities will tap the debt market in the coming weeks. This signals growing momentum in India's local municipal bond market.

Navi Mumbai could raise around ₹1,000 crore. Panvel will target a smaller amount, with the quantum still being finalised. The bankers requested anonymity as the discussions are private.

Navi Mumbai has secured a AA+ credit rating from India Ratings. Panvel received a AA- rating from Care Ratings. The strong ratings reflect strategic locations, solid revenue generation and debt-free balance sheets.

Over 20 Indian cities have raised more than ₹4,500 crore through municipal bonds over the last nine years. However, Mumbai, India's richest municipal body, has not yet entered the bond market.

In February, Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman announced a ₹100 crore incentive. The incentive applies to municipal corporations issuing bonds worth at least ₹10,000 crore. The government also provides a subsidy for maiden issues.

The Centre offers a 2 percentage point interest subvention on such borrowing. This makes maiden municipal bond issuance cheaper for local bodies entering the market for the first time.

The International Finance Corp is in talks with states and urban bodies to invest in municipal bonds. IFC could act as an anchor investor to mobilise private funding, said Imad Fakhoury, South Asia regional director.

IFC is also exploring pooled bonds for smaller cities. Growing institutional interest could deepen India's municipal debt market beyond the current ₹4,500 crore raised so far.
