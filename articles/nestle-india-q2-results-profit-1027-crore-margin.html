Nestle India Q2 Profit Jumps 36% to Rs 1,027 Crore on 20% Revenue

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Standalone net profit rose 36% YoY to Rs 1,027 crore from Rs 753 crore.
  • •- Revenue from operations grew 20% to Rs 6,788 crore from Rs 5,644 crore.
  • •- EBITDA rose 31% to Rs 1,614 crore; margins improved 200bps to 23.8%.
  • •- Management flagged food inflation as a reality, with cocoa volatile and milk prices seasonally strong.
  • •- Shares fell 7% to Rs 1,260 on the NSE despite the strong headline numbers.

Nestle India delivered one of its strongest quarters in recent memory on Thursday, reporting a standalone net profit of Rs 1,027 crore for the September quarter — up 36 per cent from Rs 753 crore a year earlier — as the world's largest food company continued to ride pricing power, premiumisation and a quick-commerce distribution revolution in one of its most important markets.

Revenue from operations rose 20 per cent to Rs 6,788 crore, from Rs 5,644 crore in the same period last year. The operating engine was even stronger: EBITDA gained 31 per cent to Rs 1,614 crore from Rs 1,232 crore, lifting margins by 200 basis points to 23.8 per cent from 21 per cent a year ago. For a consumer-staples business, that combination — double-digit volume-led revenue growth and triple-digit-basis-point margin expansion simultaneously — is the signature of a company whose brands are both pricing and mixing up, rather than buying growth through discounting.

Management was candid about the environment it is navigating. "Rising inflation, particularly food inflation, is a reality," the company said, adding that it would respond through innovation, brand investment and intensified focus on productivity and operational efficiencies to mitigate commodity cost pressures. The commodity map underneath is mixed: coffee and wheat are expected to remain range-bound; sugar and edible oils have stabilised — but at elevated levels; cocoa remains volatile on weather-driven crop concerns in key growing origins; and milk prices have strengthened seasonally, though the company expects them to ease as flush-season supplies arrive. In plain terms, the margin gains of this quarter were won partly through pricing that lags input costs, and the company is signalling it intends to keep winning them the hard way — mix, productivity and cost control.

The growth architecture is worth a closer look, because it shows where Indian consumption is actually moving. E-commerce continued to be the standout channel, powered by quick commerce — the ten-minute delivery format that has become the single most important structural shift in Indian FMCG distribution over the past two years. Nestle credited better product availability, channel-specific assortments and pack sizes, stronger digital shelf execution and targeted shopper engagement for the momentum. Organised trade, India's modern retail segment, also grew strongly on better in-store execution and store-network expansion.

Out-of-home consumption — cafés, restaurants and the office-channel recovery — added another leg, helped by innovation and premiumisation. Nestle expanded its beverage portfolio during the quarter with Nescafe Duo Gusto, which it describes as an industry-first hot-and-cold machine, alongside cold-beverage formats such as Nestle Shikanji. In foods, recipe-led innovation pushed Maggi Coconut Milk Powder into coastal cuisines and lifted Milkmaid adoption in cafés and bakeries — a quiet but effective example of a multinational adapting global brands to Indian culinary habits rather than the reverse.

Exports sustained double-digit growth on portfolio expansion, and the quarter carried a distinctly global footprint for Indian-made Nestle products: Maggi Masala-e-Magic launched in Canada, Nescafe Sunrise expanded into five West African markets, and a new instant coffee variant debuted in the Middle East. For an operation long treated as a domestic-only franchise, the export engine has become a genuine second growth lever.

The market's reaction — a 7 per cent slide to Rs 1,260 — is the puzzle the headline numbers leave. In a session where the benchmark indices rallied over a thousand points, Nestle's fall suggests profit booking after a recent run-up, or investor wariness about the sustainability of the commodity-cost commentary, or simply rotation: on a day of IT-led exuberance, defensives were among the day's laggards, and consumer staples bore the brunt. Either way, the earnings themselves give the bulls the stronger argument: a 20 per cent revenue engine, 23.8 per cent margins, and a commodity outlook that, while not benign, is at least visible and manageable.

For the wider FMCG sector, Nestle's quarter sets a high bar for the results still to come. If pricing power of this order is available to a packaged-foods leader while food inflation bites, the gap between the sector's winners and its laggards is about to get much wider.

The quarter also carries a strategic message that extends beyond one earnings print. Nestle's two-decade-old bet on India — its largest market outside the United States in revenue terms for the parent — has historically been built on a narrow set of blockbuster franchises: Maggi in noodles, Nescafe in coffee, KitKat and Munch in confectionery. The current quarter shows the model broadening: beverages into machines and cold formats, culinary products into regional recipe platforms, and Indian-origin brands into export lines for West Africa, the Middle East and North America. That diversification matters because it reduces the company's dependence on any single category's commodity cycle — the same reason the cocoa volatility flagged in management's commentary, while real, is a manageable rather than an existential risk.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT WERE NESTLE INDIA'S Q2 NUMBERS?
Standalone net profit rose 36% YoY to Rs 1,027 crore; revenue grew 20% to Rs 6,788 crore; EBITDA rose 31% to Rs 1,614 crore with margins up 200bps to 23.8%.
WHY DID THE STOCK FALL 7% AFTER STRONG RESULTS?
Profit booking after recent gains, profit-taking on defensives during an IT-led rally, and caution on the company's food-inflation commentary all played a role.
WHAT DROVE THE QUARTER'S GROWTH?
Quick-commerce-led e-commerce momentum, organised trade expansion, out-of-home consumption recovery, innovation-led premiumisation and double-digit export growth.
WHAT ARE THE KEY COMMODITY RISKS?
Cocoa remains volatile on crop concerns; milk prices are seasonally strong but expected to ease; sugar and edible oils are stable at elevated levels; coffee and wheat look range-bound. GLOSSARY: - EBITDA margin: Operating profit as a percentage of revenue; a 200bps rise means Nestle kept Rs 2 more per Rs 100 of sales as operating profit. - Quick commerce: Ten-minute grocery delivery platforms that have become a major FMCG distribution channel in Indian cities. - Premiumisation: Shifting consumers toward higher-priced, higher-margin products within a brand's range. - Standalone results: Financials of the Indian entity alone, excluding subsidiaries and joint ventures.
