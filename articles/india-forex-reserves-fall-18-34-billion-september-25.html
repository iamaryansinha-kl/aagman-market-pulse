India Forex Reserves Fall $18.34 Billion to $747.56 Billion

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/India's foreign exchange reserves fell by $18.34 billion to $747.56 billion in the week ended September 25, RBI data released on October 2 showed.
  • 02/The drop follows a separate reported decline of $14.88 billion to $765.9 billion in the previous reporting week ended September 18.
  • 03/Foreign currency assets, the largest component, fell $15.57 billion to $615.41 billion; gold reserves slipped $2.59 billion to $108.7 billion.

India's foreign exchange reserves fell by $18.34 billion to $747.56 billion in the week ended September 25. The figures come from the Reserve Bank of India's weekly statistical supplement, released on Friday, October 2.

The decline marks a second consecutive heavy weekly drop in the country's reserves. It was driven by a fall in foreign currency assets and a decline in gold reserves during the reporting week.

In the previous reporting week ended September 18, reserves had fallen by $14.88 billion to $765.9 billion. The latest decline takes the headline figure below the $750 billion level.

Foreign currency assets, the largest component of the reserves, declined by $15.57 billion during the week. They stood at $615.41 billion at the end of the reporting period.

Foreign currency assets are expressed in US dollar terms. They include the impact of appreciation or depreciation in non-US currencies such as the euro, pound and yen held as part of the reserves.

That accounting method means currency valuation effects alone can move the weekly number. A stronger or weaker dollar changes the US dollar value of holdings denominated in other major currencies, even if the underlying amounts are unchanged.

Gold reserves also declined during the week. They fell by $2.59 billion to $108.7 billion, making gold the second-largest contributor to the weekly drop after foreign currency assets.

Special Drawing Rights fell by $97 million to $18.64 billion during the week. SDRs are international reserve assets created by the International Monetary Fund and form a small part of India's holdings.

India's reserve position with the International Monetary Fund declined by $86 million to $4.8 billion. This position represents the portion of the country's IMF quota that is readily available for use, and it is the smallest of the four tracked components.

Taken together, the four components make up the total reserves. Foreign currency assets account for the bulk of the pile, followed by gold, Special Drawing Rights and the IMF reserve position.

The declines come amid volatility in global currency markets and pressure on the rupee in recent weeks. The RBI's weekly release itself does not attribute the change in reserves to any specific cause.

Foreign exchange reserves act as a buffer for the economy. They are used to cover imports, service external obligations and provide stability when financial markets come under stress.

Even after two consecutive weekly declines, the reserves stood at $747.56 billion at the end of the reporting week. The $750 billion mark is a closely watched round number, though economists tend to focus on the trend across several weeks rather than any single print.

The pace of weekly changes in the reserves tends to fluctuate. Valuation effects from currency movements alone can shift the headline figure even when underlying holdings are steady.

The next RBI weekly statistical supplement will show whether the decline continues into October or steadies. Market participants track the release for a read on the country's external buffer and overall reserve adequacy.

Reserve adequacy is typically assessed against metrics such as import cover and short-term external debt. Those measures depend on data that updates less frequently than the weekly reserve figures.
