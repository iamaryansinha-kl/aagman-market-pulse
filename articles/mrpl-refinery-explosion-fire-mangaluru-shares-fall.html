MRPL Shares Fall 10% After Explosion, Fire at Mangaluru Refinery

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/An explosion hit MRPL's Mangaluru refinery around 12:20 pm on September 30, triggering a fire in the Coker Hydrotreater Unit
  • 02/The blaze was caused by the rupture of a high-pressure Cold Separator; one minor injury was reported
  • 03/Shares slumped as much as 10.15% from the day's high to an intraday low of Rs 162.40 by 1:50 pm

The explosion was heard in the vicinity around 12:20 pm and a large fire followed, with thick smoke visible from a distance. Media reports showed smoke engulfing the area around the complex. Fire engines were rushed to the site and emergency crews worked to contain the blaze.

The fire started in MRPL's Coker Hydrotreater Unit, according to a filing-based report. The cause was the rupture of a high-pressure Cold Separator. Early reports indicated the incident occurred in the refinery's third plant area. A coker hydrotreater upgrades heavy residual crude into lighter, higher-value products, making it a critical unit within the refining complex.

One minor injury has been reported so far. No major casualties were confirmed at the time of writing. Emergency response teams were on site to contain the blaze.

MRPL is a subsidiary of Oil and Natural Gas Corporation Ltd, which holds a 71.63% stake in the company. The ONGC unit operates a 15 million tonnes per annum refinery complex located north of Mangaluru city in Dakshina Kannada district.

Investors reacted swiftly to the news. MRPL shares fell as much as 10% from the day's high, hitting an intraday low of Rs 162.40 at 1:50 pm. That marked a drop of Rs 18.35 or 10.15% from the intraday peak, as reported by CNBC-TV18 and India TV.

The stock had rallied to a day's high of Rs 180.75 before the news. It had closed at Rs 171.65 on September 29, so the intraday low of Rs 162.40 left it roughly 5.4% below that previous close.

The company had processed 17 million tonnes of crude in FY26, running at 113% of nameplate capacity. Any extended shutdown of a processing unit could affect throughput, though MRPL has not issued a statement on production impact. Units involved in such incidents are typically taken offline pending a safety audit and damage assessment, which would be the first signal of any hit to operations.

Investors will watch for an official company statement on the unit's status, any update on the injured worker, and an assessment of damage to the Coker Hydrotreater. Refineries typically isolate an affected unit and complete a safety review before restarting it, a process whose duration depends on the extent of the damage. Details on casualties, unit shutdown, or financial impact remain unconfirmed at the time of writing.
