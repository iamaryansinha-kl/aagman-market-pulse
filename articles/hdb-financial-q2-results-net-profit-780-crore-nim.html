HDB Financial Q2 Net Profit Jumps 34% to Rs 780 Crore

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- HDB Financial Services reported Q2FY27 net profit of Rs 780 crore, up over 34% from Rs 581 crore a year earlier.
  • •- Revenue from operations rose more than 10% to Rs 5,005 crore.
  • •- Net interest income grew nearly 16% to Rs 2,534 crore; net interest margin expanded to 8.23% from 7.95%.
  • •- Net profit margin improved to 15.59% from 12.79% in the year-ago quarter.
  • •- AUM increased nearly 12% to Rs 1.25 lakh crore; RoA stood at 2.42% and RoE at 14.4%.
  • •- The board declared an interim dividend of Rs 2 per share; the stock fell 4% ahead of the post-market release.

HDB Financial Services, the non-banking finance subsidiary of HDFC Bank, on Wednesday reported a July-September quarter that hit almost every mark: profit up sharply, margins expanding, costs under control and enough capital to keep growing — even as the stock market, which has knocked the shares down more than 23 per cent this year, continued to look the other way.

Net profit for Q2FY27 came in at Rs 780 crore, a 34 per cent jump from the Rs 581 crore reported in the same quarter last year. Revenue from operations rose more than 10 per cent to Rs 5,005 crore from Rs 4,545 crore. The net profit margin improved dramatically to 15.59 per cent, against 12.79 per cent in the year-ago period — a signal that the growth is coming with operating leverage, not at the expense of it.

The engine was net interest income, which grew nearly 16 per cent year on year to Rs 2,534 crore, carrying the net interest margin to 8.23 per cent from 7.95 per cent a year earlier. The sequential picture is slightly more nuanced: NIM was 8.35 per cent in Q1FY27, so the margin softened about 12 basis points quarter on quarter — a reminder that funding costs across the system are rising as the RBI tightens, and that even the best-run NBFCs cannot fully outrun a front-loaded rate cycle.

On costs, the company kept improving. The cost-to-income ratio for the lending business fell to 39.3 per cent from 40.7 per cent in the year-ago quarter and 39.9 per cent in the preceding one. Total expenses grew just over 5 per cent to Rs 3,962 crore — slower than revenue growth, which is precisely the combination that lets profit margins expand this quickly.

The balance sheet remains conservatively funded. Net worth rose to Rs 21,121 crore from Rs 18,408 crore a year earlier; the debt-to-equity ratio edged down to 4.94 from 5.01; and capital adequacy stood at 21.37 per cent — down marginally from 21.82 per cent a year ago but far above regulatory minimums and comfortably ahead of what the loan book requires. Assets under management grew nearly 12 per cent to Rs 1.25 lakh crore, while return on assets held at a healthy 2.42 per cent and return on equity at 14.4 per cent.

Alongside the results, announced after market close on Wednesday, the board declared an interim dividend of Rs 2 per share for FY27, with the record date for eligibility fixed at October 23.

The market's reaction, recorded before the numbers dropped, was characteristically unkind: the stock closed down more than 4 per cent at Rs 584, extending declines of about 3 per cent over the past week and 13 per cent over the past month. The shares have now lost over 23 per cent in 2026 and more than a fifth of their value over the past year — a performance that sits awkwardly beside what has been a steady string of operational delivery.

That divergence is the story the quarter raises rather than answers. NBFC valuations across the sector have compressed through the year as rate-tightening fears, funding-cost worries and a general rotation away from financials weighed on the space; HDB Financial's position as a large HDFC Bank group entity arguably exposes it to that rotation more than most. Yet the operating numbers — margin expansion, cost discipline, double-digit AUM growth, a 21 per cent capital adequacy cushion and a 34 per cent profit print — describe a franchise executing well in a difficult tape.

The questions for the coming quarters are the ones the whole NBFC sector now faces. Can margins keep expanding when the cost of funds is rising 20 basis points a week at the front end of the curve? Can loan growth hold near 12 per cent if the RBI's tightening cycle bites demand? And can the HDFC Bank distribution engine keep feeding customer acquisition as the group integrates its sprawling lending architecture? Wednesday's print suggests HDB is answering the first two better than most; the stock will presumably catch up if the answers keep coming.

The funding question deserves emphasis because the macro backdrop turned hostile within days of the quarter closing. Treasury bill yields spiked to eighteen-month highs this week and the premium over the repo rate hit a four-year peak, meaning the marginal cost of the short-term debt that NBFCs rely on is climbing precisely as HDB plans its next leg of growth. A 21 per cent capital adequacy ratio is the buffer against that squeeze; a debt-equity ratio under five shows the company has not levered up to chase AUM.

HDB's franchise sits in the middle of Indian credit: lending to small businesses, vehicle buyers, gold-loan customers and micro-borrowers — segments the parent bank reaches less efficiently on its own balance sheet. That positioning explains both the 12 per cent AUM growth and the market's persistent caution: the segments HDB serves are the first to wobble when rates rise and the informal economy slows. The Q2 numbers argue the wobble has not arrived; the stock's slide says investors will believe it when they see another two quarters of it.

The dividend adds a small but telling footnote. A Rs 2 per share interim payout, with a record date of October 23, signals management confidence in cash generation even while growth continues — and gives long-suffering shareholders something concrete while they wait for the market to re-rate the franchise.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT WERE HDB FINANCIAL'S Q2FY27 NUMBERS?
Net profit rose over 34% year on year to Rs 780 crore, revenue grew 10% to Rs 5,005 crore, and net profit margin improved to 15.59% from 12.79%.
HOW DID MARGINS PERFORM?
Net interest income grew nearly 16% to Rs 2,534 crore and NIM expanded to 8.23% from 7.95% a year ago, though it softened from 8.35% in Q1FY27.
WHAT ABOUT THE DIVIDEND?
The board declared an interim dividend of Rs 2 per share for FY27, with the record date set for October 23.
HOW HAS THE STOCK PERFORMED?
The shares closed down more than 4% at Rs 584 on Wednesday, and have lost over 23% in 2026 despite the strong earnings trajectory. GLOSSARY: - NBFC: Non-banking financial company; lends like a bank but cannot take demand deposits. - Net interest margin: Interest earned minus interest paid, as a percentage of average assets. - Cost-to-income ratio: Operating costs as a share of income; lower means more efficient. - AUM: Assets under management — the total loans the company manages. - RoE: Return on equity, profit as a percentage of shareholders' funds. - Interim dividend: A payout declared during the year, ahead of the final dividend.
