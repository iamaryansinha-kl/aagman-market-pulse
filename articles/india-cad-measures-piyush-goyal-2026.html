India CAD Alert: Govt Plans Steps To Stop Deficit From Widening Further

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal says multiple government departments are coordinating to address the growing current account deficit amid volatile global trade
  • 02/India's CAD widened to $13.2 billion (1.3% of GDP) in Q3 FY26, up from $11.3 billion in the year-ago quarter, per RBI data released March 2
  • 03/Despite the quarterly spike, cumulative April-December CAD at $30.1 billion (1% of GDP) is lower than the $36.6 billion (1.3% of GDP) recorded in the same period last year

Commerce and Industry Minister Piyush Goyal said India is closely tracking external sector trends. Multiple government departments are evaluating measures to prevent any sharp deterioration in the current account deficit.

His comments come amid sustained pressure on the rupee and an uncertain global trade environment. Concerns over India's trade balance have resurfaced due to weak export demand in key markets, particularly the US.

According to RBI data released on March 2, India's CAD widened to $13.2 billion in the December quarter. This represents 1.3% of GDP, compared with $11.3 billion in the corresponding period a year earlier.

The quarterly increase was largely driven by a higher merchandise trade deficit. Softer exports to the US market contributed to the widening gap between imports and exports.

Despite the Q3 spike, the cumulative position remains manageable. The CAD for April-December 2025 stood at $30.1 billion, or 1% of GDP. This is lower than the $36.6 billion, or 1.3% of GDP, recorded in the same period of FY25.

Economists note that strong services exports have provided crucial support. IT and business services, along with steady remittance inflows, have helped cushion the balance of payments position.

The rupee has faced pressure from a stronger US dollar, higher crude oil prices, and global risk aversion. Geopolitical uncertainties and concerns around slowing growth in advanced economies have added to the strain.

Goyal expressed confidence in India's ability to navigate the difficult global environment. He said the government remains optimistic about the country's resilience despite ongoing international headwinds.

Policymakers are focusing on boosting exports and strengthening manufacturing competitiveness. Diversifying trade partnerships is also a key priority to reduce dependence on limited export destinations.

India is actively pursuing free trade agreements with several countries and economic blocs. These agreements aim to improve market access for domestic exporters and build buffers against external shocks.
