Sebi to Examine Discount Brokers' Concerns Over New UPI MDR on Fund Transfers

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

The Securities and Exchange Board of India will examine concerns raised by discount brokers over the new merchant discount rate on large fund transfers through UPI, its chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey said on Thursday.

"I think there are some important issues there. We will certainly look into it and see how we can ease them," Pandey said on the sidelines of an event in Mumbai.

Under the new framework, capital market transactions, including payments to stockbrokers, mutual funds, investment advisers and dealers, will attract an MDR of 0.02%, capped at Rs 300 per transaction, from October 15.

The worry, spelled out publicly by Nithin Kamath, founder of discount broking platform Zerodha, is structural. Money transferred by a client to a broking account does not necessarily result in a trade, and brokers cannot pass the UPI charge on to the customer.

"There is essentially no limit to the cost a customer can impose on a broker without generating any revenue," Kamath wrote, noting that brokers operate on wafer-thin margins.

The problem is compounded by regulation itself. Sebi's quarterly settlement rule requires brokers to send unused client funds back every month or quarter, and most customers simply transfer the money straight back, with more than half of those transfers happening over UPI. The regulation forces this monthly churn of funds, and the broker ends up paying the MDR on money that generates no incremental revenue.

The tension is a classic distribution problem in market plumbing: the entity paying the fee is not the one that set the price, and the one benefiting, the customer, cannot practically be charged.

Brokers' margins make the math sting. Discount broking runs on zero or near-zero brokerage, so even a 0.02% charge, capped at Rs 300, on routine transfer cycles is a real cost line at scale.

Pandey also clarified that the regulator has received no proposal from the National Stock Exchange seeking approval to trade on its own platform after listing. India permits only cross-listing, which is why the NSE stock will list on the BSE.

The MDR framework takes effect in under a month, so the window for Sebi to deliver relief is short.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT IS THE NEW UPI MDR FOR CAPITAL MARKET TRANSFERS?
From October 15, payments to stockbrokers, mutual funds, investment advisers and dealers via UPI will attract an MDR of 0.02%, capped at Rs 300 per transaction.
WHAT IS THE BROKERS' OBJECTION?
Money transferred to a broker may never become a trade, brokers cannot pass the charge to customers, and Sebi's quarterly settlement rule forces monthly or quarterly churn of unused funds, more than half of which returns via UPI.
WHAT DID SEBI SAY?
Chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey said the regulator sees important issues in the concerns and will examine how to ease them before the October 15 start date.
