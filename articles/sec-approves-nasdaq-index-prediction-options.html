SEC Approves Nasdaq's Index-Linked Prediction Options on Nasdaq-100

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission on Thursday approved a proposal by Nasdaq's options trading venue to list a new class of prediction instruments tied to a major stock index.

The products, dubbed Outcome-Related Options, are cash-settled binary contracts that pay a fixed amount at expiration depending on whether the underlying index closes above or below a predetermined level.

The SEC said the options would carry a fixed, all-or-nothing exercise settlement amount of $100 if they expire in the money, making them straightforward yes-or-no bets on index performance.

Nasdaq MRX, the electronic U.S. options exchange operated by Nasdaq, will initially list the contracts linked to the Nasdaq-100 index and the Nasdaq-100 Micro index.

The Nasdaq-100 tracks 100 of the largest non-financial companies listed on Nasdaq, including heavyweights like Apple, Nvidia, and Intel. The micro index represents one-one-hundredth of the full Nasdaq-100 value.

The commission granted accelerated approval to Nasdaq's request, which it filed in March, saying the proposal was consistent with securities law and did not raise new regulatory concerns.

Nasdaq welcomed the decision, stating the approval supports its Outcome-Related Options initiative and expands the toolkit available to market participants.

The approval arrives amid a broader race among exchanges to enter the prediction markets space, which allows users to bet on real-world outcomes and is gaining legitimacy as a source of revenue and market insight.

Cboe Global Markets, Nasdaq's chief rival in the options space, is also targeting a second-quarter launch for its own all-or-none contracts focused on financial and economic events, subject to regulatory clearance.

Several firms have been increasingly seeking to enter the prediction markets arena, drawn by the potential for new revenue streams and the demand for instruments that let traders express views on discrete outcomes.

The contracts are designed to be simple and transparent, appealing to both retail and institutional participants who want exposure to index direction without the complexity of traditional options strategies.

Because the payout is fixed at $100, the maximum risk and reward are known at the time of trade, which can help traders manage their exposure more precisely than with standard options.

The listing on Nasdaq MRX, a fully electronic exchange, ensures the products will trade within the existing regulated U.S. equity options infrastructure and under standard investor protections.

Market observers expect the launch to test appetite for event-based instruments among U.S. traders, who until now have had limited access to regulated prediction-style products tied to financial indexes.

The SEC's green light could pave the way for additional exchanges to seek approval for similar products, potentially broadening the landscape of outcome-based trading instruments available to investors.
