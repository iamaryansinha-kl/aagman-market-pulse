Thales Bets on India Aerospace: ₹7.85 Lakh Cr Defence, PLI Boost

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Thales VP Ankur Kanaglekar says a PLI scheme for aero components could attract global and domestic firms to expand manufacturing in India
  • 02/India's passenger traffic may hit 1.1 billion by 2040; aircraft MRO market could reach $4 billion by 2031; UDAN 2.0 outlay is ₹28,840 crore for 100 airports
  • 03/Thales employs 2,400 people in India, plans 450 new hires in 2026; works with 75+ supply-chain partners supporting nearly 2,000 indirect jobs

Thales sees strong growth potential in India's aerospace manufacturing sector. The company's India strategy centres on "Make in India", "Innovate in India" and "Export from India".

Ankur Kanaglekar, Vice President – India at Thales, said a production-linked incentive (PLI) scheme for aero components could attract both global and domestic firms to expand manufacturing here.

The Union Budget 2026-27 allocated ₹7.85 lakh crore for defence. This marks a 15 per cent increase over the previous year, signalling continued emphasis on modernising the armed forces.

Customs duty exemptions on aircraft components, parts and raw materials for MRO activities indicate sustained policy backing for aerospace manufacturing, Kanaglekar added.

India's aviation market is expected to sustain growth. Passenger traffic could grow six-fold to around 1.1 billion by 2040, per government estimates.

The aircraft MRO market may reach $4 billion by 2031. The modified UDAN scheme with ₹28,840 crore outlay aims to develop 100 airports from unserved airstrips by 2036.

Thales currently employs around 2,400 people in India. Nearly three-fourths are in engineering roles across centres in Noida and Bengaluru.

The company works with over 75 supply-chain partners. These partnerships support nearly 2,000 indirect jobs across mechanical systems, radar structures and software development.

In 2026, Thales plans to hire around 450 employees. The roles span hardware, software and systems engineering.

Thales recently supported Air India's 787-9 aircraft induction. The airline became the first in Asia-Pacific to deploy Thales' AVANT Up inflight entertainment platform.
