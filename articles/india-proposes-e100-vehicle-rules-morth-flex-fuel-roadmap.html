India Proposes E100 Vehicle Rules: MoRTH Drafts Flex Fuel Roadmap

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/MoRTH issued draft rules on April 27 to amend CMVR 1989 for E100 and E85 vehicle certification
  • 02/India achieved 20% ethanol blending in December 2025; petrol sales hit 42.59 million tonnes in FY26
  • 03/Distillation capacity stands at 2,000 crore litres but infrastructure and mileage penalty remain hurdles

The Ministry of Road Transport and Highways issued a draft notification on April 27, 2026. It proposes amending the Central Motor Vehicles Rules, 1989 to allow E100 and E85 ethanol blends in vehicles.

The draft also introduces provisions for B100 biodiesel and raises the vehicle weight threshold from 3,000kg to 3,500kg. This brings light commercial vehicles into the ethanol framework.

India achieved its E20 blending target in December 2025, five years ahead of the original 2030 deadline. The nationwide E20 mandate took effect on April 1, 2026.

Petrol sales grew approximately 6% in 2025-26 to reach 42.59 million tonnes. Ethanol now accounts for 8.52 million tonnes of blended fuel supply across the country.

India's ethanol distillation capacity stands at around 2,000 crore litres with over 380 distilleries operational. Industry estimates suggest 10 billion litres of excess capacity remains unutilized.

"The progression from E85 to E100 signals a clear shift towards mainstreaming indigenous biofuels," said Deepak Ballani, Director General of ISMA. The move aligns with the Atmanirbhar Bharat vision.

The draft faces hurdles. E100 has lower energy density, leading to a 27-30% mileage penalty. Infrastructure at petrol pumps and feedstock supply are additional challenges.

Praful Vithalani of AISTA called for a 15-year policy with subsidy guidelines for flex fuel vehicles. The first phase of E100 rollout is expected in sugar-surplus states.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT DOES THE MORTH DRAFT NOTIFICATION PROPOSE FOR ETHANOL VEHICLES?
THE MINISTRY OF ROAD TRANSPORT AND HIGHWAYS PROPOSES AMENDING THE CENTRAL MOTOR VEHICLES RULES, 1989 TO ALLOW CERTIFICATION OF VEHICLES RUNNING ON E100 (100% ETHANOL), E85 (85% ETHANOL), AND B100 BIODIESEL. THE RULES ALSO RAISE THE VEHICLE WEIGHT THRESHOLD FROM 3,000KG TO 3,500KG TO INCLUDE LIGHT COMMERCIAL VEHICLES.
WHEN DID INDIA ACHIEVE THE E20 ETHANOL BLENDING TARGET?
INDIA ACHIEVED 20% ETHANOL BLENDING IN DECEMBER 2025, FIVE YEARS AHEAD OF THE ORIGINAL 2030 TARGET SET UNDER THE NATIONAL POLICY ON BIOFUELS. THE NATIONWIDE E20 MANDATE ROLLED OUT ON APRIL 1, 2026.
WHAT IS INDIA'S CURRENT ETHANOL PRODUCTION CAPACITY?
INDIA'S ETHANOL DISTILLATION CAPACITY STANDS AT APPROXIMATELY 2,000 CRORE LITRES WITH OVER 380 DEDICATED DISTILLERIES OPERATIONAL. THE INDUSTRY HAS AN ESTIMATED 10 BILLION LITRES OF EXCESS UNUTILIZED CAPACITY AVAILABLE.
WHAT ARE THE KEY CHALLENGES FOR E100 ADOPTION IN INDIA?
THE PRIMARY CHALLENGES INCLUDE A 27-30% MILEAGE PENALTY DUE TO LOWER ENERGY DENSITY OF ETHANOL, LACK OF FUELING INFRASTRUCTURE AT PETROL PUMPS, AND FEEDSTOCK AVAILABILITY. OEMS HAVE FLAGGED CONSUMER ACCEPTANCE AND COST AS ADDITIONAL CONCERNS.
WHAT ARE THE EXPECTED BENEFITS OF HIGHER ETHANOL BLENDING?
THE E20 PROGRAMME HAS ALREADY SAVED OVER Rs 1.44 LAKH CRORE IN FOREIGN EXCHANGE AND SUBSTITUTED 245 LAKH METRIC TONNES OF CRUDE OIL. AT E20 LEVELS, ANNUAL FARMER PAYMENTS ARE EXPECTED TO REACH Rs 40,000 CRORE AND FOREX SAVINGS APPROXIMATELY Rs 43,000 CRORE.
