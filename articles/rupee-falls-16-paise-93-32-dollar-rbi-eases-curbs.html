Rupee Falls 16 Paise to 93.32 Against US Dollar as RBI Eases FX Curbs

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/The Indian rupee declined 16 paise to 93.32 against the US dollar in early trade on Tuesday, April 21, 2026, after the RBI partially eased restrictions on speculative forex bets
  • 02/The Reserve Bank on Monday withdrew its April 1 directives capping net open positions at $100 million, allowing banks to resume non-deliverable forward contracts with some restrictions
  • 03/The US-Iran ceasefire agreement is scheduled to expire on Wednesday, keeping crude oil prices volatile and investors on edge

The Indian rupee fell 16 paise to 93.32 against the US dollar in early trade on Tuesday, April 21, 2026. The currency opened at 93.25 and touched an intra-day low of 93.37 before settling at 93.32 in early deals.

On Monday, the rupee had closed at 93.16 after losing 25 paise. The currency had gained 47 paise in the two preceding sessions.

The decline came as the Reserve Bank of India partially withdrew its April 1 directives aimed at curbing excessive speculation in the rupee. The central bank had previously capped net open positions in non-deliverable forward markets at $100 million.

Under the revised rules effective immediately, authorised dealers can resume offering non-deliverable derivative contracts involving the rupee to resident and non-resident users. However, banks cannot undertake forex derivative contracts with related parties.

The dollar index, which gauges the greenback's strength against a basket of six currencies, rose 0.04 per cent to 97.94. The US currency stayed firm amid global risk-off sentiment.

Brent crude traded 0.51 per cent lower at $94.99 per barrel. Oil prices remain volatile due to worries over supply disruptions from the Persian Gulf as the US-Iran ceasefire is set to expire on Wednesday.

Domestic equity markets provided some support to the rupee. The 30-share Sensex rose 379.33 points or 0.48 per cent to 78,899.63 in early trade. The Nifty gained 103.70 points or 0.43 per cent to 24,468.55.

Foreign Institutional Investors bought equities worth approximately Rs 2,066 crore on Monday, according to NSDL data. The inflows helped limit further rupee depreciation.

"The rollback helps restore flexibility, improve price discovery and bring stability back to the onshore FX market," said V Rama Chandra Reddy, Head of Treasury at Karur Vysya Bank. "For the rupee, the impact is largely neutral as it supports liquidity without encouraging excessive speculation."
