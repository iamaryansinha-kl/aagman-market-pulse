Adani Green Energy H1: Capacity Up 24% to 20,763 MW, Sales Jump 32%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Adani Green Energy operational capacity rose 24% YoY to 20,763 MW in H1FY27, while energy sales grew faster at 32% to 25,747 MU
  • 02/The company added 4,083 MW of greenfield capacity in H1 and scaled battery storage to 6,632 MWh, including 3,081 MWh operationalised at Khavda in Q2
  • 03/Q2 and H1 FY27 financial results will be considered by the board on October 21, 2026 — this update covers operations, not earnings

Adani Green Energy Ltd (NSE: ADANIGREEN) has reported a 24% year-on-year rise in operational capacity to 20,763 MW for H1FY27. Sale of energy rose 32% YoY to 25,747 million units (MU) over the same period.

The twin numbers matter because of the gap between them. Energy sales are growing faster than installed capacity, which points to improving utilisation and a richer generation mix across the portfolio.

The company added 4,083 MW of greenfield capacity during the first half. This includes 621 MW commissioned during the September quarter alone.

The battery energy storage story is the forward-looking edge of this update. During Q2, AGEL operationalised 3,081 MWh of battery energy storage system (BESS) capacity at Khavda. Total installed BESS capacity stood at 6,632 MWh at the end of H1FY27.

Storage is measured in MWh rather than MW for a reason. While generation capacity in MW tells you how much power a plant can produce at a given instant, storage in MWh tells you how long that power can be delivered. It is the answer to solar and wind intermittency — the sun does not shine at 7 pm, but stored energy still flows into evening peak demand.

Plant performance remained strong across technologies. The solar portfolio delivered a capacity utilisation factor (CUF) of 22.9% with plant availability of 99.3%. Wind posted a CUF of 42.9% and availability of 94.6%.

The hybrid portfolio stands out. Hybrid plants ran at a CUF of 45.2% with availability of 98.8% — meaning they were generating close to half the time, a markedly higher load factor than standalone solar.

The operational footprint is now large enough to be measured in homes and emissions. The 20,763 MW portfolio powered more than 4.4 million homes during the first half and helped avoid 17.5 million tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent (CO2e) emissions.

On recognition, AGEL was ranked number one globally in the Alternative Electricity Subsector by FTSE Russell for the second consecutive year. It also held its number one ranking in the power generation sector from CRISIL for the fifth consecutive year.

The market has rewarded the operational momentum. Shares settled at Rs 1,255.80 on the NSE, up 1.63% from the previous close of Rs 1,235.70, trading in a range of Rs 1,238.40 to Rs 1,275 during the session.

The stock has gained 22.41% so far in 2026, while the Nifty has declined by nearly 14% over the same period — a clear outperformance for a year in which benchmark indices have struggled. The 52-week range is Rs 765 to Rs 1,631.50, and market capitalisation stands at Rs 2,06,852.38 crore.

One distinction investors should keep in mind: this is an operational update, not an earnings release. The board will meet on Wednesday, October 21, 2026, to consider and approve unaudited standalone and consolidated financial results for the quarter and half year ended September 30, 2026. Revenue, profit, and margin trends will only become clear then.

The capacity math behind the numbers is worth unpacking. Adding 4,083 MW of greenfield capacity in six months — 621 MW of it in the September quarter alone — means AGEL commissioned roughly as much new clean power in half a year as some mid-sized power producers operate in total.

The Khavda project anchors that buildout. Located in Gujarat's Khavda renewable energy park — among the largest such complexes in the world — it is where the company's solar, wind and storage ambitions converge.

Battery storage is the strategic pivot in these numbers. Solar panels generate only when the sun shines; a battery system stores that energy for evening demand. Operationalising 3,081 MWh of storage at Khavda in a single quarter is one of the fastest storage deployments by any Indian generator.

The total installed storage of 6,632 MWh now gives AGEL a buffer most renewable peers lack. As power markets move toward time-of-day pricing, stored evening power earns premiums that pure solar cannot capture.

Plant reliability is the quiet enabler. Availability of 99.3% on solar and 98.8% on hybrid means turbines and panels spend almost no time idle — a direct driver of why energy sales grew faster than capacity.

The hybrid portfolio deserves its own mention. At 45.2% CUF, co-located wind-solar sites generate roughly twice what a standalone solar plant of the same capacity would — the strongest evidence yet of the portfolio shift toward hybrid assets.

The emissions arithmetic gives the scale a public frame. Avoiding 17.5 million tonnes of CO2 equivalent in half a year puts AGEL's fleet in the same decarbonisation conversation as national policy levers, not just corporate targets.

The recognition stack, though secondary, is consistent. FTSE Russell's top global ranking for a second straight year and CRISIL's fifth straight number-one in power generation suggest the operational discipline is visible to outside assessors, not just to management.

For investors, the calendar is the next checkpoint. The October 21 board meeting turns this operational story into a financial one — revenue realisation, tariffs realised per unit and debt costs will test whether 32% sales growth converts into commensurate earnings growth.

GLOSSARY: Capacity utilisation factor (CUF): The actual output of a power plant as a percentage of its maximum possible output over a period. Plant availability: The share of time a power plant is ready to generate, excluding planned maintenance. Greenfield capacity: New plants built from scratch, as opposed to acquired or brownfield projects. Battery energy storage system (BESS): Grid-scale batteries that store power for release when generation stops or demand peaks. MWh (megawatt-hour): A unit of energy — one megawatt of power sustained for one hour; storage capacity is measured this way. CO2e (carbon dioxide equivalent): A standardised measure of greenhouse-gas impact.
