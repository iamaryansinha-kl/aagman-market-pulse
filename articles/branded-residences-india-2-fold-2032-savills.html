India Branded Residences to Jump 126% by 2032: Savills

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- India's completed branded residence projects will rise to 43 by 2032 from 19 now, a 126 per cent jump, according to Savills.
  • •- Vietnam, Thailand and India feature among the world's top 10 branded residential markets by pipeline activity.
  • •- Branded residences command an average global price premium of 30-40 per cent over comparable unbranded homes.
  • •- Major developers including M3M, Signature Global, Elan, Whiteland, Gulshan, Tribeca, Smartworld and Dalcore have entered the segment.
  • •- Partnerships span hospitality and lifestyle names: Trump, Marriott, JW Marriott, Westin, Taj, Tonino Lamborghini, Jacob & Co, ELIE SAAB and YOO.
  • •- Asia Pacific accounts for about 23 per cent of completed branded projects worldwide, with 187 more expected by 2032.

India's ultra-luxury housing market is about to double its wardrobe. Real estate consultant Savills said on Tuesday that the number of completed branded residential projects in the country will rise to 43 by 2032 from 19 currently — a 126 per cent expansion that places India among the world's top ten branded-residence markets by pipeline activity, alongside Vietnam and Thailand. The numbers come from Savills' Branded Residences Asia Pacific 2026 report, which frames the shift bluntly: India's growth is supported by rapid wealth creation, expanding metropolitan populations and a growing domestic appetite for internationally branded products. The super-rich, in other words, are buying names, not just square feet. It is the strongest structural signal yet that India's luxury housing boom, already the fastest-growing tier of the residential market, is formalising into a brand-licensing industry of its own.

Branded residences are the point where real estate becomes a licensing business: ultra-luxury homes developed in association with globally renowned hospitality, design and lifestyle brands, where residents receive hotel-grade services layered over wellness, leisure and lifestyle amenities. The economics explain the developer stampede. Whiteland Corporation founder Navdeep Sardana notes that branded residences command an average global price premium of 30-40 per cent over comparable unbranded homes — a margin no rational developer leaves unharvested, and one that reframes the brand partnership fee as a construction cost with a measurable yield rather than a marketing indulgence.

The deal book backing the projection is already thick. In April, Signature Global tied up with Italian lifestyle brand Tonino Lamborghini for a Gurugram luxury project worth nearly Rs 2,900 crore. M3M India and Smartworld Developers have tied up with Trump, Jacob & Co and ELIE SAAB across recent years; Whiteland is developing Westin Residences in Gurugram with Marriott International, while Elan Group is building JW Marriott Residences there with the same hotel group; Gulshan Group is developing a Taj-branded hotel and residences in Noida, Dalcore is constructing branded homes in Gurugram with global design brand YOO, and in August Tribeca Developers — the official representative of the Trump brand in India — announced a Trump-branded luxury project in Hyderabad with Ira Realty. The roster reads like an airport duty-free catalogue, which is precisely the point: in a market where every top-tier developer now builds large-format luxury, the brand is the differentiator left.

Shveta Jain, Managing Director of Residential Services at Savills India, argues that India is entering the next phase of the category's evolution, with growth extending beyond traditional hospitality-led resort developments into urban, non-hospitality branded residences — a more demanding format, because a city apartment cannot lean on a beach or a golf course and must deliver the brand promise through service operations alone. Signature Global chairman Pradeep Aggarwal describes the demand side: buyers are far more selective than they were even a few years ago, wanting thoughtful design, identity and a dependable standard of living, and treating a known brand as assurance of quality and timely execution. Smartworld's Ashish Jerath singles out Delhi-NCR as the segment's anchor market on its premium-buyer base; Elan's Vineet Dawar frames the rise as a shift in homebuyer expectations concentrated in the luxury tier.

The regional context completes the picture. Asia Pacific is the second most established branded-residential region globally, accounting for about 23 per cent of completed projects worldwide, and Savills expects another 187 projects across the region by 2032 — forecast growth of 97 per cent. The driver is demographic arithmetic: rapid wealth creation across major Asia-Pacific economies is enlarging the pool of high-net-worth and ultra-high-net-worth individuals who prefer branded residences closer to home rather than in the traditional London-Dubai-New York circuit. India, with the youngest billionaire cohort in the region and luxury housing already its fastest-growing residential tier, is the largest single beneficiary of that re-homing.

The sceptic's case deserves an airing. A 126 per cent pipeline expansion is a projection, not a delivery: branded residences are the most execution-sensitive segment in Indian realty — brand partners demand construction and service standards that the country's delivery record only sometimes meets, and a few public failures of promised hotel-grade operations would cool the category faster than any macro headwind. Premium pricing of 30-40 per cent also concentrates exposure exactly at the top of the cycle. But the direction of the luxury market is established, the brand partners named above are committing real balance sheets, and the Savills timeline — nearly 25 ultra-luxury completions by 2032 — gives developers, and their buyers, a dated test of whether India's super-rich will pay name-brand rents on their own soil.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
HOW BIG WILL INDIA'S BRANDED RESIDENCES MARKET GET?
Completed projects will rise to 43 by 2032 from 19 now, a 126 per cent increase, according to Savills' Branded Residences Asia Pacific 2026 report.
WHAT IS A BRANDED RESIDENCE?
An ultra-luxury home developed with a hospitality, design or lifestyle brand, offering hotel-like services and wellness, leisure and lifestyle amenities.
WHICH DEVELOPERS ARE IN THIS SEGMENT?
M3M India, Tribeca, Signature Global, Smartworld, Elan Group, Whiteland, Gulshan and Dalcore, partnered with brands including Trump, Marriott, Westin, Taj, Tonino Lamborghini, Jacob & Co, ELIE SAAB and YOO.
WHAT PREMIUM DO BRANDED RESIDENCES COMMAND?
An average of 30-40 per cent over comparable unbranded homes globally, per Savills. GLOSSARY: - Branded residences: Homes developed in partnership with a hospitality or lifestyle brand, sold with brand-linked services. - Pipeline activity: Projects under development or announced, as distinct from completed stock. - High-net-worth individual: A person with substantial investable wealth, typically above $1 million. - Price premium: The percentage by which a property sells above comparable unbranded stock.
