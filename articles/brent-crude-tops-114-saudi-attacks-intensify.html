Crude Tops $114 as Saudi Attacks Intensify, Brent Up 8% Weekly

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Brent rose 50 cents to $114.02 a barrel on Friday; WTI gained to $100.
  • •- The move extends Thursday's 4% jump to a five-month high; Brent is up nearly 8% this week.
  • •- Saudi Aramco evacuated its Dhahran headquarters as fighting with the Houthis intensified near the Bab el-Mandeb chokepoint.
  • •- Goldman Sachs sees oil at $120 if Middle East attacks escalate, and $80 if export flows normalise.

Oil's war premium keeps compounding. Brent crude futures rose 50 cents, or 0.45%, to $114.02 a barrel on Friday morning, while US West Texas Intermediate gained 40 cents, or 0.42%, to $100 — a striking divergence in which the global benchmark charges ahead while the American benchmark flirts with a weekly loss. The gap tells you what is driving prices: this is not a demand story or a generalised supply squeeze, it is a disruption story concentrated on the routes and facilities that Brent-priced cargoes depend on. For a third consecutive week, Brent is set to gain — up nearly 8% in five sessions — after settling at its highest since May 4 on Thursday.

The proximate trigger is Saudi Arabia. Houthi attacks on the world's largest crude exporter escalated sharply this week, and Saudi Aramco ordered the evacuation of its headquarters in Dhahran, on the Gulf near Bahrain, on Thursday as fighting intensified between the kingdom and the Yemen-based group. The clashes matter far beyond Saudi borders because the Houthis have advanced towards Bab el-Mandeb, the shipping chokepoint at the entrance to the Red Sea through which a significant share of seaborne oil transits. Every mile the group gains towards that strait reprices the probability that tanker traffic gets interrupted, and the market is now pricing that probability in real time.

The risk map extends beyond the Red Sea. The New York Times reported that US and Israeli militaries have drawn up specific plans for renewed strikes against Iran after the American midterm elections, citing officials briefed on the discussions — potential targets include Iran's energy infrastructure, nuclear facilities, and missile and drone arsenal, with preparatory exercises already under way. A diplomatic offset exists on a separate track: Russia said it was ready to resume US-mediated talks with Ukraine next month after President Putin met US envoy Steve Witkoff, and Zelenskiy said he hoped negotiations would take place in early November. Traders are weighing both, but the strike-planning headlines have been winning.

The banks' scenario ranges make the stakes explicit. Goldman Sachs' co-head of global commodities research, Daan Struyven, said recent attacks had underscored how shipping disruptions could spread and intensify, and outlined a scenario in which crude rises to $120 a barrel if attacks on vessels in the Middle East escalate — while a return to normal export levels could pull prices back towards $80. That is a $40 range anchored almost entirely on geopolitics rather than inventories or OPEC policy. Goldman also expects natural gas and refined fuel prices to rise, noting that supply shocks hit gas and refined products harder than crude, which is precisely the channel that matters most for India's import bill since the country buys the products as well as the crude. JPMorgan's commodities team was blunter about the modelling problem: seven months into the conflict, many of the economic thresholds the bank assumed Washington would avoid have already been crossed, and a clear exit strategy remains elusive. At the outset, JPMorgan had expected the US administration to steer clear of the price levels that historically trigger demand destruction; seven months later, with Brent at $114 and no ceasefire in sight, that assumption has been retired.

For India, the arithmetic darkens with every dollar. The country imports roughly 90% of its crude, so Brent at $114 versus the $70-80 range that budget assumptions were built around adds billions to the annual import bill, widens the trade deficit, and feeds the inflation prints that are already pushing the central bank toward further tightening. Thursday's session showed the transmission in action: the rupee snapped its eight-day rally, benchmark bond yields pushed through 7.45%, and rate-hike expectations for December hardened. Friday's pre-open had GIFT Nifty signalling a positive start despite it all — equity markets have been remarkably willing to look through the oil shock so far. The longer Brent holds above $110, the harder that willingness becomes to sustain. Already this week the shock has snapped the rupee's eight-day rally, pushed benchmark bond yields through 7.45%, and hardened expectations of a December rate hike — a full macro repricing from a single input price. If Goldman's $120 scenario materialises, every one of those moves extends: the import bill widens, the currency weakens further, inflation prints force the central bank's hand, and the equity market's patience gets tested against a tightening cycle it has so far treated as hypothetical. The tail risk runs the other way too — a normalisation of export flows would, by Goldman's own math, take oil back towards $80, unwinding the entire war premium in weeks and reversing the macro trades built on it. Options desks have noticed the asymmetry too: implied volatility in crude has climbed far less than spot, leaving hedges comparatively cheap for anyone worried the Red Sea risk is being underpriced. Either tail punishes the consensus positioning in some corner of the market, which is why desks keep flagging options-market hedging costs even as spot grinds higher.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
HOW MUCH HAS OIL MOVED THIS WEEK?
Brent is up nearly 8% in the week and set for a third consecutive weekly gain, trading at $114.02 after a 4% jump on Thursday took it to a five-month high.
WHAT IS DRIVING THE RALLY?
Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia, the evacuation of Aramco's Dhahran headquarters, and the group's advance towards the Bab el-Mandeb shipping chokepoint — compounded by reported US-Israeli strike planning against Iran.
WHAT ARE GOLDMAN SACHS' SCENARIOS?
$120 a barrel if Middle East attacks on shipping escalate, and a slide towards $80 if export flows normalise. The bank sees bigger upside risk in natural gas and refined fuels than in crude itself. GLOSSARY: BRENT VS WTI — The two main oil benchmarks; Brent prices seaborne global crude and is more exposed to Middle East disruption risk, while WTI prices US inland supply. WAR PREMIUM — The portion of an oil price that reflects geopolitical supply-risk rather than underlying supply and demand fundamentals. BAB EL-MANDEB — The strait between Yemen and the Horn of Africa at the entrance to the Red Sea, a critical chokepoint for oil tanker traffic.
