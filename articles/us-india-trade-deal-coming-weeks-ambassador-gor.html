US-India Trade Deal "Will Be Finalised In Coming Weeks": Ambassador Gor

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/US Ambassador Sergio Gor said the India-US bilateral trade deal will be finalised in the "coming weeks and months" on May 21, 2026
  • 02/An Indian delegation visited Washington in April; a US delegation is expected in June to hammer out the interim pact details
  • 03/Secretary of State Marco Rubio begins a four-day India visit on May 23, 2026 to consolidate trade, defence, and energy cooperation

US Ambassador to India Sergio Gor has expressed strong confidence that the bilateral trade deal between Washington and New Delhi will be finalised in the "coming weeks and months." He made the remarks on May 21, 2026 at the Annual Leadership Summit of the American Chamber of Commerce in Delhi.

Gor stated that President Trump's goal is to facilitate bilateral trade that creates lucrative opportunities for American businesses and workers. The interim trade agreement is currently on the table awaiting finalisation.

The envoy highlighted the accelerated pace of negotiations. Talks have been ongoing for a year and a half, far shorter than the "almost 19 years" the EU took to conclude its FTA with India. That India-EU deal was signed in January 2026.

Last month, an Indian delegation travelled to Washington D.C. to advance the interim trade agreement. Next month, a US delegation will visit India to continue the discussions, Gor announced.

Commerce and Industry Minister Piyush Goyal independently confirmed the upcoming US visit. He clarified that the chief US negotiator for the Bilateral Trade Agreement will travel separately from Secretary of State Marco Rubio.

Rubio is scheduled to begin a four-day official tour of India on May 23, 2026. The visit aims to consolidate cooperation across trade, defence, and energy sectors. Preparations are also underway for a ministerial-level Quad meeting in India.

The negotiations follow a joint statement issued on February 7, 2026 that finalised the foundational framework for an interim trade arrangement. The landscape shifted after a US Supreme Court ruling struck down reciprocal tariffs.

Washington responded by imposing a 10 per cent auxiliary duty on all inbound goods under Section 122 for a 150-day window starting February 24, 2026. Dual investigations under Section 301 are also active, scrutinising excess industrial capacity and labour practices.

New Delhi has formally delivered its comprehensive responses to both federal probes. Consultative dialogues between the two economies remain underway to secure the final deal.

"We look forward to completing the details of a new bilateral trade agreement that will expand market access, reduce barriers, and create greater certainty for businesses on both sides," Gor said. The agreement aims to strengthen supply chains and drive sustained inclusive growth.
