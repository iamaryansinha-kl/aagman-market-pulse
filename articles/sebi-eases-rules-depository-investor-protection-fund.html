Sebi Eases Rules on Use of Depository Investor Protection Fund Income

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

The Securities and Exchange Board of India has relaxed norms for depositories' investor protection funds.

The regulator eased rules governing how income generated from these funds can be used. Depositories — National Securities Depository and Central Depository Services — now have greater flexibility in managing the funds.

Investor protection funds are maintained by depositories to compensate investors who suffer losses due to fraud, default, or negligence by depository participants. These funds are built through contributions from participants and income from investments.

Previously, the use of fund income was tightly restricted. The new rules allow depositories to deploy the income more broadly, potentially improving returns while maintaining adequate reserves for investor protection.

Sebi said the changes are intended to ensure the funds remain solvent and effective while allowing better utilisation of the income they generate. The regulator has mandated that depositories must maintain sufficient reserves to cover all valid claims.

The move affects millions of demat account holders in India. Any changes to investor protection frameworks have significant implications for market confidence and retail participation.

Rules on how depositories can use income from investor protection funds.

Funds maintained by depositories to compensate investors for losses from fraud or default by depository participants.

National Securities Depository (NSDL) and Central Depository Services (CDSL).
Why did Sebi make this change?
To give depositories more flexibility in managing fund income while maintaining adequate reserves.

Yes, it affects the framework that protects millions of demat account holders in India.
