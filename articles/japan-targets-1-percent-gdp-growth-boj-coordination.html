Japan Targets Over 1% Real GDP Growth, Urges BOJ Policy Coordination

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Japan's government targets annual real economic growth above 1%, more than double the 0.4% average of the past five years.
  • 02/Draft policy projects combined public-private investment exceeding 370 trillion yen by fiscal 2040.
  • 03/Government seeks close BOJ coordination to keep borrowing costs low, potentially creating tension with the central bank.

Japan's government has set an ambitious target of over 1% annual real economic growth. The draft policy framework was reviewed by Reuters on June 29, 2026.

The plan aims to achieve sustained real growth above 1% "as early as possible." It also maintains a nominal growth target above 3%.

Japan's economy has grown at just 0.4% annually over the last five years. The new target would more than double that pace.

Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi is pushing the agenda to revive domestic growth. The draft reflects a decisive shift away from years of underinvestment.

Combined public and private investment is projected to exceed 370 trillion yen ($2.29 trillion) through fiscal 2040. Annual private-sector capital expenditure is targeted at around 230 trillion yen by the same year.

The government aims to expand Japan's economy to nearly 1,100 trillion yen. Strategic industries will receive priority focus under the plan.

Despite the growth push, the framework reaffirms fiscal discipline. The government intends to steadily reduce the debt-to-GDP ratio over time.

The draft underscores close coordination with the Bank of Japan on monetary policy. It signals a preference for maintaining low borrowing costs to support expansion.

This stance could create tensions with the BOJ. The central bank may face pressure to keep rates low even as inflation dynamics evolve.
