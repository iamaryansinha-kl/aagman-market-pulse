Reliance Cuts Alkylate Output, Boosts LPG Production 3x Amid India Gas Crisis

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Reliance Industries is running its alkylation unit at minimum rates to boost LPG output more than threefold from pre-war levels
  • 02/India's 704,000 bpd Jamnagar refinery is diverting feedstock from U.S.-bound alkylate exports to domestic cooking gas production
  • 03/The move follows a March 2026 government directive as India faces its worst LPG crisis in decades due to Strait of Hormuz closure

Reliance Industries Ltd (NSE: RELIANCE) has slashed alkylate production at its Jamnagar refinery. The company is diverting feedstock to sharply increase liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) output as India faces a severe cooking fuel shortage.

LPG production at Reliance has risen more than threefold from pre-war levels. The company confirmed the shift in a statement issued on May 4, 2026.

The operator of the world's biggest refining complex is running its alkylation unit at minimum rates. This curtailment directly hits exports of alkylates, a premium gasoline-blending component typically shipped to the United States.

The Jamnagar refinery processes 704,000 barrels per day and is export-focused. Redirecting output from alkylates to LPG signals the severity of India's domestic gas crisis.

India is the world's second-largest LPG importer. The country relies on the Middle East for about 90% of its LPG imports, and the Strait of Hormuz closure has choked this supply corridor since late February 2026.

In March 2026, the federal government directed all refiners to maximise LPG yields. The LPG Control Order mandated channeling the entire C3 and C4 hydrocarbon stream to state-run oil marketing companies.

Domestic LPG production across India has increased by 60%, reaching approximately 54,000 tonnes per day. Despite this, India still faces a daily shortfall of roughly 16,000 tonnes against baseline consumption of 80,000 tonnes.

The government has cut commercial gas supplies to protect household cooking fuel needs. Black market cylinder prices have surged to ₹1,200–1,500 in some areas.

"This has been done to bridge part of the gap in loss of LPG imports from Middle Eastern countries," Reliance said in its statement. The move highlights how geopolitical shocks are forcing private refiners to prioritise domestic fuel security over export revenue.
