NSE Launches Nifty500 Ahimsa Index to Track Companies Aligned with Non-Violence

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

The NSE is bringing spirituality to investing.

The National Stock Exchange has launched the Nifty500 Ahimsa Index, a first-of-its-kind ESG-themed index that tracks companies whose business activities are aligned with the principle of non-violence. The index excludes companies involved in weapons manufacturing, tobacco, alcohol, gambling, and other activities deemed inconsistent with the Jain and Hindu concept of ahimsa, offering a unique investment product for ethically conscious investors.

The index is built from the Nifty500 universe. It applies exclusionary filters to remove companies that derive significant revenue from activities considered violent or harmful, then selects the remaining companies based on their market capitalisation and liquidity. The resulting index provides a broad-based exposure to India's large and mid-cap companies while maintaining a strict ethical overlay.

Ahimsa is a core principle in Jainism and Hinduism. The concept of non-violence extends beyond physical harm to include mental and emotional harm, and in the business context, it translates to avoiding activities that cause suffering to humans, animals, or the environment. The index is likely to appeal to Jain and Hindu investors, as well as to a broader audience of ethically conscious investors who want their portfolios to reflect their values.

The launch reflects the growing demand for ESG and values-based investing in India. Globally, ESG-themed indices and funds have seen massive inflows, with investors increasingly demanding that their capital be deployed in ways that are consistent with environmental, social, and governance principles. India has been slower to adopt ESG investing, but the launch of the Ahimsa Index suggests that the market is catching up.

The index could attract significant capital from religious and charitable institutions. Jain and Hindu temples, trusts, and charitable organisations manage substantial assets and have historically been conservative investors, favouring gold, real estate, and fixed deposits. An index that aligns with their religious principles could encourage these institutions to diversify into equities, bringing a new class of long-term investors into the market.

The commercial viability depends on performance. Values-based indices often underperform their broader benchmarks because the exclusionary filters remove high-performing sectors like defence, alcohol, and tobacco. Investors who choose the Ahimsa Index will be making a trade-off between returns and values, and the index's success will depend on whether enough investors are willing to make that trade-off. If the index performs well relative to the Nifty500, it could attract a wider audience; if it underperforms, it may remain a niche product.
