Vedanta Shares Rebound 1.56% Ahead of April 30 Demerger Ex-Date

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Vedanta Limited shares climbed 1.56% to ₹754.10 on April 28, recovering from a low of ₹728.70 ahead of the demerger ex-date
  • 02/The stock has rallied 81% over the past year and hit a 52-week high of ₹795 on April 21 before pulling back 3%
  • 03/Record date is fixed at May 1, 2026; April 29 is the last day to buy shares to qualify for 1:1 allotment in four demerged entities

Vedanta Limited shares rose 1.56% to ₹754.10 on the NSE on Tuesday morning. The rebound comes a day before the April 30 special pre-open price-discovery session when the stock will trade ex-demerger.

The stock had recovered from an intraday low of ₹728.70. It hit a 52-week high of ₹795 on April 21 following the record date announcement but has since pulled back over 3%.

The recent weakness was driven by profit-booking after an 81% one-year rally. Traders were also unwinding positions ahead of the F&O contract expiry on April 29, according to Khushi Mistry, Research Analyst at Bonanza.

April 29 is the last date to buy Vedanta shares and qualify for demerger benefits. April 30 is the ex-date — the share price will mechanically drop as the value of four spun-off entities is stripped out.

Vedanta will split into five separately listed entities. Shareholders will receive shares in all four demerged businesses in a 1:1 ratio. The new entities are Vedanta Aluminium, Vedanta Oil & Gas, Vedanta Power, and Vedanta Iron & Steel.

On April 30, a Special Pre-Open Session will run from 9:15 AM to 9:45 AM for ex-demerger price discovery. Normal trading will resume at 10:00 AM.

At current levels, Vedanta trades at a P/E of 17.52 with a free-float market cap of ₹1.28 lakh crore. Delivery volume stands at 62.93%, suggesting conviction buying.

Risks remain post-demerger. Debt distribution across five entities, the promoter holding company's reliance on dividend streams, and commodity price volatility are key variables. If listings slip beyond June, the new entities could miss the September index rebalance.
