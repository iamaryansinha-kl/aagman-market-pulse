India Peak Power Demand Hits Record 256 GW as Heatwave Intensifies

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/India's peak power demand reached a record 256.11 GW at 1538 hrs on Saturday, April 25, 2026
  • 02/The new high eclipsed the previous record of 252.07 GW set just a day earlier on Friday, April 24
  • 03/Experts say demand is inching toward the power ministry's summer estimate of 270 GW as mercury soars

India's peak power demand hit an all-time high of 256.11 GW on Saturday. The record was set at 1538 hrs on April 25, 2026, according to the latest power ministry data.

The new peak eclipsed the previous record of 252.07 GW. That mark was hit just a day earlier on Friday, April 24, at 1548 hrs.

The demand was fully met by the country's power grid. No supply shortfall was reported at the time of peak consumption.

Experts stated that peak demand is now inching toward the power ministry's estimate of 270 GW for this summer season. Mercury levels have begun to rise sharply across parts of the country.

Delhi recorded its highest maximum temperature of the year so far at 42.8 degrees Celsius on Saturday. This marked the highest April maximum since 2022, when the city hit 43.5 degrees Celsius on April 28.

Saturday was the third consecutive day of a heatwave in the capital, according to the India Meteorological Department. Temperatures in several regions have been significantly above normal by 5 degrees Celsius or more.

The country's peak power demand has risen steadily with temperatures in April. The highest electricity supply was 239.70 GW on April 22, which rose to 240.12 GW on April 23.

In the first fortnight of April 2026, peak demand remained lower than the 235.32 GW recorded in April 2025. The surge in the latter half of the month reflects the intensifying heat stress scenario.

Last summer, the peak power demand was 242.77 GW, recorded in June 2025. It stayed below the government's estimate of 277 GW for that season.

In May 2024, the peak power demand was 250 GW, a record at the time. That figure exceeded the previous all-time peak of 243.27 GW set in September 2023.

The IMD has projected harsh summers this year. Experts believe demand and consumption of electricity may rise further as domestic and commercial consumers increase use of air conditioners and coolers.
