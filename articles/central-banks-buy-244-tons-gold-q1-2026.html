Central Banks Buy 244 Tons Gold in Q1 2026 as Prices Dip 12%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Central banks purchased 244 tons of gold in Q1 2026, the fastest pace in more than a year
  • 02/Gold prices fell 12% in March after hitting a record $5,600/oz in January, enabling the buying spree
  • 03/Turkey, Russia and Azerbaijan sold 115 tons combined; watch for further price volatility amid US-Iran war fallout

Central banks added gold at the fastest pace in over a year during Q1 2026. Net official-sector purchases reached 244 tons, up from 208 tons in the prior quarter.

The World Gold Council reported the data on April 29, 2026. Poland, Uzbekistan and China were the largest reported buyers.

Gold prices swung wildly during the period. Prices peaked at a record near $5,600 per ounce on January 29, before slumping sharply in March.

The March drop of 12% was the biggest monthly decline since 2008. The fall followed the outbreak of the US-Iran war and soaring energy prices.

Lower prices allowed reserve managers to step in. "It's the first time in a while that we've seen a decent correction in gold," said John Reade, chief strategist at the World Gold Council.

Some institutions did sell holdings. Turkey, Russia and Azerbaijan shed an estimated 115 tons to meet budget deficits and shield currencies.

Much of the central bank buying remains undisclosed. Consultancy Metals Focus Ltd. calculates estimates using public data, trade statistics and field research.

Spot gold was trading near $4,600 an ounce ahead of the report. Bullion faces headwinds as central banks may hold rates steady to contain inflation.
