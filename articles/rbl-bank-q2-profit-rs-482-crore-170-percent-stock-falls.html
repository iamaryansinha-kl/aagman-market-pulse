RBL Bank Q2 Profit Jumps 170% To Rs 482 Crore But Stock Slides 8%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/RBL Bank's September-quarter net profit jumped 170% year on year to Rs 482 crore, with net interest income up 37.4%.
  • 02/Asset quality improved: gross NPAs fell to 3.80% from 4.00% three months earlier, while deposits grew 12.4% and advances 12%.
  • 03/Despite the beat, the stock slid about 8% on Tuesday — a sell-the-news move after a strong rally that had carried it near 52-week highs.

RBL Bank delivered one of the strongest profit performances in the private banking space this earnings season. The lender's September quarter numbers, filed with stock exchanges, showed net profit surging 170 per cent year on year to Rs 482 crore.

The bottom line also jumped 90 per cent quarter on quarter, underscoring the pace of the earnings recovery. Net interest income, the core of a bank's earnings, grew 37.4 per cent year on year.

RBL attributed the performance to three factors: improving asset quality, robust loan growth and higher net interest income. All three showed up in the headline numbers.

Gross non-performing assets improved to 3.80 per cent at the end of September, from 4.00 per cent three months earlier. A year ago, in June 2025, the ratio stood at 4.79 per cent, so the cleanup has been sharp.

Funding and lending both grew in low double digits. Deposits rose 12.4 per cent, while advances grew 12 per cent, with domestic loans up 11 per cent and improvement across products. Growth was evenly spread rather than reliant on any single product line, the bank indicated.

The microfinance book, a historical stress point for RBL, remains on the watchlist. Provisions for the segment rose 26 per cent to Rs 313 crore in the quarter.

That increase is worth noting. Microfinance stress has been building across the industry, driven by over-indebtedness in certain geographies and collections pressure, and RBL is among the lenders with meaningful exposure.

Yet the market's reaction was brutal. Shares of RBL Bank slid about 8 per cent on Tuesday morning even as the headline numbers beat expectations.

The fall is best read as classic profit booking after a one-way run. The stock had rallied hard into results — Emirates NBD's $2.75 billion investment, completed in June, reset investor expectations for the franchise and the shares had been trading near 52-week highs.

When expectations run that hot, even a 170 per cent profit jump can disappoint if investors were hoping for more on margins or guidance. The higher microfinance provisions likely added to the nervousness.

The broader tape was supportive, which makes the slide starker. The Sensex surged over 700 points on Tuesday, the rupee was stabilising after the RBI's recent measures, and banking peers mostly traded firm.

RBL's numbers still tell a fundamentally positive story. A 37 per cent rise in net interest income implies both volume growth and margin resilience, while the NPA ratio below 4 per cent restores the bank to near its best-ever asset-quality position.

The strategic backdrop has also changed. With Emirates NBD as a major shareholder, RBL has access to cheaper capital, Gulf corridor business and a credibility upgrade that could help deposit pricing and wholesale funding over time.

The bank's challenge now is consistency. Investors will want to see the September-quarter momentum — loan growth above 10 per cent, controlled credit costs, stable margins — repeated through FY27 before re-rating the stock further.

RBL's numbers fit a wider pattern in private banking this season. Mid-sized lenders have generally reported loan growth in the low-to-mid teens with stable margins, and market reaction has hinged on guidance for the rest of FY27 and commentary on unsecured and microfinance exposure.

On valuation, the stock's re-rating since June means it trades richer than its own historical averages. That premium requires double-digit loan growth with credit costs in check — exactly what September showed, but for only one quarter.

For shareholders, Tuesday's slide is a valuation debate, not a fundamentals break. The earnings trajectory improved materially; the question is how much of that improvement the share price had already priced in.

The next checkpoints are the December-quarter update on microfinance collections and any commentary from the new strategic shareholders on capital and strategy. Both will shape whether the post-results dip proves a buying opportunity or the start of a longer consolidation.

WHAT IS THE STRATEGIC CONTEXT FOR RBL BANK?
EMIRATES NBD COMPLETED A $2.75 BILLION INVESTMENT IN RBL BANK IN JUNE 2026, BECOMING A MAJOR SHAREHOLDER AND GIVING THE BANK A GULF CORRIDOR PARTNERSHIP, CAPITAL ACCESS AND A CREDIBILITY UPLIFT. GLOSSARY: Net interest income (NII): The difference between interest a bank earns on loans and what it pays on deposits, its core revenue line. Gross NPA ratio: The percentage of a bank's total loans that are classified as non-performing. Provisions: Money set aside against expected loan losses; rising provisions signal caution on asset quality. Sell-the-news: A market pattern where a stock falls after good results because the optimism was already priced in. Microfinance: Small-ticket lending to low-income borrowers, typically through joint-liability groups. Quarter-on-quarter (QoQ): A comparison of one quarter's numbers against the immediately preceding quarter.
