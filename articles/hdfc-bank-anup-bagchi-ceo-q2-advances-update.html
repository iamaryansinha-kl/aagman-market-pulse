HDFC Bank Names Anup Bagchi MD & CEO; Q2 Advances Up 16.3%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/HDFC Bank has named Anup Bagchi as its new MD & CEO; he joined the board on October 2 and takes the top job as Sashidhar Jagdishan's term ends October 26.
  • 02/The stock reacted positively, gaining 2% to a day's high of ₹734 on the BSE on Monday.
  • 03/Separately, HDFC Bank's Q2 FY27 update showed period-end gross advances up 16.3% YoY to ₹32.20 lakh crore.

HDFC Bank Ltd (NSE: HDFCBANK) has appointed Anup Bagchi as its new Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer. The move ends months of uncertainty over the top job at India's largest private-sector lender.

Bagchi joined HDFC Bank's board as an Additional Director effective October 2. He takes a three-year term as MD & CEO, stepping in after Sashidhar Jagdishan's term ends on October 26.

According to Citi's note on the appointment, Bagchi's MD & CEO term runs from October 27. That sequencing gives the bank a clean handover at the end of Jagdishan's tenure.

Jagdishan had announced he would not seek reappointment when his term ends later this month. That decision had left the bank's leadership question open and hanging over the stock.

Bagchi is an ICICI Group veteran with over three decades of experience in financial services. He is currently the outgoing MD & CEO of ICICI Prudential Life Insurance.

Shares of HDFC Bank gained 2% to the day's high of ₹734 on the BSE on Monday. The appointment and the accompanying Q2 business update were both in focus.

On the numbers, average advances under management stood at ₹31.87 lakh crore in the September 2026 quarter. That is up around 14% from ₹27.95 lakh crore a year earlier.

Period-end advances under management stood at approximately ₹33.08 lakh crore as of September 30, 2026. That compares with ₹28.69 lakh crore a year earlier, a rise of around 15.3%.

Period-end gross advances rose around 16.3% year-on-year to approximately ₹32.20 lakh crore. They stood at ₹27.69 lakh crore as of September 30, 2025.

Deposit growth ran ahead of loan growth. Average deposits stood at ₹31.67 lakh crore during the quarter, up around 16.8% from ₹27.11 lakh crore a year earlier.

Average CASA deposits rose around 10.7% year-on-year to ₹9.71 lakh crore. They stood at ₹8.77 lakh crore in the September 2025 quarter.

Jefferies retained its Buy rating on HDFC Bank with a target price of ₹880 per share. The brokerage said RBI approval of the appointment provides clarity on management succession and strategic direction.

Jefferies argued that timely team changes, if any, along with improving retail credit growth, better cross-sell fees and calibrated re-risking of the loan mix could support earnings. It also said the stock trades at an attractive 1.4x FY28E adjusted price-to-book valuation and could re-rate.

The brokerage noted that around 70% of HDFC Bank's loans are linked to EBLR or floating rates. That means the bank, like most large banks, could benefit from any rate hikes.

Jefferies added that a recalibration towards higher risk-adjusted segments could support return-on-assets expansion. Those segments include SME and business banking loans, gold loans and unsecured personal loans.

Morgan Stanley maintained its Overweight rating with a target price of ₹1,025, implying 42% upside from the current price. The brokerage said underlying deposit growth remained strong at around 15% YoY excluding FCNR deposits.

Morgan Stanley also flagged a softer lending trend. It said underlying loan growth moderated to 14.3% excluding FCTL, remaining slower than the system, while CASA growth improved sequentially but stayed muted.

Citi retained its Buy rating with a target price of ₹970, implying around 35% upside. The brokerage described the appointment as strategically positive.

Citi identified execution, strategic direction and the continuity of Kaizad Bharucha as key factors to watch. Its note also pins the start of Bagchi's MD & CEO term to October 27.

The succession resolution removes a known overhang on the stock. What investors will watch next is whether loan growth can catch up to system levels and whether CASA traction improves under the new CEO.
