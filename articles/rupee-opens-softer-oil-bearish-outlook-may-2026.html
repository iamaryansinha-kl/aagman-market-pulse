Rupee Opens At 94.95 On Softer Oil But Bearish Outlook Persists

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/The Indian rupee opened at 94.95 against the US dollar on May 4, supported by a pullback in Brent crude to near $108 per barrel
  • 02/Portfolio outflows reached nearly $6.5 billion in April alone, with cumulative FII withdrawals in 2026 at roughly $20.6 billion surpassing all of 2025
  • 03/Traders expect the rupee to trade in the 94.55 to 95.40 range; the RBI's $103 billion forward book limits intervention capacity

The Indian rupee opened at 94.95 against the US dollar on Monday, May 4, 2026. It was 4 paise weaker than the previous close of 94.92, itself a record closing low set on April 30.

The rupee found brief support from a pullback in oil prices. Brent crude July futures slid to $105.50 in the Asia session before trading near $108 a barrel. US President Donald Trump said the US would help clear ships stranded in the Strait of Hormuz.

Trump offered scant details, calling the move a humanitarian effort for neutral nations. Meanwhile, the lack of progress toward a peace deal is expected to limit oil's downside, analysts said.

The rupee has slid nearly 2% over the past eight trading days. It hit an all-time intraday low of 95.33 on Thursday, April 30, before settling at 94.91.

High oil prices have eroded sentiment and prompted heavier hedging by importers. Portfolio outflows reached nearly $6.5 billion in April. Cumulative withdrawals in 2026 stand at roughly $20.6 billion, surpassing outflows seen in all of 2025.

"The rupee's outlook remains challenging from both sentiment and flow perspectives, with the ballooning Reserve Bank of India FX forward book an added headwind," a currency trader at a bank said.

Anindya Banerjee of Kotak Securities has flagged 96 as the next critical level for USD/INR. Kunal Sodhani of Shinhan Bank projects the pair trading in the 94.55 to 95.30 to 95.40 range while the Hormuz situation remains unresolved.
