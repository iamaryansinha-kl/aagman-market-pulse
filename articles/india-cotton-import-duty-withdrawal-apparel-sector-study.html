India Cotton Import Duty: Study Urges 11% Withdrawal for Apparel Sector

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/A Gherzi-ICAC study urges the government to withdraw the 11 per cent import duty on raw cotton to help Indian mills compete with Asian rivals
  • 02/India's textile and apparel exports declined 2.2 per cent YoY in FY26 to $35.79 billion as competitors enjoy duty-free cotton access
  • 03/The Textile and Agriculture Ministries are in talks, with a proposal for temporary duty removal during the lean season

A joint study by Gherzi and the International Cotton Advisory Committee has called for the withdrawal of India's 11 per cent import duty on raw cotton. The report, released by the Confederation of Indian Textile Industry, was published on May 7, 2026.

The study noted that India's competitors in Asia have free access to international cotton without any import duty. The import duty puts Indian mills at a significant disadvantage in global markets.

India's textile and apparel exports, the country's second-biggest employer, declined 2.2 per cent year-on-year in FY26. The sector's exports stood at $35.79 billion in dollar terms.

The Textile Ministry has been pushing for the removal of the 11 per cent import duty on raw cotton, which was reinstated this year. The Agriculture Ministry has withheld consent and is consulting stakeholders including farm groups.

The Textile Ministry suggested that the removal of import duty could be temporary, covering only the lean season for domestic cotton. The Ministries of Textiles, Finance and Agriculture are currently discussing the matter.

The Gherzi-ICAC report also proposed a Cotton Price Stabilisation Fund with 5 per cent interest subvention. This would help mitigate working capital needs for cotton procured during November to March.

The report suggested the Cotton Corporation of India maintain a strategic inventory of about three months of consumption. This would cushion price volatility, similar to policies adopted by China.

Long-term policy must address productivity constraints to ensure economic viability for cotton growers. The policy framework needs to balance the interests of the entire value chain, the report noted.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT DOES THE GHERZI-ICAC STUDY RECOMMEND REGARDING COTTON IMPORT DUTY?
THE STUDY URGES THE WITHDRAWAL OF THE 11 PER CENT IMPORT DUTY ON RAW COTTON TO ALLOW INDIAN MILLS TO ACCESS COMPETITIVE PRICES AND GAIN A LEVEL PLAYING FIELD WITH ASIAN COMPETITORS WHO HAVE DUTY-FREE ACCESS.
HOW MUCH DID INDIA'S TEXTILE AND APPAREL EXPORTS DECLINE IN FY26?
EXPORTS FROM THE TEXTILE AND APPAREL SECTOR DECLINED 2.2 PER CENT YEAR-ON-YEAR IN FY26 TO $35.79 BILLION IN DOLLAR TERMS.
WHY IS THERE A DISPUTE BETWEEN THE TEXTILE AND AGRICULTURE MINISTRIES?
THE TEXTILE MINISTRY WANTS TO REMOVE THE 11 PER CENT IMPORT DUTY TO BOOST GARMENTS PRODUCTION AND EXPORTS. THE AGRICULTURE MINISTRY HAS WITHHELD CONSENT TO PROTECT FARMERS AND IS CONDUCTING STAKEHOLDER CONSULTATIONS.
WHAT IS THE PROPOSED COTTON PRICE STABILISATION FUND?
THE REPORT PROPOSES A COTTON PRICE STABILISATION FUND WITH 5 PER CENT INTEREST SUBVENTION TO MITIGATE WORKING CAPITAL NEEDS FOR COTTON PROCURED DURING NOVEMBER TO MARCH.
WHAT OTHER MEASURES DOES THE STUDY SUGGEST TO SUPPORT THE COTTON SECTOR?
THE STUDY SUGGESTS THE COTTON CORPORATION OF INDIA MAINTAIN A STRATEGIC INVENTORY OF ABOUT THREE MONTHS OF CONSUMPTION TO CUSHION PRICE VOLATILITY. IT ALSO RECOMMENDS CCI ADOPT A DYNAMIC SELLING POLICY THROUGH UPCOUNTRY WAREHOUSES NEAR MAJOR TEXTILE CLUSTERS.
