India's Forex Reserves Up $963 Million to $672.58 Billion as of June 19

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/India's foreign exchange reserves rose by $963 million to $672.58 billion in the week ended June 19, 2026
  • 02/Gold reserves surged $4.1 billion to $107.93 billion, offsetting a $3.07 billion decline in foreign currency assets
  • 03/On a year-on-year basis, forex reserves are down $25.3 billion, with the RBI continuing to monitor currency market stability

India's foreign exchange reserves increased by $963 million to $672.58 billion in the week ended June 19, 2026. The data was released by the Reserve Bank of India on Friday.

Foreign currency assets, the largest component of the reserves, fell by $3.07 billion to $541.21 billion. This decline in FCAs was more than offset by a sharp rise in gold reserves.

Gold reserves jumped $4.1 billion to $107.93 billion during the week. Special Drawing Rights declined by $52 million to $18.64 billion.

India's reserve position with the IMF slipped by $22 million to $4.79 billion. The net result was a healthy increase in the overall forex buffer.

On a year-on-year basis, India's foreign exchange reserves have slipped by $25.3 billion. However, FCAs are up by $47.85 billion compared to the same period last year.

The sizeable forex buffer continues to provide crucial stability to the economy. It helps cushion against external shocks and supports the rupee during periods of volatility.

The Reserve Bank of India closely monitors the foreign exchange market. It intervenes when necessary to ensure orderly conditions without targeting any specific exchange rate.
