Rupee Opens 32 Paise Higher At 94.93 Against US Dollar On Crude Dip

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Indian rupee opened at 94.93 per dollar on July 2, gaining 32 paise from previous close after crude prices declined further
  • 02/The currency had fallen 0.6% on Wednesday, its steepest single-day drop in three weeks, slipping below the 94.80-95.00 support range
  • 03/RBI reportedly sold dollars at 94.75 before stepping back; traders see limited recovery amid weak Asian cues and elevated US Treasury yields

The Indian rupee opened at 94.93 against the US dollar on Thursday, July 2. The currency gained 32 paise, tracking a further decline in crude oil prices.

On Wednesday, the rupee had fallen 0.6%. That marked its steepest single-day decline in three weeks.

The rupee slipped below the 94.80-95.00 range. Traders had viewed this zone as a key near-term support level.

The Reserve Bank of India reportedly sold dollars around 94.75 on Wednesday. The central bank later stepped back, triggering stop-loss orders that accelerated the fall.

RBI has been seen intervening in recent sessions. Its goal has been to prevent excessive depreciation beyond the 95-per-dollar mark.

Market experts expect RBI to continue absorbing foreign inflows. The central bank is likely to rebuild forex reserves rather than allow sharp rupee appreciation.

India's manufacturing PMI eased to 54.2 in June. That marks one of the slowest expansions since mid-2022.

In the US, private employers added 98,000 jobs in June. That was below May's revised 122,000 and expectations of 113,000.

Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh, speaking at the ECB Forum, said inflation expectations have improved. However, inflation remains above the Fed's comfort zone.

Amit Pabari, MD at CR Forex Advisors, said USDINR could move towards 95.30-95.50. He added that the bias remains to the upside, with 94.80 acting as an important support level.
