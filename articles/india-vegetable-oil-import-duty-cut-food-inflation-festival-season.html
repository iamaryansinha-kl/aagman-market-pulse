India Considers Cutting Vegetable Oil Import Duties by 5% as Prices Climb 20% Ahead of Festival Season

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/India, the world's biggest vegetable oils importer, is considering lowering import taxes to curb food inflation ahead of the festival season, government and industry sources said.
  • 02/Domestic vegetable oil prices have climbed nearly 20% over the last year; the government could lower the basic import duty by 5%.
  • 03/India meets nearly two-thirds of its vegetable oil demand through imports, mainly palm, soy and sunflower oil from Malaysia, Indonesia, Argentina, Russia and Ukraine.
  • 04/A duty cut would support Malaysian palm oil and US soyoil futures, but could also lift prices in exporting countries, an industry official cautioned.
  • 05/In May 2025, India halved the basic import tax on crude edible oils to 10%, taking the total duty on crude palm, soy and sunflower oil to 16.5%.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
Why is India considering cutting vegetable oil import taxes?
A/01/ Domestic vegetable oil prices have climbed nearly 20% over the past year, feeding food inflation at the start of the September-November festival season. India, the world's biggest vegetable oils importer, is weighing a basic duty cut of around 5% to shield consumers while keeping local soybean prices above support levels for farmers.
What is the current import duty on edible oils in India?
A/02/ In May 2025, India halved the basic import tax on crude edible oils to 10%. With the Agriculture Infrastructure and Development Cess and Social Welfare Surcharge, the total effective duty on crude palm oil, crude soyoil and crude sunflower oil stands at 16.5%.
How much of India's vegetable oil demand is imported?
A/03/ India imports nearly two-thirds of its vegetable oil consumption, mainly palm oil from Malaysia and Indonesia and soyoil and sunflower oil from Argentina, Russia and Ukraine.
Would an import duty cut actually lower domestic prices?
A/04/ Not necessarily. After the May 2025 duty cut, global palm oil and soyoil prices moved higher. An industry official warned that cutting duties could spur Indian demand and push up prices in exporting countries instead — arguing duty cuts are not an effective way to manage prices.
