2 Lakh Micro Food Processing Enterprises Covered Under Credit-Linked Subsidy

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

Small food businesses are getting government backing.

The government has extended credit-linked subsidies to 2 lakh micro food processing enterprises under the Pradhan Mantri Formalisation of Micro Food Processing Enterprises scheme. The initiative provides financial support for upgrading equipment, improving food safety standards, and expanding production capacity, aiming to bring informal food businesses into the organised sector.

The scheme addresses a critical gap. India's food processing sector is dominated by micro and small enterprises operating in the informal economy. These businesses lack access to formal credit, modern equipment, and technical knowledge, limiting their growth potential and keeping food wastage high. The credit-linked subsidy is designed to bridge this gap.

Each eligible enterprise can receive a subsidy of up to 35% on bank loans. The credit-linked component means that businesses must first secure a bank loan, and the government then reimburses a portion of the interest or principal. This design ensures that funds reach genuine businesses with bankable projects rather than being dispersed as grants.

The food processing sector has significant untapped potential. India is one of the world's largest producers of food grains, fruits, and vegetables, yet only a small fraction is processed. The government's target is to increase the food processing level from the current 10% to 25%, which would reduce wastage, improve farmer incomes, and create employment.

Food safety is a key focus. Many micro enterprises operate without proper hygiene standards or certification. The scheme includes provisions for training, certification, and equipment upgrades that help businesses meet FSSAI standards. This is critical for accessing organised retail channels and export markets.

The 2 lakh figure shows scale but also reveals the challenge. With millions of micro food processing units across India, covering 2 lakh is just a start. The scheme's success will depend on how quickly it can scale, whether banks are willing to lend to small food businesses, and whether the subsidy disbursal process is efficient.
