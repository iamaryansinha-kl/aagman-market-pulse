From Rs 228 to Rs 7.5 a GB: Jio Data Revolution Meets IPO Test

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Mobile data cost Rs 228 per GB a decade ago; today it is about Rs 7.5, with download speeds up from 2.5 Mbps to over 68 Mbps.
  • •- Average monthly data consumption rose from under 0.2 GB to 26.7 GB; Jio's own customers average 43.7 GB.
  • •- Jio Platforms' Rs 30,213 crore fresh-issue IPO opens October 21 at a price band up to Rs 1,119, implying about Rs 10.3 lakh crore market capitalisation.
  • •- FY26 revenue rose to Rs 1,46,886 crore; EBITDA grew 16.4% to Rs 73,382 crore at a 50% margin.
  • •- ARPU improved from Rs 181.7 in FY24 to Rs 215.6 in the June 2026 quarter; 284.9 million customers are on 5G.
  • •- Up to Rs 27,500 crore of proceeds will repay Reliance Jio Infocomm's borrowings.

A decade ago, a gigabyte of mobile data in India cost Rs 228. Today it costs about Rs 7.5. That 97 per cent collapse is not merely a tariff story — it is the entire investment case for the Rs 30,213 crore IPO that opens on October 21, compressed into a single number. Jio did not just cut prices when Mukesh Ambani's Reliance entered telecom in 2016; it rewired how India uses the internet, and on Tuesday the company formally asks public-market investors to price the machine that did the rewiring at something close to Rs 10.3 lakh crore.

The transformation, measured by Systematix, is stark. Industry download speeds have risen from around 2.5 megabits per second to more than 68 Mbps by FY26. Average monthly data consumption per customer climbed from less than 0.2 GB to 26.7 GB across the industry — and Jio's own subscribers now average 43.7 GB a month, a level that would have been science fiction at 2016 prices. For context, that means the typical Jio customer burns through in one day what the average Indian mobile user consumed in an entire quarter a decade ago. The scale behind those numbers: 533.3 million customers as of June 2026, network coverage of more than 99 per cent of India's population, and roughly 60 per cent of the country's wireless data traffic carried in FY26. Cheaper data created the audience; the audience is now the asset.

The financial engine has followed. Revenue rose to Rs 1,46,886 crore in FY26 from Rs 1,28,218 crore a year earlier, while EBITDA grew 16.4 per cent to Rs 73,382 crore and the EBITDA margin expanded to 50 per cent from 49.2 per cent, according to Ventura Securities. Cash flow from operations reached Rs 77,556 crore. Average revenue per user — the metric that determines whether India's data habit actually makes money — climbed from Rs 181.7 in FY24 to Rs 214 in FY26 and Rs 215.6 in the June 2026 quarter, as tariff hikes and 2G-to-4G migration did quietly what the price war loudly did not. More than 260 million Indians remain on 2G networks; each migration is a revenue upgrade walking through the door.

The IPO structure itself tells you how management frames the moment. The issue is entirely fresh — no offer-for-sale, so no existing shareholder exits — with up to Rs 27,500 crore earmarked to repay or prepay borrowings at the operating company, Reliance Jio Infocomm. That is a balance-sheet deleveraging exercise dressed as an IPO: proceeds cut financing costs and strengthen the company whose revenue and profits Jio Platforms substantially depends on. At the upper price band of Rs 1,119, the implied market capitalisation is about Rs 10.3 lakh crore, or roughly 33 times FY26 earnings by Systematix's math — a multiple the brokerage defends with a subscribe recommendation on the strength of market leadership, profitability and cash generation.

The pitch beyond connectivity is what justifies the multiple, and it is broader than a phone company has any right to expect. Jio's fixed broadband base reached 28.6 million in June, including 14 million JioAirFiber subscribers, attacking the household segment where fibre economics have long been hostile. The enterprise stack — cloud, internet of things, private 5G, cybersecurity, communications platforms and enterprise AI — turns the network from a pipe into a platform. JioBrain, the company's proprietary AI engine, already runs network optimisation and predictive fault monitoring, and the group has filed 7,220 patent applications across 4G, 5G, 6G, cloud-native architecture and AI-driven automation; some 12,477 employees, about 45 per cent of the full-time workforce, work on digital products and technology. Jio holds 284.9 million 5G customers and India's largest spectrum bank at 26,800.8 MHz with an average remaining life of sixteen years.

The risks are the mirror image of the ambition: relentless capital expenditure on networks, spectrum, AI and digital infrastructure; the possibility that rapid technological change strands existing platforms; satellite broadband arriving as a genuinely new competitive vector; and the structural dependence of Jio Platforms on Reliance Jio Infocomm for revenue and on Reliance Retail for prepaid distribution. Spectrum renewals, cyber threats and regulation sit on the list too, as does the sheer weight of expectation embedded in a record anchor book. Disclosed earlier this week, that anchor book drew more than Rs 2.5 lakh crore of institutional bids — a record for an Indian IPO — which suggests the professional money has already weighed those risks and leaned in. But the framing question for retail subscribers is the one the data chart answers in Jio's favour: India went from 0.2 GB to 26.7 GB a month in ten years because this company decided it should. The IPO tests whether 5G, broadband, cloud and AI can turn the audience that decision created into the earnings stream the valuation already assumes.

GLOSSARY: - ARPU: Average revenue per user per month, the standard telecom monetisation metric. - EBITDA margin: Operating profit as a share of revenue before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation. - Fresh issue: An IPO where new shares are created and the company receives the proceeds, rather than existing holders selling. - Spectrum bank: The total holding of frequency rights a telecom operator owns across bands.
