RBI Tightens Funding Norms for Prop Traders: 100% Collateral Rule From 1 July

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/RBI's new collateral norms effective 1 July 2026 require proprietary traders to provide 100% collateral for bank overdrafts, up from 25% cash earlier
  • 02/Weekly Nifty options expiry-day volumes account for nearly two-fifths of weekly trading; BSE Sensex expiry volumes hit almost half
  • 03/FPIs may gain edge using 50% cash plus SBLC from foreign banks; domestic prop traders face leverage loss

The Reserve Bank of India's tightened collateral norms for proprietary traders took effect on 1 July 2026. Prop traders must now provide 100% collateral to access bank funding for stock market trading.

Earlier, traders could obtain overdrafts by pledging just 25% in cash. The remaining balance was covered through shares as collateral. This facility was widely used as trading margin on weekly options expiry days.

Under the new rules, a trader must put up 50% in cash. The remaining 50% must be in specified collateral like government bonds and mutual fund or ETF units. Accessing overdrafts for trading has become significantly harder.

Expiry-day volumes dominate the derivatives market. Nifty options expiry-day volumes this Tuesday accounted for nearly two-fifths of the week's trading volumes. For BSE Sensex weekly options, expiry-day volumes made up almost half of the week.

"With overdraft for prop literally being withdrawn, thanks to full collateral requirement, the weekly options volumes could get impacted," said Ketan Marwadi, MD of Marwadi Share & Finance Pvt Ltd.

Foreign players may exploit the leverage loss for domestic prop traders. FPIs will put up 50% cash and an SBLC issued by a foreign bank to acquire bank credit. "FPIs will put up 50% cash and an SBLC issued by a foreign bank to acquire bank credit, giving them an edge over domestic prop traders," said Rajesh Baheti, director of Crosseas Capital.

The RBI issued the circular on 13 February 2026. It was initially planned for 1 April but deferred to 1 July to give banks time to upgrade systems. The existing bank guarantees will mature over time, so the full impact may not be immediate.

Proprietary traders, through associations including ANMI, BSE Brokers Forum, and CPAI, urged the RBI to withdraw the measures. The pushback was unsuccessful. Options trading contributed 59%-64% of NSE transaction charge revenue during FY24-FY26.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT ARE THE NEW RBI COLLATERAL NORMS FOR PROPRIETARY TRADERS?
EFFECTIVE 1 JULY 2026, PROPRIETARY TRADERS MUST PROVIDE 100% COLLATERAL TO ACCESS BANK OVERDRAFT FACILITIES FOR STOCK MARKET TRADING. THIS INCLUDES 50% IN CASH AND THE REMAINING 50% IN SPECIFIED COLLATERAL LIKE GOVERNMENT BONDS AND MUTUAL FUND OR ETF UNITS.
HOW DID THE OLD RULES DIFFER FROM THE NEW NORMS?
UNDER THE OLD RULES, A PROP TRADER COULD OBTAIN AN OVERDRAFT OF RS.100 BY PLEDGING JUST RS.25 IN CASH. THE REMAINING RS.75 WAS COVERED BY SHARES AS COLLATERAL. NOW, THE FULL 100% MUST BE BACKED BY ELIGIBLE COLLATERAL.
WHICH TRADING VOLUMES WILL BE MOST IMPACTED BY THESE NEW NORMS?
WEEKLY NIFTY AND SENSEX OPTIONS EXPIRY-DAY VOLUMES WILL BE MOST AFFECTED. NIFTY OPTIONS EXPIRY-DAY VOLUMES ACCOUNT FOR NEARLY TWO-FIFTHS OF WEEKLY TRADING VOLUMES. BSE SENSEX EXPIRY-DAY VOLUMES MAKE UP ALMOST HALF OF THE WEEK'S TOTAL.
WHY DO FOREIGN PORTFOLIO INVESTORS HAVE AN ADVANTAGE OVER DOMESTIC PROP TRADERS?
FPIs CAN PUT UP 50% CASH AND USE A STAND-BY LETTER OF CREDIT (SBLC) ISSUED BY A FOREIGN BANK TO ACQUIRE BANK CREDIT. DOMESTIC PROP TRADERS LOSE THE LEVERAGE THEY PREVIOUSLY ENJOYED, GIVING FPIS A CLEAR EDGE.
WHEN DID THE RBI ISSUE THE CIRCULAR AND WHY WAS IMPLEMENTATION DELAYED?
THE RBI ISSUED THE CIRCULAR ON 13 FEBRUARY 2026. IMPLEMENTATION WAS INITIALLY SET FOR 1 APRIL BUT DEFERRED TO 1 JULY 2026 TO GIVE BANKS ADDITIONAL TIME TO UPGRADE THEIR SYSTEMS FOR THE NEW COLLATERAL REQUIREMENTS.
