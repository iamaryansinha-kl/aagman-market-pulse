Mylan to Sell Up to $363 Million Stake in India's Biocon

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

Mylan is cashing out part of its Biocon bet.

The US pharmaceutical giant is planning to sell up to $363 million worth of its stake in India's Biocon, according to a term sheet seen by Reuters, marking a partial exit from the Indian biotech company that the two partnered with to develop and commercialise biosimilars. The sale reflects both Mylan's strategic restructuring and the maturing of the biosimilars partnership, which has produced several successful products but may no longer fit Mylan's evolving business model.

The $363 million stake sale is a significant block trade. Mylan has been one of the largest foreign shareholders in Biocon since the two companies formed a joint venture to develop biosimilars — generic versions of biologic drugs that are used to treat cancer, diabetes, and autoimmune diseases. The partnership has been one of the most successful in the Indian pharmaceutical sector, producing multiple approved biosimilars that compete with blockbuster drugs from companies like Roche, Sanofi, and Eli Lilly.

The timing is linked to Mylan's merger with Pfizer's Upjohn unit. In 2020, Mylan merged with Pfizer's off-patent branded and generic established medicines business to form Viatris, a new company focused on off-patent drugs. The restructuring has involved portfolio rationalisation, cost-cutting, and a strategic review of partnerships and investments that no longer align with the company's core priorities.

Biosimilars are a growing but competitive market. The global biosimilars market is expected to reach $60 billion by 2030, driven by the expiration of patents on major biologic drugs and the pressure on healthcare systems to reduce costs. However, the market is becoming increasingly crowded, with companies from India, South Korea, and Eastern Europe competing aggressively on price. The margin pressure may be reducing the strategic value of the Biocon partnership for Mylan.

Biocon's stock could face pressure from the block sale. A $363 million stake sale, even if executed through a block trade or book-building process, represents a significant increase in the stock's available float. If the sale is not absorbed by institutional buyers, the share price could come under pressure in the near term. However, the long-term impact depends on the buyer — if the stake is picked up by a strategic investor or a long-term fund, it could be neutral or even positive for Biocon.

The partnership's legacy products remain important. The Biocon-Mylan joint venture has commercialised several biosimilars, including trastuzumab (for breast cancer), pegfilgrastim (for neutropenia), and adalimumab (for autoimmune diseases). These products continue to generate revenue for both companies, and the stake sale does not necessarily signal a dissolution of the commercial partnership. Mylan may retain a smaller stake and continue to co-market the products.
