US Waiver to Keep Russian Oil Flows to India Near Record 2.25 Million Bpd

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/The US Treasury extended its sanctions waiver on Russian oil until May 16, 2026, reversing Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's earlier stance
  • 02/India imported a record 2.25 million barrels per day of Russian crude in March 2026, nearly double February's volumes and making up 50% of total imports
  • 03/Russian crude arrivals at Indian ports are set to hit 2.1 million bpd for the week of April 20-27, with May deliveries already secured by refiners

The US Treasury has extended its sanctions waiver on Russian oil until May 16, 2026, via OFAC General License 134B. The extension applies to crude loaded on vessels on or before April 17.

This marks a sharp U-turn. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said on April 16 the waiver would not be renewed. The reversal came after pressure from Asian importers and concerns over global supply stability.

India imported a record 2.25 million barrels per day of Russian crude in March 2026. That is nearly double February's volumes and accounts for roughly 50% of India's total oil imports for the month, per Kpler data.

Russia's share of India's crude imports has surged from just 2% in 2021 to about 47% in March 2026. State-owned refiners increased their Russian intake by 148% month-on-month in March.

India's Russian crude bill tripled to $5.8 billion in March from $1.54 billion in February, according to data from the Centre for Research on Energy and Clean Air (CREA).

Russian crude arrivals at Indian ports are set to reach 2.1 million bpd for the week of April 20 to 27. That is up from 1.67 million bpd the previous week, Kpler data showed.

A brief mid-April dip was likely caused by Ukrainian drone attacks on Russian Baltic ports at the end of March. Supplies are expected to average above 2 million bpd for April and hold near that level in May.

Indian refiners are paying premiums of $7 to $9 per barrel over dated Brent for Russian cargoes delivered in May. That is similar to what they paid for April imports.

India has expanded the pool of Russian insurers eligible for ships docking at its ports from 8 to 11, per the Directorate General of Shipping. This eases maritime logistics.

The waiver helps India offset lost Gulf supplies amid the Strait of Hormuz disruption. Around 2.5 to 2.7 million bpd of India's imports typically transit Hormuz.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has said the waiver could generate up to $10 billion in additional revenue for Moscow. That would directly fund Russia's war effort in Ukraine.

India depends on imports for over 88% of its crude oil needs. The country is the world's third-largest oil consumer and the largest seaborne buyer of Russia's flagship Urals grade.
