Nifty September F&O Series Ends Worst in 25 Years With 6.56% Fall; Shorts at 6-Month High

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Nifty futures closed the September 2026 series down 6.56%, the worst September series in 25 years, SBI Securities data shows
  • 02/Rollover into the October series was below average at 74.29%, while Nifty shorts climbed to a six-month high
  • 03/A seventh straight weekly fall was completed into expiry; an eighth would be the longest losing streak in about 25 years

The Nifty September F&O series has ended as the worst September series in 25 years. Nifty futures closed the series with a loss of 6.56%, according to SBI Securities data.

The series opened in late August at around 24,440 and expired on Tuesday, September 29 at levels below 22,900. That is a drop of well over 1,500 points in a single series.

The September monthly expiry fell on Tuesday, September 29, 2026. NSE monthly contracts expire on the last Tuesday of the month.

Short positioning in Nifty is at a six-month high as the October series begins. Traders have carried that bearish bias into the new series.

Rollover activity into October was 74.29%, below the historical average. Low rollovers signal reluctance to carry positions at current levels.

Positioning was already defensive at the start of the September series. FII index-futures long exposure was just 10.3% in late August, with net short positioning of around 1.84 lakh contracts.

Foreign institutions had piled up roughly 2.5 lakh index shorts early in the series. That short build-up proved prescient as the index slid through September.

The cash market told the same story. FPIs sold Rs 23,676 crore of Indian equities in September through September 19, NSDL data shows.

That is a six-month-high monthly outflow for foreign investors. Domestic institutional buying of Rs 36,219 crore cushioned the fall.

The decline has been relentless. The index has fallen for seven straight weeks into the September 29 expiry.

Nifty traded below 22,700 intraday on September 30. An eighth consecutive weekly decline would be the longest losing streak in about 25 years.

The backdrop has turned hostile for equities. Crude is near $107 a barrel, while the US 10-year yield sits around 5.24%, near a two-decade high.

The rupee is around 96 per dollar. BofA Securities has an outlier forecast of RBI repo-rate hikes totaling 100 basis points through H1 2027.

On September 28, Nifty 50 closed at 22,780.25, down 360.25 points or 1.56%. The India VIX spiked 12.58% to 13.69 that day.

The put-call ratio fell to 0.62, indicating traders were positioned for further downside. Bank Nifty ended September 28 at 54,471.65, down 1,108.75 points or 1.99%.

Bank Nifty traded below 54,000 for the first time since June on September 29-30. September is seasonally one of the softer months for Indian equities.

CONTEXT: The September 2026 series combined an adverse seasonal window with a hostile macro backdrop. Elevated crude, high US yields and rupee weakness pulled foreign money out of Indian equities, and derivatives positioning amplified the slide as shorts accumulated early in the series.

IMPACT: The heavy short positioning cuts both ways for October. Six-month-high Nifty shorts leave the market primed for a sharp short-covering rally if any positive trigger emerges, while the below-average 74.29% rollover shows traders are unwilling to commit at current levels. The next data points to watch are FPI flow numbers, crude near $107 and any shift in the put-call ratio from 0.62.
