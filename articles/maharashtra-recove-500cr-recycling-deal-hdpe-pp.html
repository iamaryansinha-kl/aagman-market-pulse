Maharashtra Signs ₹500 Cr Recycling Deal With Recove Ventures

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Maharashtra Government signed a 10-year MoU with Recove Ventures committing over ₹500 crore for a statewide plastic recycling network on May 7, 2026
  • 02/The first project is a 1,100-tonne-per-month HDPE and PP facility at Jalgaon MIDC with ₹35 crore investment, while India's HDPE/PP recovery remains below 30%
  • 03/Commissioning is targeted for October-December 2026 with 100 direct and 1,500 indirect jobs; EPR norms will push recycled content to ~60% within 2-3 years

The Maharashtra Government signed a 10-year MoU with Recove Ventures Private Ltd on May 7, 2026. The deal commits over ₹500 crore for a large-scale plastic recycling network across the State. It was signed at Mantralaya in Mumbai.

Dr P Anbalagan, Principal Secretary for Industries, Investment and Services, represented the State. Recove co-founders Kunal Kumar and Viral Chhajer signed for the company. Chief Minister Devendra Fadnavis held a subsequent meeting.

The first project is a 1,100-tonne-per-month HDPE and PP recycling facility at Additional Jalgaon MIDC. It sits in a priority D+ industrial zone. The investment for this plant exceeds ₹35 crore.

The plant will use extrusion, granulation and deep-vacuum deodorisation technology. It will produce industrial-grade granules for packaging, automotive and consumer goods. Commissioning is targeted between October and December 2026.

The project will create 100 direct jobs and over 1,500 indirect positions. Recove plans to scale capacity to 5,000 tonne monthly in the first phase. The eventual target is 1,00,000 tonne annually over the partnership's duration.

India recovers roughly 95 per cent of PET waste. However, HDPE and PP recovery remains below 30 per cent. These materials underpin most packaging and automotive components in the country.

Extended producer responsibility norms currently require 30 per cent recycled plastic content. This will rise to around 60 per cent within two to three years. India risks losing recyclables worth $36 billion between 2025 and 2030.

Recove already runs a Pune facility that began test runs in April 2026. Its Bangalore plant processes over 415 tonnes monthly since late 2025. The company reports 12x revenue growth over six months.
