$14 Billion IPO Lock-in Expiry: 91 Companies by December

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Lock-ins in 91 companies expire between October 5 and December 29, 2026, making about $14 billion in shares eligible for release
  • 02/Eligibility is not the same as supply — a sizeable portion is held by promoters, and actual selling depends on individual shareholder decisions
  • 03/The biggest unlocks are CMPDI (464 million shares, 65% of its base, October 6), Turtlemint (160 million, 54%, December 28) and CMR Green Technologies (142 million, 65%, December 9)

Shareholder lock-ins in 91 companies are set to expire between October 5 and December 29, 2026. Shares worth about $14 billion will become eligible for release over this period, according to estimates by Nuvama Alternative & Quantitative Research.

Lock-ins are restrictions that prevent early investors, promoters and other pre-listing shareholders from selling their shares for a fixed period after an IPO. When a lock-in expires, those shareholders regain the right to sell — which is why expiry calendars matter for market participants tracking potential supply.

The headline number requires an important caveat. The $14 billion figure represents the total value of shares whose lock-ins are expiring. It does not mean that the entire pool will hit the market.

A sizeable portion of this pool is held by promoters and promoter groups. Actual supply on Dalal Street will depend on what individual shareholders decide to do once their lock-ins end, company by company.

The Nuvama analysis covers both promoter and non-promoter shareholders. It includes companies listed up to September 30, 2026, giving a broad view of the unlock pipeline for the remainder of the year.

The largest single unlock belongs to Central Mine Planning & Design Institute. A total of 464 million shares, equivalent to 65% of its shares, become eligible on October 6.

Two other significant unlocks open on October 5 itself, the very first day of the window. Amir Chand Jagdish Kumar has 62 million shares, or 60% of its share base, becoming eligible. Sai Parenteral has 21 million shares, or 48% of its base, unlocking the same day.

October also brings a set of smaller unlocks across recently listed companies. Rentomojo has 5 million shares, or 4%, eligible on October 15. Purple Style Labs has 3 million shares, or 3%, eligible on October 5, and SBI Funds Management has 23 million shares, or 1%, eligible on October 16.

November and December add several more names to the calendar. Juniper Green Energy has 12 million shares, or 2%, eligible on November 2. Dhoot Transmission has 5 million shares, or 3%, eligible on November 11, and Milky Mist Dairy Food has 17 million shares, or 2%, eligible on November 12.

The National Stock Exchange of India also appears on the list. NSE of India has 1 million shares, or 0.1% of its share base, becoming eligible on November 20 — a small fraction compared with the larger unlocks in the same window.

December carries some of the heaviest unlocks of the entire period. CMR Green Technologies has 142 million shares, or 65%, eligible on December 9. Hexagon Nutrition has 67 million shares, or 54%, eligible on December 15, and Turtlemint Fintech Solutions has 160 million shares, or 54%, eligible on December 28.

For investors, these dates mark windows when selling becomes possible, not necessarily probable. Lock-in expiry calendars are best read as supply risk schedules — the actual flow will be decided by promoters and early shareholders, name by name.
