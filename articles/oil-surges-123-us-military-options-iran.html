Oil Surges to $123.30 on Report US Eyes Military Strikes on Iran

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Brent crude rises $5.27 to $123.30 per barrel, up 4.5%, after gaining 6.1% in the previous session
  • 02/US President Trump is reportedly reviewing military strike plans on Iran to break nuclear negotiation deadlock
  • 03/Strait of Hormuz remains closed; OPEC+ expected to approve 188,000 bpd output hike on Sunday

Brent crude futures for June rose $5.27, or 4.5%, to $123.30 a barrel. WTI June futures gained $2.42, or 2.3%, to $109.30.

Both benchmarks are heading for their fourth consecutive month of gains. Brent has climbed roughly 75% since the US-Israel air strikes on Iran began February 28.

The surge follows an Axios report that President Trump is set to receive a briefing on military strike plans against Iran. The goal is to force Tehran back to nuclear negotiations.

Iran closed the Strait of Hormuz after the February 28 strikes. The chokepoint carries about 20% of global oil supplies. Traffic has fallen to roughly 10% of normal levels.

"The oil market has moved from over-optimism to the reality of the supply disruption," ING analysts said. "Prospects for any near-term resolution remain dim," added IG market analyst Tony Sycamore.

Trump reportedly spoke with oil companies about managing a months-long US blockade of Iranian ports. The White House has signaled pump price relief is unlikely soon.

OPEC+ is expected to raise output quotas by around 188,000 barrels per day on Sunday. The UAE exits OPEC on May 1, though analysts say Gulf producers will need months to restore pre-war volumes.
