FPIs Pull Out Rs 48,056 Crore From Equities in October So Far

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Foreign portfolio investors have withdrawn Rs 48,056 crore from Indian equities in October so far, CDSL data shows.
  • •- The October outflow follows Rs 35,861 crore of net selling in September; the last two weeks are a sharp reversal from July's Rs 20,200 crore inflow and August's Rs 29,631 crore inflow.
  • •- Cumulative FPI selling in 2026 now stands at Rs 3.08 lakh crore — nearly double the Rs 1.66 lakh crore withdrawn in all of 2025.
  • •- One softening signal: FPIs moderated selling sharply in the week ended October 23 and turned net buyers on two trading days.

The number that best explains India's autumn market is not the Nifty's level or the rupee's slide — it is the running total on foreign selling. Foreign portfolio investors withdrew another Rs 48,056 crore from Indian equities in October so far, according to depository data, extending a withdrawal that has now run for the better part of two months. Add that to September's Rs 35,861 crore and the arithmetic turns stark: a market that attracted Rs 20,200 crore in July and Rs 29,631 crore in August has given it all back and more. Cumulative FPI outflows from Indian equities in 2026 have reached Rs 3.08 lakh crore — nearly double the Rs 1.66 lakh crore pulled out in the whole of 2025, with two months still left on the calendar. On the current trajectory, 2026 will be remembered as the heaviest year of foreign withdrawal in the market's history — a fact that frames every other market story of the season, from the ten-week Nifty slide to the record IPO pipeline that domestic money is funding in foreign investors' absence.

The causes are the ones this column has tracked all month, and they compound rather than alternate. Crude near $114 a barrel — elevated by Middle East tensions — is the largest single driver, because it worsens India's terms of trade, keeps the inflation outlook sticky and gives the RBI every reason to stay calibrated-tight. Weak corporate earnings relative to expectations, uncertainty over domestic liquidity, and a US 10-year yield that has spent the autumn near 5% all raise the relative appeal of dollar assets. In debt, foreign investors remained net sellers in September through the Fully Accessible Route (Rs 3,858 crore withdrawn) and the Voluntary Retention Route (Rs 1,332 crore), partly offset by Rs 3,737 crore through the general route — a picture of selective, risk-managed retreat rather than a blanket exit. One domestic amplifier deserves note: the Rs 30,213-crore Jio Platforms offering absorbed a meaningful slug of market liquidity in October, and strategists say some of the FPI selling reflects rotation out of existing holdings to fund IPO allocations — selling that is mechanical rather than a verdict on India.

The more interesting development is the most recent one. In the week ended October 23, FPI selling moderated sharply and foreign investors turned net buyers on two trading days. The catalysts cited by market participants are the easing in the US 10-year yield from 5.36% to around 5%, the rupee's recovery to 94.94 against the dollar, and valuations in large-cap stocks that have begun to look, in the words of one strategist, "attractive" after a 15%-plus correction from the highs. October has also delivered Q2 earnings from Shriram Finance, AU Small Finance Bank, Kotak Mahindra Bank and IDFC First Bank that comfortably beat expectations — the operating performance that a falling market has been ignoring. None of this guarantees the selling is over; the same strategists who flagged the moderation noted that elevated crude and broad market weakness still pose downside risks. But a two-week trend in flows is how inflections start, and the market has been searching for one.

There is a structural reading beneath the flow data that cuts both ways. Foreign ownership of Indian equities is already at multi-year lows, and domestic investors — through SIPs and institutional buying — now carry a larger share of the market than at any point in the past decade; October's steady DII absorption of FPI supply is the visible evidence. The bearish interpretation is that when the marginal price-setter leaves, the market loses its most valuation-sensitive buyer. The bullish one is that a pool of foreign capital at multi-year-low exposure has, by definition, less left to sell — and that flows through IPOs suggest foreign money is reallocating within India rather than leaving it. The debt-market numbers from September, where selling was selective across routes, support the rebalancing-over-exit reading. Both can be true simultaneously, which is why the next fortnight matters more than the last one: the US Federal Reserve's FOMC decision, the Bank of Japan's policy call and US GDP data all land in the coming week, and each has the potential to move the global rate calculus that has been driving these outflows. Until then, the tape belongs to whoever is still buying — and lately, in India, that has been the domestic bid.

Neither this column nor the flow data can time a turn. What the data does establish is the setup: the heaviest annual foreign withdrawal on record, occurring against a domestic bid that has absorbed most of it, at valuations that have corrected sharply from the highs, with earnings from the country's lenders quietly beating expectations and a global rate calendar that could cut either way in the coming week. The October number is bad; the trajectory within it is less bad. Markets bottom when the second derivative changes before the first, and the moderation in the week to October 23 is the first honest second-derivative signal the flow data has produced since August. It deserves to be watched, not celebrated — but it deserves to be watched.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
HOW MUCH HAVE FPIS SOLD IN OCTOBER?
Rs 48,056 crore net from Indian equities so far in October, per CDSL data, after Rs 35,861 crore of net selling in September.
WHAT IS DRIVING THE OUTFLOWS?
Crude near $114 a barrel amid Middle East tensions, sticky inflation, a US 10-year yield near 5%, weak relative earnings, and liquidity absorbed by the Rs 30,213-crore Jio Platforms IPO.
IS SELLING EASING?
Yes — FPI selling moderated sharply in the week ended October 23, with net buying on two trading days, helped by the US yield easing to around 5%, the rupee recovering to 94.94 and cheaper large-cap valuations. GLOSSARY: FPI — Foreign Portfolio Investor; institutional investors holding Indian equities and debt without controlling stakes, whose flows heavily influence market direction. CDSL — Central Depository Services Ltd, one of India's two securities depositories and a standard source for FPI flow data. FOMC — The Federal Open Market Committee of the US Federal Reserve, whose rate decisions set the global discount rate for risk assets. SIP — Systematic Investment Plan; the recurring monthly mutual-fund investments that form India's domestic liquidity backstop.
