Swiggy Crashes 13.8% to 52-Week Low, Five Brokers Cut Targets

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Swiggy shares fell 13.8% to a 52-week low of Rs 182.20 after its Q2 loss widened sequentially to Rs 922 crore.
  • •- Jefferies cut its target to Rs 330, Nomura to Rs 300 from Rs 435, Bernstein to Rs 350 from Rs 430 and Motilal Oswal to Rs 280.
  • •- The common complaint: quick-commerce losses remain the key investor concern, with Instamart's turnaround timeline slipping.
  • •- Elara was the outlier, upgrading to Buy but cutting its target to Rs 300 — arguing the market now values Instamart at zero.

The market has delivered its verdict on Swiggy's September quarter, and it is brutal. Shares of the food delivery and quick-commerce company tanked 13.8% to a 52-week low of Rs 182.20 on the BSE on Friday, taking the stock's decline over the past three months to roughly 22%. The trigger was a familiar pattern in the results: the consolidated net loss of Rs 922 crore was an improvement on the Rs 1,092 crore loss a year earlier, but it widened sequentially from Rs 792 crore in the June quarter — and the adjusted EBITDA loss of Rs 760 crore, against Rs 695 crore a year ago, landed at a three-quarter high.

Revenue growth was never the problem. Revenue from operations rose 36% year-on-year to Rs 7,571 crore, and Instamart's gross order value grew 31% to Rs 9,230 crore. What investors are punishing is the shape of the loss: management's own assertion that quick-commerce losses peaked in the September quarter did not survive contact with the adjusted EBITDA print, and the brokerage community moved in force. Five houses cut target prices on Friday, and the spread of their conclusions tells the story of a stock being repriced around a single question — when, exactly, does Instamart stop losing money?

Jefferies cut its sum-of-the-parts target to Rs 330, noting that adjusted EBITDA losses hit a three-quarter high and raising its loss estimates across the forecast period while lowering the valuation multiple on the food delivery business. Its verdict: the current share price does not fully reflect the value of the food delivery franchise and the company's cash, but performance remains lacklustre against market leader Eternal, and reducing quick-commerce losses is essential for any value discovery. Nomura, which retains a Buy, slashed its target to Rs 300 from Rs 435, arguing that Eternal's signal that it will defend margins and reinvest in growth constrains Swiggy's scope for margin expansion; it cut its FY28 adjusted EBITDA margin estimate by 40 basis points to 4%.

Bernstein stayed at Outperform but trimmed its target to Rs 350 from Rs 430, homing in on the growth gap: Swiggy's core food delivery GOV grew around 18% year-on-year against 21.5% for Zomato, a gap Bernstein attributes largely to self-cannibalisation from the Toing discounting venture, which it treats as a separate loss-making business funded by food delivery cash flows. The contrast with Blinkit's performance, it added, is testing investor patience with Instamart's turnaround. Motilal Oswal was the harshest, cutting its target to Rs 280 and stating that Instamart currently carries negative value in its DCF model; it cut its FY26-30 net order value CAGR assumption from 38% to 32%, citing competitive intensity that will delay operating leverage and extend the loss period.

The backdrop is a quick-commerce war with no visible truce. Eternal — the renamed Zomato parent — has signalled it will defend margins even as it reinvests for growth, a combination that squeezes Swiggy from both directions: it limits the price increases Swiggy can take in food delivery while keeping the discounting pressure on in quick commerce. Blinkit, Eternal's quick-commerce arm, has been outgrowing Instamart, and every quarter that gap persists, the market assigns Instamart less credit for its Rs 9,230 crore of quarterly GOV. That is the trap Swiggy's results laid bare: food delivery is generating record adjusted EBITDA — Rs 341 crore in the quarter — but all of it, and more, is being consumed by the quick-commerce buildout.

The most interesting call came from Elara Capital, which upgraded the stock to Buy from Accumulate even as it cut its target to Rs 300 from Rs 350 — on the logic that the 22% three-month slide has already stripped Instamart's valuation to zero. By Elara's math, the value of the core food delivery business combined with net cash fully accounts for the current price, meaning investors are paying nothing for a quick-commerce unit doing Rs 9,230 crore of quarterly GOV. That is either a compelling asymmetry or a warning about how far the market's patience has eroded; Friday's 13.8% decline suggests the market currently leans toward the latter. The recovery path runs through the December quarter: management has said Instamart losses peaked in September, so the next print must show the adjusted EBITDA loss narrowing from the Rs 895 crore level the quick-commerce unit reported, dark-store additions moderating from the 69 net adds of the September quarter, and food delivery GOV growth recovering toward the top of the 18-20% guidance band. Until then, every brokerage note will keep asking the same question — what exactly are shareholders paying for — and the 52-week low suggests they have yet to hear an answer they believe. The next catalyst arrives soon enough: the December-quarter print will be measured entirely against the September-quarter trough, and the stock's bounce potential rests on management's peak-loss claim holding for even one more quarter.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHY DID SWIGGY SHARES FALL ON FRIDAY?
The stock fell 13.8% to a 52-week low of Rs 182.20 after its Q2 adjusted EBITDA loss widened to a three-quarter high of Rs 760 crore, and five brokerages cut their target prices on concerns about quick-commerce losses.
WHAT ARE THE NEW TARGET PRICES?
Jefferies Rs 330, Nomura Rs 300 (from Rs 435), Bernstein Rs 350 (from Rs 430), Motilal Oswal Rs 280, and Elara Rs 300 (from Rs 350) with an upgrade to Buy.
WHAT IS THE CORE DISPUTE AMONG ANALYSTS?
Timing of Instamart's profitability. Bears see competitive intensity from Eternal and Blinkit extending losses; Elara argues the market already values Instamart at zero, leaving food delivery and cash as the floor. GLOSSARY: SUM-OF-THE-PARTS VALUATION — Valuing each business line separately (food delivery, Instamart, cash) and adding them up; analysts use it to argue which segment the share price is actually paying for. GOV (GROSS ORDER VALUE) — The total value of orders placed on a platform before discounts and cancellations; the standard scale metric for food delivery and quick commerce. DCF (DISCOUNTED CASH FLOW) — A valuation model built on forecast future cash flows; assigning negative value to a unit means its projected losses exceed its projected cash generation.
