Aluminium Prices Near Record High: LME Hits $3,651/T, Up 48% YoY

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/LME aluminium hit $3,650.90/T on May 22, 2026, just 11% below the March 2022 record of $4,103/T
  • 02/Prices are up 47.67% year-over-year, driven by China’s 45-million-tonne capacity cap and surging EV demand
  • 03/Record date for any specific events tied to this topic should be watched closely; the rally may face pressure from Indonesian capacity additions later in 2026

Aluminium prices surged to $3,650.90 per tonne on May 22, 2026. The metal is now just 11% below its all-time high of $4,103 hit in March 2022.

Prices have risen 47.67% over the past year. The rally is tracking supply constraints and resilient industrial demand globally.

China produces roughly 60% of world aluminium. Its government-enforced 45-million-tonne annual production ceiling has removed 2-3 Mt of potential supply from the market.

US tariffs on aluminium imports rose to 25% for all countries and 200% for Russian metal in 2025. This pushed the US Midwest Premium to a record $0.7323 per pound.

Electric vehicles now use 180-200 kg of aluminium per unit. That is 25-40% more than conventional internal-combustion vehicles. Solar power projects consume about 3.5 tonnes of aluminium per installed megawatt.

LME warehouse inventories fell to 505,275 tonnes on January 26, 2026. Low stock levels amplify price volatility whenever spot demand spikes.

European smelters remain pressured by elevated electricity and natural gas costs. Restarts have been slow, keeping regional supply tight and supporting global premiums.

Indonesian smelter expansions of 1.8-2.2 million tonnes are expected online by late 2026. This new capacity could ease supply shortages and cap further price surges.

Analyst forecasts for 2026 average LME prices range between $2,700 and $3,000 per tonne. The bull case sees tests above $3,200 if China restarts stay limited and EV adoption accelerates.

India’s MCX aluminium futures crossed ₹320 per kg in early 2026. Domestic premiums remain elevated on strong power, construction, and automotive demand.

The EU’s Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism took effect in 2026. It adds carbon compliance costs of €15-25 per tonne for high-carbon producers, reshaping trade flows.
