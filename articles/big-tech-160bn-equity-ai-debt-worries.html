Big Tech Raises $160B Equity as AI Debt Binge Worries Grow

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Big Tech firms raised roughly $160 billion in equity this month, led by Alphabet Inc's $85 billion sale and SpaceX's $75 billion IPO
  • 02/JPMorgan Chase now forecasts $5.5 trillion in AI and data center spending through 2030, up $400 billion from prior estimates
  • 03/Risk premiums on US high-grade tech bonds climbed to 0.79 percentage point in June from 0.74 pp at end of May

Alphabet Inc (NASDAQ: GOOGL) sold $85 billion in stock this month. SpaceX priced a record $75 billion initial public offering. The deals signal tech giants are gearing up for heavier AI spending.

Nvidia Corp (NASDAQ: NVDA) sold $25 billion in bonds, joining Alphabet and Amazon in flooding debt markets. JPMorgan Chase sees $2.1 trillion in data center bond financing over five years.

"It's telling us that the amount of capital expenditure that they're going to do is probably going to go up," said Tom Murphy, head of investment grade credit at Columbia Threadneedle.

JPMorgan Chase now expects $5.5 trillion in AI and data center spending through 2030. That is $400 billion higher than its November forecast.

Risk premiums on US high-grade tech bonds widened to 0.79 percentage point in June. SpaceX's $25 billion bond issue shed roughly $360 million in paper losses relative to Treasuries.

S&P Global expects SpaceX to burn through $113 billion by end of 2027. The company holds $100.8 billion in cash on its books.

Epoch AI estimates aggregate free cash flow across major AI builders crosses zero around Q3 2026. Capex is growing at 70% annually versus 23% for operating cash flow.

Amazon.com Inc (NASDAQ: AMZN) spent $44 billion in Q1 capex against $26 billion in operating cash flow. Meta Platforms (NASDAQ: META) is weighing a share sale to fund $125-$145 billion in 2026 capex.
