Sensex Crashes 1,400 Points From Peak, Rs 8 Lakh Crore Wiped Out

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- The Sensex fell about 1,400 points from its intraday high of 74,682 to 73,258; the Nifty dropped over 450 points to below 22,700.
  • •- More than Rs 8 lakh crore of market capitalisation was erased, pulling the BSE total below Rs 460 lakh crore.
  • •- Brent crude crossed $110 a barrel on Middle East supply concerns, including a mine strike on a supertanker near the Strait of Hormuz.
  • •- US bond yields rose, with the 10-year Treasury at 5.12% and the 30-year above 5.4%.
  • •- FIIs have been net sellers in 10 of 12 sessions this month, offloading Rs 1,149 crore on Monday alone.
  • •- IT stocks bucked the fall, rising up to 3%, alongside UltraTech Cement.

The morning's rally was borrowed time, and the afternoon collected. Indian equities opened Wednesday with genuine momentum — the Sensex up nearly 1% at an intraday high of 74,682 and the Nifty at 23,121 — and then surrendered everything in a relentless afternoon slide. By the close of the selling, the Sensex had dropped around 1,400 points from its peak to 73,258 and the Nifty had given up more than 450 points to trade below 22,700, nearly 2% below the day's highs. The reversal erased over Rs 8 lakh crore in market capitalisation, dragging the BSE's total below Rs 460 lakh crore, and the damage was broad: 3,033 stocks declined on the NSE against just 496 advancers.

For a market that began the day celebrating a broad-based rebound, the speed of the give-back was the story in itself; a 1% gap-up and a two-percent round trip in the same session is the signature of a market where positioning, not conviction, sets the opening price.

Three forces did the pushing, and all three have been visible for days. First, oil. Brent crossed $110 a barrel again on Wednesday, up more than a dollar, with WTI near $99, as Middle East supply risks compounded: a supertanker caught fire after striking a sea mine near the Strait of Hormuz, according to Iran's Revolutionary Guards Navy, and shiptracker Kpler counted at least 18 vessel attacks over two weeks. India imports nearly 90% of its crude, so every escalation feeds directly into inflation expectations, the trade deficit and rate policy — the benchmark domestic bond yield had already risen to its highest since March in Wednesday morning's session. Second, US yields. The American 10-year Treasury climbed to 5.12% and the 30-year pushed above 5.4%, making risk assets globally less attractive and tightening the screws on emerging-market equities. Third, persistent foreign selling. FIIs were net buyers of Indian equities only once this month; they have sold in 10 of 12 October sessions, offloading Rs 1,149 crore on Monday alone, and Wednesday's reversal gave them every reason to continue.

The internals told a rotation story. Eternal, down 5% ahead of its results, led the Sensex lower; Bajaj Finance fell 4%, extending its post-earnings slide, while Maruti Suzuki, Trent, NTPC, BEL, InterGlobe Aviation, Axis Bank, Bajaj Finserv, M&M, Tata Steel, Adani Ports and Kotak Mahindra Bank dropped as much as 3%. The carnage hit the broader market harder than the benchmarks — Nifty Midcap 100 and Smallcap 100 indices crashed as much as 3%, and auto, metal and consumer durables sectors all lost over 2%. Against that, IT was the day's haven: Infosys, Tech Mahindra, TCS and HCL Technologies rose as much as 3%, and UltraTech Cement added 2% after its morning's earnings beat — the classic defensive bid for exporters when oil rises and domestic demand comes into question.

The strategist consensus is resigned rather than panicked. "With Brent crude prices and US bond yields remaining elevated, the market is likely to remain under pressure. The sell-on-rally market construct will continue in the near-term," said Vijayakumar of Geojit Investments, adding that improving earnings growth, fair large-cap valuations and strong domestic liquidity should support declines. He expects action to turn stock-specific as the Q2 season progresses — a view the day's cross-currents already illustrate, with results saving UltraTech and punishing Bajaj Finance while the index sank around them. Technically, Geojit's Anand James holds hope for an Nifty resumption above 22,840, but a fall past 22,700 would, in his framing, abandon the recovery thesis entirely.

The domestic-versus-foreign flow split remains the market's structural support. Retail and domestic institutional buying has repeatedly absorbed FII selling this year, which is why the index has held a range even through ten sessions of foreign outflows; Wednesday's breadth — six decliners for every advancer — shows what that support looks like when it steps back. Whether it returns at lower levels, as Geojit expects, is the near-term question that the October FII streak has made the market's defining test.

Wednesday's session is best read as the equity market catching up to a repricing that bonds and oil had already made. The morning rally was built on domestic liquidity and earnings optimism; the afternoon reversal priced the external ledger — crude at five-month highs, American yields at cycle highs, foreign money leaving for ten straight sessions. None of those three forces resolves quickly: oil now trades on Hormuz headlines, US yields on their own fiscal arithmetic, and FII flows on the relative risk premium of India versus an 8% domestic bond yield. The market's own verdict, per Geojit, is a range under pressure until one of those pivots — with rallies to be sold, dips to be bought selectively, and the index direction hostage to the strait through which a fifth of the world's oil sails.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
HOW SHARP WAS WEDNESDAY'S MARKET FALL?
The Sensex fell about 1,400 points from its intraday high to 73,258, and the Nifty dropped over 450 points below 22,700 — nearly 2% below their peaks, wiping out over Rs 8 lakh crore of market value.
WHAT CAUSED THE SELLOFF?
Brent crude crossing $110 on Middle East supply risks, rising US bond yields, and persistent foreign institutional selling — FIIs have been net sellers in 10 of 12 October sessions.
WHICH STOCKS HELD UP?
IT stocks — Infosys, Tech Mahindra, TCS and HCL Technologies — rose up to 3%, and UltraTech Cement gained 2% after its quarterly results beat estimates.
WHAT ARE THE KEY LEVELS TO WATCH?
Analysts see Nifty support around 22,700; a fall below that level would weaken the case for the index's recovery resuming. GLOSSARY: - FII: Foreign Institutional Investor — overseas funds investing in Indian markets. - Strait of Hormuz: The chokepoint between Iran and Oman through which a fifth of global oil supply transits. - Sell-on-rally: A market pattern where investors use price rises to exit positions rather than add to them. - Breadth: The ratio of advancing stocks to declining ones, a gauge of market internals.
