DMart Q2 Results: Profit Up 8.5% But Margins Shrink, Stock Falls 3.3%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Avenue Supermarts reported an 8.5% YoY rise in consolidated net profit to Rs 743 crore for Q2FY27, on revenue of Rs 19,644 crore, up 17.8%
  • 02/EBITDA margin contracted to 7.1% from 7.3% and the stock fell as much as 3.3% to a low of Rs 3,405 on the BSE
  • 03/Motilal Oswal stays Neutral with a Rs 3,925 target; Nuvama cut its target to Rs 3,784 while maintaining a Hold rating

Avenue Supermarts Ltd (NSE: DMART), the operator of DMart stores promoted by investor Radhakishan Damani, saw its shares fall as much as 3.3% on Monday. The stock touched a day's low of Rs 3,405 on the BSE after the company reported its Q2FY27 numbers.

The market verdict looks harsh at first glance. Consolidated net profit rose 8.5% year-on-year to Rs 743 crore for the quarter ended September 30, 2026. In the same quarter last year, profit stood at Rs 685 crore.

Revenue growth was even stronger. Consolidated total revenue came in at Rs 19,644 crore for the quarter, up 17.8% from Rs 16,676 crore in Q2FY26.

So why did the stock fall? Margins. EBITDA margin declined to 7.1% in Q2FY27 from 7.3% in the year-ago period, a contraction of 0.2 percentage points.

Profitability at the bottom line told the same story. PAT margin fell to 3.8% from 4.1%, down 0.3 percentage points, meaning earnings growth of 8.5% badly lagged revenue growth of 17.8%.

The pressure, according to brokerages, is coming from quick commerce. Motilal Oswal, which has a Neutral rating on the stock with a target price of Rs 3,925, flagged the discounting intensity in dense metro catchments.

"While the QC dent remains concentrated in dense metro catchments for now, we believe overall discounting and customer acquisition intensity are unlikely to moderate in the near term and could weigh on DMART's growth and margins over the medium term," Motilal Oswal said in its note.

That Rs 3,925 target still implies roughly 12% upside from current market levels. But the brokerage is clearly not betting on margin relief anytime soon.

Nuvama Institutional Equities was more direct with the scissors. It cut its target price to Rs 3,784 from Rs 4,174 while maintaining a Hold rating on the stock.

Nuvama said the EBITDA margin contracted 28 basis points year-on-year. It attributed this to higher wage inflation and other expenses, reversing the margin improvement DMart had delivered over the past three quarters.

The brokerage also noted that working capital metrics remained elevated. It linked this to festive stocking by the company during the quarter.

Interestingly, Nuvama did not slash its growth forecasts. It revised FY27E and FY28E revenue estimates up by 0.3% and 0.7% respectively, while cutting PAT estimates by 5.7% and 9.8% respectively.

The message from that split is clear: the top line is holding up, but the market is pricing in weaker profitability for longer.

The online business remained a drag. DMart Ready, the company's e-commerce arm, saw revenue decline 4% during the quarter. The segment posted losses of Rs 60.6 crore.

On the ground, expansion continued at a steady clip. DMart added 18 stores in H1FY27, taking its total store network to 518 outlets.

Investors will now watch whether margin pressure stabilises in the second half. The quick-commerce discounting war in metro catchments, wage inflation, and the path to profitability at DMart Ready are the three variables to track.

For now, the brokerage community is hedged. A Neutral here, a Hold there, and target prices that still sit above the market but well below where they used to be.

The results land at the sharpest edge of India's retail story right now: traditional value retail is still growing volumes and revenue, but quick-commerce rivals are spending aggressively on discounts and customer acquisition in exactly the dense metro catchments where DMart's stores are strongest. That is the "QC dent" Motilal Oswal flags — a profitability tax imposed by competition rather than by weak demand.

The margin math shows where it bites. An EBITDA margin of 7.1 per cent leaves little room: grocery retail is a high-volume, thin-margin business at the best of times, and a 20-basis-point contraction reverses the gradual improvement DMart had strung together over the past three quarters. Nuvama's diagnosis points to wage inflation and other expenses as the proximate causes, and its estimate revisions capture the read-across — revenues nudged up, profit forecasts cut by 5.7 per cent this fiscal year and 9.8 per cent next.

The online business remains a work in progress. DMart Ready's revenue actually shrank 4 per cent in the quarter while losing Rs 60.6 crore — a reminder that DMart is building its e-commerce arm against rivals who treat losses as the price of market share.

The store roll-out continues regardless: 18 new stores in the first half of the fiscal year takes the network to 518. DMart's long game has always been network expansion at the cost of near-term optics, and the quarter's numbers will test whether investors stay patient while the quick-commerce bill keeps arriving.
