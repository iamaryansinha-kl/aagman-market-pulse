Adani Enterprises Launches $1.05 Billion QIP, Second Tranche of ₹16,600 Crore Fundraise

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Adani Enterprises launches QIP to raise at least ₹10,000 crore ($1.05 billion) from institutional investors
  • 02/Issue priced at ₹2,883/share, 9.27% discount to closing price; 34.7 million new shares
  • 03/Second tranche of ₹16,600 crore programme; first tranche raised ₹4,200 crore in October 2024

Gautam Adani-led Adani Enterprises is returning to the equity market with a qualified institutional placement. The company aims to raise at least ₹10,000 crore, or $1.05 billion, from institutional investors.

The QIP comes less than three months after completing a ₹25,000 crore rights issue in March 2026. Since promoters own 74%, the Adani family invested about ₹18,500 crore in that rights issue.

Jefferies, SBI Capital Markets, ICICI Securities, and IIFL Securities are acting as the book-running lead managers for the QIP.

Unlike a rights issue, which is offered to existing shareholders in proportion to their holdings, a QIP is open only to qualified institutional investors. This will broaden the company's institutional investor base.

As of March 2026, domestic institutional investors held a 6.65% stake in Adani Enterprises. Foreign institutional investors held 13.91%.

An executive privy to the development said the company saw strong demand from both FIIs and DIIs, especially mutual funds, after completing its rights issue. "Until now, there was a view that mutual funds may not be long-term investors. But we see that changing," the executive said.

Adani Enterprises has announced a ₹35,000 crore capex plan for FY27. The bulk of this will help scale up data centre and airport expansion plans. The QIP money will fund the Group's capex rather than pare debt, since deleveraging was done through the rights issue.

Through subsidiary Adani Airport Holdings, the company is investing more than ₹20,000 crore to develop integrated airport cities across six airports. These include Mumbai, Navi Mumbai, Ahmedabad, Lucknow, Jaipur, and Guwahati.

The Ahmedabad-headquartered conglomerate has committed $100 billion to develop 5GW of green-energy-powered, hyperscale, AI-ready data centres by 2035. It recently signed an agreement with Jabil Inc. to build AI data centre infrastructure.

Jay Agarwal, analyst-advisory at PL Capital, said the Adani Group will have to raise funds from multiple sources to execute a capital expenditure programme of this scale. "If Adani is accounting for 38% of India's total capex, continued fundraising through instruments such as rights issues and QIPs is a natural part of its financing strategy," he said.

The QIP marks the second tranche of the flagship company's ₹16,600 crore equity fundraising programme approved by its board in May 2024. It had raised ₹4,200 crore in the first tranche in October 2024.

On Wednesday, Adani Enterprises informed the exchanges that it will issue shares at ₹2,883 a share under the QIP process. The base price is 9.27% lower than the stock's closing price of ₹3,177.50 on July 2. It is also 5% below the Sebi-prescribed floor price of ₹3,034.68 per share.

Shareholders approved the QIP on June 24. The company's QIP committee approved the opening of the issue, the preliminary placement document, and the floor price.

The fundraising comes after the Adani Group settled its key legal cases in the US. This includes a $275-million settlement in May between Adani Enterprises and the US Treasury over the purchase of sanctioned Iranian gas. Gautam Adani and his nephew Sagar Adani also settled with the US SEC and Justice Department over bribery allegations.
