Jet Fuel Prices Surge 84% as Iran War Threatens Airlines' Summer Season

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Jet fuel prices have surged 84% since the Iran war began on February 28, threatening airline profitability across Europe and Asia
  • 02/Middle Eastern airline flights dropped 50% YoY in March; Gulf hub bookings for Q2-Q3 are down 42.5%
  • 03/IATA head Willie Walsh warns of potential fuel rationing in Asia and Europe as airline hedges expire

European airlines face their biggest challenge since COVID-19. The Iran war has pushed jet fuel prices up 84% since February 28.

Carriers have used hedging strategies to tame costs so far. But these hedges are running out as the conflict drags on.

Willie Walsh, head of the International Air Transport Association, warned of potential fuel rationing in Asia and Europe. "There is a risk that we'll see rationing of fuel supply," he told Reuters.

Middle Eastern carriers are hardest hit. Cirium Ascend data shows their flights dropped 50% year-on-year in March.

Bookings for Q2 and Q3 via Gulf hubs fell 42.5%. Yet global passenger capacity remains up 2% versus 2025.

Wizz Air CEO Jozsef Varadi said summer bookings remain strong. But easyJet and TUI issued profit warnings amid weaker forward demand.

Air France-KLM, IAG and Lufthansa have raised prices and cut capacity. Finnair, however, reported a net positive impact on Asian routes.

"Even if the war is stopped in Iran, I don't think this is going to put the fuel price back," Varadi told reporters in London.
