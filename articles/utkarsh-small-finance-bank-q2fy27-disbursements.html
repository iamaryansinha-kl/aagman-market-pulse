Utkarsh Small Finance Bank Q2: Disbursements Up 55% YoY to ₹3,525 Cr

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Utkarsh Small Finance Bank (NSE: UTKARSHBNK) shares jumped 7% to ₹14.47 on the BSE after its Q2FY27 provisional update showed total disbursements of ₹3,525 crore, up 55% YoY
  • 02/Non-JLG disbursements nearly doubled, rising 94% YoY to ₹2,602 crore, while JLG microfinance loans slipped 1% to ₹923 crore — a deliberate pivot away from group lending
  • 03/Watch funding costs: the RBI raised the repo rate 25 bps to 5.50% on the same day, testing whether the bank's improving CASA ratio can keep its cost of funds competitive

Utkarsh Small Finance Bank Ltd (NSE: UTKARSHBNK) shares rallied 7% to ₹14.47 on the BSE on Wednesday. The move followed the bank's provisional business update for Q2FY27, released after market hours on Tuesday.

Total disbursements for the July-September quarter stood at ₹3,525 crore. That is a 55% surge from ₹2,275 crore in Q2FY26, and a further 5% higher than the ₹3,370 crore disbursed in Q1FY27.

The growth engine is non-JLG lending, which jumped 94% YoY to ₹2,602 crore. JLG loans — group borrowings by 4-10 individuals, the backbone of microfinance — slipped about 1% YoY to ₹923 crore.

That mix shift is deliberate. Utkarsh is shrinking its reliance on microfinance and pushing into MSME, housing, vehicle, gold, and construction-equipment lending, where ticket sizes are larger and asset quality behaves differently from group-lending cycles.

Deposits grew a slower 7% YoY to ₹23,869 crore. But the quality improved: CASA deposits rose 14% to ₹5,121 crore and retail term deposits grew 12% to ₹14,442 crore, while wholesale bulk term deposits were cut 13.5% to ₹4,307 crore.

The CASA ratio stood at 21.5% in Q2FY27, against 20% a year earlier and 21.1% in Q1FY27. CASA plus retail term deposits now make up 82% of the deposit book, versus 77.8% in Q2FY26.

The timing makes the funding question immediate. The RBI hiked the repo rate by 25 bps to 5.50% on Wednesday, raising funding costs across small finance banks — precisely the environment where cheap, sticky CASA matters most.

Small finance banks already run a structurally higher cost of funds than universal banks. In a tightening cycle, term deposits reprice faster than the loan book, so net interest margins can compress even as disbursement volumes surge.

The rally needs context too. The stock is still down 28% over one year, 73% over three years, and 8% in 2026, with a market capitalisation of roughly ₹2,406 crore. The 52-week range of roughly ₹10 to ₹22 shows how far the stock has fallen from its post-listing highs.

The disbursement momentum is real, and the deposit franchise is clearly improving. The next test is the margin maths: whether a 21.5% CASA ratio can absorb a 5.50% repo rate while the loan book moves into newer, less-seasoned segments.

THE KEY NUMBER TO TRACK IS WHETHER THE 21.5% CASA RATIO KEEPS RISING EVEN AS RATES CLIMB. GLOSSARY: Small Finance Bank (SFB): A niche bank licensed by the RBI to focus on underserved segments — small borrowers, micro-enterprises, and depositors — while allowed to operate as a full bank. Utkarsh began as a microfinance institution. JLG (Joint Liability Group) Loans: Group lending where 4-10 individuals borrow together and guarantee each other's repayment. It is the classic microfinance model, and Utkarsh's JLG book shrank 1% YoY as it pivots away. Non-JLG Loans: Lending outside the group model — individual business, housing, vehicle, and gold loans. This book surged 94% YoY to Rs 2,602 crore in Q2FY27. CASA (Current Account, Savings Account): The cheapest source of bank funding. A rising CASA ratio — 21.5% from 20% a year ago — lowers overall funding cost and supports margins. Bulk Deposits: Large-value deposits typically raised from institutions, at higher rates and with less stickiness than retail deposits. Utkarsh cut these 13.5% YoY in favour of retail money.
