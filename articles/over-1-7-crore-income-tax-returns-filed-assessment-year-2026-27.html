Over 1.7 Crore Income Tax Returns Filed for Assessment Year 2026-27

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

Tax filing season is in full swing.

Over 1.7 crore income tax returns have been filed for Assessment Year 2026-27, according to the Income Tax department. The figure reflects strong taxpayer compliance as the July 31 deadline approaches, with the government continuing its push toward digital filing through the e-filing portal and faceless assessment system.

The filing pace is significant. With the deadline still three weeks away, 1.7 crore returns represents a substantial portion of the expected total filings. The government has been actively encouraging taxpayers to file early through reminders, extended portal hours, and simplified forms for certain categories of taxpayers.

The e-filing portal has been the primary channel. The Income Tax department's online portal allows taxpayers to file returns, verify them digitally, and track refund status. The portal has been upgraded over the years to handle peak traffic, though it has occasionally experienced slowdowns during the final days before the deadline.

Faceless assessment is the new normal. The government has moved to a faceless assessment system where tax returns are processed by automated systems and reviewed by officers without the taxpayer knowing who is handling their case. This is meant to reduce discretion and harassment, though it has also faced criticism for lacking human interaction and clarity in notices.

The ITR filing deadline is July 31 for most taxpayers. Individuals who are not required to get their accounts audited must file by this date. For those with audit requirements, the deadline is later, typically October 31. Missing the deadline attracts a penalty of up to Rs 5,000 for returns filed after July 31 but before December 31.

Refund processing is a key concern. Many taxpayers file returns primarily to claim refunds for excess tax deducted at source. The speed of refund processing has improved in recent years, with the government claiming that most refunds are issued within a few weeks of filing and verification. However, delays still occur, particularly for returns with high refund claims or those flagged for scrutiny.
