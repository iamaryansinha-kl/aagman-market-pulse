1. Market Snapshot: Nifty and Sensex Open Mixed Amid Earnings Deluge

1.1 Benchmark Index Performance

Indian equities started the trading session on a cautious note on April 29, 2026. The Nifty 50 index opened at 24,838, down approximately 0.4% from the previous close. The Sensex traded at 81,437, reflecting a modest 0.3% decline. The Bank Nifty index stood at 54,467, indicating that financial stocks were also seeing some consolidation after a strong run in recent sessions. The India VIX, which measures market volatility, was at 12.84, suggesting that while there was some nervousness around the heavy results calendar, traders were not pricing in extreme volatility.

1.2 Sectoral Breadth

Market breadth was relatively balanced, with 1,346 stocks advancing against 989 decliners at the time of the morning update. The advance-decline ratio of roughly 1.36:1 indicated that despite the benchmark indices trading lower, there was selective buying across mid-cap and small-cap counters. The mixed opening reflected a market caught between two forces: the relief from a strong results season so far, and the anticipation of what heavyweights like Bajaj Finance and Adani Power might reveal later in the day.

1.3 Macro Context

The rupee was trading at 86.46 against the US dollar, while Brent crude oil hovered near $62.99 per barrel, providing some relief on the import bill front. Gold prices were firm at $3,295, reflecting global safe-haven demand. The 10-year government bond yield was at 6.54%, and the 3-year yield at 5.64%, suggesting that the fixed income market was pricing in a stable interest rate environment. These macro variables provided a relatively supportive backdrop for corporate earnings, though currency volatility remained a concern for importers and exporters alike.

2. Upcoming Q4 Results: The Heavyweights Still to Come

2.1 Financial Sector in Focus

The banking and non-banking financial sectors are under intense scrutiny today. Bajaj Finance, one of India's largest NBFCs, is scheduled to announce its Q4 results later today, with the market watching closely for any signs of stress in asset quality or loan growth momentum. Federal Bank, Indian Bank, and Indian Overseas Bank (IOB) are also on the calendar, and their results will feed into the broader narrative around public sector bank profitability and credit growth. Dhanlaxmi Bank, which has already announced its results, posted a 39.4% jump in net interest income to ₹187 crore and a 50.1% surge in adjusted profit to ₹43 crore, suggesting that smaller banks are also seeing robust operational momentum.

2.2 Energy and Power

Adani Power and Vedanta are two of the most anticipated results today. Adani Power, which has been riding a wave of strong power demand and capacity utilization, will provide clarity on its revenue trajectory and fuel cost management. Vedanta, a diversified natural resources major, is expected to give insights into commodity price impacts, particularly in zinc, aluminium, and oil & gas segments. The stock has been volatile in recent months due to fluctuations in global metal prices, and today's numbers will be critical for sentiment in the metals and mining space.

2.3 New-Age and Infrastructure Names

Waaree Energies, one of India's leading solar module manufacturers, will announce its Q4 results amid growing investor interest in renewable energy infrastructure. With India's solar capacity additions accelerating, Waaree's order book and margin profile will be closely watched. KFin Technologies, a key player in the investor services and registrar space, and Motilal Oswal, a diversified financial services firm, will round out the day's significant announcements. Additionally, companies like Advanced Enzyme Technologies, Anant Raj, Apollo Micro Systems, Delta Corp, Elecon Engineering, and KNR Constructions are also on the schedule, making this one of the busiest single-day results calendars of the entire Q4 season.

3. Maruti Suzuki Q4: Record Revenue but Shrinking Margins

3.1 Revenue Crosses ₹52,000 Crore

Maruti Suzuki India, the country's largest passenger vehicle manufacturer, delivered a mixed bag in Q4 FY26. The company's revenue from operations surged 28.2% year-on-year to ₹52,449 crore, marking a new quarterly record for the automaker. This top-line growth was driven by strong volume performance across its SUV and hatchback portfolio, as well as higher average selling prices due to a richer product mix. However, the sharp jump in revenue also came with rising input costs, and the company's ability to fully pass these on to consumers remained constrained in a competitive market.

3.2 Profitability Under Pressure

Despite the impressive revenue figure, Maruti Suzuki's consolidated net profit declined 6.9% year-on-year to ₹3,590.5 crore. Earnings before interest, tax, depreciation, and amortization (EBITDA) stood at ₹6,157 crore, with the EBITDA margin compressing to 11.7%. The margin pressure reflected higher raw material costs, increased marketing expenditure, and competitive pricing pressures, particularly in the entry-level segment where rivals have been aggressive. The quarter-on-quarter profit decline was even sharper at 20%, suggesting that the margin squeeze intensified as the fiscal year drew to a close.

3.3 Dividend Announcement

In a move that may cushion the stock's reaction to the profit decline, the Maruti Suzuki board recommended a final dividend of ₹140 per share for FY26. This is marginally higher than the ₹135 per share declared in FY25. The record date for the dividend has been fixed as August 7, 2026, with the payment scheduled for September 9, 2026. The dividend yield at current prices provides some downside support for the stock, though the key investor debate will centre on whether the margin compression is a temporary phenomenon or a structural challenge in an increasingly competitive Indian automotive market.

4. Auto Ancillary Sector: CEAT and Eternal Deliver Strong Numbers

4.1 CEAT's Profit More Than Doubles

Tyre manufacturer CEAT reported a stellar set of Q4 FY26 numbers, with consolidated net profit jumping more than two-fold year-on-year to ₹243.8 crore. The company, which is part of the RPG Group, benefited from strong volume growth across original equipment (OEM) and replacement segments, stable raw material prices, and improved operating leverage. The result comes on the back of a strong Q3, where standalone profit had already surged nearly 100% year-on-year to ₹191.6 crore. The full-year performance signals that CEAT has successfully navigated the cyclical challenges of the tyre industry and is well-positioned to capitalize on the ongoing automotive upcycle.

4.2 Eternal Ltd's Explosive Growth

Eternal Ltd, a key player in the consumer and industrial segments, delivered an extraordinary performance in Q4 FY26. Revenue surged 196.5% year-on-year to ₹17,292 crore, while EBITDA jumped an even more impressive 575% to ₹486 crore. The EBITDA margin stood at 2.8%, which, while modest in absolute terms, represented a significant improvement from the year-ago period. Adjusted profit after tax came in at ₹174 crore, comfortably beating analyst estimates of ₹160.6 crore. The stock reacted positively to the numbers, reflecting investor confidence that the company's rapid scaling is translating into bottom-line profitability. The year-on-year comparisons are amplified by a low base effect, but the sequential growth of 6% in revenue and 32.1% in EBITDA confirms genuine operational momentum.

4.3 Sectoral Outlook

The auto ancillary space is seeing a clear divergence in performance between companies exposed to the passenger vehicle cycle and those tied to commercial vehicles. CEAT's strong result reflects the buoyancy in passenger car and two-wheeler demand, while its commercial vehicle segment is also showing signs of recovery. Eternal's performance, meanwhile, underscores the strength of industrial demand. With commodity prices largely stable and the government's infrastructure push continuing, the ancillary sector appears well-placed to sustain earnings momentum into FY27, though margin pressures from rising input costs remain a watchpoint.

5. Banking Results: Bandhan Bank Surprises, Dhanlaxmi Bank Scales

5.1 Bandhan Bank Beats Estimates

Bandhan Bank delivered a significant earnings beat in Q4 FY26, with adjusted profit after tax surging to ₹534 crore against analyst expectations of ₹410 crore. The year-on-year comparison was even more striking — profit jumped from ₹318 crore in Q4 FY25 and from just ₹206 crore in the preceding quarter. Net interest income grew a modest 1% year-on-year to ₹2,795.6 crore, suggesting that the profit surge was driven more by operating leverage and potentially lower provisioning rather than core lending growth. The net interest margin (NIM) stood at a healthy 6.2%, while return on assets (ROA) improved to 1.1%.

5.2 Asset Quality Metrics

On the asset quality front, Bandhan Bank reported a gross non-performing asset (NPA) ratio of 3.27% and a net NPA ratio of 0.97%. While the gross NPA level remains elevated compared to larger peers, the trend appears to be stabilizing. The bank's microfinance-heavy book has been under stress in recent quarters due to collection challenges in certain states, but the Q4 numbers suggest that the worst of the asset quality deterioration may be behind it. The market will closely watch management commentary on collection efficiency and fresh slippages in the upcoming earnings call.

5.3 Dhanlaxmi Bank's Turnaround

Smaller private lender Dhanlaxmi Bank reported a 39.4% jump in net interest income to ₹187 crore and a 50.1% surge in adjusted profit to ₹43 crore. Asset quality improved meaningfully, with gross NPA declining to 1.89% and net NPA to 0.51%. The stock trades at a modest ₹32, reflecting its small-cap status, but the earnings trajectory suggests that the bank's turnaround story is gaining credibility. For investors in the small-cap banking space, Dhanlaxmi's performance offers a template of how focused lenders can deliver outsized profit growth when credit costs normalize.

6. Insurance and FMCG: Star Health Swings to Profit, AWL Agri Disappoints

6.1 Star Health's Remarkable Turnaround

Star Health and Allied Insurance Company reported one of the most dramatic turnarounds of the Q4 season. The standalone health insurer posted a net profit of ₹111.34 crore for Q4 FY26, compared to just ₹5 crore in the year-ago quarter — a more than 22-fold increase. For the full fiscal year FY26, the company reported a profit after tax of ₹911 crore under Ind-AS, compared with ₹787 crore in FY25. The surge in profitability was driven by lower loss ratios and improved underwriting discipline, as the company tightened its risk selection and claims management processes after a challenging FY25.

6.2 AWL Agri Business Faces Headwinds

AWL Agri Business, formerly known as Adani Wilmar, delivered Q4 FY26 revenue of ₹21,464.78 crore, up 17.75% year-on-year. However, the market reaction was negative, with the stock trading down around 3% at ₹239. The company's FMCG segment remained flat during the quarter, which disappointed investors who had been hoping for continued momentum in the food and fast-moving consumer goods business. The edible oils segment, which remains the core revenue driver, saw volume growth of 17% year-on-year, but margin pressure from cheap refined oil imports from neighbouring SAARC countries continued to weigh on profitability.

6.3 Sapphire Foods and Castrol India

Quick-service restaurant operator Sapphire Foods, which operates the KFC and Pizza Hut franchises in India, reported an 11.4% year-on-year revenue increase to ₹792.2 crore and a 17.2% EBITDA jump to ₹124.5 crore. However, adjusted profit was a razor-thin ₹0.2 crore, highlighting the challenging operating environment for the food services industry. Lubricant maker Castrol India fared better, with revenue rising 8.7% to ₹1,545.3 crore, EBITDA up 7.1% to ₹329.3 crore, and adjusted profit at ₹242.2 crore. The 21.3% EBITDA margin demonstrated Castrol's pricing power in a competitive lubricants market.

7. Infrastructure and Materials: Dalmia Bharat and Vardhman Steels Shine

7.1 Dalmia Bharat's Cement Performance

Dalmia Bharat, one of India's leading cement manufacturers, reported Q4 FY26 revenue of ₹4,245 crore, up 3.8% year-on-year. While the topline growth appears modest, the company's profitability metrics were strong — EBITDA jumped 13.7% to ₹902 crore, and the EBITDA margin expanded to 21.2%. Adjusted profit after tax came in at ₹397 crore, comfortably ahead of analyst estimates of ₹341.8 crore. The cement sector has been grappling with demand volatility and rising power and fuel costs, but Dalmia's result suggests that efficient operations and a strong brand position in the eastern and southern markets are helping the company outperform.

7.2 Vardhman Special Steels

Specialty steel manufacturer Vardhman Special Steels reported a solid operational quarter. Revenue grew 7% year-on-year to ₹457.9 crore, while EBITDA surged 46.6% to ₹46.1 crore, reflecting strong operating leverage. The EBITDA margin expanded to 10.1%, and adjusted profit after tax stood at ₹34 crore. The stock was trading at ₹298, and the result underscores the demand strength in the specialized steel segment, which serves automotive, engineering, and infrastructure customers.

7.3 Sectoral Context

The infrastructure and materials space is at an interesting inflection point. Government capital expenditure on roads, railways, and urban infrastructure continues to provide a floor for cement and steel demand. However, private capex recovery remains patchy, and rural demand for construction materials has been uneven due to erratic monsoon patterns in certain regions. Dalmia Bharat's margin expansion is a positive signal, but investors will need to see sustained demand growth in the first half of FY27 to confirm that the sector is entering a durable upcycle rather than a temporary margin-led recovery.

8. Global Cues and Broader Market Context

8.1 US Futures Signal Caution

Global cues were largely neutral to slightly negative as Indian markets opened. S&P 500 futures were flat at 5,558, Nasdaq 100 futures edged up 0.02% to 19,831, and Dow Jones futures were down 0.11% at 40,196. The muted US futures suggested that Wall Street was in a wait-and-watch mode ahead of key economic data releases and the ongoing corporate earnings season in America. For Indian markets, which have been correlated with US tech and broader emerging market sentiment, this neutral backdrop provided neither a strong tailwind nor a headwind.

8.2 Commodity and Currency Watch

Brent crude oil at $62.99 provided some relief for India's oil import bill, though the recent volatility in crude prices remains a concern for inflation management and the current account deficit. Gold at $3,295 reflects continued global demand for safe-haven assets amid geopolitical tensions and tariff uncertainties. The rupee at 86.46 against the dollar was relatively stable, though the Reserve Bank of India's intervention strategy continues to be a key factor in currency movement. For Indian corporates with dollar-denominated debt or significant import/export exposure, currency hedging costs have risen, adding another layer of complexity to financial planning.

8.3 The Q4 Season So Far

With the bulk of Q4 FY26 results now in, the earnings season has largely met expectations. Revenue growth has been broad-based, with most sectors reporting double-digit top-line expansion. However, margin pressure is evident across the board, reflecting higher input costs, competitive pricing, and elevated marketing and employee expenses. The IT sector, which reported earlier in the season, showed signs of demand stabilization but no clear acceleration. The financial sector has been a mixed bag, with private banks delivering steady growth while NBFCs have shown divergence in asset quality trends. As the season enters its final stretch, the focus is shifting from revenue growth to margin sustainability and management guidance for FY27.
