Axis Bank Q2 Profit Jumps 38% to Rs 7,003 Crore, NII Up 12%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Net profit rose 38% YoY to Rs 7,003 crore from Rs 5,089 crore.
  • •- Net interest income grew 12% to Rs 15,371 crore; NIM stood at 3.36%.
  • •- Advances grew 23% YoY and 9% QoQ.
  • •- Gross NPAs fell 30 bps to 1.16%; net slippage ratio improved to 0.75%.
  • •- The bank mobilised $10.62 billion in FCNR(B) deposits by September 30.
  • •- H1FY27 net profit rose 30% to Rs 14,117 crore.

Axis Bank has closed the second quarter with the kind of comprehensive print that settles arguments rather than starting them. The private lender on Saturday reported net profit of Rs 7,003 crore for Q2FY27, up 38 per cent from Rs 5,089 crore a year earlier, on the back of 12 per cent net interest income growth to Rs 15,371 crore and improvements across every asset-quality metric that matters. For the first half, profit rose 30 per cent to Rs 14,117 crore.

The growth engine ran hot without running loose. Advances expanded 23 per cent year-on-year and 9 per cent sequentially — a pace at the aggressive end of the large private banks — while asset quality improved simultaneously: gross NPAs fell 30 basis points to 1.16 per cent, net NPAs eased 9 basis points to 0.35 per cent, and the net slippage ratio declined 30 basis points to 0.75 per cent. Provisions and contingencies for the quarter came in at Rs 1,694 crore, including specific loan-loss provisions of Rs 1,564 crore, and the bank holds a further Rs 15,860 crore of cumulative standard and additional provisions sitting outside its provision-coverage calculations — a buffer stock of caution that most peers cannot match.

The most strategically interesting line in the filing is the FCNR one. Axis Bank mobilised $10.62 billion in FCNR(B) deposits by September 30 — the largest single-bank number reported under the RBI's special window — and used a portion of the proceeds to reduce non-retail term deposits, which declined 8.6 per cent sequentially. It also issued standby letters of credit totalling $4.69 billion to other lenders against FCNR-backed loans, effectively recycling the diaspora dollar flow into the broader banking system. Federal Bank, which reported on Friday, showed the same pattern at smaller scale. The FCNR window has thus done something subtler than bolster reserves: it has let banks with strong NRI franchises cheapen and stabilise their liability bases while competitors bid up domestic deposit rates.

The margin picture reflects that trade. Net interest margin came in at 3.36 per cent for the quarter — solid, though the FCNR mix and the broader rate environment mean trajectory matters more than level. Core operating profit rose 11 per cent year-on-year to Rs 10,998 crore, fee income for the half grew 6 per cent to Rs 12,536 crore, and the 12 per cent NII growth against 23 per cent advances growth implies yield compression that the bank is choosing to wear for market share. It is a familiar Axis posture: grow the book fast when credit demand is strong, and let the margin find its level later.

Managing director and CEO Amitabh Chaudhry framed the quarter around franchise quality rather than arithmetic, citing "innovation, trust and execution at scale," leadership in digital payments and an AI-driven technology agenda. The AI language is now standard across bank earnings calls, but at Axis it carries more operational substance than most — the bank's digital payments franchise is genuinely among the deepest in the system, and its partnerships in this space have been setting the pace for the sector.

In the context of the earnings season, Axis Bank's print completes a portrait of private-bank strength that has become hard to argue with. ICICI Bank's results earlier set the tone; HDFC Bank's steadied the franchise question; Federal Bank's record on Friday added the mid-tier dimension; and Axis has now delivered growth and quality in the same quarter. The system's aggregate credit growth above 16 per cent, combined with falling NPAs at every major private lender, describes a banking cycle in its sweet spot — margins compressed by competition, but volumes, asset quality and profitability all cooperative.

The watch items for the December quarter are three. First, whether the FCNR-fuelled deposit advantage persists now that the window's exceptional pricing has normalised — Axis's non-retail deposit reduction will need to be replaced with stable retail money over time. Second, whether 23 per cent loan growth can coexist with the slippage ratio at 0.75 per cent if the rate environment tightens further into the RBI's December decision. Third, the quantum of the treasury hit: like every bank holding a large government-bond book, Axis faces mark-to-market pressure from the nine-week bond-market slide, and the gap between operating profit growth of 6 per cent and core operating profit growth of 11 per cent hints at exactly that drag. None of these detracts from the quarter; all of them shape the next one.

Against the macro backdrop, the print carries an additional quiet significance. The RBI's tightening bias, the bond market's nine-week slide and the currency's fragile recovery all describe a system leaning on its banks to keep credit flowing while external conditions harden. Axis growing advances 23 per cent with improving asset quality is, in that sense, part of the economy's defence mechanism: private-sector balance sheets expanding healthily are what let the central bank fight the external battle without choking domestic credit. The season's private-bank results — from ICICI's steadiness to Federal Bank's record to this — collectively answer the question that matters most for FY27: whether Indian credit remains an engine or becomes a casualty. So far, convincingly, an engine.

Chaudhry's tenure has been defined by the deliberate, sometimes unglamorous work of fixing asset quality and rebuilding the deposit franchise; this quarter shows both engines firing at once, with the FCNR windfall merely accelerating a trajectory that was already his design. The 38 per cent profit jump will draw the headlines, but the more durable number is the 1.16 per cent GNPA — the lowest clean read on the book in years, achieved while growing at 23 per cent. That combination is the actual story, and it will outlast whatever the December quarter's treasury marks say.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT WERE AXIS BANK'S Q2 HEADLINES?
Net profit rose 38% YoY to Rs 7,003 crore, NII rose 12% to Rs 15,371 crore, and advances grew 23% YoY with GNPA down to 1.16%.
HOW MUCH DID AXIS RAISE VIA FCNR?
$10.62 billion by September 30, used partly to cut non-retail term deposits by 8.6% QoQ, with $4.69 billion of standby letters of credit issued to other lenders.
HOW DID PROVISIONS LOOK?
Rs 1,694 crore for the quarter, plus Rs 15,860 crore of cumulative standard and additional provisions held outside PCR calculations.
WHAT WERE THE H1 NUMBERS?
H1FY27 NII rose 10% to Rs 30,018 crore, fee income grew 6% to Rs 12,536 crore, and net profit rose 30% to Rs 14,117 crore. GLOSSARY: - FCNR(B) deposits: Foreign-currency non-resident deposits — dollar-denominated money raised from the Indian diaspora. - Standby letter of credit: A guarantee a bank issues so another lender will honour a loan backed by FCNR deposits. - Net slippage ratio: New loans turning bad minus recoveries and upgrades, as a share of advances. - Core operating profit: Operating profit excluding treasury and one-off items — a cleaner read of lending economics.
