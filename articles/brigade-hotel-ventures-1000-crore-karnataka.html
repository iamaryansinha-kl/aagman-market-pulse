Brigade Hotel Ventures to Invest ₹1,000 Crore in Karnataka Hospitality

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Brigade Hotel Ventures (NSE: BRIGHOTEL) announces ₹1,000 crore investment in Karnataka over five years for new hotels and asset modernisation
  • 02/Under Vision 2031, BHVL targets 1,700 new room keys with ₹3,600 crore total capex; currently operates 9 hotels with 1,604 keys
  • 03/Key monitorables: occupancy ramp-up in new properties, project execution timeline, and Mysuru hospitality market CAGR of 8-10%

Brigade Hotel Ventures Limited (NSE: BRIGHOTEL) plans to invest ₹1,000 crore in Karnataka over the next five years. The investment covers new hotel projects and modernisation of existing assets across the state.

The announcement coincides with the 10th anniversary of Grand Mercure Mysuru, BHVL's flagship 146-key property launched in 2016. The hotel has helped establish the viability of luxury leisure hospitality in Tier-II markets.

Under its broader "Vision 2031" roadmap, BHVL plans to add approximately 1,700 room keys over five years. The total capital outlay under this plan is ₹3,600 crore, targeting roughly double its current inventory.

"Mysuru has transitioned from a weekend getaway destination into a robust business and heritage hub," said Nirupa Shankar, Managing Director, BHVL. "The success we've achieved here has provided us with the operational confidence and the financial blueprint to aggressively scale our footprint across Karnataka."

BHVL currently operates 9 hotels with 1,604 keys across South India. Properties are managed under global brands including Marriott, Accor, and InterContinental Hotels Group.

The company reported revenue of ₹468 crore in FY25 with an average occupancy rate of 76.8%. This significantly exceeds the industry average of 64.5%.

BHVL expects Mysuru's hospitality market to grow at a compounded annual growth rate of 8-10%. Growth is driven by rising domestic and international tourist inflows seeking experiential and wellness-led travel.

Risks include high capital expenditure commitments, hospitality demand cyclicality, and occupancy ramp-up challenges in new properties. An economic slowdown affecting discretionary travel could impact returns on invested capital.
