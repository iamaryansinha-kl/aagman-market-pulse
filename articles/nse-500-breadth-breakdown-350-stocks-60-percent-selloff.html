Code Red On Market Breadth: 350 Of NSE 500 Stocks Crash Up To 60% In Two-Month Selloff

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Roughly 350 of the 500 stocks in the NSE 500 universe have crashed up to 60% from their highs over a two-month selloff, ET Markets reported on October 1, 2026
  • 02/The Nifty 50 closed September 30 at 22,620.45, down 0.42%, and is testing its 200-week moving average — the 22,600 zone is the key support traders are watching
  • 03/Foreign investors sold about Rs 25,662 crore of Indian equities in the September series, the worst September series in 25 years, leaving the headline index far above the typical stock

The headline numbers do not tell the full story of this market. Beneath the Nifty's relatively contained decline, the damage is far deeper and far broader.

According to an ET Markets report on October 1, 2026, roughly 350 of the 500 stocks in the NSE 500 universe have fallen up to 60% from their highs. The drawdowns have built up over a two-month-long selloff.

The drivers are not mysterious. Coverage of the breakdown points to mounting economic pressures and persistent foreign investor selling. Both have been tightening their grip through September.

Start with the index itself. The Nifty 50 ended September 30 at 22,620.45, down 95.75 points or 0.42% on the day. The Sensex closed at 72,480.29, down 48.78 points or 0.07%.

Those index prints look calm. They are not the story.

The story is in the breadth. Eight BSE 100 stocks hit fresh 52-week lows in the same session. The list includes PB Fintech, Havells India, Wipro, Britannia Industries, Tata Consumer Products, Godrej Consumer Products, Hindustan Unilever and ONGC.

That is a striking mix. It is not only high-beta growth names under pressure. Consumer staples bellwethers and an oil major are also making new annual lows.

PB Fintech shows how violent the moves have become. The stock has crashed about 42% in just four sessions, wiping out roughly Rs 37,000 crore in market value. The trigger was Irdai's proposed reforms targeting insurance commissions and dark patterns.

Brokerages have moved quickly. Bernstein, Jefferies and Morgan Stanley have all cut their price targets on PB Fintech, flagging risks to earnings, distribution economics and valuations.

The selling has a foreign face. Foreign portfolio investors sold about Rs 25,662 crore of Indian equities in the September F&O series. The Nifty fell roughly 6.5% in the series, its worst September showing in 25 years.

Domestic institutions have absorbed part of the supply, but the pressure has not relented. Every attempted bounce in the past two weeks has been sold into.

Technically, the market is standing on a narrow ledge. The Nifty is testing its 200-week moving average. The 22,600 zone has become the level traders are watching as the line between a correction and something worse.

The divergence is the point. When 70% of the broader market is down as much as 60% from highs while the headline index sits near a major moving average, narrow leadership is doing the heavy lifting. A handful of heavyweights are holding up the index prints.

This is what a breadth breakdown looks like. The average stock has already experienced a bear market. The index simply has not confirmed it yet.

For investors, the practical takeaway is that index-level performance has become a poor proxy for portfolio experience. A portfolio built on the Nifty's resilience could still be sitting on heavy drawdowns in individual names.

History suggests breadth extremes eventually resolve. Either the majority of stocks stabilize and the rally broadens again, or the index catches down to the typical stock. With foreign selling unrelenting and macro pressure mounting, the market is pricing a rising probability of the second path.
