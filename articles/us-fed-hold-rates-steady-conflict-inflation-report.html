US Fed to Hold Rates Steady Amid Conflict-Driven Inflation: Report

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/ICICI Bank expects the US Federal Reserve to hold rates steady at the April 28-29 FOMC meeting amid inflation risks from Middle East conflict
  • 02/US GDP growth revised down to 2% for 2026 from 2.2%; Governor Stephen Miran likely to dissent for a 25 basis point cut
  • 03/FOMC decision due at 2:00 PM ET on April 29; Jerome Powell press conference follows at 2:30 PM ET

The US Federal Reserve is expected to keep interest rates unchanged at its April 28-29 policy meeting. An ICICI Bank report said the FOMC will likely signal a prolonged pause.

Governor Stephen Miran is expected to dissent. He will likely vote for a 25 basis point rate cut instead of holding steady.

The report noted that ongoing Middle East conflict poses an inflation shock. Elevated energy prices are already pushing inflation above target.

US GDP growth projections have been revised downward. Growth is now seen at 2% for 2026, down from 2.2% previously. The 2027 estimate was cut to 1.9% from 2.0%.

"The case for the FOMC to maintain status quo and signal that it is likely going to be on a prolonged pause has increased considerably," the ICICI Bank report stated.

The conflict creates dual risks for the US economy. There are upside risks to inflation and downside risks to the labour market.

"The US central bank will want to give itself some time to assess the impact of the conflict on the economy before it responds," the report noted.

The FOMC will announce its decision at 2:00 PM ET on April 29. Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell will hold a press conference at 2:30 PM ET.
