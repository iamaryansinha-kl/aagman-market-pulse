Retail Space Rent in Delhi's Khan Market Rises 9% Annually in April-June

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

Prime retail rents are climbing in Delhi.

Retail space rent in Khan Market, one of India's most expensive shopping destinations, rose 9% annually in the April-June quarter. The increase reflects strong demand for prime retail locations as consumer spending recovers and brands compete for space in high-footfall areas.

Khan Market is a retail bellwether. Located in the heart of New Delhi, the market has long been one of the country's most expensive retail destinations, with rents rivalling those of premium shopping districts in global cities. The market attracts a mix of luxury brands, international retailers, high-end restaurants, and boutique stores catering to Delhi's affluent population.

The 9% annual increase signals strong demand. After several years of sluggish rent growth due to the pandemic and the shift toward online shopping, physical retail is showing signs of revival. Brands are recognising that prime locations still drive discovery, brand building, and premium customer acquisition in a way that e-commerce cannot replicate.

Footfall has been recovering. With offices returning to full occupancy and tourism picking up, Khan Market has seen a steady increase in visitors. The market's compact layout and curated mix of stores make it a destination for both shopping and dining, driving longer dwell times and higher spending per visit.

The rent increase is part of a broader trend in prime retail. Across major Indian cities, rents in top shopping districts have been recovering as the worst effects of the pandemic fade. Markets like Linking Road in Mumbai, Brigade Road in Bangalore, and Park Street in Kolkata have all seen similar trends.

However, challenges remain. The shift to online shopping is permanent, and many categories have seen a structural decline in physical retail demand. Only brands that can offer an experiential or premium positioning are willing to pay the high rents for prime locations. Mass-market retailers are increasingly focusing on smaller, more efficient formats or online-only presence.
