ATMA Seeks Customs Duty Relief on Tyre Raw Materials as Costs Surge

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/ATMA urges government to extend customs duty relief beyond June 30 on synthetic rubber, carbon black, bead wire and other critical tyre raw materials
  • 02/Crude-linked inputs account for 60-70% of raw material costs; Strait of Hormuz and Suez Canal disruptions raise freight and insurance charges
  • 03/Domestic bead wire makers face industrial gas and LPG shortages; ATMA requests temporary QCO exemption to ensure production continuity

The Automotive Tyre Manufacturers Association (ATMA) has urged the government to extend customs duty relief on critical raw materials. The plea comes as crude price volatility and shipping route disruptions strain tyre makers' supply chains.

ATMA welcomed the temporary customs duty exemption on synthetic rubber and select resins until June 30, 2026. However, several other key inputs continue to attract significant import duties.

Polyester and nylon tyre cord fabric, bead wire, steel tyre cord, carbon black, processing oils and petrochemical-based chemicals remain dutiable. Crude-linked inputs account for nearly 60-70 per cent of total raw material costs in tyre manufacturing.

The Strait of Hormuz and Suez Canal shipping routes are under stress from the ongoing West Asia crisis. The industry faces rising input costs, shipment delays and higher freight and insurance charges.

ATMA flagged supply constraints for bead wire, a critical component for tyre safety. Domestic manufacturers are struggling with shortages of industrial gas and LPG needed for production.

The association has requested temporary exemption from Quality Control Order requirements for bead wire imports. It also sought assured sulphur supply and sustained refinery output of rubber process oils, SBPS and furnace oil.

Arun Mammen, Chairman of ATMA, said extending duty relief to all critical raw materials is essential. He cited the need to maintain production stability and cost competitiveness amid global uncertainties.

India's tyre industry recorded exports of ₹25,051 crore in FY25, a 9 per cent year-on-year increase. With annual turnover estimated at ₹1 lakh crore, any sustained cost pressure could squeeze margins across the sector.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT RAW MATERIALS DOES ATMA WANT CUSTOMS DUTY RELIEF FOR?
ATMA HAS SOUGHT DUTY RELIEF ON POLYESTER/NYLON TYRE CORD FABRIC, BEAD WIRE, STEEL TYRE CORD, CARBON BLACK, PROCESSING OILS AND PETROCHEMICAL-BASED CHEMICALS. THESE INPUTS CONTINUE TO ATTRACT SIGNIFICANT IMPORT DUTIES DESPITE ONGOING SUPPLY CONSTRAINTS.
WHAT IS THE CURRENT CUSTOMS DUTY EXEMPTION ALREADY IN PLACE?
THE GOVERNMENT HAS GRANTED TEMPORARY CUSTOMS DUTY EXEMPTION ON SELECT RAW MATERIALS INCLUDING SYNTHETIC RUBBER AND CERTAIN RESINS UNTIL JUNE 30, 2026. ATMA DESCRIBED THIS AS A TIMELY AND SUPPORTIVE STEP BUT SAID IT IS INSUFFICIENT.
WHY IS THE TYRE INDUSTRY FACING SUPPLY CHAIN PRESSURES?
CRUDE-LINKED INPUTS ACCOUNT FOR 60-70 PER CENT OF RAW MATERIAL COSTS IN TYRE MANUFACTURING. WITH CRUDE PRICES VOLATILE AND KEY SHIPPING ROUTES LIKE THE STRAIT OF HORMUZ AND SUEZ CANAL UNDER STRESS, THE INDUSTRY FACES RISING COSTS, SHIPMENT DELAYS AND HIGHER FREIGHT CHARGES.
WHAT IS ATMA'S REQUEST REGARDING BEAD WIRE IMPORTS?
ATMA HAS REQUESTED TEMPORARY EXEMPTION FROM QUALITY CONTROL ORDER REQUIREMENTS FOR BEAD WIRE IMPORTS. DOMESTIC BEAD WIRE MANUFACTURERS ARE FACING PRESSURE DUE TO SHORTAGES OF INDUSTRIAL GAS AND LPG, THREATENING TYRE PRODUCTION.
WHAT OTHER POLICY SUPPORT HAS ATMA SOUGHT FROM THE GOVERNMENT?
ATMA HAS SOUGHT TARGETED INTERVENTIONS TO ENSURE ADEQUATE SULPHUR SUPPLY FOR TYRE MANUFACTURERS. IT ALSO WANTS REFINERIES TO SUSTAIN SUPPLIES OF RUBBER PROCESS OILS, SBPS AND FURNACE OIL, AND ALIGN OUTPUT TOWARD HIGH-VALUE MATERIALS LIKE NEODYMIUM POLYBUTADIENE RUBBER AND SOLUTION SBR.
