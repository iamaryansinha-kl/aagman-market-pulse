India flips AB InBev from witness to target in antitrust probe, triggers global probe

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //

**The Flip**

India's Competition Commission (CCI) changed AB InBev's status from a third-party witness to a "party under investigation" in November 2025. The Belgian brewer had cooperated with authorities for four years, providing sensitive business information including details of retailer incentives it offered in Telangana state.

The case began in July 2022 when the CCI launched an investigation into 42 alcohol retailers in Telangana — India's largest beer-consuming state — over allegations they formed a cartel to boycott all brands except AB InBev's. The brewer's offices in Hyderabad were raided in December 2024 alongside those of rival Pernod Ricard.

AB InBev argued in court papers reviewed by Reuters that the status change was illegal because "no prior notice, hearing, or reasoned order preceded this drastic alteration." The company said it continued cooperating between 2023 and 2025 before discovering in November 2024 that it was itself under investigation.

**The Court Block**

On April 16, 2026, a judge in Karnataka state granted AB InBev a temporary injunction, putting the investigation on hold. The court order has not been made public, but two sources with direct knowledge told Reuters the judge saw merit in AB InBev's concerns about procedural fairness.

If the CCI succeeds in overturning the court block, AB InBev faces penalties of up to three times its profit or 10% of its turnover for each year of alleged wrongdoing. Making a company a "party under investigation" from third-party status signals investigators believe they have discovered evidence against it, according to lawyers familiar with CCI procedure.

"Your rights of defence are compromised. Now the company needs to defend itself," said Avaantika Kakkar, head of competition practice at Cyril Amarchand Mangaldas, which is not involved in the case.

**The Market Context**

India's beer market is worth $10 billion, according to the Brewers Association of India. Heineken-controlled United Breweries holds roughly 50% market share, while AB InBev and Carlsberg each account for 19%.

This is not the sector's first brush with Indian antitrust regulators. In 2021, United Breweries and Carlsberg were fined more than $100 million collectively for price collusion in a case where AB InBev acted as the whistleblower and received 100% penalty immunity under the CCI's leniency programme. The cartel ran from 2009 to at least October 2018.

The CCI's initial assessment in the Telangana case found merit in allegations that retailers entered arrangements to stock and sell AB InBev beers exclusively, while boycotting products from Heineken, United Breweries, and Carlsberg. The regulator also found retailers were eligible for a special incentive for promoting AB InBev products exclusively.

**The Global Dimension**

The case carries international implications. AB InBev, headquartered in Leuven, Belgium, is the world's largest brewer with operations across 50 countries. A confirmed finding of cartel conduct in India could prompt competition authorities in the European Union, United States, and other jurisdictions to examine whether similar arrangements exist in their markets.

Belgium's Competition Authority is already conducting a separate formal antitrust probe into AB InBev's commercial terms with wholesalers and hospitality operators, launched in January 2025. That investigation is examining potential abuse of dominance and anticompetitive agreements, distinct from the Indian cartel allegations.

AB InBev reported all-time high revenue of $59.8 billion in 2024, with normalized EBITDA of $20.96 billion. The company did not respond to Reuters requests for comment on the Indian case. The CCI also declined to comment.
