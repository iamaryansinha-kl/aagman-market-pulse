JPMorgan Names Doug Petno, Troy Rohrbaugh as Co-Presidents in Dimon Succession Move

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/JPMorgan Chase appoints Doug Petno, 61, and Troy Rohrbaugh, 56, as co-presidents in a major succession planning move
  • 02/Each receives $30 million retention RSU awards vesting over 3 years, subject to 12% average ROTCE performance hurdle
  • 03/Marianne Lake exits after 25 years, narrowing the field to replace CEO Jamie Dimon who has led the bank since 2006

JPMorgan Chase & Co. (NYSE: JPM) has appointed Doug Petno and Troy Rohrbaugh as co-presidents of the firm. The board approved the leadership changes on June 25, 2026.

Petno, 61, will become sole CEO of the Commercial & Investment Bank. Rohrbaugh, 56, will take over as CEO of Consumer & Community Banking.

The appointments signal a narrowing of the succession field to replace Jamie Dimon. Dimon has led JPMorgan as CEO since 2006, marking 20 years at the helm of Wall Street's largest bank.

Marianne Lake, the previous head of Consumer & Community Banking, has decided to retire. She leaves after more than 25 years of service at the firm.

The bank's Compensation Committee approved one-time retention awards for four top executives. Petno and Rohrbaugh will each receive $30 million in Restricted Stock Units.

Mary Erdoes, CEO of Asset & Wealth Management, and Jennifer Piepszak, Chief Operating Officer, will each receive $20 million retention awards.

The RSUs cliff-vest after three years. Vesting requires JPMorgan to achieve a 12% average return on tangible common equity across 2026, 2027, and 2028.

JPMorgan shares closed at $335.53 on June 25. The stock trades near its 52-week high of $343.45, up from a low of $279.10.

The bank reported a $54 billion profit in 2025. It maintains a market capitalization of approximately $899 billion and a dividend yield of 1.77%.

"The changes announced today mark an important step in our Board's thoughtful process around succession planning," Dimon said in a statement.
