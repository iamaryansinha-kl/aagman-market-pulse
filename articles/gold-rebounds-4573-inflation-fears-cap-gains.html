Gold Rebounds 0.7% to $4,573/oz as Dip Buying Emerges After One-Month Low

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Spot gold rose 0.7% to $4,573.09 per ounce after falling to its lowest level since March 31 in the prior session
  • 02/The Federal Reserve held rates steady but three officials dissented, signaling rising inflation concerns amid Brent crude above $120
  • 03/Traders are now pricing out Fed rate cuts entirely for 2026, with a 30% chance of a hike by March 2027

Spot gold rose 0.7% to $4,573.09 per ounce as of 0229 GMT on April 30. The metal had fallen to its lowest level since March 31 in the previous session.

U.S. gold futures for June delivery gained 0.5% to $4,585.10. Dip buying is driving the modest recovery from oversold levels.

"Gold is representing a value proposition for traders at current levels. So, dip buying is playing a part in gold's recovery efforts today," said Tim Waterer, chief market analyst at KCM Trade.

However, strong oil prices and fresh inflation risks are capping the upside. Brent crude hovered above $120 a barrel as deadlocked U.S.-Iran negotiations raised concerns about prolonged Middle Eastern supply disruptions.

Elevated oil prices add to inflation risks, increasing the likelihood of higher interest rates. While gold is traditionally a hedge against inflation, high rates weigh on its appeal as a non-yielding asset.

The Federal Reserve held interest rates steady on Wednesday in its most divided decision since 1992. The policy statement drew three dissents from officials who no longer feel the central bank should signal a bias toward cutting borrowing costs.

Traders are now pricing out Fed rate cuts entirely for this year. Markets now see a 30% chance of a hike by March 2027, up sharply from roughly 5% a day prior.

Meanwhile, U.S. President Donald Trump discussed a possible months-long blockade of Iran's ports with oil companies. He urged Tehran to "get smart soon" and sign a deal.

Spot silver rose 1.6% to $72.63 per ounce, platinum gained 1.8% to $1,913.86, and palladium was up 0.4% at $1,465.14.
