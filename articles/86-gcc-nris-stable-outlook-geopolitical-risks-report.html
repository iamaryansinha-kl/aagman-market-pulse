86% GCC NRIs Show Stable Outlook Despite Geopolitical Risks: Report

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/86% of GCC-based NRIs report stable or improved financial confidence despite 41% citing geopolitical instability as the top risk
  • 02/73% are increasing exposure to Indian equities and mutual funds while 40% are reducing India real estate holdings
  • 03/Remittance intent has shifted from family support to strategic investment, with 49% earmarking funds for India investment and retirement

Despite geopolitical uncertainty, 86 per cent of Non-Resident Indians in the Gulf Cooperation Council region reported stable or improved financial confidence. The findings come from a survey conducted by Equirus Wealth.

The report noted that these investors are not retreating but recalibrating their strategies. "Despite global uncertainty, 86% report stable or improved confidence -- anchored in long-term earning visibility," the survey said.

While 83 per cent of respondents acknowledged the impact of global developments, their reactions remained defensive and disciplined. Investors are choosing to save more and cut discretionary spending while maintaining conviction in the Indian economy.

Across the surveyed group, the mean financial confidence score stood at 3.50 out of 5. Only 45 per cent maintained a cautious-neutral stance, while 46 per cent reported high long-term optimism. Under 10 per cent reported low confidence.

The year-on-year trend showed 53 per cent with stable confidence, 33 per cent reporting improvement, and just 14 per cent reporting a decline. In the context of heightened geopolitical activity and GCC labour market pressures, this stability is remarkable.

Risk perception is dominated by macro factors. Regional geopolitical instability was cited by 41 per cent as the single biggest risk. Inflation followed at 23 per cent, and global market volatility at 13 per cent. Notably, job and visa security ranked fourth at just 12 per cent, signalling structural confidence in income stability.

The survey highlighted a decisive portfolio reallocation. Indian equities and mutual funds are being accumulated aggressively, with 73 per cent of respondents increasing exposure. Meanwhile, real estate in India is being exited in large numbers, with 40 per cent reducing holdings. This marks a structural migration from physical to financial assets.

Remittance patterns have also shifted. Investment in India accounts for 27 per cent of stated remittance intent, and retirement planning for 22 per cent. Together these total 49 per cent, ahead of family support at 26 per cent. This signals a transition from obligation-led to strategy-driven remittances.

The forward investment stance remains active. Seventy-five per cent of the community identifies as either active long-term or balanced investors. Only 10 per cent operate in pure capital preservation mode. India continues to be viewed as the central geography for wealth creation among GCC NRIs.
