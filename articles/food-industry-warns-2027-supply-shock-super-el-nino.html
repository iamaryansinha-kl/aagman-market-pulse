El Nino Supply Shock 2027: Sugar Prices May Cross Rs 50 Per Kg

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/India's food industry warns of a 2027 supply shock as a developing Super El Nino threatens sugar, pulses and oilseeds production
  • 02/Experts predict ex-mill sugar prices could cross Rs 50 per kg during June-October 2027; their note "A Comfortable Diwali, an Uncomfortable 2027" went to the RBI
  • 03/A UN body says the El Nino could rank among the strongest since 1950, peaking in December and lasting through February

India's food industry is warning of a potential supply shock in 2027. A developing Super El Nino threatens to disrupt crop production, squeeze supplies and fuel food inflation beyond this year's monsoon season.

The warning is unusual for its timing. Industry bodies typically flag immediate shortages; this one points to a shock roughly 15 months out and frames the next six months as the window to prepare.

"We are not so much worried for the next few months. What concerns us is the next year -- 2027," said Bimal Kothari, president of the Indian Pulses and Grains Association.

Sugar producers fear two difficult seasons ahead. "We are afraid that 2026-27 and 2027-28 can be extremely challenging for sugar production and availability," said Dilip Patil of the Indian Federation of Grain Energy.

Patil and sugar expert Shekhar Gaikwad recently presented a note to the Reserve Bank of India titled "A Comfortable Diwali, an Uncomfortable 2027." It argues the structural inflation risk in sugar is a 2027 problem, not a Diwali 2026 problem.

The duo predicted ex-mill sugar prices could cross Rs 50 per kilogram during June-October 2027. Thin closing stock, they wrote, will become the market's focal point -- and the real inflation test will arrive in mid-2027, not October 2026.

On October 5, El Nino-induced Pacific sea surface temperatures set a new record and are predicted to rise further. A UN body says the event could rank among the strongest since 1950, peaking in December and lasting through February.

Kothari said the real challenge is 2027 and much depends on how the rabi plantation takes place. Maharashtra and Karnataka, both critical chana states, are in drought. Prices of tur, chana, urad and yellow peas have already risen.

Atul Chaturvedi, director of Shree Renuka Sugars and head of the Asian Palm Oil Alliance, expects a big hit to sugar and oilseeds output. Sugarcane is drying up in Karnataka and parts of Maharashtra, and India is already import-dependent in oilseeds. "It would be interesting to see how this drought is going to impact rabi and beyond," he said.

The livestock sector is bracing for higher feed costs. Food inflation is India's most persistent inflation driver, so a Super El Nino peaking in December becomes a 2027 monetary policy problem. These are projections, not certainties.

The warning is unusually specific for a commodity outlook. Most food-price commentary lives three months ahead; the industry is now publishing a twelve-month map with a price target attached.

Sugar is where the map is most precise. The note presented to the RBI projects ex-mill prices crossing Rs 50 per kilogram between June and October 2027, driven by thin closing stock rather than any single failed harvest.

That framing matters for readers outside the farm economy. Sugar is both a consumer staple and a politically managed commodity — a 2027 squeeze would test buffer policies, import timing and ethanol-blending schedules simultaneously.

The weather mechanics sit underneath the commodity math. Sea surface temperatures in the Pacific set a fresh record on October 5, and the event is forecast to peak in December and persist through February.

A strong El Nino's fingerprints are already visible. Tur, chana, urad and yellow pea prices have moved up before any official production shock — markets price the forecast, not the harvest.

Pulses carry a different risk profile from sugar. India has no meaningful import buffer for chana the way it does for edible oils, which makes the rabi planting window in Maharashtra and Karnataka a single point of failure for 2027 prices.

The oilseed story loops back to trade. Atul Chaturvedi's warning on soyabean and groundnut lands on a country already dependent on imports for edible oils — a domestic shortfall cannot be solved without widening the import bill.

Livestock is the sleeper casualty. Feed costs were already rising before the El Nino peak, and animal protein prices tend to follow feed with a lag — a second-round inflation channel most forecasts skip.

The RBI presentation is the detail worth holding onto. When industry analysts take a commodity note to the central bank, they are asking for the risk to be priced into monetary policy — a signal that food inflation is again a rate-setting variable.

The note's own authors are careful about timing. "A Comfortable Diwali, an Uncomfortable 2027" is a forecast with a six-month preparation window, not a prediction of imminent shortage — the distinction is the story.

WHAT SUPPLY SHOCK IS INDIA'S FOOD INDUSTRY WARNING ABOUT?

HOW HIGH COULD SUGAR PRICES GO IN 2027?
SUGAR EXPERTS DILIP PATIL AND SHEKHAR GAIKWAD PREDICT EX-MILL SUGAR PRICES COULD CROSS RS 50 PER KILOGRAM DURING JUNE-OCTOBER 2027. THEY SAY THE 2026-27 BALANCE SHEET CARRIES VERY LITTLE BUFFER AND THE REAL INFLATION TEST WILL ARRIVE IN MID-2027, NOT IN OCTOBER 2026.
WHAT DID THE NOTE PRESENTED TO THE RBI SAY?
TITLED "A COMFORTABLE DIWALI, AN UNCOMFORTABLE 2027," THE NOTE PRESENTED TO THE RESERVE BANK OF INDIA SAYS THE STRUCTURAL INFLATION RISK IN SUGAR IS A 2027 PROBLEM, NOT A DIWALI 2026 PROBLEM. IT WARNS THAT THE WINDOW TO PREPARE FOR IT IS THE NEXT SIX MONTHS. SUGAR PRODUCERS FEAR THAT 2026-27 AND 2027-28 CAN BE EXTREMELY CHALLENGING FOR SUGAR PRODUCTION AND AVAILABILITY, EVEN IF THE NEAR TERM LOOKS COMFORTABLE.
WHICH CROPS AND STATES ARE MOST AT RISK FROM THE SUPER EL NINO?

ATUL CHATURVEDI, DIRECTOR OF SHREE RENUKA SUGARS, SAYS SOYABEAN AND GROUNDNUT ARE IN SERIOUS TROUBLE AND DOMESTIC EDIBLE OIL PRODUCTION WILL SUFFER SIGNIFICANTLY DURING KHARIF -- A CONCERN BECAUSE INDIA IS ALREADY IMPORT-DEPENDENT IN OILSEEDS.
HOW STRONG IS THE CURRENT EL NINO AND WHAT HAPPENS NEXT?

FORMER EARTH SCIENCES SECRETARY MADHAVAN RAJEEVAN WARNS OF A WARMER WINTER, A HOT AND CHALLENGING PRE-MONSOON SEASON AND AN INCREASED RISK OF SEVERE HEAT-WAVE EVENTS. GLOSSARY: Super El Nino: An unusually strong warming phase of Pacific sea surface temperatures that disrupts monsoon and rainfall patterns across Asia. Ex-mill price: The price of a commodity at the factory or mill gate, before transport and distribution costs. Rabi season: India's winter cropping season, typically sown from October and harvested in spring. Kharif season: India's monsoon cropping season, sown with the June-September rains. Closing stock: The inventory of a commodity remaining at the end of a marketing year, used as a buffer against supply shocks. Balance sheet (commodity): An accounting of a commodity's annual supply, demand and leftover stock.
