India GDP Q4 FY26 Seen At 7.3% As Domestic Demand Offsets Iran War Impact

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Median estimate of 10 economists pegs India's Q4 FY26 GDP growth at 7.3% YoY, down from 7.8% in Q3
  • 02/Full-year FY26 growth projected at 7.6%, matching the government's February estimate
  • 03/Sharply higher crude prices and energy supply disruptions from the Iran war could weigh on FY27 growth

The Indian economy likely closed FY26 on a firm footing. Growth in the March quarter was supported by robust domestic demand, agricultural activity, and services sector performance.

The median estimate from an ET poll of 10 economists pegged Q4 FY26 GDP growth at 7.3% year-on-year. Individual forecasts ranged from 6.7% to 7.4%.

Growth in Q4 represents a three-quarter low. It slowed from 7.8% in Q3 and 8.4% in Q2, reflecting the drag from the Iran conflict that began on February 28.

For the full fiscal year FY26, economists projected a median growth of 7.6%. Estimates ranged between 7.4% and 7.6%, aligning with the government's February forecast.

"The activity momentum in January and February continued to remain strong and likely offset the impact of the conflict in March," said Sakshi Gupta, principal economist at HDFC Bank.

Rajani Sinha, chief economist at CareEdge Ratings, noted that mining, utilities, and financial, real estate, and professional services maintained healthy momentum through the quarter.

Consumption data remained resilient. Domestic tractor retail volumes rose 23% in Q4. Two-wheeler sales climbed 25% during the same period.

However, industrial production growth moderated to 4.8% from 5.2% in Q3. Manufacturing expansion slowed, reflecting cost pressures that built across supply chains.

Radhika Rao, senior economist at DBS Bank, said companies likely absorbed these pressures by tapping inventories rather than scaling back production meaningfully.

Sharply higher crude prices and continued energy supply disruptions from the Iran war could weigh on growth in the current fiscal year. The National Statistical Office will release official Q4 and FY26 GDP data on June 5.
