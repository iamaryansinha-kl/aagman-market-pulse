India Extends RoDTEP Scheme Till December 31, 2026; Rates Unchanged

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/The government has extended the RoDTEP export remission scheme until December 31, 2026, keeping export refund rates unchanged
  • 02/The scheme was set to expire on September 30, 2026; remission rates range from roughly 0.3% to 4.3% of export value depending on the product category
  • 03/Exporters had sought continuity amid West Asia conflict-driven freight costs; the next watchpoint is a further extension or rate rationalisation beyond December 2026

The government on September 30, 2026 extended the Remission of Duties and Taxes on Exported Products (RoDTEP) scheme until December 31, 2026. Export refund rates under the scheme have been kept unchanged.

The decision gives exporters a three-month runway beyond the scheme's earlier expiry date of September 30, 2026. It follows sustained pressure from exporter groups dealing with continuing trade stress.

Without the extension, remission of embedded taxes on shipments would have stopped from October 1, 2026. The move removes that near-term policy cliff for export-facing businesses.

RoDTEP remits embedded central, state and local duties and taxes that are not refunded under any other mechanism. These levies include VAT on fuel, electricity duty and mandi tax.

Such embedded taxes raise the cost of Indian goods in overseas markets. By remitting them, the scheme aims to protect export competitiveness without altering headline tax rates.

The scheme works through e-scrips. Exporters receive these digital instruments against eligible shipments.

E-scrips can be used to pay basic customs duty. They can also be transferred or sold, giving exporters flexibility in how they monetise the benefit.

Remission rates vary by product category. They range from roughly 0.3% to about 4.3% of export value, depending on the product.

The scheme's validity was earlier set to expire on September 30, 2026. It had been extended from April 1 to September 30, 2026 at the rates applicable as on March 31, 2026.

The latest move continues that rolling approach. Rates applicable as on March 31, 2026 remain the reference point for benefits through the end of this extension.

In March 2026, the commerce and industry ministry withdrew a notification capping RoDTEP benefits at 50% of applicable rates. The decision restored full benefits to exporters.

That withdrawal came as exporters grappled with elevated freight costs. War-related trade risks linked to West Asia and Gulf maritime disruptions had squeezed shipping economics for Indian shippers.

The March decision meant exporters received the full notified rate rather than half of it. For high-volume shippers, even small rate changes carry a large absolute impact on margins.

Exporters were pushing for the latest extension as the September 30 expiry loomed. Continuing West Asia conflict-related trade stress kept the scheme in focus through September.

Reporting around the scheme has cited a budget allocation of about Rs 18,232 crore for FY26, underscoring the programme's fiscal scale for the exchequer.

What comes after December 31, 2026 remains open. Exporter bodies are expected to press for a longer runway and possible rationalisation of rates.

A longer-dated extension, rather than another short rollover, is what exporters have been seeking. That would give them certainty across full export order cycles rather than quarter by quarter.

Any fresh notification from the commerce and industry ministry covering the period beyond December 2026 will be the next signal. Exporters typically price contracts around rate certainty, so the timing of clarity will matter for order books.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT IS THE RODTEP SCHEME?
RODTEP STANDS FOR REMISSION OF DUTIES AND TAXES ON EXPORTED PRODUCTS. IT IS A GOVERNMENT SCHEME THAT REMITS EMBEDDED CENTRAL, STATE AND LOCAL DUTIES AND TAXES THAT ARE NOT REFUNDED UNDER ANY OTHER MECHANISM, SUCH AS VAT ON FUEL, ELECTRICITY DUTY AND MANDI TAX.
WHAT CHANGED ON SEPTEMBER 30, 2026?
ON SEPTEMBER 30, 2026, THE GOVERNMENT EXTENDED THE RODTEP SCHEME UNTIL DECEMBER 31, 2026. EXPORT REFUND RATES UNDER THE SCHEME WERE KEPT UNCHANGED FROM THE EXISTING STRUCTURE.
HOW DO E-SCRIPS WORK UNDER RODTEP?
EXPORTERS RECEIVE E-SCRIPS AGAINST ELIGIBLE SHIPMENTS, CALCULATED AS A PERCENTAGE OF EXPORT VALUE. THESE DIGITAL INSTRUMENTS CAN BE USED TO PAY BASIC CUSTOMS DUTY, OR TRANSFERRED AND SOLD, ALLOWING EXPORTERS TO MONETISE THE BENEFIT.
WHY WAS THE SCHEME IN FOCUS AMID THE WEST ASIA CONFLICT?
EXPORTERS FACED ELEVATED FREIGHT COSTS AND WAR-RELATED TRADE RISKS FROM WEST ASIA AND GULF MARITIME DISRUPTIONS. IN MARCH 2026, THE COMMERCE AND INDUSTRY MINISTRY WITHDREW A NOTIFICATION CAPPING RODTEP BENEFITS AT 50% OF APPLICABLE RATES, RESTORING FULL BENEFITS TO SUPPORT EXPORTERS.
WHAT HAPPENS AFTER DECEMBER 31, 2026?
THE SCHEME'S VALIDITY NOW ENDS ON DECEMBER 31, 2026. EXPORTERS ARE EXPECTED TO SEEK A FURTHER EXTENSION OR RATE RATIONALISATION, AND ANY NOTIFICATION FROM THE COMMERCE AND INDUSTRY MINISTRY ON THE PERIOD BEYOND DECEMBER 2026 WILL BE WATCHED CLOSELY.
