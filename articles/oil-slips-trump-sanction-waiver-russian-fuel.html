Oil Slips as Trump's Waiver Opens Door to Russian Fuel Supply

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Brent fell $1.43, or 1.3%, to $108.43 a barrel in early Asian trade after Friday's close at its highest since May.
  • •- Trump signed an executive order waiving sanctions and tariffs under the Graham Act, the law he signed last month to pressure Moscow.
  • •- Secretary of State Rubio said the president will take up to 60 days to assess the waiver's economic impact.
  • •- The waiver follows the Trump-Putin diesel export deal that Ukrainian President Zelenskyy said will hurt Ukraine.
  • •- Gulf crude flows collapsed to about 8.5 million barrels a day from over 20 million, Kpler data show, with 10+ vessel incidents last week.
  • •- Egypt offered Saudi Arabia support against Houthi attacks, adding another layer of risk.

The oil market spent the weekend absorbing a paradox, and on Monday it traded one side of it. Brent crude futures fell $1.43, or 1.3 per cent, to $108.43 a barrel in early Asian trading — a retreat from Friday's settlement, the highest since May 19, after prices briefly touched $110 for the first time in five months. The proximate cause: President Trump's executive order on Friday waiving key sanctions and tariffs under the Graham Act, the legislation he himself signed last month to pressure Moscow over Ukraine, which traders read as a door reopening to Russian fuel exports.

The waiver's mechanics matter more than its headline. The Graham Act framework imposed 500 per cent tariffs on Russian goods and 100 per cent tariffs on buyers of Russian oil; the executive order suspends those measures, potentially returning sanctioned Russian barrels to a market that has spent a month pricing their absence alongside a Middle East war. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the president will take up to 60 days to assess the economic impact of the waiver — a review window that keeps the relief reversible and, therefore, keeps a geopolitical premium in the price even as the physical relief looms. The move follows Trump's deal with Vladimir Putin to export Russian diesel to the United States, an arrangement Zelenskyy said openly will hurt Ukraine.

The price action is the market arbitrating between two forces pulling in opposite directions, and on Monday the supply side won the session. But the disruption side of the ledger did not soften overnight. Kpler data showed Gulf crude flows collapsed to roughly 8.5 million barrels per day on a seven-day average last week, down from more than 20 million the week before, with at least ten confirmed incidents involving commercial vessels — the tanker attacks and Strait of Hormuz insecurity that drove Friday's $110 spike. And over the weekend, Egypt said it stands ready to provide Saudi Arabia the support it needs to defend itself against Yemen's Iran-allied Houthis, a widening of the conflict's diplomatic perimeter that, if activated, threatens the Red Sea route as well as the Gulf.

For India, the arithmetic is immediate and unforgiving. Every dollar of Brent translates directly into the import bill, the current account deficit and the rupee's defence costs; a sustained move above $100 costs the economy several billion dollars a quarter, and Friday's $110 print was already feeding the inflation forecasts that pushed the RBI to tighten this month. A genuine Russian supply return — barrels that once flowed to India at a discount before the tariff regime redirected them — would ease that pressure materially, which is precisely why the Graham Act waiver of October 16, and its aftermath in the oil market, matters more to India's macro outlook than most domestic policy moves on the calendar. The RBI's oil-window facility for public-sector refiners exists for weeks like the last one; it would be gratefully retired for months like the next one.

The 60-day review window is where the trade lives now. If the assessment concludes that Russian supply can return without funding the war in a way Congress cannot tolerate, the waiver hardens into policy, the physical market loosens, and the geopolitical premium bleeds out of Brent — a bearish resolution consistent with the Monday morning price action. If the review instead collides with escalation — an Egyptian deployment, a Houthi strike on Saudi infrastructure, another week of Kpler flows near 8.5 million barrels — the waiver becomes a footnote to a supply crisis, and $110 becomes the floor rather than the ceiling. Both markets are real. Monday's $1.43 decline bet on the first one.

The episode also inverts the usual reading of sanctions politics. The Graham Act was designed to constrict Russian revenue; its waiver is now functioning as an oil-market loosening tool, with the American consumer — facing diesel prices elevated by the Gulf disruption — as the intended beneficiary of the Russian barrels it once barred. That the waiver came five weeks after the law's signing, and days after the US began buying Russian diesel itself, tells exporters everywhere that American energy sanctions now carry an implicit expiry tied to pump prices at home. For India, which absorbed months of tariff pressure for its own Russian-oil purchases before Washington reversed course, the lesson is less about oil than about negotiating from strength: the waiver India's refiners would have welcomed in July arrived in October, on American terms, with the 60-day clock already running.

Traders watching the tape this week will hold both scenarios without choosing between them — the waiver's supply arithmetic and the Gulf's disruption arithmetic are not mutually exclusive, and a market that just printed $110 and $108 within thirty hours of the same weekend is confessing that it cannot yet price their combination. The options market, where crude volatility has jumped alongside the tanker incidents, is charging for that uncertainty. Whoever resolves it first — the 60-day reviewers in Washington or the shipping insurers in the Gulf — will set the direction of the year's most important macro variable for India.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHY DID OIL PRICES FALL ON MONDAY?
Brent slipped 1.3% to $108.43 after Trump's executive order waiving Graham Act sanctions, which traders expect could return Russian fuel exports to global markets.
WHAT IS THE GRAHAM ACT WAIVER?
An executive order suspending sanctions and tariffs the US imposed on Russia and its oil buyers, signed Friday and reversible during a 60-day economic review.
WHAT IS HAPPENING TO GULF SUPPLY?
Kpler data show Gulf crude flows fell to about 8.5 million barrels a day from over 20 million the prior week, with at least 10 commercial vessel incidents reported.
HOW DOES THIS AFFECT INDIA?
Sustained oil above $100 enlarges the import bill and current account deficit; a Russian supply return would ease that pressure and reduce the cost of defending the rupee. GLOSSARY: - Graham Act: US legislation imposing tariffs on Russian goods and on buyers of Russian oil, now partially waived. - Kpler: A commodities analytics firm tracking shipping and energy flows. - Geopolitical premium: The portion of an oil price reflecting supply-risk fears rather than current supply and demand. - Seven-day average: A rolling weekly mean used to smooth daily volatility in flow data.
