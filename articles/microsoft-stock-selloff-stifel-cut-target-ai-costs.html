Microsoft Stock Selloff Deepens: Stifel Cuts Target to $400 on AI Cost Worries

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Microsoft stock fell 3.45% to $352.83 on June 25, touching a 52-week low of $349.20 amid AI investment cost concerns and Xbox price hikes
  • 02/Stifel lowered its price target from $415 to $400 while maintaining a Hold rating, citing margin pressure from heavy AI infrastructure spending
  • 03/Analysts remain bullish long-term with an average price target of $561, viewing Azure growth and Copilot adoption as key recovery drivers

Microsoft Corporation (NASDAQ: MSFT) closed at $352.83 on June 25, 2026. The stock dropped 3.45%, or $12.62, hitting a new 52-week low of $349.20.

Stifel cut its price target on Microsoft from $415 to $400. The firm maintained a Hold rating, flagging concerns over the company's aggressive AI spending.

Microsoft has fallen 28% year-to-date and 27% over the past year. The stock now trades well below its 52-week high of $555.45 reached in mid-2025.

The selloff intensified after Microsoft announced higher Xbox console prices. The company cited rising component costs driven by global AI chip shortages.

Analysts say the pullback reflects margin worries, not weak demand. Microsoft's trailing P/E has compressed to 21x from over 35x at its peak.

Wall Street still sees upside. The average analyst price target stands at $561.11, implying 59% potential upside from current levels.

Microsoft's partnership with OpenAI and rapid Azure AI expansion remain core strengths. Copilot integration across Office and enterprise tools continues to drive revenue.

Investors are watching Azure cloud growth, Copilot adoption rates, and capital expenditure trends. Microsoft reports Q4 FY2026 earnings on July 29, 2026.
