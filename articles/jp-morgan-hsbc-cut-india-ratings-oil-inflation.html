JP Morgan, HSBC Slash India Ratings on Oil Surge and Inflation Risks

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/JP Morgan downgrades Indian equities to Neutral from Overweight, HSBC cuts to Underweight amid oil and inflation concerns
  • 02/Both brokerages flag energy supply disruptions, elevated valuations, and earnings risks as key headwinds for 2026
  • 03/Long-term structural growth story remains intact; investors should watch oil prices and earnings revisions

JP Morgan has downgraded Indian equities to Neutral from Overweight. The brokerage cited elevated valuations, earnings risks, and limited exposure to next-generation technology themes.

HSBC separately cut India to Underweight from Neutral. The firm flagged macroeconomic vulnerabilities tied to energy dependence and inflation risks.

JP Morgan said energy supply disruptions are likely to pressure earnings across sectors. The brokerage has already revised down earnings estimates for multiple companies.

The firm also warned that aggressive promoter stake sales and record capital issuance are capping upside potential. This dilution concerns existing shareholders.

HSBC noted that India's reliance on imported energy creates knock-on effects on inflation and domestic demand. A renewed rise in inflation could undermine demand recovery.

The brokerage warned higher inflation may contribute to elevated non-performing loans. This creates downside risks to 2026 earnings forecasts.

Both firms said current geopolitical tensions and elevated crude prices have shifted risk-reward toward other emerging markets. JP Morgan sees better opportunities elsewhere in EM until valuations de-rate further.

However, both brokerages maintained that India's long-term fundamentals remain intact. JP Morgan emphasised that policy stability and sustained domestic inflows support the structural growth story.
