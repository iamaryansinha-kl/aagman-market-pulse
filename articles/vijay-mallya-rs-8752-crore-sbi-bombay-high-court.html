Vijay Mallya Owes Rs 8,752 Crore More, Banks Tell Court

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/SBI-led consortium told Bombay High Court that Rs 8,751.86 crore remained recoverable from Vijay Mallya as of August 31, excluding legal and other expenses
  • 02/The debt recovery officer has "temporarily recovered" Rs 10,270 crore so far, backed by a bond undertaking from the consortium
  • 03/Mallya's plea to quash the money laundering case comes up next on October 13; the ED says recovery of assets does not extinguish PMLA proceedings

A State Bank of India-led consortium of lenders told the Bombay High Court that nearly Rs 8,752 crore remained recoverable from fugitive businessman Vijay Mallya as of August 31. The submission directly contradicts Mallya's argument that banks have already recovered amounts exceeding their dues.

The debt recovery officer has reclaimed Rs 10,270 crore so far, the banks said in an affidavit. They described the sum as "temporarily recovered," backed by a bond undertaking from the consortium.

Another Rs 544.58 crore had been recovered before the lenders filed their suit in the Debt Recovery Tribunal. At the time of an earlier affidavit filed in January 2026, Rs 8,135.63 crore remained recoverable, excluding legal and other costs.

The outstanding amount has since climbed to Rs 8,751.86 crore as of August 31, excluding legal and other expenses. Banks say the rising figure reflects subsequent accruals under the recovery certificate.

"The recovery figure cannot be viewed in isolation from the amount that continues to remain due under the recovery certificate, including subsequent accruals," a senior banker familiar with the matter said. "The consortium position is that substantial sums are still legally recoverable despite the assets and proceeds realised so far."

Mallya is the founder of defunct Kingfisher Airlines and former chairman of United Breweries Group. He moved the high court in 2020 seeking dismissal of criminal cases against him, arguing his dispute with the banks stood settled because they had fully recovered their dues.

The consortium has opposed the plea. It contends the criminal application, which challenges an earlier order dated December 31, 2019, is misconceived and should be dismissed.

The Enforcement Directorate has filed its say in the matter. The agency says assets worth about Rs 14,132 crore have been restored to SBI, but maintains such recoveries settle civil claims and do not by themselves extinguish criminal proceedings under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act.

"The restoration of assets under Section 8(8) of the PMLA is a statutory mechanism for restitution to a claimant having a legitimate interest in the property and does not, by itself, determine the existence of the scheduled offence or the offence of money laundering," the ED said. "The subsequent restoration or recovery of assets cannot be construed as extinguishing or rendering infructuous the pending proceedings under the PMLA."

The dispute runs on two parallel tracks. Recovery of bank dues proceeds through the Debt Recovery Tribunal and the sale or restitution of attached assets. Criminal prosecution, pursued by the ED and CBI, covers allegations of bank fraud, criminal conspiracy and money laundering linked to loans extended to Kingfisher Airlines.

Banks also told the court that certain shares attached in the case remain intact and have not been sold. Last month, the high court directed SBI to file its affidavit in reply.

Mallya was declared a fugitive economic offender in 2019 and has argued that lenders' recoveries exceed the original decreed debt. The matter is scheduled to be heard next on October 13.

MALLYA, FOUNDER OF DEFUNCT KINGFISHER AIRLINES, ARGUES THAT BANKS HAVE RECOVERED AMOUNTS EXCEEDING THE ORIGINAL DECREED DEBT. THE CONSORTIUM REJECTS THIS, SAYING SUBSTANTIAL SUMS REMAIN LEGALLY RECOVERABLE UNDER THE RECOVERY CERTIFICATE, INCLUDING SUBSEQUENT ACCRUALS.

THE ED SAYS ASSETS WORTH ABOUT RS 14,132 CRORE HAVE BEEN RESTORED TO SBI. IT ARGUES THAT RESTORATION UNDER SECTION 8(8) OF THE PMLA SETTLES CIVIL CLAIMS BUT DOES NOT EXTINGUISH PENDING CRIMINAL PROCEEDINGS UNDER THE PMLA.

THE BOMBAY HIGH COURT IS SCHEDULED TO HEAR MALLYA'S PLEA SEEKING TO QUASH THE MONEY LAUNDERING CASE NEXT ON OCTOBER 13. THE CONSORTIUM HAS OPPOSED THE PLEA, CALLING THE CRIMINAL APPLICATION MISCONCEIVED.
