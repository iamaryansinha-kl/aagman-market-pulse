Torrent Power Plans ₹4,000 Crore Bond Sale to Fund Nabha Power Deal

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Torrent Power plans to raise up to ₹4,000 crore via its largest-ever corporate bond issue to fund the Nabha Power acquisition from Larsen & Toubro
  • 02/The deal values Nabha Power at approximately ₹6,900 crore including debt; India's competition regulator approved the transaction earlier this month
  • 03/Bonds are rated AA+ by Crisil and India Ratings; market entry is expected in May 2026 with banker discussions likely concluding within the next week

Torrent Power Ltd plans to raise as much as ₹4,000 crore ($421.8 million) through its largest-ever corporate bond issue. The power producer expects to tap the market in May 2026. Bankers say discussions are likely to conclude within the next week.

Proceeds will fund Torrent Power's acquisition of Nabha Power from Larsen & Toubro for approximately ₹6,900 crore including debt. India's competition regulator approved the transaction earlier this month.

Nabha Power operates a 1,400-megawatt coal plant in Punjab under a 25-year power purchase agreement. It supplies all output to the state power corporation.

The planned bond issue will feature staggered redemptions with principal repaid in multiple installments. The company may also issue three-year bonds to attract large mutual funds and lower the weighted average cost of borrowing.

"The company has also tied up with some mutual funds, so it is highly possible that they end up splitting the quantum in shorter as well as longer tenors," one of the bankers said. The bankers requested anonymity as they are not authorised to speak to the media.

In March, Torrent Power raised ₹2,000 crore via staggered redemption bonds at 7.97% annual coupon. Those bonds carried maturities of eight, nine and ten years.

The new bonds are rated AA+ by Crisil and India Ratings. Bond issuance for acquisitions gathered pace in 2025 as firms sought to diversify beyond bank loans.

The acquisition will add to Torrent Power's push to scale its thermal portfolio. Utilities are turning to coal-based capacity to meet India's rising electricity demand.
