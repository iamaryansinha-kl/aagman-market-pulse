NSE IPO Grey Market Premium Slides to Lowest Level Since Issue Launch

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

The grey market is losing some of its enthusiasm for the National Stock Exchange's blockbuster IPO. The grey market premium on the Rs 22,569 crore issue has fallen to its lowest level since the IPO was announced, signalling that expectations of a big listing-day pop are fading.

The numbers tell the story. Before the price band was announced, the GMP peaked at around Rs 310 per share in early September. It has slid steadily since: Rs 273 on September 6, Rs 228 by September 9, Rs 198 by September 15, Rs 145 on September 17 and around Rs 142, or an 8% premium over the Rs 1,785 upper price band, as the issue sailed through its subscription window. Current grey market levels of roughly Rs 140 put the indicative listing price near Rs 1,925.

The structure of the issue is a key reason. The IPO is entirely an offer for sale of 12.64 crore shares, so NSE itself receives no proceeds and no fresh capital enters the business. Investors buying at listing are paying early shareholders, including LIC with a 10.72% stake and Temasek with roughly 4.5%, rather than funding growth.

Earnings momentum is another factor. NSE's revenue from operations fell 3.1% to Rs 16,601 crore in FY26, and profit after tax dropped to Rs 10,302 crore from Rs 12,188 crore, hit by regulatory changes to derivatives volumes. Margins remain exceptional, with EBITDA margins above 67%, but the direction of earnings has given some investors pause.

Valuation is rich but not extreme. At the upper price band, NSE is valued at 42.9 times FY26 earnings, a 21% discount to BSE's 54.3 times, according to YES Securities. Brokerages including Angel One, LKP and SBI Securities have still recommended subscribing, citing NSE's market leadership, debt-free balance sheet and structural tailwinds from India's deepening capital markets.

The IPO closes on September 21, allotment is on September 22 and shares are set to list on September 24. Grey market premiums are an unofficial and volatile indicator, but the trajectory is clear: from Rs 310 to Rs 140 in two weeks. The market still expects a premium debut, just a more measured one.

For applicants, the question has shifted. It is no longer whether NSE will list above its issue price, but whether an 8% grey market premium justifies expectations that were, just weeks ago, more than double that.
