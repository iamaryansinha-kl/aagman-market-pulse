SP Group ₹22,000 Cr Refinancing: LTV Extended to September 30

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Anchor investors including Ares, Cerberus, and Deutsche Bank agree to extend LTV ratio relaxation on pledged Tata Sons securities until September 30, 2026
  • 02/The approval clears the way for SP Group's ₹22,000 crore dual-currency refinancing, planned for launch in the first week of July
  • 03/Porteast Investment's $3.4 billion bonds, issued at 19.75% yield with 18.38% Tata Sons stake as collateral, face near-term maturities requiring immediate liquidity

Shapoorji Pallonji Group has secured a critical approval from its anchor bond investors. Global funds including Ares Management, Cerberus Capital Management, Deutsche Bank, Davidson Kempner, and Farallon Capital have agreed to extend the relaxation on loan-to-value ratios.

The LTV covenant relaxation will now remain in effect until September 30, 2026. This extension was essential for the group to proceed with its refinancing plans.

The group is targeting a ₹22,000 crore dual-currency refinancing deal led by Deutsche Bank. The transaction is planned for launch in the first week of July and closure by mid-month.

This marks one of the largest private refinancing transactions in India. The funds will address near-term maturities on Porteast Investment's $3.4 billion bonds.

The bonds were issued last year at a yield of 19.75%. The group's 18.38% stake in Tata Sons was pledged as collateral.

Lenders had previously allowed the LTV threshold to increase to 40% from 34% until July 15. The extension to September provides additional breathing room.

Earlier, some investors led by Ares had demanded clarity on Tata Sons stake monetisation. They also sought specifics on the group's broader deleveraging roadmap.

"The discussion was around obtaining clarity on the LTV covenant rather than seeking any commitment on a Tata Sons listing," one person involved said.

Separately, Goswami Infratech's ₹14,300 crore bond investors approved a maturity extension to July 31. The company agreed to pay a 30 basis point consent fee.
