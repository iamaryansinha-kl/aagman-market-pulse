Rupee Hits Record Low 95.33 Per Dollar as Oil Spike, FII Outflows Batter Currency

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Indian rupee plunges to record low of 95.33 per dollar on April 30, breaching prior low of 95.21 from late March
  • 02/Brent crude surges to $126.41 per barrel, highest in four years; foreign investors pull $20 billion in March-April
  • 03/Barclays warns rupee could hit 96.80 by year-end; RBI faces pressure to tighten policy or curb dollar demand

The Indian rupee fell to a record low of 95.33 per dollar on April 30, 2026. The currency dropped 0.5% during the session, eclipsing its previous all-time low of 95.21 hit in late March.

It later pared losses to close at 94.91, only marginally weaker than the previous session. The intraday swing reflects the tug-of-war between depreciation forces and central bank intervention.

Brent crude futures surged to $126.41 per barrel, the highest in four years. Oil-sensitive Asian currencies, including the Indonesian rupiah, also weakened on the day amid rising geopolitical risk.

The rupee had rallied to 92.40 earlier in April following rare regulatory measures by the Reserve Bank of India. Those gains have now been fully erased, leaving the currency flat month-on-month.

Foreign investors have offloaded over $20 billion of Indian stocks and bonds in March and April combined. This is nearly double the $11.8 billion of outflows seen across all of 2025.

"After breaking through the key psychologically important level of USD/INR 95.0, risks of further INR weakness remain," Barclays analysts said in a note. They see potential to hit their year-end forecast of 96.80 sooner than expected.

"India's historical patterns also show that higher oil prices often feed into inflation and eventually force the Reserve Bank of India to respond," said Vivek Rajpal, Asia macro strategist at JB Drax Honore.

The rupee has declined nearly 5% so far in 2026, adding to a similar drop last year. India imports roughly 85% of its crude oil, leaving the current account vulnerable to supply shocks in the Strait of Hormuz.
