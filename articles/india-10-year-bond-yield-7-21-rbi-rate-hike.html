India 10-Year Bond Yield Hits 7.21%, Highest in 2.5 Years

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/India's 10-year benchmark bond yield closed at 7.21% on October 1, breaching the psychologically significant 7.20% mark and hitting a 2.5-year high
  • 02/The domestic bond tracked its US counterpart, where the 10-year yield stands at 5.33%, the highest since 2002
  • 03/All eyes are now on the RBI's rate decision next week, with markets pricing in a hike to 5.50%, plus Rs 3.3-3.5 lakh crore of state government bond supply

India's 10-year benchmark bond yield closed at 7.21% on Thursday, October 1. That is its highest level in two and a half years, according to CCIL data.

The yield breached the psychologically significant 7.20% threshold during the session. It had closed at 7.18% in the previous session.

Traders are now positioning for a rate hike from the Reserve Bank of India next week. Markets widely expect the central bank to raise the repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.50%.

Yields are also being pushed higher by crude oil prices, which remain elevated. The combination of expensive oil and tightening expectations has turned sentiment firmly bearish.

The next target for market participants is 7.50%. Yields last touched that level in October 2022, nearly three years ago.

The domestic bond market did not move in isolation. India's 10-year tracked its US counterpart, where the yield stands at 5.33%, the highest since 2002, according to Reuters.

Supply pressure is adding to the weakness. The government sold debt worth Rs 33,000 crore on Thursday, keeping the borrowing calendar heavy.

More supply is on the way. State governments are expected to sell bonds worth Rs 3.3 lakh crore to Rs 3.5 lakh crore next week, which could further weaken the supply-demand balance.

"There is a healthy supply of bonds, both central and state, and as per today's price action, it seems like the market is pricing in a 50 bps hike. Yields will likely inch up further if there is a 25 bps hike, and we may see a relief rally if the RBI hikes by 50 bps. Nevertheless, I think we would touch a peak of 7.50% in benchmark yields in this cycle," said Rajeev Pawar, head of treasury at Ujjivan Small Finance Bank.

Pawar's comments highlight how much hawkishness is already baked into bond prices. If the RBI delivers only the expected 25 basis points, some traders believe yields could still grind higher before finding a ceiling.

Not everyone expects a straight line upward. A bond trader at a large public sector bank said buying interest may emerge once yields reach 7.25%.

"Market sentiment will remain bearish until oil prices cool, the MPC is further adding to the volatility. I think yields would retrace from 7.25%, as there could be some buying interest that emerges at that level," the trader said.

The Monetary Policy Committee's decision is the key event risk. Any surprise in the size of the hike, or in the commentary around future tightening, is likely to drive the next leg of the move.

For context, a rise in bond yields pushes up government borrowing costs across the economy. It also tightens financial conditions for banks, companies, and home loan borrowers.

The timing adds another complication. India's bond market will remain shut on Friday, October 2, for Gandhi Jayanti. Trading resumes with the RBI's rate announcement as the next major catalyst.
