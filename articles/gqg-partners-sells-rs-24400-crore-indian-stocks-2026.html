GQG Partners Sells Rs 24,400 Crore of Indian Stocks in 2026

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/GQG Partners has trimmed Indian stakes worth about Rs 24,400 crore in 2026, including a Rs 9,395 crore ITC block sale on October 8
  • 02/The firm has cut Adani Group holdings by roughly Rs 13,200 crore since January, reversing the rescue trade it made during the 2023 Hindenburg selloff
  • 03/June quarter adds of JSW Energy (~Rs 1,941 crore) and JSW Steel (~Rs 3,054 crore) point to a rotation into power and commodities

GQG Partners has sold roughly Rs 24,400 crore of Indian stocks so far in 2026. The figure, compiled from Prime Database and BSE data, includes the firm's latest and largest single exit — a block sale of nearly 36.51 crore ITC shares worth about Rs 9,395 crore on October 8.

The size of that ITC sale stands out. As of the June 2026 quarter, GQG held 3.12% of ITC, equal to 38.99 crore shares. The October 8 block deal accounted for nearly all of that position.

The Florida-based asset manager oversees more than $156 billion in assets globally. Even so, the pace of its India exits this year has been striking — before the ITC deal, the firm had already pared around Rs 15,000 crore of stakes across its portfolio since January 2026.

GQG, founded by Rajiv Jain, occupies a unique place in India's market history. It is best known for its 2023 bet on Adani Group stocks, buying aggressively during the sharp selloff that followed Hindenburg Research's allegations against the group.

That rescue trade is now being unwound. Since January, GQG has cut its Adani Group exposure by about Rs 13,200 crore. Adani Enterprises saw the biggest trim at roughly Rs 6,375 crore.

The other Adani reductions were smaller but broad-based, touching nearly every listed group company. Adani Green was cut by about Rs 2,382 crore, Adani Power by Rs 1,910 crore, Adani Ports by Rs 1,344 crore and Adani Energy Solutions by Rs 1,226 crore.

Beyond the Adani names and ITC, GQG's name disappeared from the shareholding lists of Lodha Developers and ITC Hotels by the June 2026 quarter. Those positions were worth about Rs 1,060 crore and Rs 811 crore respectively at the end of December 2025.

The disappearance from the quarterly lists does not confirm a full exit. Shareholding patterns only disclose investors at or above the 1% threshold, so GQG may still hold smaller residual stakes in both companies.

The selling has come alongside fresh buying, which is what makes the 2026 pattern a rotation rather than a retreat. In the June quarter, GQG added JSW Energy (~Rs 1,941 crore) and JSW Steel (~Rs 3,054 crore).

The direction of the new money is telling. A firm that once championed Adani, held consumer staple ITC and backed real estate and hotels is now concentrated in steel and power — cyclical, commodity-linked businesses.

That contrast is the real story for foreign value investors watching India. GQG appears to be exiting defensive and consumer names in favour of commodity and energy exposure, a judgement call on where relative value sits in the Indian market.

The market felt the selling on Thursday. In a broad selloff, Adani Green dropped 7.5%, Adani Enterprises fell 5.4%, Adani Energy Solutions 5.1%, Adani Power 4.4%, Adani Total Gas 3.5% and Adani Ports 2.6%.

September-quarter shareholding data, due later this month, will show whether GQG's exit from ITC was completed and whether the Adani trims continued through July and September.
