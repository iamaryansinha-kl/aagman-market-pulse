Dr Reddy's Shares Crash 9% in 2 Days; Brokerages Slash Target Prices After Semaglutide Delay

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

The selloff in Dr Reddy's is accelerating.

Shares of the pharmaceutical company have crashed 9% in just two trading sessions after it reported delays in semaglutide supplies due to quality issues. Brokerages have responded by slashing target prices, with several cutting their estimates by 10-15% to reflect the lost revenue and reputational damage.

The semaglutide delay is more serious than initially thought. What was first reported as a minor supply disruption has now emerged as a significant manufacturing issue that could take weeks or months to resolve. The company has not provided a clear timeline, adding to investor anxiety.

Brokerages are recalculating their models. Morgan Stanley, Citigroup, and ICICI Securities have all reduced their revenue and earnings estimates for the current and next fiscal years. The semaglutide launch had been priced in as a major growth driver, and the delay removes that upside.

The stock has now fallen below key technical levels. After breaking below the 50-day and 200-day moving averages, the shares are in a clear downtrend. Technical analysts see further downside to the Rs 1,100-1,150 range if sentiment does not improve.

Quality issues raise broader questions. Dr Reddy's has built a reputation for complex generic drug manufacturing. The semaglutide problems — a biological product that requires specialised facilities — suggest that the company may have overstretched its capabilities.

Competitors are watching closely. Other Indian pharma companies with semaglutide ambitions, including Sun Pharma and Cipla, may see this as an opportunity to accelerate their own launches. The diabetes and obesity market in India is large and growing rapidly.
