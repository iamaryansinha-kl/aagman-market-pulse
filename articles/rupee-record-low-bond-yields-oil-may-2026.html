Rupee Hits Record Low: Slides to 96.39 on Bond Yields, Oil Price Shock

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/The Indian rupee hit a record low of 96.3875 per US dollar on May 18, falling for the seventh consecutive trading session amid a global bond selloff and elevated oil prices
  • 02/HSBC economists forecast India's balance of payments deficit at approximately $65 billion for the fiscal year ending April 2027, marking a third straight year of deficit
  • 03/Overseas investors have pulled over $23 billion from Indian equities and bonds since March, while US 10-year Treasury yields surged to a 15-month high of 4.631%

The Indian rupee fell to a fresh record low of 96.3875 per US dollar on Monday, May 18, 2026. The currency closed the session at 96.3450, down 0.4% from Friday's close. This marked the seventh consecutive trading session of decline.

The rupee has now slumped 2% over the past seven sessions. Traders said losses would have been steeper without intervention by the Reserve Bank of India. The RBI has been actively selling dollars to manage volatility.

The latest slide eclipsed the previous all-time low of 96.1350 recorded just days earlier. The rupee remains Asia's worst-performing currency in 2026 so far.

Elevated energy prices and weak capital flows have left India staring at a third consecutive fiscal year of balance of payments deficit. HSBC economists forecast a BoP deficit of around $65 billion for FY2026-27.

"The continued distribution of exchange market pressure between currency weakness and the use of FX reserves should likely continue," HSBC economists wrote in a Monday note. They added that rupee weakness can help lower the trade deficit by boosting export competitiveness.

Overseas investors have sold over $23 billion of Indian stocks and bonds on a net basis since March. This capital flight has strained the external account at a time when import costs are already elevated.

A global bond market selloff has intensified the pressure on emerging market currencies. Yields on US 10-year Treasury notes surged to a 15-month high of 4.631%. Japan's 10-year yield hit its highest level since 1996, while India's benchmark 10-year yield climbed 6 basis points.

"High oil prices and now a selloff at the long end of the bond market are a bearish double whammy for EMFX and for risk assets in general," analysts at ING said in a client note.

Brent crude futures hovered around $110 per barrel on Monday. The Iran war has disrupted energy supplies, with efforts to end the conflict appearing to stall after a drone strike at a nuclear power plant in the UAE.

India imports nearly 90% of its crude oil requirements. The sustained spike in energy prices is widening the current account deficit and driving up dollar demand from importers.
