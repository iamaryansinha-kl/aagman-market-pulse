India Accelerates Trade Talks With Canada and Mexico as $8 Trillion Alliance Seeks Cover From Trump Tariffs

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

India is accelerating trade negotiations with Canada and Mexico as all three countries seek to diversify their commercial ties amid disruptions caused by US President Donald Trump's tariff policies.

India-Canada Fifth Round From October 5

Negotiators from India and Canada plan to hold a fifth round of talks from October 5, building on discussions that concluded last week. A deal is likely to be signed in December when Prime Minister Narendra Modi is expected to visit Canada.

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney on Tuesday confirmed Modi's visit to Canada in December, around the time the Indian leader is expected to head to the G20 meeting in Miami. Carney described Canada's relationship with India as "exceptionally important."

India-Mexico Framework

Separately, New Delhi is finalizing a framework with Mexico to begin formal trade negotiations. Officials are seeking faster progress as all three countries look to diversify their commercial ties.

Mexico's Economy Ministry said there is ongoing analysis for a potential deal, although no concrete or final proposal has been made yet.

$8 Trillion Combined Output

The three economies have a combined economic output of more than $8 trillion. They are all grappling with disruption from Trump's trade policies.

Mexico is scrambling to reach an agreement with the US as the two sides negotiate changes to their existing pact. Canada-US talks collapsed last month. The US is by far the biggest export market for both Canada and Mexico.

India is contending with fresh tariff threats from a new US law that gives Trump the power to impose levies of as much as 100% on countries that purchase Russian oil and gas. New Delhi reached an interim trade deal with the US earlier this year and has been locked in negotiations over the tariff rate.

India's two-way trade with Canada and Mexico stood at nearly $8 billion each in 2025-26.

Mexico is seeking greater access to India's market for tequila, fruit and auto components. New Delhi wants Mexico to lower barriers on automobiles, auto parts and steel.

India's Broader Trade Push

India's outreach to Canada and Mexico is part of a broader push to expand trade ties. Over the past year, New Delhi has signed trade agreements with the UK, Oman and New Zealand and wrapped up years of negotiations with the European Union.
