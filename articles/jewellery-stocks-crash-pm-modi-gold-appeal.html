Jewellery Stocks Crash Up to 12% After PM Modi's Gold Purchase Appeal

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Titan, Kalyan Jewellers, and Senco Gold crash up to 12% after PM Modi asks citizens to postpone non-essential gold purchases to conserve forex reserves
  • 02/India's gold import bill hit a record $72 billion in FY26, up from $58 billion in FY25, straining foreign exchange reserves
  • 03/Analysts warn of near-term demand pressure for jewellery companies, though long-term cultural demand may remain resilient

Jewellery stocks crashed on Monday, May 11, 2026, after Prime Minister Narendra Modi urged citizens to postpone non-essential gold purchases. The appeal aims to conserve foreign exchange reserves amid rising crude oil prices and global uncertainty.

Titan Company (NSE: TITAN) shares closed 6.73 per cent lower at ₹4,205.60. The stock had tumbled as much as 8 per cent to ₹4,150.10 during the session.

Kalyan Jewellers India (NSE: KALYANKJIL) shares tanked over 9 per cent to end at ₹385.20. The stock hit a low of ₹382.10 from its previous close of ₹424.55.

Senco Gold (NSE: SENCO) shares slumped 8.5 per cent to ₹334.25, touching a low of ₹325.05. Thangamayil Jewellery fell nearly 6 per cent to close at ₹4,007.10. Sky Gold and Diamonds shares tumbled more than 12 per cent to ₹475 during the session. P N Gadgil Jewellers and Bluestone Jewellery also declined up to 9 per cent.

India is among the world's largest gold importers. The country's gold import bill surged to a record $72 billion in FY26 from $58 billion in FY25, despite lower import volumes, as global gold prices rose sharply. India's forex reserves have declined from a peak of $728 billion in February 2026 to around $691 billion by early May. The RBI has intervened to support the rupee as foreign investors pulled money out of domestic equities.

Khushi Mistry, Research Analyst at Bonanza, said the jewellery sector could face near-term demand pressure after the Prime Minister's appeal. She noted that gold demand in India remains deeply rooted culturally and is unlikely to see sustained impact unless accompanied by policy measures such as higher import duties.

Abhinav Tiwari, Research Analyst at Bonanza, said the appeal aims to ease pressure on India's foreign exchange reserves. He added that India imports nearly 88 per cent of its oil requirements, and the ongoing West Asia conflict has pushed crude prices higher, widening the trade deficit.

Jateen Trivedi, VP Research Analyst - Commodity and Currency at LKP Securities, said the appeal is unlikely to significantly change long-term Indian demand for gold. He noted that gold remains deeply linked to savings, investment, and cultural buying patterns.
