ALPA Demands Full FDTL Rules After Two Pilot Deaths, DGCA Under Fire

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Two pilots from Air India and Akasa Air died of cardiac arrest within 24 hours this week, prompting ALPA to demand action
  • 02/ALPA India says DGCA exemptions to airlines have diluted FDTL rules originally meant as transitional measures
  • 03/IndiGo was previously granted exemptions until February 10 after 5,500+ cancellations; Air India got relaxations in March 2026

Airline Pilots' Association of India (ALPA) has written to the Directorate General of Civil Aviation demanding full implementation of flight duty time limitation rules. The demand follows the untimely deaths of two pilots from Air India and Akasa Air within a 24-hour span this week.

ALPA India president Captain Sam Thomas said DGCA extensions to airlines have "materially diluted" the FDTL regulations. The revised rules came into effect on November 1, 2025, but carriers continue to operate under exemptions.

The revised FDTL norms mandate 48 consecutive hours of weekly rest, up from 36 hours previously. Night duty is now defined as 12 am to 6 am, and pilots are capped at two night landings with no more than two consecutive night duties.

IndiGo received exemptions until February 10, 2026, after cancelling over 5,500 flights and stranding approximately 3 lakh passengers in December 2025. The DGCA had penalized the airline with Rs 22.2 crore for the operational collapse.

In March 2026, Air India was granted relaxation in duty time for long-haul flights. The exemption was necessitated by airspace closures following the Iran-US conflict, but ALPA says such variations have "effectively become the norm."

India's aviation market handled over 16 crore domestic passengers in FY24-25. IndiGo controls over 60 percent of the domestic market, making its compliance critical for overall sector safety.

ALPA has asked DGCA to initiate a time-bound withdrawal of all variations. The association also wants airlines to submit quarterly fatigue reports in a standardized format and make the data public.

"Recent aviation incidents further reinforce the need to ensure that flight crew are not merely compliant with limits but are genuinely well-rested and operationally fit," ALPA said in its letter to the regulator on Thursday.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT HAS ALPA INDIA DEMANDED FROM THE DGCA?
ALPA INDIA HAS DEMANDED A TIME-BOUND ROADMAP FOR FULL IMPLEMENTATION OF FLIGHT DUTY TIME LIMITATION (FDTL) RULES AND THE WITHDRAWAL OF ALL EXEMPTIONS GRANTED TO AIRLINES. THE ASSOCIATION SAYS THESE VARIATIONS HAVE DILUTED SAFETY STANDARDS.
WHY DID ALPA RAISE THIS DEMAND NOW?
THE DEMAND FOLLOWS THE DEATHS OF TWO PILOTS FROM AIR INDIA AND AKASA AIR WITHIN 24 HOURS THIS WEEK. ALPA SAYS THESE INCIDENTS HIGHLIGHT THE RISKS OF FATIGUE AND THE MISUSE OF CONSECUTIVE NIGHT DUTIES.
WHAT ARE THE KEY PROVISIONS OF THE REVISED FDTL RULES?
THE RULES MANDATE 48 CONSECUTIVE HOURS OF WEEKLY REST, DEFINE NIGHT DUTY AS 12 AM TO 6 AM, CAP NIGHT LANDINGS AT TWO PER PILOT, AND RESTRICT CONSECUTIVE NIGHT DUTIES TO TWO PER WEEK. THEY TOOK EFFECT ON NOVEMBER 1, 2025.
WHICH AIRLINES HAVE RECEIVED FDTL EXEMPTIONS?
INDIGO WAS GRANTED EXEMPTIONS UNTIL FEBRUARY 10, 2026, AFTER CANCELLING OVER 5,500 FLIGHTS IN DECEMBER 2025. AIR INDIA RECEIVED A RELAXATION IN MARCH 2026 FOR LONG-HAUL FLIGHTS DUE TO AIRSPACE CLOSURES.
WHAT DOES ALPA WANT AIRLINES TO DO ABOUT FATIGUE REPORTING?
ALPA HAS DEMANDED THAT AIRLINES BE MANDATED TO SUBMIT QUARTERLY FATIGUE REPORT DATA IN A STANDARDIZED FORMAT. THE ASSOCIATION WANTS THIS DATA PLACED IN THE PUBLIC DOMAIN FOR TRANSPARENCY.
