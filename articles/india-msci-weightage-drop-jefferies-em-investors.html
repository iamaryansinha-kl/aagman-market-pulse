India MSCI Weightage Drops to 11.5% But Jefferies Says EM Investors Should Still Care

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/India's MSCI Emerging Markets weightage has dropped from 19.5% to 11.5% since early 2025 due to the "reverse AI trade" favoring semiconductor-heavy markets
  • 02/Despite $21.1 billion in foreign selling year-to-date, the Nifty MidCap 100 Index surged 19.2% from its April 2 low to a peak of 62,094
  • 03/Domestic mutual fund inflows hit ₹500 billion in March — the highest in eight months — offsetting foreign capital outflows

India's weightage in the MSCI Emerging Markets Index has fallen sharply to 11.5 per cent. It stood at 19.5 per cent at the start of last year, according to a Jefferies Greed & Fear report.

The drop reflects a "reverse AI trade" that has shifted global capital toward semiconductor-heavy markets. Korea and Taiwan now command 20.6 per cent and 25 per cent weightings, respectively.

"If India has been out of favour as the reverse AI trade, at least it has not been reduced to complete benchmark irrelevance for emerging market investors, which is the real risk now facing Asean markets," the Jefferies report stated.

The Nifty MidCap 100 Index climbed 19.2 per cent from its April 2 intraday low. It peaked at 62,094, sharply outperforming the blue-chip Nifty 50 Index.

The Nifty 50 gained 9.7 per cent from its April low. However, it remains 7.8 per cent below the peak it reached in early January.

Since the beginning of 2023, the MidCap index has rallied 97 per cent. The Nifty 50 gained 34 per cent over the same period.

Foreign investors sold a net $21.1 billion of Indian equities year-to-date. This already exceeds the record $18.8 billion in net selling recorded throughout all of 2025.

Domestic equity mutual fund inflows accelerated to ₹500 billion in March. That marked the highest monthly inflow level in eight months.

Systematic Investment Plans (SIPs) contributed ₹321 billion of the March total. The National Pension Scheme added approximately $1.7 billion per month into equities during Q1 2026.

"This mid-cap segment remains the most interesting part of the Indian market to GREED & fear, though the recent rally means the stocks are looking relatively expensive again," the report noted.

Samsung and Hynix are forecast to earn profits totalling W452 trillion ($307 billion) this year. That is three times the $102 billion total forecast profits for India's entire Nifty 50 universe.

In the Asia Pacific ex-Japan relative-return portfolio, Jefferies reduced India's weighting by one percentage point to 12 per cent. Korea and Taiwan weights were raised to 18 per cent and 21 per cent, respectively.
