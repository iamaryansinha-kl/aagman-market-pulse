US Natural Gas Glut Shields Economy From Iran War Energy Crisis

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Permian natural gas prices hit record low of -$9.60/MMBtu on April 24 as shale output overwhelms pipelines
  • 02/US benchmark futures slipped 10% since Iran conflict began while European and Asian prices surged 40-50%
  • 03/Record US shale production is insulating domestic economy from war-driven energy shocks, with utility gas prices down 0.9% in March CPI

US natural gas prices have turned negative in the Permian Basin as the Iran war drives global energy costs higher. Producers in West Texas and New Mexico are paying buyers to take excess gas as pipeline constraints trap supply.

Permian gas hit an all-time low of negative $9.60 per million British thermal units on April 24. US benchmark futures have recently traded below $3 per MMBtu, even as the Iran conflict triggered global supply fears.

European natural gas futures have surged about 40% since the Middle East conflict began. Asian prices have jumped more than 50% as nations ration fuel and endure blackouts.

Cheap domestic gas is muting inflation for American consumers. Utility gas prices fell 0.9% in March's Consumer Price Index report, even as broader costs rose.

Inexpensive gas is lowering power costs and supporting manufacturing and AI data center expansion. Data-center developers including Meta Platforms are favoring gas over cleaner alternatives for reliability.

"US gas prices have not just remained lower than global benchmarks, but have remained insulated from the volatility," said Chris Louney, director of global commodity strategy at RBC Capital Markets.

US government forecasts show gas averaging well below $4 through 2027. Production is poised to hit fresh records, keeping American industry competitive while global rivals face shortages.

The Blackcomb Pipeline is expected to enter service around October, flipping Permian prices positive. Five new conduits will add 11 billion cubic feet per day of capacity by end of 2028.
