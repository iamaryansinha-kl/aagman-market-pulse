IT Valuations Crash to 2008 Subprime Levels: TCS, Infosys at 15-18 P/E

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Top Indian IT exporters now trade at trailing P/E multiples of just 15-18, matching 2008-09 subprime crisis levels
  • 02/TCS is at 16.7 P/E and Infosys at 15.7, halved from COVID-era peaks; sector has lost nearly 30% in 2026
  • 03/AI advancements in coding and testing threaten headcount-based revenue models, capping medium-term upside

Indian IT stocks have cratered to valuations last seen during the 2008-09 global financial crisis. Tata Consultancy Services, Infosys, HCL Technologies, and Wipro now trade at trailing P/E multiples between 15 and 18.

The selloff has wiped out nearly 30% from IT stocks so far in 2026. Valuations have halved from their COVID-era peaks four years ago, when pandemic-driven demand for enterprise IT solutions sent multiples soaring.

TCS currently trades at a P/E of 16.7, while Infosys is at 15.7. Both have fallen below the 20x threshold breached during the 2013 Taper Tantrum and the 2017 H1B visa crackdown.

The current retreat is steeper than those episodes. During the subprime crisis, IT P/Es hovered between 12 and 15 before briefly diving to high single digits.

The driving force is artificial intelligence. Rapid improvements in AI-powered coding, testing, and debugging are threatening the headcount-based execution model that Indian IT firms have relied on for decades.

OpenAI has announced a new subsidiary, OpenAI Deployment Company, to help enterprises integrate AI solutions directly. This marks a direct challenge to Indian IT's stronghold in enterprise process management.

IT shares saw a brief two-day bounce on hopes that a weakening rupee would boost export realisations. However, analysts expect the AI overhang to limit gains in the medium term.

TCS closed at ₹2,283.20 on May 18, up 0.85%. Infosys gained 2.11% to ₹1,142.50, while Wipro rose 1.15% to ₹192.17.
