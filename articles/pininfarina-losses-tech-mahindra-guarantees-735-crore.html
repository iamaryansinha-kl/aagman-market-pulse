Pininfarina Losses Quadruple Tech Mahindra Guarantees to ₹735 Crore

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Pininfarina posts €6.7 million net loss in 2025, its fourth loss in five years
  • 02/Tech Mahindra quadruples corporate guarantees to ₹735 crore to support the Italian design house
  • 03/Revenue rose 13% to $118 million but automakers are cutting external design spending

Tech Mahindra's acquisition of Pininfarina, once touted as a strategic jewel, has become a financial burden. The Italian design house posted a net loss of €6.7 million ($7.6 million) in 2025.

This marks Pininfarina's fourth loss in the last five years. The loss was driven by one-time expenses including €3.5 million in severance to former CEO Silvio Pietro Angori and €3.3 million to settle a lawsuit with hydrogen fuel cell company GreenGT.

Tech Mahindra's corporate guarantees for Pininfarina quadrupled year-on-year to ₹735 crore (about $80 million) in FY26. This means the IT firm would have to repay Pininfarina's lenders if the design house failed to meet its debt obligations.

"The performance in 2025 was driven primarily due to a market environment characterized by persistent uncertainty, weaker demand and increasing competitive pressure," said a Pininfarina spokesperson.

The design firm's revenue has grown just 25% from $94 million in FY15. Over the same period, Tech Mahindra's revenue grew 73% from $6.4 billion. The acquisition was meant to bolster engineering and design capabilities.

Pininfarina's challenges stem from automakers bringing vehicle design in-house to cut costs. European car brands such as Volkswagen, Renault, and Stellantis have reduced spending on external design houses.

"The cost-cutting measures affect designing as companies have moved it internally to save costs," said Amit Kaushik, founder of auto tech consultancy Mobidx AI.

The Italian business reported a €14 million loss, higher than the group loss, because it wrote down investments in Chinese and German subsidiaries. This reduced shareholders' equity to €29.6 million against share capital of €56 million.

Under Italian law, if a company loses more than a third of its share capital, the parent must invest to keep it running. The Dutch parent was forced to write off about €10 million of the €14 million it loaned to Pininfarina.

Pininfarina has been diversifying into residential projects, consumer products, and nuclear reactors to mitigate losses. Still, immediate recovery seems distant.

While Pininfarina struggles, Tech Mahindra is charting a turnaround. Operating margins jumped 290 basis points year-on-year to 12.6% in FY26. CEO Mohit Joshi is confident of navigating AI's challenges.
