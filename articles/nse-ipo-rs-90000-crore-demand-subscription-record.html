NSE IPO Draws Rs 90,000 Crore Demand, Sets Subscription Record Among India's 5 Largest IPOs

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

The National Stock Exchange of India (NSE) has set a new subscription record among the country's five largest IPOs, attracting demand worth approximately Rs 90,000 crore for its Rs 22,561.57 crore public issue.

The IPO, which is the second-largest by issue size after Hyundai Motor India's Rs 27,858.75 crore offering, received bids for 50.58 crore shares against 8.86 crore shares on offer. This translated to an overall subscription of 5.71 times at the close of the bidding window.

QIBs Drive Demand

Qualified institutional buyers led the subscription, with their reserved portion subscribed 12.68 times. Non-institutional investors (NIIs) subscribed 6.55 times their allocated quota, while retail individual investors subscribed 1.39 times.

The strong response puts the NSE issue ahead of the other four largest IPOs in terms of overall subscription. Hyundai Motor India's IPO was subscribed 1.93 times, LIC's Rs 20,557.23 crore issue was subscribed 2.05 times, One 97 Communications' Rs 18,300 crore IPO was subscribed 1.48 times, and Tata Capital's Rs 15,511.87 crore offering was subscribed 1.65 times.

IPO Details

The NSE IPO is entirely an offer for sale (OFS) by existing shareholders. The issue comprises the sale of up to 12.64 crore shares in the price band of Rs 1,700-Rs 1,785 per share. The IPO opened on September 17 and closed on September 21, 2026.

The basis of allotment is expected to be finalised on September 22, with shares scheduled to list on the BSE on September 24.

Grey Market Premium

NSE unlisted shares were changing hands at around Rs 1,840 per share in the grey market, implying a GMP of approximately Rs 55 per share or 3.08% over the upper end of the price band.

Rahul Sharma of Equity99 said the post-listing rerating will depend more on derivatives-volume trajectory, transaction revenue, earnings growth and the valuation investors assign to NSE's market dominance than on the subscription multiple itself.
