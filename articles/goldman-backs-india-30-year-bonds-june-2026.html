Goldman Sachs Backs India 30-Year Bonds: $4.2B Foreign Inflows in June

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Goldman Sachs analysts including Danny Suwanapruti recommend buying India 30-year government bonds, citing easing inflation and lower oil prices
  • 02/Foreign holdings of index-eligible Indian debt rose ₹397 billion ($4.2 billion) in June, on track for the biggest monthly purchase on record
  • 03/Goldman estimates 0.7% index weight in Bloomberg Global Aggregate, potentially bringing $15 billion in passive inflows over the phase-in period

Goldman Sachs Group Inc. has recommended buying India's 30-year government bonds. The call comes as inflation expectations ease and lower oil prices reduce fiscal risks.

The US-Iran ceasefire has improved the macro outlook. The overall impact of the war was less than initially feared, analysts said in a note released Saturday.

Goldman prefers the ultra-long-end of the bond curve. Shorter-term bonds have already rallied sharply on lower oil prices and the pricing out of RBI rate hike expectations.

Analysts including Danny Suwanapruti wrote that more ultra-long debt is entering the fully accessible route. This improves market depth for foreign investors.

Overseas holdings of index-eligible Indian debt have risen ₹397 billion ($4.2 billion) in June so far. This is on course for the biggest-ever monthly purchase, per Clearing Corp. of India data.

Inflows surged after India scrapped taxes on debt for foreigners. The government also expanded the pool of securities eligible for index inclusion.

Goldman estimates India's weight in the Bloomberg Global Aggregate Index at around 0.7%. This could prompt approximately $15 billion of passive inflows over the phase-in period.

A mid-year announcement on inclusion is likely, analysts wrote. The question is now one of timing rather than direction.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHY HAS GOLDMAN SACHS RECOMMENDED INDIA'S 30-YEAR GOVERNMENT BONDS?
GOLDMAN SACHS ANALYSTS INCLUDING DANNY SUWANAPRUTI RECOMMEND BUYING INDIA'S 30-YEAR BONDS DUE TO EASING INFLATION EXPECTATIONS, LOWER OIL PRICES, AND REDUCED FISCAL RISKS FOLLOWING THE US-IRAN CEASEFIRE.
HOW MUCH HAVE FOREIGN INVESTORS INVESTED IN INDIAN GOVERNMENT BONDS IN JUNE 2026?
OVERSEAS HOLDINGS OF INDEX-ELIGIBLE INDIAN DEBT HAVE RISEN BY ₹397 BILLION ($4.2 BILLION) SO FAR IN JUNE 2026. THIS IS ON COURSE FOR THE BIGGEST-EVER MONTHLY PURCHASE, ACCORDING TO CLEARING CORP. OF INDIA DATA.
WHAT IS THE FULLY ACCESSIBLE ROUTE (FAR) AND WHY DOES IT MATTER?
THE FULLY ACCESSIBLE ROUTE IS A MECHANISM THAT ALLOWS FOREIGN INVESTORS TO BUY SPECIFIED INDIAN GOVERNMENT SECURITIES WITHOUT LIMITS. MORE ULTRA-LONG BONDS ENTERING THE FAR IMPROVES MARKET DEPTH AND ACCESSIBILITY FOR FOREIGN PORTFOLIOS.
HOW MUCH PASSIVE INFLOW COULD INDIA SEE FROM BLOOMBERG INDEX INCLUSION?
GOLDMAN SACHS ESTIMATES INDIA'S INDEX WEIGHT AT AROUND 0.7% BASED ON CURRENT OUTSTANDING FAR BONDS. THIS COULD PROMPT APPROXIMATELY $15 BILLION OF PASSIVE INFLOWS OVER THE PHASE-IN PERIOD.
WHAT IS THE TIMELINE FOR INDIA'S INCLUSION IN THE BLOOMBERG GLOBAL AGGREGATE INDEX?
GOLDMAN ANALYSTS EXPECT A MID-YEAR ANNOUNCEMENT ON INDIA'S INCLUSION. THEY VIEW ENTRY INTO THE BLOOMBERG GLOBAL AGGREGATE INDEX AS INCREASINGLY "A QUESTION OF TIMING RATHER THAN DIRECTION."
