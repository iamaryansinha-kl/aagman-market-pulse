Urea Subsidy: Centre Absorbs ₹2,700 Per Bag as Costs Hit ₹3,000

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/The Centre absorbs a subsidy of around ₹2,700 on every bag of urea while farmers pay roughly ₹300, Sitharaman said
  • 02/Global urea costs rose as high as ₹3,000 per bag on pandemic and West Asia supply disruptions
  • 03/The notified MRP of a 45-kg urea bag stands at ₹242, excluding neem-coating charges and taxes

Union Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman said on Friday that the Centre has been absorbing a subsidy of around ₹2,700 on every bag of urea. Global costs have risen as high as ₹3,000 per bag, while the price paid by farmers stays at around ₹300.

She was speaking at a gathering in Madanapalle, Andhra Pradesh. The remarks were shared through posts on X by the Finance Minister's Office.

"Despite severe global headwinds, the pandemic and geopolitical strife in West Asia driving import costs up to ₹3,000 per bag of urea, PM ensured our farmers continued to receive it at just ₹300, providing a subsidy of ₹2,700 per bag, directly absorbing the global burden," the post said.

The government has sought to shield farmers from the sharp rise in fertiliser costs caused by global disruptions. Those disruptions include the pandemic and ongoing geopolitical tensions in West Asia, both of which pushed import costs higher.

Fertiliser subsidies work by keeping farm input prices below their delivered cost. Under the Urea Subsidy Scheme, the difference between the delivered cost of urea and the amount realised from its sale is reimbursed by the government to fertiliser manufacturers and importers.

The government-notified maximum retail price of a 45-kg bag of urea currently stands at ₹242. That figure excludes neem-coating charges and applicable taxes, and Sitharaman's remarks referred to the farmer price in rounded terms.

The Finance Minister said ensuring adequate availability of essential farm inputs remains a government priority. That effort runs alongside income support measures and expanded access to institutional credit for farmers.

"Under Narendra Modi's leadership, farmer welfare has been at the centre of every step, from direct benefit transfers through PM-Kisan Samman Nidhi to timely institutional credit via Kisan Credit Cards. Ensuring zero shortage of essential inputs has been our utmost priority," she said.

Sitharaman was speaking at an event where initiatives aimed at developing horticulture and agriculture in Andhra Pradesh were announced. The Centre and the state government are working together to expand investment and support for agriculture and allied sectors.

The subsidy burden highlights the cost of insulating Indian agriculture from global price shocks. With imported urea costing up to ₹3,000 per bag against a farmer price of roughly ₹300, the gap of around ₹2,700 per bag is being borne entirely by the exchequer.

The remarks come at a time when fertiliser affordability remains central to farm economics. Keeping urea cheap supports input costs for the upcoming cropping seasons, while the size of the per-bag absorption underscores how much fiscal room the government is committing to the farm sector.

For Andhra Pradesh, the event carried an additional layer of significance. The initiatives announced there target horticulture and agriculture development in the state, signalling closer coordination between the Centre and the state government on farm-sector investment.

The scale of the per-bag support also frames the debate on fertiliser pricing policy. With global costs of ₹3,000 against a rounded farmer price of ₹300, any further escalation in international urea markets would flow straight into a larger subsidy bill unless prices ease.

For now, the government has signalled no change in its approach. Sitharaman's emphasis on zero shortage of essential inputs suggests the subsidy architecture will remain in place as long as global fertiliser markets stay elevated.
