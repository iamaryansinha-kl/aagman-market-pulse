India Consumer Inflation Likely Breached RBI's 4% Target in June

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

Inflation is creeping back above the RBI's comfort zone.

A poll of economists suggests India's consumer inflation likely breached the Reserve Bank of India's 4% target in June. The data, due for release later this week, is expected to show price pressures re-emerging after a period of moderation.

Food prices are the primary driver. Tomatoes, onions, and other vegetables have seen sharp price increases due to supply disruptions. The monsoon has been uneven, affecting crop yields and transportation in some regions.

Fuel prices have also contributed. Crude oil surged past $80 per barrel following US strikes on Iran, pushing up petrol, diesel, and LPG costs. Higher transportation costs feed through to prices of goods across the economy.

The inflation breach matters for monetary policy. The RBI has held interest rates steady in recent meetings, waiting for inflation to sustainably return to the 4% target. If June inflation comes in above target, the central bank may delay any rate cuts further.

Economists are divided on the outlook. Some believe the inflation spike is temporary, driven by seasonal food price volatility that will reverse as fresh supplies arrive. Others worry that persistent fuel costs and strong domestic demand could keep inflation elevated.

The RBI's next monetary policy meeting is scheduled for early August. The June inflation data will be a key input. If inflation is significantly above 4%, the central bank may adopt a more hawkish tone even if it keeps rates unchanged.
