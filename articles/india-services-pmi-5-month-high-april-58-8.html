India Services PMI Hits 5-Month High Of 58.8 In April On Domestic Demand

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/India's services PMI climbed to 58.8 in April, the highest reading in five months and up from 57.5 in March
  • 02/Domestic demand drove the expansion, while new export orders fell amid West Asia tensions and subdued tourism
  • 03/Input cost inflation moderated but stayed elevated; firms hired more workers though optimism dipped from March levels

India's services sector Purchasing Managers' Index rose to 58.8 in April. S&P Global reported the figure on Wednesday, marking a five-month high.

The reading improved from 57.5 recorded in March. A PMI above 50 signals expansion, while below 50 indicates contraction.

New work inflows increased to the greatest extent in five months. Survey respondents cited competitive pricing, e-commerce, and strong demand for relocation and logistics services.

Consumer Services led the expansion in output and new orders. Transport and Information & Communication followed as the next strongest performers.

International demand for Indian services lost momentum in April. The New Export Business Index fell by over five points to its second-lowest level in more than a year.

"Activity and new orders strengthened, even as new export orders eased, suggesting that demand is rotating from overseas markets to domestic consumers amid the Middle East conflict," said Pranjul Bhandari, Chief India Economist at HSBC.

Companies indicated that the war in the Middle East and subdued inbound tourism dampened export growth. Inflationary pressures saw mild reduction during the month.

Input cost inflation moderated but remained elevated. Output price inflation stayed subdued, suggesting some firms absorbed higher costs instead of passing them to customers.

Firms recruited more workers at the start of the fiscal quarter. Employment rose across all four broad service categories monitored by the survey.

Sustained hiring enabled companies to reduce outstanding business for the first time in four months. The backlog depletion was marginal, S&P Global noted.

Business confidence for the coming 12 months remained positive. However, optimism fell from March levels due to worries over the Middle East conflict and cost pressures.

THE NEW EXPORT BUSINESS INDEX FELL BY MORE THAN FIVE POINTS TO ITS SECOND-LOWEST LEVEL IN OVER A YEAR. FIRMS CITED THE WAR IN THE MIDDLE EAST AND SUBDUED INBOUND TOURISM AS KEY REASONS FOR WEAKER OVERSEAS DEMAND.
