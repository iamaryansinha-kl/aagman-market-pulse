No change in domestic ATF prices, international aviation fuel rates revised upward

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

State-owned oil marketing companies have kept Aviation Turbine Fuel prices unchanged for scheduled domestic carriers in the latest monthly revision effective May 1, 2026.

The decision provides relief to domestic airline operations amid persistently high global fuel costs.

ATF prices for scheduled domestic airlines remain steady across all major metro cities.

In Delhi, the rate stands at ₹1,04,927 per kilolitre.

Prices in Kolkata remain at ₹1,09,450 per kilolitre.

Mumbai continues at ₹98,247 per kilolitre.

Chennai remains at ₹1,09,873 per kilolitre.

However, prices of bulk diesel and ATF for international airline operations have been revised upward as part of routine monthly adjustments.

This follows the broader pricing approach adopted in April 2026, when the government capped domestic ATF price hikes at 25 percent despite global benchmarks signaling an increase of over 100 percent.

The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas had then stated that a full pass-through would have raised prices by over ₹60 per litre.

Instead, only ₹15 per litre was passed on to domestic carriers.

International routes bore the full increase, consistent with global pricing.

The pricing divergence stems from extraordinary conditions in global energy markets.

The closure of the Strait of Hormuz amid the ongoing U.S.-Iran conflict has severely disrupted crude oil supplies.

Global oil prices have surged past $100 per barrel.

Jet fuel prices more than doubled between late February and late March 2026.

The refinery margin, or crack spread, nearly tripled from $27.83 to $81.44 per barrel in just three weeks.

India relies on the Strait of Hormuz for roughly 40 percent of its crude oil imports.

The Federation of Indian Airlines, which represents Air India, IndiGo, and SpiceJet, had just a day earlier raised concerns over elevated ATF prices.

The industry body sought government intervention on aviation fuel pricing.

According to the FIA, fuel costs traditionally account for 30 to 40 percent of airline expenditure.

Now they make up nearly 55 to 60 percent of operating costs.

Airlines are struggling to sustain both domestic and international operations amid the surge.

Air India and IndiGo have already revised fuel surcharge structures to partially offset rising costs.

Domestic fuel charges now range from ₹299 to ₹899 per passenger per sector based on distance.

International surcharges saw even steeper revisions due to the absence of regulatory mitigation.

The government has assured that India has 60 days of uninterrupted ATF supply.

Nearly half of India's ATF production is used for domestic consumption, with the remainder exported.

The pricing strategy aims to insulate domestic consumers and air travelers from international volatility.

It also reflects a calibrated approach to managing inflationary pressures within the aviation sector.
