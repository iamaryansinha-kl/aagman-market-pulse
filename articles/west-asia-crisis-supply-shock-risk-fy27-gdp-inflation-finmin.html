West Asia Crisis Poses Supply Shock Risk, FY27 GDP Clouded: FinMin

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Finance Ministry's April review warns West Asia crisis could trigger a supply shock, with Gulf oil infrastructure repairs taking several months
  • 02/FY27 GDP forecast of 7-7.4% is now clouded; RBI revised growth down to 6.9% and inflation up to 4.6% amid crude above $100/barrel
  • 03/IMD's El Nino warning adds risk: below-normal monsoon could push cost-push inflation higher and weaken kharif output

The Finance Ministry has warned that the ongoing West Asia crisis poses a serious supply shock risk to the Indian economy. Its April monthly economic review states that repairing damaged oil and gas infrastructure in the Gulf region may take several months.

If the recovery is delayed and coincides with a weak kharif season, headline inflation could spill over to core inflation through cost-push pressures. The ministry flagged this as a key risk to India's macroeconomic stability.

The report said India enters FY2026-27 on domestic strength, having recorded 7.6% real GDP growth in FY2026. However, the FY27 growth forecast of 7-7.4% now faces uncertainty due to the West Asia conflict.

The Reserve Bank of India has already revised its FY27 GDP projection down to 6.9% from 7.4% in February 2026. It raised the CPI inflation forecast to 4.6% from 4.2%, with quarterly inflation peaking at 5.2% in Q3.

Governor Sanjay Malhotra cited crude oil above $100 per barrel, supply chain disruptions, and El Nino as key upside risks to inflation. The RBI held the repo rate at 5.25% in April, signalling a cautious stance.

Higher petroleum costs are expected to squeeze margins across downstream industries. Businesses may pass on input costs to consumers, turning price shocks into broader cost-push inflation.

The IMD has warned of possible El Nino conditions that could bring below-normal rainfall to most districts. This would strain kharif sowing and exacerbate fertiliser supply risks, as India imports 56% of its DAP and 100% of MOP.

The IMF projects India's FY27 GDP growth at 6.5%, a 0.1 percentage point upgrade from January. The reduction in US tariffs on Indian goods from 50% to 10% offers some offset to external pressures.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT IS THE FINANCE MINISTRY'S WARNING ABOUT THE WEST ASIA CRISIS?
THE FINANCE MINISTRY'S APRIL ECONOMIC REVIEW WARNS THAT THE ONGOING WEST ASIA CRISIS COULD TRIGGER A SUPPLY SHOCK FOR INDIA. REPAIRING DAMAGED OIL AND GAS INFRASTRUCTURE IN THE GULF REGION MAY TAKE SEVERAL MONTHS, AND DELAYED RECOVERY COULD SPIKE INFLATION.
WHAT IS THE FY2027 GDP GROWTH FORECAST FOR INDIA?
THE FINANCE MINISTRY HAD FORECAST 7-7.4% GDP GROWTH FOR FY2026-27, BUT THIS IS NOW CLOUDED BY THE WEST ASIA CONFLICT. THE RBI HAS REVISED ITS FY27 PROJECTION DOWN TO 6.9% FROM 7.4% IN FEBRUARY 2026. THE IMF PROJECTS 6.5% GROWTH.
WHAT IS THE RBI'S INFLATION FORECAST FOR FY2027?
THE RBI FORECASTS CPI INFLATION AT 4.6% FOR FY2027, REVISED UPWARD FROM 4.2% IN FEBRUARY 2026. THE QUARTERLY PATH SHOWS INFLATION PEAKING AT 5.2% IN Q3 (OCTOBER-DECEMBER 2026) BEFORE EASING TO 4.7% IN Q4.
HOW DOES EL NINO ADD TO THE INFLATION RISK?
THE INDIA METEOROLOGICAL DEPARTMENT HAS INDICATED POSSIBLE EL NINO CONDITIONS, WHICH COULD LEAD TO BELOW-NORMAL RAINFALL ACROSS MOST DISTRICTS. A WEAK KHARIF SEASON WOULD STRAIN FOOD SUPPLIES AND PUSH UP PRICES, AMPLIFYING THE COST-PRESSURE FROM HIGH OIL PRICES.
WHAT DOMESTIC FACTORS COULD BUFFER INDIA AGAINST THIS SHOCK?
INDIA'S RESILIENCE STEMS FROM STRONG DOMESTIC DEMAND, POLICY SUPPORT, A STABLE FINANCIAL SYSTEM, AND SUSTAINED PUBLIC INVESTMENT. THE REDUCTION IN US TARIFFS ON INDIAN GOODS FROM 50% TO 10% ALSO PROVIDES RELIEF. HOWEVER, THE FINANCE MINISTRY NOTES THESE BUFFERS MAY BE TESTED IF ENERGY DISRUPTIONS PERSIST.
