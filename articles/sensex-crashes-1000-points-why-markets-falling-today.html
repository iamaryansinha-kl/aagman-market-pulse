Sensex Crashes 1,000 Points: Why Markets Are Falling Today

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Sensex crashed over 1,000 points below 74,300 and Nifty sank 300+ points to 23,328 on May 18
  • 02/Five factors driving the bloodbath: Iran tensions, soaring global bond yields, rupee at record low 96.18, oil above $111, and weak global cues
  • 03/Rs 7 lakh crore wiped out from BSE market cap; India VIX jumped 5% to 19.78 as bearish sentiment dominates

Indian stock markets plunged into deep red on Monday, May 18, 2026. Sensex crashed over 1,000 points to drop below 74,300 while Nifty 50 sank over 300 points to 23,328.

The sharp decline wiped out nearly Rs 7 lakh crore in total market capitalisation. BSE-listed companies now command a combined mcap of Rs 454 lakh crore.

India VIX, the volatility gauge, jumped over 5% to hover around 19.78. The selloff was broad-based across midcaps and smallcaps.

Nifty Midcap 100 and Nifty Smallcap 100 indices plummeted over 1% each. All sectoral indices except Nifty IT traded in deep red.

Nifty Consumer Durables led the losses, crashing over 2%. Around 2,152 stocks declined on NSE against just 488 advancing.

US President Donald Trump issued a fresh threat to Iran, warning "the clock is ticking." Tensions escalated after a drone strike caused a fire at a UAE nuclear power plant.

Global bond yields soared to record highs. The US 10-year yield hit 4.632% and the 30-year yield surged to 5.156%.

The Indian rupee tumbled to a fresh all-time low of 96.18 against the US dollar. The currency has dropped 5.5% since the Iran-US war erupted on February 28.

Brent crude soared back above $111 per barrel while WTI crude jumped to $108. Elevated oil prices are fueling inflation fears and denting risk appetite.

"Elevated crude may force another round of price hikes in petrol and diesel, which will have negative implications for inflation. The spike in US 10-year bond yield to 4.62% is another negative factor for EM equity markets," said VK Vijayakumar, Chief Investment Strategist at Geojit Investments.

Global markets mirrored the weakness. Japan’s Nikkei and Hong Kong’s Hang Seng both declined around 1%. Germany’s DAX, France’s CAC and UK’s FTSE had tumbled around 2% each on Friday.

FIIs remained net buyers for a second straight session, purchasing Rs 1,329 crore on Friday. However, they have been net sellers in 7 out of 10 sessions in May so far.

Bajaj Broking sees Nifty support at 23,350 and resistance at 23,840. The index needs to reclaim the 23,800-23,900 zone to signal a pause in the downtrend.
