Kevin Warsh Fed Chair: Markets Brace for Slower Rate Cuts Amid Inflation Spike

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/President Trump nominates Kevin Warsh as next Fed chair; Jerome Powell's term ends May 15, 2026
  • 02/U.S. inflation hit 3.3% in March, a two-year high; unemployment holds at 4.3%
  • 03/Futures markets price no Fed rate cut until October 2027; Warsh faces 11 other voters reluctant to ease

President Donald Trump has nominated Kevin Warsh as the next Federal Reserve chair. Warsh would replace Jerome Powell when his term ends on May 15, 2026.

Warsh previously served as a Fed governor from 2006 to 2011. He is known for advocating lower interest rates and a smaller Fed balance sheet.

However, markets are pricing in virtually no chance of rate cuts until October 2027. Inflation surged to 3.3% in March, a two-year high well above the Fed's 2% target.

The Fed's rate-setting committee voted 11-1 to hold rates steady at approximately 3.6% in March. Most policymakers are reluctant to cut with inflation running hot and jobs data stable.

Warsh pledged independence from White House pressure at his Senate hearing. He stated that President Trump never asked him to predetermine any interest rate decision.

Some analysts, including Oxford Economics, view Warsh as a "more dovish voice." But BofA's Aditya Bhave said his outlook is consistent with an "extended hold" rather than additional cuts.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent provided cover for patience, saying he understands if the Fed wants to "wait for some clarity." Warsh himself warned of a "short window to bring inflation back down."

Gold prices plunged 9% when Warsh's nomination was announced in January, the largest one-day drop since 2013. The U.S. dollar strengthened as fears of currency debasement eased.
