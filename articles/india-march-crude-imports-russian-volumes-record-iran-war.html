India's March Crude Imports Fall 15% as Russian Volumes Hit Near Record

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/India's total crude imports fell 15% month-on-month to 4.4 million b/d in March 2026 as the Iran war choked Middle East supply
  • 02/Russian crude imports surged 94% to 2.06 million b/d, accounting for 46.8% of India's total oil supply, the highest since May 2023
  • 03/A US sanctions waiver on March 12 enabled state refiners to resume Russian purchases; state-owned refineries' imports jumped 148%

India's total crude oil imports fell to 4.4 million barrels per day in March 2026. This marks a 15% decline from February's 5.2 million b/d.

The drop reflects severe supply disruptions after US-led airstrikes on Iran began on February 28. The Strait of Hormuz, which handles about 40% of India's oil imports, was effectively closed.

India effectively lost access to Iraq, its second-largest supplier delivering around 1 million b/d. Saudi Arabia's volumes halved to roughly 500,000 b/d, and Kuwaiti supplies were completely cut off.

Russia emerged as the dominant replacement supplier. Crude imports from Russia surged to a near-record 2.06 million b/d, representing a 94% month-on-month jump from February's 1.06 million b/d.

Russian barrels now account for 46.8% of India's total crude imports. This is the highest share since the all-time peak of 2.15 million b/d seen in May 2023.

India's Russian oil import bill more than tripled to €5.3 billion in March. Total hydrocarbon purchases from Russia reached €5.8 billion, making India the second-largest buyer after China.

State refiners drove the bulk of the increase after a US sanctions waiver on March 12. State-owned refinery imports from Russia jumped 148% month-on-month.

Private refineries also increased purchases by 66%, though volumes remained lower than the prior year. Mangalore and Visakhapatnam refineries, which had halted Russian imports in November 2025, resumed purchases in March.

India's import dependency stands at roughly 88-90% of total crude needs. March volumes were also down 16% compared to March 2025.

"Energy trade flows remain highly fluid amid ongoing developments in the Middle East," said Nikhil Dubey, senior research analyst at Kpler. "If the situation does not improve, energy import volumes are expected to trend lower from March levels."
