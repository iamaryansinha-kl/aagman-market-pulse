David Swensen on Equity Bias: Why Long-Term Investors Should Load Up on Stocks

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/David Swensen, who grew Yale's endowment from $1.3 billion to over $40 billion, advised long-term investors to maintain an equity-heavy portfolio
  • 02/The Yale endowment delivered 13.7% annualized returns over 36 years, outperforming the average university endowment by 3.4 percentage points
  • 03/S&P 500 has delivered 15.2% annualized returns over the past decade, with positive returns in 26 of the last 33 years

David Swensen, the legendary investor who managed Yale University's endowment from 1985 to 2021, believed time is an investor's greatest ally. His advice is simple: if your investment horizon is long, load up on equities.

"If you're investing with a long time horizon, having an equity bias makes sense; stocks go up in the long run," Swensen said. This quote, featured by ETMarkets.com on June 26, 2026, captures his core philosophy.

Under Swensen's 36-year leadership, Yale's endowment grew from $1.3 billion to over $40 billion. The fund delivered an annualized return of 13.7%, crushing the average university endowment by 3.4 percentage points.

Swensen argued that stocks outperform bonds and cash over extended periods because companies can grow earnings, expand operations, and innovate. This growth ultimately reflects in stock prices and shareholder wealth.

Historical data backs his view. The S&P 500 has delivered 15.2% annualized returns over the past decade and 12% over the past 20 years. The index posted positive returns in 26 of the last 33 years, a 79% success rate.

However, Swensen warned against trying to time the market. Investors who jump in and out risk missing the strongest recovery days, which can severely dent long-term returns. Patience and discipline matter more than perfect timing.

For retail investors, Swensen's simplified portfolio model recommends 30% US stocks, 15% developed international equities, 5% emerging markets, 15% intermediate treasuries, 15% TIPS, and 20% REITs. This allocation has delivered an 8.1% CAGR over 32 years in backtests.

Volatility is the price investors pay for higher returns. Swensen viewed market swings as temporary noise, not a reason to abandon equities. His advice remains relevant for anyone saving for retirement, children's education, or long-term wealth creation.
