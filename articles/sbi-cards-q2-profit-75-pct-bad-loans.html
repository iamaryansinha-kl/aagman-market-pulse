SBI Cards Profit Jumps 75% as Bad Loan Costs Crash

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Net profit rose 75% year-on-year to Rs 778 crore, from Rs 445 crore a year earlier.
  • •- Impairment losses and bad debt expenses fell 37% to Rs 819 crore; gross write-offs dropped 34% to Rs 849 crore.
  • •- Gross NPA improved to 1.90% from 2.85%; net NPA fell to 0.77% from 1.29%.
  • •- But growth stayed modest: spends rose just 3% to Rs 1.11 lakh crore, corporate card spends fell 12%, and net interest margin narrowed 42 basis points to 10.8%.

The credit card business is a leveraged bet on the Indian consumer's willingness to borrow, and for one quarter at least, SBI Cards' bet paid off handsomely on almost every metric that matters to a lender. The company on Friday reported a 75% year-on-year jump in net profit to Rs 778 crore for the September quarter, from Rs 445 crore a year earlier, powered almost entirely by one line item: impairment losses and bad debt expenses fell 37% to Rs 819 crore, from Rs 1,293 crore in the same period last year. When a lender's biggest cost line collapses by that magnitude, the profit-and-loss statement takes care of itself — the question that matters is why it collapsed, and whether it can last.

The asset quality story is genuinely strong, and it is the kind of strength that compounds quietly. Gross non-performing assets improved to 1.90% at September-end, from 2.85% a year earlier; net NPAs fell to 0.77% from 1.29%; and gross write-offs declined 34% to Rs 849 crore. Each of those three numbers moving in the right direction simultaneously is what a real credit-cycle turn looks like, not a one-quarter statistical fluke driven by a single large recovery. For a lender whose stock spent two years being punished by the market for post-pandemic delinquencies, that is a meaningful clean-up. Management is not declaring victory, though: the company made an additional impairment provision of Rs 30 crore during the quarter, citing uncertainty in the macroeconomic environment due to prolonged geopolitical tensions, taking cumulative additional provisions to Rs 100 crore. Prudent, and telling — the lender that just posted 75% profit growth is still building buffers against the macro environment it sees outside its window.

The caution shows up in the operating numbers, which were much less celebratory than the profit line. Total income rose just 3% to Rs 5,302 crore. Overall credit card spends grew only 3% year-on-year to Rs 1.11 lakh crore; retail spends rose 6% to Rs 95,299 crore, but corporate card spends contracted 12% to Rs 15,390 crore, from Rs 17,452 crore a year ago. Outstanding receivables shrank 2% to Rs 58,376 crore even as the card base expanded, with interest-earning receivables making up roughly 55% of the portfolio. Net interest margin narrowed 42 basis points year-on-year to 10.8%. Put plainly: the book got cleaner and smaller-to-flat, and the profit surge is the reward for surviving the messy expansion of earlier years rather than the beginning of a new growth cycle.

There is growth to report, just not dramatic growth. Cards in force rose 8% to 2.32 crore, and new account acquisitions jumped 18% to 11.06 lakh, from 9.36 lakh in the year-ago quarter — evidence that the acquisition engine still runs even as the company chases quality over volume and walks away from corners of the market where repayment discipline was weakest. Market share tells a nuanced story: SBI Cards' share of outstanding cards slipped to 18.5% from 19%, but its share of industry spending improved to 18.2% from 17.7%, meaning its cardholders are transacting more than the industry average. In a business where transaction value, not card count, ultimately funds the revenue model — spends generate interchange income, receivables generate interest income, and it is the first of those that compounds with customer quality — that mix shift is worth considerably more than the headline share loss suggests. The 12% contraction in corporate card spends, concentrated as it is in a single customer segment, weighs on volumes but says little about retail credit health.

"During Q2 of FY27, we continued to focus on building a healthy, sustainable and well-balanced portfolio, while maintaining our priorities around profitable customer acquisition, customer engagement, operating efficiency and portfolio quality," managing director Salila Pande said on the analyst call. Her five forward levers — deeper Tier 2 and Tier 3 penetration, banker channel expansion, premiumisation, credit cost optimisation and digital capability — are, in effect, an admission that the low-hanging fruit of India's credit card boom has been picked, and that the next leg of growth has to be built market by market, customer by customer, at a measured pace that the balance sheet can absorb.

For investors, the quarter poses a familiar dilemma in a new form. The profit number is exceptional, but it is exceptional because the denominator of last year's losses shrank — not because the numerator of growth accelerated. A lender trading on asset-quality repair eventually runs out of bad loans to stop writing off, and then has to deliver the top-line growth that this quarter, with 3% income growth and shrinking receivables, did not quite show. The stock will have a chance to make that argument in the coming sessions; Friday's print gave it a strong quarter to stand on, if not yet a strong story to carry into the next one.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT DROVE SBI CARDS' 75% PROFIT JUMP?
Almost entirely lower credit costs: impairment losses and bad debt expenses fell 37% year-on-year to Rs 819 crore, gross write-offs fell 34% to Rs 849 crore, and gross NPAs improved to 1.90% from 2.85% a year earlier.
WAS THE UNDERLYING BUSINESS GROWING?
Modestly. Total income rose 3%, card spends rose 3%, receivables shrank 2% and net interest margin narrowed 42 basis points to 10.8%. Corporate card spends fell 12%.
WHAT CAUTION DID MANAGEMENT SIGNAL?
It booked an additional Rs 30 crore impairment provision — Rs 100 crore cumulative — citing macroeconomic uncertainty from prolonged geopolitical tensions, even as profits surged. GLOSSARY: GNPA (GROSS NON-PERFORMING ASSET) — The share of loans on which borrowers have stopped paying, before provisioning; a fall from 2.85% to 1.90% signals a markedly cleaner loan book. NET INTEREST MARGIN (NIM) — The difference between what a lender earns on its assets and pays on its liabilities, as a percentage of assets; narrowing NIM means each rupee lent earns less. WRITE-OFF — A loan the lender removes from its books as unrecoverable; falling write-offs mean fewer borrowers are defaulting in absolute terms. RECEIVABLES — The total outstanding balances cardholders owe the company; a shrinking book means lending is contracting even as card numbers grow.
