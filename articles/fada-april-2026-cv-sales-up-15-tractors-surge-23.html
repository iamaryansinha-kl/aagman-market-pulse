FADA April 2026: CV Sales Up 15%, Tractors Surge 23% on Rural Demand

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Commercial vehicle retail sales rose 15.02% YoY to 99,339 units in April 2026, with MCVs leading at 27% growth
  • 02/Tractor sales surged 23.22% to 75,109 units, the fastest-growing segment, driven by strong rural cash flows post-rabi season
  • 03/Rural markets outpaced urban demand sharply, with rural CV growth at 20.25% versus 10.22% in cities

India's automobile retail sales hit a record 26.1 lakh units in April 2026. The Federation of Automobile Dealers Associations (FADA) reported 12.94% year-on-year growth.

Five of six vehicle segments posted all-time April highs. Only construction equipment declined, slipping 2.25%.

Commercial vehicle retail sales rose 15.02% to 99,339 units. This was up from 86,364 units in April 2025.

Medium commercial vehicles led the CV pack with 27% growth. This signals a shift toward mid-distance, intra-regional freight over long-haul routes.

Tata Motors (NSE: TATAMOTORS) retained CV leadership with 37.14% market share. It sold 36,891 units, growing 20.3% — well above the industry average.

Mahindra & Mahindra (NSE: M&M) held steady at roughly 24.5% share with 24,308 units. VE Commercial Vehicles grew 18.3%, while Ashok Leyland ceded share despite double-digit growth.

Tractor sales recorded the strongest growth at 23.22%. Volumes hit 75,109 units versus 60,956 in April last year.

Mahindra retained tractor dominance with a combined ~42% share including its Swaraj brand. CNH Industrial grew over 50% YoY, making it the fastest-expanding player. Sonalika and Escorts Kubota both posted 30%-plus gains.

Rural demand clearly outpaced urban markets. Rural passenger vehicle sales grew 20.4% compared to just 7.1% in urban areas. Commercial vehicle demand in rural regions rose 20.25% against 10.22% in cities.

"The structural demand momentum which defined the second half of FY26 has carried into the new financial year," FADA Vice President Sai Giridhar said. He pointed to sustained demand across segments.

Dealer sentiment remains positive. Over 55% of dealers expect growth in coming months, and about 36% have revised their FY27 outlook upward.

Total retail sales fell roughly 3% sequentially from March. FADA attributed this to a typical post year-end slowdown, not structural weakness. Passenger vehicle inventory stands at 28–30 days, slightly above FADA's preferred range.
