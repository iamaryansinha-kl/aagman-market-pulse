Eqyizen Raises $460 Million in Zero-Coupon Rupee Bonds

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Eqyizen Investment, a Shapoorji Pallonji Group unit, raised ₹44.5 billion ($462.2 million) via zero-coupon rupee bonds at a yield of 18.95%, maturing July 2029.
  • 02/The proceeds will meet an obligation of Porteast Investment, another SP Group subsidiary, which has to pay around ₹35 billion on debt raised in May 2025.
  • 03/The bonds are secured by SP Group's stake in Tata Sons through Cyrus Investments, and sentiment has improved since Tata Trusts disclosed a proposed ₹250 billion stake sale.

Eqyizen Investment, a unit of the Shapoorji Pallonji Group, has raised ₹44.5 billion ($462.2 million) in fresh rupee debt. The funds came through zero-coupon bonds maturing in July 2029, sold at a yield of 18.95%.

The proceeds have a specific purpose. They will meet an obligation of Porteast Investment, another SP Group subsidiary, which has to pay around ₹35 billion on debt raised in May 2025.

The structure has an offshore leg. Mercury Finance, another SP Group firm, recently raised $125 million via zero-coupon US dollar bonds, also due July 2029. Those dollar funds were then invested in the Eqyizen rupee bond sale.

This is not the first time the group has run this playbook. In July, Mercury Finance had raised $650 million, which was invested in rupee debt sold by Eqyizen.

That July episode was larger. Bankers said Eqyizen had raised ₹213.5 billion via another issue at the same 18.95% yield. The current ₹44.5 billion issue repeats the structure at the same pricing.

The pattern is consistent: offshore zero-coupon dollars are routed into onshore zero-coupon rupee paper. The rupee proceeds then service legacy group obligations as they fall due.

Investor protection rests on one asset. The bonds are secured by SP Group's stake in Tata Sons, held through Cyrus Investments.

The pricing tells its own story. An 18.95% yield on secured paper, backed by a Tata Sons stake, signals stressed-credit pricing rather than market-rate borrowing.

Market sentiment, however, has improved recently. The turning point came earlier this month, when Tata Trusts, Tata Sons' controlling shareholder, said SP Group had proposed selling ₹250 billion worth of its Tata Sons stake.

That disclosure matters because the Tata Sons stake is the collateral underpinning this debt. A credible route to monetise the stake strengthens the security behind bonds like Eqyizen's.

Taken together, the issues frame the group's deleveraging story. Offshore dollars in, high-yield rupee paper out, legacy obligations serviced, and the Tata Sons stake sitting behind it all.

SP Group did not respond to Reuters' request for comment on the latest raising.

GLOSSARY: Zero-coupon bond: A debt instrument sold at a discount to face value that pays no periodic interest. The investor's return is the gap between the purchase price and the amount received at maturity. Rupee bond: A bond denominated in Indian rupees, issued and repaid in the local currency. Borrowers use rupee issues to service onshore obligations without currency mismatch. Yield: The effective return an investor earns on a bond, taking into account its price, face value, and time to maturity. Higher yields generally reflect higher perceived risk. Refinancing: Replacing or servicing existing debt with newly raised funds, often to match repayment timelines with fresh borrowing. Security/collateral: Assets pledged to bondholders as protection. Here, the SP Group's Tata Sons stake, held through Cyrus Investments, backs the Eqyizen bonds. Escrow: An arrangement where funds or assets are held by a third party until specified obligations are met, commonly used to ring-fence repayment sources in bond structures.
