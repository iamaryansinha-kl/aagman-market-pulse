10 Stocks With Highest Q4 Retail Shareholding Surge: Vedanta, MCX Lead

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Vedanta led with ₹2,191 crore increase in retail holding value during Q4 FY2026, stock gained 8.34%
  • 02/Top 10 stocks saw combined retail holding value surge of over ₹10,500 crore in March 2026 quarter
  • 03/Metals, energy and capital markets drew retail inflows amid Iran conflict, elevated crude prices and persistent FII selling

Retail investors increased their holdings across 10 domestic stocks by over ₹10,500 crore during the March 2026 quarter. Data from Primeinfobase shows selective buying despite heightened market volatility.

Vedanta Ltd saw the highest increase in retail holding value. Holdings rose by ₹2,191 crore to ₹28,570 crore from ₹26,379 crore in December 2025. The stock gained 8.34% during the period.

Hitachi Energy India ranked second with a ₹1,745 crore increase in retail holdings. Retail investors held ₹8,907 crore worth of shares at March-end versus ₹7,163 crore three months earlier. The stock surged 32.36%.

National Aluminium Company (NALCO) recorded a ₹1,176 crore rise in retail holding value. Holdings climbed to ₹7,698 crore from ₹6,522 crore in the previous quarter. The stock gained 22.84%.

Acutaas Chemicals saw retail holding value jump by ₹1,006 crore to ₹3,103 crore from ₹2,096 crore. The stock price rallied 50.07% during the same period.

Tata Steel witnessed a ₹970 crore increase in retail holding value. Holdings rose to ₹38,131 crore from ₹37,161 crore, while the stock gained 6.54%.

Oil & Natural Gas Corporation (ONGC) recorded an ₹843 crore rise in retail holdings. Investors held ₹10,090 crore worth of shares compared with ₹9,247 crore in December 2025. The stock climbed 18.42%.

GE Vernova T&D India, Bharat Forge, Sterlite Technologies and MCX rounded out the top 10. Their retail holdings rose by ₹653 crore, ₹624 crore, ₹612 crore and ₹602 crore respectively.

Sterlite Technologies was the top performer with a 70.89% stock gain. Retail holdings in the company more than doubled from ₹988 crore to ₹1,600 crore during the quarter.

The buying came amid the Iran conflict, elevated crude oil prices and persistent foreign investor selling. Retail investors gravitated toward metals, energy, industrials and capital markets.
