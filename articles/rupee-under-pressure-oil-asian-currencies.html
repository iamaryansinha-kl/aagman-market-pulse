Rupee Under Pressure as Oil Nears $109 and Asian Currencies Weaken

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Rupee expected to open at 94.25-94.30, weakening from 92.70 high hit 10 days ago
  • 02/Brent crude for June climbed above $109.40 a barrel, extending potential winning streak to seven sessions
  • 03/RBI intervening selectively to support rupee; heavy dollar buying by oil refiners continues

The Indian rupee is set to slip at Tuesday's open, trading in the 94.25-94.30 range. The currency settled marginally higher at 94.19 on Monday.

The rupee has weakened steadily from around 92.70 hit 10 days ago. Oil-linked flows and hedging-related dollar demand have chipped away at positive sentiment.

Brent crude for June climbed more than 1% to $109.40 a barrel on Tuesday. This could extend its winning streak to seven consecutive sessions.

Oil flows through the Strait of Hormuz have not resumed since the U.S. and Israel attacked Iran two months ago. A ceasefire has held since early April, but analysts see the lack of shipments as a major obstacle to cooling prices.

"Oil appears to have settled at a higher level, leaving limited scope for sustained relief for the rupee," a currency trader at a private-sector bank said.

The Reserve Bank of India has been intervening to alleviate pressure on the rupee. However, the support has been selective rather than aggressive, traders noted.

On a day-to-day basis, there is heavy dollar buying by oil refiners. Little offsetting supply is expected, with limited RBI intervention anticipated.

Most Asian currencies weakened on Tuesday, compounding pressure on the rupee. The dollar index inched up against its peers.
