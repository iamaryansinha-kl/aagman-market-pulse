UAE Quits OPEC: Third-Largest Producer Exits Cartel in Shock Move

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/UAE announces sudden exit from OPEC after six decades, becoming a wild-card player free from production quotas
  • 02/Strait of Hormuz closure has forced Gulf producers to shut in 10 million barrels per day, or 10% of global supplies
  • 03/Analysts warn OPEC's market power will diminish and fear a domino effect as other members may follow Abu Dhabi out

The United Arab Emirates announced on Tuesday it will quit OPEC within days. The shock move blindsided partner nations after six decades of membership.

The UAE is OPEC's third-largest producer. It has long chafed against production quotas imposed by the cartel's de facto leader, Saudi Arabia.

The exit was years in the making. Tensions between Abu Dhabi and Riyadh over oil policy date back to the COVID-19 pandemic at the start of the decade.

The Strait of Hormuz closure has forced Gulf producers to shut in at least 10 million barrels per day. That equals 10% of world supplies, according to the International Energy Agency.

UAE Energy Minister Suhail Al Mazrouei said the Hormuz shutdown made the exit less disruptive. The country will now be able to boost output freely once the war ends.

The UAE produced 3.64 million barrels per day in February, far above official numbers. Analysts believe the real figure was even higher.

"OPEC's market power will diminish," said Greg Brew, analyst at Eurasia Group. "The UAE's departure will undermine the group's credibility."

"If oil producing capacity is leaving the cartel's influence, that's bearish over a three-to-five year timeframe," said Clayton Seigle of CSIS. He warned of a potential domino effect.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHY IS THE UAE LEAVING OPEC?
THE UAE ANNOUNCED ITS EXIT FROM OPEC ON TUESDAY, APRIL 28, 2026, AFTER DECADES OF TENSIONS WITH SAUDI ARABIA OVER PRODUCTION QUOTAS. ENERGY MINISTER SUHAIL AL MAZROUEI CITED THE WAR IN IRAN AND THE RESULTING STRAIT OF HORMUZ CLOSURE AS THE TIPPING POINT.
HOW MUCH OIL DOES THE UAE PRODUCE?
THE UAE PRODUCED 3.64 MILLION BARRELS PER DAY IN FEBRUARY 2026, ACCORDING TO THE INTERNATIONAL ENERGY AGENCY. THIS FIGURE IS FAR ABOVE OFFICIAL OPEC NUMBERS, AND SEVERAL TRADERS BELIEVE THE REAL OUTPUT WAS EVEN HIGHER.
WHAT IS THE IMPACT OF THE STRAIT OF HORMUZ CLOSURE?
THE EFFECTIVE CLOSURE OF THE STRAIT OF HORMUZ HAS FORCED GULF PRODUCERS INCLUDING SAUDI ARABIA, THE UAE, IRAQ AND KUWAIT TO SHUT IN AT LEAST 10 MILLION BARRELS PER DAY, OR 10 PER CENT OF GLOBAL SUPPLIES.
WHAT DO ANALYSTS SAY ABOUT OPEC'S FUTURE?
GREG BREW OF EURASIA GROUP SAID "OPEC'S MARKET POWER WILL DIMINISH" AND THE UAE'S DEPARTURE WILL UNDERMINE THE GROUP'S CREDIBILITY. CLAYTON SEIGLE OF CSIS CALLED THE MOVE "BEARISH" OVER A THREE-TO-FIVE YEAR TIMEFRAME AND WARNED OF A POTENTIAL DOMINO EFFECT.
WILL OTHER OPEC MEMBERS FOLLOW THE UAE?
SEVERAL OPEC+ DELEGATES SAID THEY DO NOT PLAN TO FOLLOW THE UAE OUT THE DOOR AND DO NOT EXPECT THE DEPARTURE TO TRIGGER A WIDER EXODUS IMMEDIATELY. HOWEVER, ANALYSTS FEAR THAT IF MORE CAPACITY LEAVES THE CARTEL, OPEC'S ABILITY TO MANAGE PRICES WILL WEAKEN FURTHER.
