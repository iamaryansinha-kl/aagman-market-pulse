Bajaj Finance QIP: Board Approves Rs 17,500 Crore Fundraise

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Bajaj Finance board approves raising up to Rs 17,500 crore through a qualified institutions placement and preferential warrants
  • 02/Rs 11,700 crore will come via a QIP to institutional investors, while Rs 5,800 crore will come via warrants to promoter Bajaj Finserv
  • 03/The company will seek shareholder approval for the QIP through an Extra Ordinary General Meeting

Bajaj Finance Ltd (NSE: BAJFINANCE) will raise up to Rs 17,500 crore in fresh capital. The board of directors approved the fundraising on Friday, October 2, 2026.

The company disclosed the plan in a notice to the stock exchanges. The total is split across two separate instruments.

The larger component is a Rs 11,700 crore qualified institutions placement, or QIP. Under this route, shares will be allotted to institutional investors.

The second component is Rs 5,800 crore. This will be raised through the issue of warrants on a preferential basis to promoter Bajaj Finserv (NSE: BAJAJFINSV).

Warrants are instruments that convert into equity shares at a later stage. Issuing them to the promoter is likely intended to ensure the promoter holding is not diluted by the fresh shares sold through the QIP.

The company will seek approval of shareholders for the proposed QIP. It plans to do this by convening an Extra Ordinary General Meeting.

A QIP is a SEBI-sanctioned route that lets listed companies issue equity to qualified institutional buyers. It is faster than a public offer and is a common way for large Indian lenders to top up capital.

The fundraise comes at a time when Bajaj Finance is expanding at a pace well ahead of the broader lending system. The non-banking finance company reported assets under management of roughly Rs 5.47 lakh crore as of June 30, 2026.

That AUM figure marks a 24% rise from a year earlier. The company booked 16.13 million new loans in the June quarter alone.

Bajaj Finance is India's largest private-sector NBFC. Its customer franchise stood at about 124 million as of June 30, 2026, up from roughly 107 million a year earlier.

Rapid balance-sheet growth of this kind consumes capital continuously. Lenders that outgrow the system typically need periodic equity injections to hold their capital ratios steady.

The wider NBFC sector has also been operating under tighter rules on unsecured consumer credit and revised bank risk weights. Well-capitalised, diversified lenders have been gaining market share from smaller rivals in this environment.

Bajaj Finserv, the promoter entity, is the Bajaj group holding company. Its financial services businesses include Bajaj Finance, the Bajaj Allianz insurance ventures, Bajaj Housing Finance and the group's asset management business.

The promoter's participation through warrants means it will put fresh money in alongside the institutions buying into the QIP. That alignment is typically read as a confidence signal, though the structure also protects the promoter group's ownership level.

Key details remain undisclosed in the exchange notice. The conversion terms for the warrants, including the price at which they convert into shares, have not been announced.

The pricing of the QIP shares and the identity of the institutional allottees will also be watched closely. Those details typically emerge once the placement process concludes.

The next formal step is the Extra Ordinary General Meeting. The date and agenda for the meeting will be announced in due course.

WHAT IS A QIP AND WHO PARTICIPATES IN IT?
A QUALIFIED INSTITUTIONS PLACEMENT, OR QIP, IS A SEBI-SANCTIONED ROUTE THROUGH WHICH A LISTED COMPANY ALLOTS SHARES TO QUALIFIED INSTITUTIONAL BUYERS. THESE BUYERS INCLUDE MUTUAL FUNDS, INSURANCE COMPANIES, PENSION FUNDS AND FOREIGN PORTFOLIO INVESTORS. IN THIS CASE, INSTITUTIONAL INVESTORS WILL BE ALLOTTED SHARES OF BAJAJ FINANCE UNDER THE RS 11,700 CRORE QIP.
WHY ARE WARRANTS BEING ISSUED TO BAJAJ FINSERV?
WARRANTS ARE FINANCIAL INSTRUMENTS THAT CAN BE CONVERTED INTO EQUITY SHARES AT A LATER DATE. BY TAKING WARRANTS, PROMOTER BAJAJ FINSERV WILL SUBSCRIBE TO RS 5,800 CRORE ON A PREFERENTIAL BASIS. THIS STRUCTURE IS LIKELY DESIGNED TO ENSURE THAT THE PROMOTER'S STAKE IS NOT DILUTED AS NEW SHARES ARE ISSUED TO INSTITUTIONS THROUGH THE QIP.
DOES BAJAJ FINANCE NEED SHAREHOLDER APPROVAL FOR THE FUNDRAISE?
YES. THE COMPANY WILL SEEK APPROVAL OF SHAREHOLDERS FOR THE PROPOSED QIP. IT PLANS TO DO THIS BY CONVENING AN EXTRA ORDINARY GENERAL MEETING, THE DATE FOR WHICH WILL BE ANNOUNCED IN DUE COURSE.

BAJAJ FINANCE IS INDIA'S LARGEST PRIVATE-SECTOR NON-BANKING FINANCE COMPANY. ITS ASSETS UNDER MANAGEMENT STOOD AT ROUGHLY RS 5.47 LAKH CRORE AS OF JUNE 30, 2026, UP 24% FROM A YEAR EARLIER. ITS CUSTOMER FRANCHISE CROSSED 124 MILLION, AND IT BOOKED 16.13 MILLION NEW LOANS IN THE JUNE QUARTER OF FY2027.
