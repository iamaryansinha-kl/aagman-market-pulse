HAL Hands Over 3 Indigenous Aerospace Platforms to IAF; Goldman Sachs Reiterates Buy

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

Hindustan Aeronautics Ltd handed over three indigenously developed platforms to the Indian Air Force and Pawan Hans on Monday. Shares gained 2% to a day’s high of Rs 4,889 on the BSE.

Defence Minister Rajnath Singh attended the handover ceremony. Three milestones were marked: the indigenously designed and developed Dhruv-Next Generation civil helicopter received Type Certification from the DGCA and was handed over; the final batch of LCA Final Operational Clearance (FOC) Trainer aircraft was delivered to the IAF; and the first aircraft from the HTT-40 Basic Trainer series production was handed to the IAF.

What was delivered? Four Dhruv-NG helicopters were handed to Pawan Hans Limited. Two LCA FOC Trainer aircraft were delivered to the IAF, completing twin-seat deliveries under the FOC contract. The first HTT-40 Basic Trainer was delivered to the IAF, beginning deliveries under a 70-aircraft contract that will replace the ageing HPT-32 Deepak fleet.

The HTT-40 is a tandem-seat, fully aerobatic basic trainer powered by a Honeywell turboprop engine. It features a glass cockpit and Martin-Baker zero-zero ejection seats.

The LCA Trainer is the twin-seat variant of the indigenous Tejas fighter, used for advanced combat flight training. The Dhruv-NG is the next-generation civil version of HAL’s Advanced Light Helicopter, developed for offshore and passenger operations.

Singh said the HTT-40 handover was a step towards ending India’s dependence on foreign countries for basic trainers. He added that the aircraft’s capabilities could create export opportunities.

What is Goldman Sachs saying? Goldman Sachs maintains a Buy rating on HAL with a target price of Rs 5,870. The brokerage noted that deliveries are picking up across fighter, trainer, and helicopter platforms. GE has delivered another three F404 engines for the LCA Mk1A.

Goldman Sachs said supply constraints are gradually easing. The focus is now shifting towards converting HAL’s large order backlog into revenues. The latest handovers align with the government’s push to expand indigenous defence manufacturing.

HAL said it is well positioned across aircraft, helicopters, aero engines, avionics, and MRO projects. Expected orders for fighter aircraft, rotary-wing platforms, and upgrade projects are likely to provide strong medium- to long-term revenue visibility.

Last month, HAL signed a long-term agreement with Safran Aircraft Engines for the production and supply of turbine ring forgings in superalloys for the CFM LEAP engine programme.
