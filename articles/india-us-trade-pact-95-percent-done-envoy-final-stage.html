India-US Trade Pact 95% Done, Envoy Says Deal in Final 1-2% Stage

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/US Ambassador Sergio Gor announces the India-US trade pact is in the final 1-2% of legal text completion
  • 02/The deal faced delays after a Supreme Court intervention but was revived by US Trade Representative Jamieson Lee Greer's New Delhi visit
  • 03/Both nations are targeting $500 billion in bilateral trade, with $20.5 billion in new US-bound investments already facilitated this year

US Ambassador to India Sergio Gor announced the bilateral trade agreement is nearing completion. Speaking at the USISPF Leadership Summit 2026, he said negotiators are hammering out the final "1 or 2 per cent" of the legal text.

The trade pact has been under active negotiation for approximately 18 months. It faced a brief delay due to a recent Supreme Court intervention that disrupted the initial timeline.

US Trade Representative Jamieson Lee Greer visited New Delhi for two days last week. His visit successfully salvaged momentum and pushed the deal back toward the finish line.

Ambassador Gor dismissed rumours of diplomatic friction between the two nations. He confirmed President Donald Trump remains deeply committed to a "win-win" alignment with New Delhi.

The US Embassy in New Delhi facilitated $20.5 billion in new investments flowing back to the United States this year. This figure dramatically outpaced traditional European outposts which announced fractions of that amount.

India continues to export more goods and services to the United States than to any other single nation. On defence, the US conducts more joint military exercises with India than with any other global partner.

Both administrations are now focused on clearing the remaining legal language. The immediate goal is to lock in the $500 billion bilateral trade target originally championed by President Trump and Prime Minister Narendra Modi.

A RECENT SUPREME COURT INTERVENTION DISRUPTED THE INITIAL TIMELINE. THE DEAL WAS NEARLY COMPLETE BEFORE THIS JUDICIAL INTERRUPTION OCCURRED.

BOTH NATIONS ARE TARGETING $500 BILLION IN BILATERAL TRADE. THIS TARGET WAS ORIGINALLY CHAMPIONED BY PRESIDENT DONALD TRUMP AND PRIME MINISTER NARENDRA MODI.
