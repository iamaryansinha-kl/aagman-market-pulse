Tata Agratas Increases UK Loan to £1.15 Billion, Extends Repayment to 2027

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Tata Agratas increases UK loan to £1.15 billion, a 53% jump from earlier £750 million commitment
  • 02/Lenders extend repayment deadline to September 2027, with option to push to March 2028
  • 03/Somerset gigafactory on track for 2027; first JLR battery revenue expected this fiscal year

Tata Group's UK-based battery firm Agratas Ltd will draw £1.15 billion (₹14,000 crore) in loans. This represents a 53% increase from the earlier agreed £750 million (₹9,500 crore).

UK company filings reviewed by Mint show lenders have extended the repayment deadline. The deadline moved from July 2026 to September 2027. There is also an option to further extend until March 2028.

The additional funding comes after Agratas secured a £380 million grant from the UK government in April. The company signed an agreement with Standard Chartered Bank in June to create collateral using its gigafactory land in Somerset.

"The Company has received credit committee approval from the banking syndicate for this loan to be increased from £750m to £1,150m and for the repayment date to be extended to 30 September 2027," Agratas noted in its annual return.

The battery plant is scheduled to be ready by 2027. Agratas is gearing up to record ₹400 crore in revenue for the first time this fiscal. This is part of a ₹5,000 crore, seven-year deal with Jaguar Land Rover.

In 2023, the Tata Group established Agratas Energy Storage Solutions Pvt. Ltd. Its UK subsidiary, Agratas Ltd, is building a 40 GWh plant in Somerset. The Indian unit is building a 20 GWh facility in Sanand, Gujarat.

Together, the plants are expected to meet Tata Group's automobile businesses' lithium-ion cell requirements. They will also offer battery solutions for battery energy storage system services.

Agratas' capital expenditure increased to £279 million in FY26 from £173 million in FY25. Research and development expenditure nearly tripled from £7.7 million to £20.9 million. Employee count rose from 193 to 320.

"The necessary investment is expected to be funded primarily through Agratas Ltd securing debt facilities and supplemented by equity investment being provided by Agratas Energy Storage Solutions Pvt. Ltd," the management said.

Tata Sons has invested £247 million in Agratas through equity. Natarajan Chandrasekaran, chairman of Tata Sons, is also on the board of Agratas.

However, Agratas has acknowledged risks around EV adoption speed. "A delay or extension of this deadline may impact the Group's growth plans," the company said on UK and EU legislation driving towards 100% electric vehicle sales by 2035.
