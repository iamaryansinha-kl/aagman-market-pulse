Subway India Operator EverBrands Files Rs 600 Crore IPO With Sebi

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/EverBrands India, the master franchise operator of Subway in India, filed its DRHP with Sebi on September 29, 2026 for a Rs 600 crore fresh-issue IPO
  • 02/The issue has no offer-for-sale component; proceeds will fund new company-owned stores and repay debt; the network stands at 1,008 Indian stores plus 8 in Sri Lanka
  • 03/Watch IPO pricing and listing-date performance against listed QSR peers Devyani, Sapphire Foods and Restaurant Brands Asia

EverBrands India Limited has filed its Draft Red Herring Prospectus with the Securities and Exchange Board of India. The filing was made on September 29, 2026.

The company is the master franchise operator of Subway in India. It is seeking to raise up to Rs 600 crore through the IPO.

The issue is structured entirely as a fresh issue of equity shares. There is no offer-for-sale component, meaning no existing shareholder is cashing out at the offer stage.

According to the DRHP, the proceeds will be used to fund new COCO stores. COCO stands for company-owned, company-operated outlets. A portion will also go towards repaying debt.

CONTEXT:

EverBrands runs Subway India under a master franchise from Subway International. The global Subway brand is owned by Roark Capital, a US-based private equity firm.

The Everstone connection runs deep. The Everstone Group, a Singapore-headquartered private investment firm with over US$6 billion in assets, signed the master franchise agreement with Subway in November 2021. It was described at the time as one of the largest master franchise agreements in QSR history.

That 2021 deal covered India, Sri Lanka and Bangladesh, with a commitment to add more than 2,000 restaurants over ten years. Everstone is also the master franchisee of Burger King in India.

The model has since shifted heavily towards ownership. COCO stores give the company full control of operations and economics, but they are capital-intensive. That shift explains the funding logic behind this IPO.

The store mix has moved decisively. COCO outlets grew from 311 stores on March 31, 2024 to 434 stores on March 31, 2025, and then to 678 stores on March 31, 2026.

As of March 31, 2026, the network stood at 1,008 Subway stores across India. Of these, 678 are COCO stores and 330 are franchise-owned, franchise-operated or FOFO stores.

The company also operates 8 FOFO stores in Sri Lanka. That takes the total network to 1,016 stores.

On the current footprint, roughly 67 percent of Indian stores are now company-owned. That is a sharp reversal from the franchise-heavy structure Everstone inherited in 2021.

The revenue numbers reflect the expansion. Revenue from the QSR vertical rose to Rs 693.09 crore in FY26. That compares with Rs 480.38 crore in FY25 and Rs 355.31 crore in FY24.

That is growth of about 44 percent year-on-year in FY26. The prior year had grown roughly 35 percent.

EverBrands is not a pure-play Subway story. It also runs a beverages vertical built around installed coffee machines.

The installed coffee machine base rose to 9,455 machines as of March 31, 2026. That is up from 8,322 machines on March 31, 2025 and 7,217 machines on March 31, 2024.

Beverages revenue climbed to Rs 240.57 crore in FY26. It stood at Rs 206.36 crore in FY25 and Rs 172.16 crore in FY24.

Combined revenue from operations across both verticals topped Rs 933 crore in FY26. The QSR and beverages segments contributed Rs 693.09 crore and Rs 240.57 crore respectively.

For context, the combined figure was around Rs 687 crore in FY25 and Rs 527 crore in FY24. The two-year growth trajectory has been steep.

IMPACT:

The filing lands in a crowded primary market. As many as 14 companies filed IPO papers with Sebi in the week of September 21-26 alone.

The QSR pipeline gives investors a familiar frame of reference. Devyani International, Sapphire Foods and Restaurant Brands Asia are already listed, and their trading multiples will shape how this issue is priced.

A pure fresh issue means all the money comes into the company. For investors, that is a cleaner structure than an offer-for-sale, though it also means post-issue shareholding dilution without any secondary supply.

The debt repayment component signals the COCO buildout has been funded partly through leverage. The fresh equity should reduce that burden while funding the next leg of store expansion.

Risks are visible too. Company-owned stores carry higher fixed costs, and any slowdown in footfalls or input-cost inflation hits margins faster than a franchise-heavy model.

Subway's global brand recognition remains a draw in India's expanding QSR market. But the chain competes in a segment crowded with burger, pizza and fried-chicken players, all chasing the same urban consumer.

What to watch next: the RHP will reveal pricing, profitability, and the exact split between store funding and debt repayment. Peer valuations and the performance of recent mainboard listings will set the tone for demand.
