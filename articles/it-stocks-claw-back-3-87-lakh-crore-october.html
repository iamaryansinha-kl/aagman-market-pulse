IT Stocks Claw Back Rs 3.87 Lakh Crore as FIIs Return

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Nifty IT stocks have recovered about Rs 3.87 lakh crore in market value in October, with the sector's combined capitalisation rising from Rs 22.01 lakh crore to Rs 25.88 lakh crore.
  • •- October gains are broad: TCS up 23%, Tech Mahindra 20%, Mphasis and Wipro 19% each, Infosys 17% and HCL Technologies 16%.
  • •- Foreign portfolio investors bought Rs 6,463 crore of IT shares in the September quarter, but the buying is selective rather than sector-wide.
  • •- Jefferies says the rally is a price-to-earnings re-rating without revenue or EPS upgrades, driven by hopes for terminal growth.
  • •- HSBC, Axis Mutual Fund and PL Capital remain cautious, flagging AI-led deflation and weak near-term earnings growth.

After nearly a year as the market's most reliable source of disappointment, Indian information technology has produced October's most decisive rally. The combined market capitalisation of Nifty IT stocks has climbed from about Rs 22.01 lakh crore to Rs 25.88 lakh crore this month — a recovery of roughly Rs 3.87 lakh crore — and foreign portfolio investors have returned to the sector with Rs 6,463 crore of buying in the September quarter, according to NSDL data. The question the market is now arguing over is whether this is the long-awaited IT reversal or a valuation-led rebound that will fade into another difficult year.

The breadth of the October move is striking. TCS, the month's biggest large-cap gainer, has jumped 23%; Tech Mahindra has risen 20%; Mphasis and Wipro have each advanced 19%; Infosys is up 17%; and HCL Technologies has gained 16%. The only significant laggard is Oracle Financial Services Software, which has declined about 5%. The Nifty IT index itself has risen roughly 17% in October, clawing back a chunk of the near-20% decline it suffered in the first two months of 2026, and it has outperformed the Nifty 50 by about twelve percentage points since February — with most of that relative gain arriving in the last three weeks.

Jefferies' verdict on what is driving the rally is the one that matters, because it cuts against the happy narrative. Analysts Akshat Agarwal and Ayush Bansal say the October surge is primarily a price-to-earnings re-rating: revenue growth and earnings estimates have not been upgraded. What has changed, in their telling, is sentiment on the long-term outlook. Consensus dollar-revenue growth expectations for FY28 have not been materially cut after the September-quarter results — a first, after the downgrades that followed the March and June quarters — and with IT companies still delivering growth despite a weak global macro and the deflationary pressure of AI, investors are willing to revisit the terminal growth assumptions they had spent a year slashing.

The ownership data tells its own more cautious story. Foreign buying in the September quarter was real but selective. FII ownership of Wipro rose from 8.85% in June to 9.46% in September, and Infosys saw foreign ownership climb from 27.09% to 27.44%; Oracle Financial Services Software attracted enough buying to lift foreign ownership by 173 basis points to 11.43%. But Persistent Systems saw no change, and TCS and Mphasis recorded marginal declines. The pattern reads less like a foreign-capital stampede back into the sector and more like a handful of institutional investors upgrading specific names they had abandoned — early positioning, not yet a trend.

The sceptics' case rests on earnings, and it is not trivial. HSBC notes that the FTSE India IT index trades at eighteen times forward earnings against a five-year average of about twenty-three times, but argues the discount is earned: sector earnings growth has slowed from an already modest 4-5% in recent years to about 2-3%, largely because of AI-led deflation, and HSBC expects FY27 to be the sector's most challenging year before a recovery in FY28 and FY29 takes growth back only to the mid-single digits. Axis Mutual Fund, which remains underweight, argues that AI spending is still concentrated in infrastructure build-out rather than the discretionary technology services that fill Indian vendors' order books — meaning AI acts as a margin and growth overhang before it becomes a meaningful revenue contributor — and flags intensifying competition among large IT firms, mid-sized rivals, consultancies and specialised AI providers. PL Capital has also retained an underweight stance with a 150-basis-point sector underweight, cautioning that pressure on realisations, growth and margins will keep volatility intermittent even if the largest companies emerge as survivors.

The market, in other words, is pricing a selective recovery rather than a clean sector-wide turnaround — and even the bulls concede the distinction. Jefferies estimates that current prices imply organic constant-currency revenue growth of 0-9% for large IT companies over FY26 to FY36 and 6-14% for mid-sized firms, rates that sit two to four percentage points below what the sector actually delivered over FY16 to FY26. Infosys and Sagility are the most heavily discounted relative to their own histories, with implied growth roughly six percentage points below their past delivery, and Jefferies sees Infosys and TCS as the only large-cap names with scope for further re-rating, with Sagility standing out among mid-caps; beyond seventeen to eighteen times earnings for the large caps, valuation upside thins unless the growth outlook improves materially.

For investors, the practical framing is this: the October rally has repaired portfolios, not fundamentals. The sector's earnings engine is still growing at 2-3%, FY27 is still forecast as the trough year, and the AI question — overhang first, revenue later — is unresolved. What has genuinely changed is that the relentless estimate cuts have stopped, and in a market where midcaps are in correction and the Nifty is down 15% for the year, a sector with stable-to-slightly-improving expectations and a cheaper-than-average multiple is exactly where nervous money goes. Whether that money stays will be decided not by sentiment but by the December-quarter prints — the first real test of whether the no-more-cuts verdict on FY28 growth survives contact with client budgets.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
HOW MUCH MARKET VALUE HAVE IT STOCKS RECOVERED?
About Rs 3.87 lakh crore in October, taking the Nifty IT cohort's combined market capitalisation from Rs 22.01 lakh crore to Rs 25.88 lakh crore.
WHICH IT STOCKS LED THE OCTOBER RALLY?
TCS rose 23%, Tech Mahindra 20%, Mphasis and Wipro 19% each, Infosys 17% and HCL Technologies 16%; Oracle Financial Services Software fell about 5%.
WHAT DO BROKERAGES SAY IS DRIVING THE RALLY?
Jefferies calls it a price-to-earnings re-rating without earnings upgrades, driven by hopes for long-term growth after FY28 estimate cuts stopped.
ARE FOREIGN INVESTORS BUYING THE WHOLE SECTOR?
No. FIIs bought Rs 6,463 crore of IT in Q3, but buying was concentrated in names like Wipro, Infosys and Oracle Financial; TCS and Mphasis saw marginal declines in foreign ownership. GLOSSARY: - Re-rating: A rise in the valuation multiple investors pay for a stock, independent of earnings changes. - Terminal growth: The long-run growth rate investors assume for a business when valuing it. - AI deflation: Falling prices for technology services as artificial intelligence automates work previously billed by the hour. - FII ownership: The proportion of a company's shares held by foreign portfolio investors.
