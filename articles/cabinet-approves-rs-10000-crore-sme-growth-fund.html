Union Cabinet Approves Rs 10,000 Crore SME Growth Fund for MSMEs

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/The Union Cabinet approved a Rs 10,000 crore SME Growth Fund on October 6, 2026, operationalising a Budget 2026-27 promise
  • 02/The fund provides patient equity capital to high-potential SMEs, with the majority of allocation going to manufacturing-focused units
  • 03/Watch rollout: detailed fund guidelines, deployment timelines, and the accompanying TReDS liquidity reforms

The Union Cabinet, chaired by Prime Minister Narendra Modi, approved the Rs 10,000 crore SME Growth Fund on Tuesday, October 6, 2026. The fund will provide long-term equity support to high-potential small and medium enterprises.

The fund was first announced in Budget 2026-27 in February by Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman. Tuesday's Cabinet approval operationalises the announcement and clears the path for rollout.

The SME Growth Fund sits under the first pillar, "Equity Support," of the government's three-pronged "Creating Champion MSMEs" strategy. The package targets enterprises with demonstrated business viability and the ability to scale.

The fund will invest patient capital rather than hand out loans. Eligible SMEs can deploy the equity for innovation, acquisitions, and strategic investments.

The capital can also fund scaling operations, expanding manufacturing capacity, and adopting advanced technologies. Other uses include entering international markets, integrating into global value chains, and boosting export competitiveness.

The majority of the fund's allocation will go to manufacturing-focused SMEs. It will also back enterprises in services, technology, innovation-driven sectors, and strategic value chains.

The fund is designed to fix a specific gap: the shortage of growth capital for small and medium enterprises. Equity for scaling — patient, long-horizon risk capital — has been the hardest kind of financing for this segment to raise.

Alongside the main fund, the Budget package includes a Rs 2,000 crore top-up of the Self-Reliant India Fund. That fund was set up in 2021 to widen micro enterprises' access to risk capital.

The strategy's second pillar is liquidity support, built around the Trade Receivables e-Discounting System (TReDS). All purchases by central public sector enterprises from MSMEs will be settled through TReDS as a mandate.

The Credit Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro and Small Enterprises (CGTMSE) will guarantee credit for invoice discounting on TReDS. The Government e-Marketplace (GeM) will also be linked with TReDS for financier information sharing.

TReDS receivables will be packaged as asset-backed securities. The aim is to build a secondary market, giving financiers an exit and unlocking more working-capital flow to MSME suppliers.

The scale of the target base justifies the push. MSMEs contribute 35.4% of India's manufacturing output and 48.58% of exports. The sector accounts for 31.1% of GDP.

The sector spans more than 7.47 crore enterprises employing over 32.82 crore persons. Turning more of them from sub-scale units into exporters is the economic logic behind the champion strategy.

The government's MSME Champions Scheme already identifies and nurtures select enterprises, upgrading processes and competitiveness. The SME Growth Fund extends that approach from process improvement to balance-sheet scale.

The Budget allocation to the Ministry of MSME stands at Rs 24,566.27 crore. That includes Rs 22,647.26 crore in revenue spending and Rs 1,919.01 crore in capital spending.

What to watch next: the detailed fund guidelines, the instrument structure, and how quickly capital reaches eligible SMEs. The Budget promise now has Cabinet sign-off — delivery timelines will be the real test.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT IS THE SME GROWTH FUND AND WHO IS ELIGIBLE?
THE RS 10,000 CRORE SME GROWTH FUND, APPROVED BY THE UNION CABINET ON OCTOBER 6, 2026, PROVIDES LONG-TERM EQUITY CAPITAL TO HIGH-POTENTIAL SMALL AND MEDIUM ENTERPRISES. IT TARGETS VIABLE SMEs WITH DEMONSTRATED BUSINESS VIABILITY AND THE ABILITY TO SCALE. THE MAJORITY OF THE ALLOCATION GOES TO MANUFACTURING-FOCUSED SMEs, WITH SERVICES, TECHNOLOGY, AND INNOVATION-DRIVEN SECTORS ALSO COVERED.
HOW DOES PATIENT CAPITAL DIFFER FROM A LOAN OR SUBSIDY?
PATIENT CAPITAL IS EQUITY INVESTED WITH A LONG TIME HORIZON. UNLIKE A LOAN, IT CARRIES NO MONTHLY REPAYMENT OBLIGATION; UNLIKE A SUBSIDY, IT IS INVESTED WITH AN EXPECTATION OF RETURNS AND SHARES OWNERSHIP. THIS STRUCTURE SUITS SMEs FUNDING ACQUISITIONS, CAPACITY EXPANSION, AND TECHNOLOGY ADOPTION, WHERE RETURNS TAKE YEARS TO MATERIALISE.
WHAT TREDS REFORMS ACCOMPANY THE FUND?
FOUR MEASURES FORM THE LIQUIDITY PILLAR. ONE, TReDS BECOMES THE MANDATED SETTLEMENT PLATFORM FOR ALL CPSE PURCHASES FROM MSMEs. TWO, CGTMSE PROVIDES CREDIT GUARANTEES FOR INVOICE DISCOUNTING ON TReDS. THREE, GeM WILL BE LINKED WITH TReDS FOR FINANCIER INFORMATION SHARING. FOUR, TReDS RECEIVABLES WILL BE ISSUED AS ASSET-BACKED SECURITIES TO BUILD A SECONDARY MARKET.
WHY DO MSMEs STRUGGLE TO ACCESS GROWTH CAPITAL?
THE GOVERNMENT'S STATED RATIONALE FOR THE FUND IS THE SHORTAGE OF GROWTH CAPITAL FOR SMALL AND MEDIUM ENTERPRISES — LONG-HORIZON EQUITY FOR SCALING IS SCARCE FOR THIS SEGMENT. MICRO ENTERPRISES GET A SEPARATE Rs 2,000 CRORE TOP-UP OF THE SELF-RELIANT INDIA FUND, ORIGINALLY SET UP IN 2021, TO IMPROVE THEIR ACCESS TO RISK CAPITAL.
HOW DOES THE FUND FIT THE WIDER MSME PACKAGE?
IT IS THE EQUITY SUPPORT PILLAR OF THE THREE-PRONGED "CREATING CHAMPION MSMEs" STRATEGY FROM BUDGET 2026-27, ALONGSIDE THE TReDS-BASED LIQUIDITY PILLAR. THE MINISTRY OF MSME'S BUDGET ALLOCATION IS RS 24,566.27 CRORE. MSMEs ACCOUNT FOR 35.4% OF MANUFACTURING OUTPUT, 48.58% OF EXPORTS, AND 31.1% OF GDP, ACROSS 7.47 CRORE+ ENTERPRISES EMPLOYING 32.82 CRORE+ PERSONS. GLOSSARY: MSME: Micro, small and medium enterprises — India's 7.47 crore+ enterprises that produce 31.1% of GDP and employ over 32.8 crore people. PATIENT CAPITAL: Long-term equity investment that does not demand quick returns, allowing companies to fund growth, innovation and acquisitions. TReDS: Trade Receivables e-Discounting System — an RBI-regulated platform where MSMEs discount their invoices to unlock early payment. CGTMSE: Credit Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro and Small Enterprises — a government guarantee scheme backing loans to MSMEs, now extended to TReDS invoice discounting. ASSET-BACKED SECURITIES: Financial instruments backed by a pool of receivables; Sebi's Budget proposal would package TReDS receivables into tradable securities.
