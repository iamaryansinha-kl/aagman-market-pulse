PE-VC Investments Down 32% in April 2026: Deal Value at $1.9B

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/PE-VC investments fell 32% YoY in April 2026 to $1.9 billion across 87 deals, down from $2.8 billion in April 2025
  • 02/January-April 2026 cumulative investment stood at $12 billion across 435 deals versus $14.6 billion across 467 deals last year
  • 03/Mega deals over $100 million declined to $1.1 billion across six deals; Kreditbee led with a $280 million fundraise

Private Equity-Venture Capital investments in India dropped 32% year-on-year in April 2026. Deal value stood at $1.9 billion across 87 deals, according to data from research firm Venture Intelligence.

This marks a sharp slowdown from April 2025, when PE-VC firms deployed $2.8 billion across 124 deals. On a sequential basis, investment value nearly halved from the $4.7 billion recorded in March 2026.

For the first four months of 2026, cumulative PE-VC investments reached approximately $12 billion across 435 deals. This compares to $14.6 billion across 467 deals in the same period last year.

Mega deals over $100 million also lost momentum in April 2026. Six such deals worth $1.1 billion were recorded, down from seven deals worth $1.9 billion in April 2025.

The largest deal of the month was digital lending firm Kreditbee raising $280 million. Investors included Motilal Oswal Alternates, Advent International, and PremjiInvest.

Other notable fundraises included Apothecon Pharmaceuticals securing $270 million from Everstone. Hospital chain Baby Memorial Hospital raised $186 million from KKR.

Early-stage investments declined to $253 million across 40 deals in April 2026. Growth-stage funding was flat at $520 million across 26 deals, while late-stage investments fell sharply to $376 million from $653 million.

The average early-stage deal size doubled to $6 million from $3 million in April 2025. Growth-stage average deal size rose to $20 million from $12 million, while late-stage deal size dropped to $25 million from $33 million.

"With historically high dry powder, the ecosystem remains active, but clearly more disciplined and selective. Geopolitical uncertainty might add time and caution, but it is not expected to reduce capital deployed," said Vikas Choudhary, Founding Partner at Playbook Partners.

Arun Natarajan, Founder of Venture Intelligence, noted that the largest deal in April was much smaller than top mega deals seen in Q1 2026. He added that the prolonging US-Iran war could impact funding for tech start-ups planning IPOs this year.
