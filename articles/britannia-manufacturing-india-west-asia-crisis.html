Britannia Shifts Export Production to India Amid West Asia Crisis

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Britannia Industries shifts North American export manufacturing from Oman to Mundra, Gujarat amid Strait of Hormuz disruptions
  • 02/Fuel and packaging costs surged ~20% due to West Asia geopolitical tensions; company plans selective price hikes and grammage cuts
  • 03/Q4 FY26 net profit rose 21.56% to ₹679.68 crore on ₹4,685.95 crore sales, but stock fell 5% on margin concerns

Britannia Industries Ltd (NSE: BRITANNIA) has shifted its North American export manufacturing from Oman to Mundra, Gujarat. Managing Director and CEO Rakshit Hargave confirmed the move during the Q4 FY26 analyst call on May 8, 2026.

The decision follows disruptions caused by the closure of the Strait of Hormuz. West Asia tensions have severely impacted global shipping routes and supply chains.

The maker of Good Day, Marie Gold, Milk Bikis, and Tiger biscuits faced export delays during the quarter. "We have moved our manufacturing to Mundra," Hargave stated in the call.

Fuel and packaging costs have jumped approximately 20% due to ongoing geopolitical issues. LPG, PNG, and laminate prices are highly inflationary. Palm oil prices remain elevated despite the company being partially covered.

Britannia will counter these costs through selective price increases and grammage adjustments. Packs priced above ₹10 will see direct hikes. Lower price-point packs will see reduced grammage instead.

Wheat remains deflationary, offering some relief. Sugar prices are more or less normal. However, the overall cost basket is under pressure from energy and packaging materials.

Q4 FY26 consolidated net profit rose 21.56% to ₹679.68 crore. Revenue grew 7% to ₹4,685.95 crore. EBITDA increased 5.9% to ₹852.9 crore, but margin was nearly flat at 18.07%.

Despite the profit beat, shares fell nearly 5% to ₹5,524 on May 8. Investors reacted to margin stagnation and the 189 bps sequential EBITDA margin decline.

Quick commerce now drives about 70% of Britannia's online business. Hargave expects this to reach 85% as Amazon scales hyperlocal delivery. E-commerce contributes roughly 6% of domestic sales.

Nomura maintained a Buy rating with a target price of ₹7,275. Nuvama also rates it Buy with a target of ₹7,530. Both see upside despite Q4 revenue growing 6.5% YoY, below Nomura's 9% forecast.

Britannia also declared a final dividend of ₹90.50 per share. FY26 consolidated revenue stood at ₹19,375.62 crore.

BRITANNIA PLANS SELECTIVE PRICE HIKES AND GRAMMAGE ADJUSTMENTS. PACKS PRICED ABOVE ₹10 WILL SEE DIRECT PRICE INCREASES, WHILE LOWER PRICE-POINT PACKS WILL SEE REDUCED GRAMMAGE TO MAINTAIN PRICE POINTS.

BRITANNIA REPORTED A 21.56% RISE IN CONSOLIDATED NET PROFIT TO ₹679.68 CRORE FOR Q4 FY26. REVENUE GREW 7% TO ₹4,685.95 CRORE, AND EBITDA ROSE 5.9% TO ₹852.9 CRORE. HOWEVER, EBITDA MARGIN WAS NEARLY FLAT AT 18.07% VERSUS 18.17% LAST YEAR.
