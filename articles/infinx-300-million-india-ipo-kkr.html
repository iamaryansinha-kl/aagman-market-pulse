KKR-Backed Infinx Plans $300 Million India IPO for FY28 Listing

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Infinx Technology Solutions has appointed Kotak Mahindra Capital, Nomura and Avendus for a planned $300 million India IPO.
  • •- The KKR and Norwest Venture Partners-backed company is targeting a listing in FY28, potentially combining a fresh issue with an offer for sale.
  • •- Infinx provides AI-powered revenue cycle management services to US healthcare providers, with teams across the US, India and the Philippines.
  • •- It joins a growing queue of healthcare companies — ASG Hospital, Eclat Health, Knack RCM and AGS Health — planning Indian share sales.

India's IPO window is attracting companies that do not technically call India home. Healthcare IT platform firm Infinx Technology Solutions has appointed Kotak Mahindra Capital, Nomura and Avendus to manage its proposed $300 million initial public offering in India, according to people familiar with the development, with a listing targeted in FY28. The KKR and Norwest Venture Partners-backed company is weighing a structure that combines a fresh issue of shares with an offer for sale — the fresh component funding growth and the secondary component giving the sponsors a partial exit, the standard architecture of sponsor-owned Indian listings. Emails sent to the company and its lead bankers did not elicit a response until press time.

The business behind the filing is thoroughly American in its revenue base and increasingly Indian in its cost base. Founded in 2012 by brothers Sandeep and Jaideep Tandon, Infinx provides revenue cycle management services to US healthcare providers — the unglamorous, mission-critical plumbing of American medicine that spans patient access, insurance verification, prior authorisation, medical coding, billing and collections. Its clients are hospitals, health systems and physician groups that outsource these administrative processes to improve payment recovery, and the company operates delivery teams across the US, India and the Philippines. The model has proven catnip for private equity: revenue that recurs because healthcare administration never stops, contracts that renew because ripping out a billing system mid-year is near-impossible, and delivery costs anchored in India and the Philippines against revenues denominated in dollars. That offshore-delivery, onshore-revenue model is the classic template of the India-listed IT services sector — familiar to every Indian institutional investor who has spent two decades valuing exactly this architecture — which is precisely what makes an Indian listing logical even for a company headquartered abroad.

The private-equity trail tells a story of accelerating institutional conviction. Infinx remained bootstrapped for its first nine years, raising its first institutional capital from Norwest Venture Partners only in 2021 — a long discipline that kept dilution low and forced the business to prove its economics before anyone else's money rode on them. KKR then acquired a significant minority stake in 2024 through its Asian Fund IV, with Norwest also participating in the transaction, and the company has raised a total of $186 million to support expansion and fund acquisitions. The sequencing matters: Norwest's 2021 entry bought growth capital, KKR's 2024 entry bought an exit timeline. The largest of those came in May 2025, when Infinx acquired the healthcare revenue cycle management business of Nasdaq-listed i3 Verticals for $96 million, including the associated proprietary technology — a deal that broadened its platform mid-market and signalled that the sponsors were building toward a scale event rather than a lifestyle business. Bolt-on acquisitions of listed US companies' non-core units have become a standard playbook for sponsor-owned Indian IT services platforms: the target arrives with revenue, technology and clients, and the acquirer gains cross-sell into its existing base. Infinx's filing suggests the sponsors now see the portfolio as assembled enough to underwrite a public-market valuation.

The IPO joins a conspicuously healthcare-heavy pipeline. Infinx lines up alongside ASG Hospital, Eclat Health Solutions, Knack RCM and AGS Health, which are also reportedly planning share sales — a cluster that reflects both the profitability profile of healthcare services (steady, dollar-linked, recession-resistant) and the valuation premium Indian investors have been willing to pay for it. Four sponsor-backed healthcare companies independently converging on the same exit window is rarely a coincidence: it usually means the bankers' early soundings found appetite, and that the first credible filer sets the valuation anchor the rest of the queue trades against. Even after this week's brutal market correction wiped out Rs 10 lakh crore in market value in a single session, the institutional logic of the healthcare-IT trade has not visibly cracked: the Nifty IT index was the only sector in the green on Friday while everything else bled.

For the Indian IPO market, the significance is structural. The country's exchanges have spent two years absorbing record issuance — including the Jio Platforms offer that closed on Friday as the first issue globally to cross one crore applications — and the pipeline keeps broadening from domestic champions to global companies seeking a more receptive shareholder base. A US-revenue healthcare IT company listing in Mumbai rather than New York is a small data point on its own; multiplied across Infinx's peers watching its valuation, it becomes a trend. For US healthcare-services firms, an Indian listing offers something American exchanges currently do not: a deep pool of investors who already understand and value the offshore-delivery model at tech-sector multiples, and who have just spent two years proving they will absorb size — this week's wobble notwithstanding. The FY28 timeline gives the sponsors room to ride out the current volatility, but the banker appointments signal the clock has started.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT IS INFINX PLANNING?
A $300 million IPO in India, with Kotak Mahindra Capital, Nomura and Avendus appointed as bankers. The listing is targeted for FY28 and may combine a fresh issue with an offer for sale.
WHO OWNS INFINX?
The company was founded by brothers Sandeep and Jaideep Tandon, remained bootstrapped until 2021, and is now backed by KKR (significant minority via Asian Fund IV, 2024) and Norwest Venture Partners, which together have funded $186 million in total capital.
WHAT DOES INFINX DO?
AI-powered revenue cycle management for US healthcare providers — insurance verification, prior authorisation, medical coding, billing and collections — delivered by teams in the US, India and the Philippines. GLOSSARY: REVENUE CYCLE MANAGEMENT — The administrative backbone of US healthcare finance: verifying insurance, obtaining authorisations, coding treatments, billing insurers and collecting payments. OFFER FOR SALE — A component of an IPO where existing shareholders sell their stakes to the public, as distinct from a fresh issue where the company raises new capital. BOOTSTRAPPED — Built without external institutional capital, funded by the founders' own resources and operating cash flow.
