Cabinet Approves ₹1.86 Lakh Crore PM DHARA Green Energy Corridor III

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Union Cabinet approves PM DHARA, the Rs 1,86,405 crore Green Energy Corridor Phase III scheme, to evacuate 135 GW of renewable power
  • 02/Of the outlay, Rs 1,36,378 crore goes to intra-state transmission systems and Rs 50,000 crore to 50 GWh of battery energy storage
  • 03/Watch beneficiary segments: renewable generation, transmission equipment, capital goods and cables, as capacity additions outpace grid buildout

The Union Cabinet approved the Green Energy Corridor Phase III scheme on September 30, 2026. Union Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw announced the decision at the Cabinet briefing in New Delhi.

The scheme has been branded PM DHARA, short for PM-Developing Harmonized and Accelerated Renewable-energy Access. It carries a total outlay of Rs 1,86,405 crore, roughly Rs 1.86 lakh crore.

Of the total, Rs 1,36,378 crore is earmarked for developing intra-state transmission systems under GEC-III. A further Rs 50,000 crore is allocated for deploying 50 GWh of battery energy storage systems.

The programme targets the evacuation and grid integration of 135 GW of renewable power capacity across states and union territories. Implementation is targeted for completion by FY32-33, that is 2032-33.

The timing matters because renewable capacity additions have been running ahead of transmission buildout. That gap has raised curtailment concerns in states with high solar and wind generation, and this phase is designed to close it.

PM DHARA follows Green Energy Corridor Phase I and Phase II, which funded earlier rounds of transmission infrastructure. The third phase shifts the focus firmly to intra-state networks, where most renewable integration bottlenecks now sit.

The battery storage component is aimed at intermittency, transmission congestion and peak-hour curtailment. The 50 GWh of storage will also help meet electricity demand during non-solar hours.

Market coverage identifies renewable energy generation, power transmission equipment, capital goods and cables as the expected beneficiary segments. The scheme supports India's 500 GW renewable capacity target for 2030 and the 900 GW non-fossil capacity goal for 2035.

The battery systems will be located at renewable developer or generator ends, or at other points considered important for grid flexibility. Siting flexibility is central to how the storage component will be used.

Central support under the scheme is structured to offset intra-state transmission charges. That is intended to keep end-user power costs down and benefit electricity consumers directly.

The scheme will also generate direct and indirect employment across the power, manufacturing and construction sectors, according to official coverage of the Cabinet decision.

Greenfield transmission projects under the scheme will be implemented through tariff-based competitive bidding. Brownfield upgrades and network strengthening will run on a cost-plus basis, with state transmission utilities as the implementing agencies.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT IS THE PM DHARA SCHEME APPROVED BY THE UNION CABINET?
PM DHARA, WHICH EXPANDS TO PM-DEVELOPING HARMONIZED AND ACCELERATED RENEWABLE-ENERGY ACCESS, IS THE GREEN ENERGY CORRIDOR PHASE III SCHEME. THE UNION CABINET APPROVED IT ON SEPTEMBER 30, 2026, WITH UNION MINISTER ASHWINI VAISHNAW ANNOUNCING THE DECISION AT THE CABINET BRIEFING. IT HAS A TOTAL OUTLAY OF RS 1,86,405 CRORE. IT IS THE THIRD PHASE OF THE GREEN ENERGY CORRIDOR PROGRAMME, FOLLOWING GEC-I AND GEC-II.
HOW IS THE RS 1,86,405 CRORE OUTLAY SPLIT BETWEEN TRANSMISSION AND STORAGE?
OF THE TOTAL PROJECT OUTLAY OF RS 1,86,405 CRORE, RS 1,36,378 CRORE IS EARMARKED FOR THE DEVELOPMENT OF INTRA-STATE TRANSMISSION SYSTEMS UNDER GEC-III. THE REMAINING RS 50,000 CRORE IS ALLOCATED FOR THE DEPLOYMENT OF 50 GWH OF BATTERY ENERGY STORAGE SYSTEMS AT RENEWABLE ENERGY DEVELOPER OR GENERATOR ENDS, OR AT OTHER GRID-FLEXIBILITY LOCATIONS.
WHAT IS THE CAPACITY TARGET OF THE GREEN ENERGY CORRIDOR PHASE III?
THE SCHEME AIMS TO EVACUATE AND INTEGRATE 135 GW OF RENEWABLE POWER CAPACITY INTO THE GRID ACROSS STATES AND UNION TERRITORIES. IMPLEMENTATION IS TARGETED FOR COMPLETION BY FY 2032-33. THE SCHEME SUPPORTS INDIA'S 500 GW RENEWABLE CAPACITY TARGET FOR 2030 AND THE 900 GW INSTALLED NON-FOSSIL CAPACITY GOAL FOR 2035.
WHY DOES INDIA NEED PM DHARA NOW?
RENEWABLE CAPACITY ADDITIONS HAVE BEEN RUNNING AHEAD OF TRANSMISSION BUILDOUT, CREATING CURTAILMENT RISKS IN HIGH-GENERATION STATES. THE 50 GWH OF BATTERY STORAGE IS DESIGNED TO ADDRESS INTERMITTENCY, TRANSMISSION CONGESTION AND PEAK-HOUR CURTAILMENT, WHILE HELPING MEET ELECTRICITY DEMAND DURING NON-SOLAR HOURS. PM DHARA FOLLOWS GREEN ENERGY CORRIDOR PHASE I AND PHASE II. THE SCHEME WILL ALSO GENERATE DIRECT AND INDIRECT EMPLOYMENT IN THE POWER, MANUFACTURING AND CONSTRUCTION SECTORS.
WHICH SEGMENTS ARE EXPECTED TO BENEFIT FROM THE SCHEME?
MARKET COVERAGE POINTS TO RENEWABLE ENERGY GENERATION, POWER TRANSMISSION EQUIPMENT, CAPITAL GOODS AND CABLES AS THE EXPECTED BENEFICIARY SEGMENTS. ON EXECUTION, GREENFIELD TRANSMISSION PROJECTS WILL BE AWARDED THROUGH TARIFF-BASED COMPETITIVE BIDDING, WHILE BROWNFIELD UPGRADATION AND NETWORK STRENGTHENING WILL BE EXECUTED ON A COST-PLUS BASIS, WITH STATE TRANSMISSION UTILITIES AS THE IMPLEMENTING AGENCIES. CENTRAL SUPPORT UNDER THE SCHEME IS STRUCTURED TO OFFSET INTRA-STATE TRANSMISSION CHARGES AND KEEP END-USER POWER COSTS DOWN.
