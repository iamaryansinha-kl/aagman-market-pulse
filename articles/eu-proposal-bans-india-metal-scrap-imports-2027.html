EU Proposal Would Bar India From Importing Bloc's Metal Scrap From May 2027

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

India's metal importers face a new hurdle from Brussels. The European Commission has proposed that India be among the countries barred from importing the bloc's metal waste, including aluminium scrap, under a new law due to take effect in May 2027.

The proposal, published on Friday, sets out exemptions for non-OECD countries under the Waste Shipment Regulation and opens a public consultation on the exemptions list until October 16.

The twist is what came before. Earlier this month, the Commission dropped a plan to impose a 15% export duty on aluminium scrap exports after pressure from India, the biggest buyer of the material. Disagreements among EU officials diluted that trade proposal by exempting India, and the Commission now argues the same objective can be achieved through the bloc's waste shipment rules instead of trade measures.

Industry group European Aluminium remains unconvinced, having viewed the proposed export duty as crucial to keeping more of the low-cost raw material within Europe for struggling smelters.

Thirty-two non-OECD countries applied for exemptions covering non-hazardous waste categories including paper, plastics, rubber, metal and glass. Exemptions are granted only where applicants can demonstrate the waste will be treated in an environmentally sound manner. Metal waste faces stricter scrutiny because it presents a higher hazard profile due to the potential presence of persistent and toxic heavy metals, the Commission said.

The exemptions list, known as a delegated act, will be updated regularly at least every two years, and countries will be able to reapply. That leaves a window for India to secure access, but the default from May 2027 is restricted.

For India, the stakes centre on its secondary aluminium industry, which depends heavily on imported scrap as feedstock. India has been the largest buyer of EU aluminium scrap, and a supply cut would raise input costs for domestic recyclers and metal producers at a time when the country is trying to expand local value addition.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT DOES THE EU PROPOSAL SAY?
India would be barred from importing EU metal waste, including aluminium scrap, when the Waste Shipment Regulation takes effect in May 2027, unless it secures an exemption.
WHY DID THE COMMISSION DROP THE EARLIER EXPORT DUTY?
India, the biggest buyer of EU aluminium scrap, pushed back hard, and disagreements among EU officials diluted the 15% duty plan by exempting India.
CAN INDIA REGAIN ACCESS LATER?
Yes. The exemptions list will be updated at least every two years and countries can reapply; a public consultation runs until October 16.
