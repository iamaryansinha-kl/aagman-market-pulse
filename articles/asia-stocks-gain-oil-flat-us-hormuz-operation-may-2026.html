Asia Stocks Rise 3%, Oil Flat Amid US Hormuz Operation Plans

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/MSCI Asia-Pacific shares outside Japan gained 3%, led by South Korea's 4.6% rally on May 4, 2026
  • 02/Brent crude futures edged up just 0.2% to $108.36 after recovering from an initial drop over 2%
  • 03/US Central Command plans Hormuz escort with 15,000 troops; traders eye Fed policy and 100+ earnings reports

Asian stock markets rose on May 4, 2026, as investors weighed US military plans for the Strait of Hormuz. Oil prices held steady after an early drop.

MSCI's broadest Asia-Pacific index outside Japan gained 3%. South Korean stocks returned from holiday with a 4.6% jump. Hong Kong's Hang Seng added 1.7%.

Brent crude futures recovered from a 2% drop to close up 0.2% at $108.36 per barrel. U.S. crude edged down 0.1% to $101.85.

The US Central Command announced an escort plan with guided-missile destroyers, over 100 aircraft, and 15,000 service members. A bulk carrier was reportedly attacked near Iran on Sunday.

The Japanese yen surged as the dollar fell 0.9% to 155.7 yen. Last week's intervention by Tokyo may have totaled around $35 billion.

Goldman Sachs analysts said S&P 500 EPS growth was running at 25%. More than 100 companies report earnings this week including AMD and Disney.

Markets implied just 2 basis points of Fed easing by year-end. Australia's central bank meets Tuesday and is expected to hike for the third time.

"Given the size of the move, I doubt it was the MoF stepping in again," said Carol Kong, currency strategist at Commonwealth Bank of Australia. "But fundamentals remain in favour of USD/JPY."
