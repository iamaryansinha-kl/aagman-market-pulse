Oil Price Today: Crude Snaps 3-Day Fall on Iran Talks, May 22

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Crude oil prices rose on May 22, snapping a three-day losing streak as traders weighed geopolitical risks
  • 02/Iran peace talks delivered conflicting reports, keeping markets uncertain about Middle East supply stability
  • 03/The Strait of Hormuz remains a critical chokepoint; experts warn supply disruption risks still linger

Crude oil prices rose on Friday, May 22, ending a three-day decline. Both Brent and WTI futures edged higher in early trading sessions.

Investors are closely tracking Iran peace negotiations. Conflicting reports from ongoing talks have created fresh uncertainty in energy markets.

Iran remains a major OPEC producer with significant crude export volumes. Any disruption to its supply flows would directly impact global oil balances.

The Strait of Hormuz serves as a critical chokepoint for oil shipments. Roughly one-fifth of global crude passes through this narrow waterway daily.

Supply concerns have kept a floor under prices despite the recent three-day slide. Geopolitical risk premiums remain embedded in crude valuations.

Analysts note that clarity on Iran negotiations could trigger sharp price moves. Until then, market volatility is likely to persist through the sessions.
