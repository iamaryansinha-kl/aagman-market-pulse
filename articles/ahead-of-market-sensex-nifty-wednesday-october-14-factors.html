What Will Drive Indian Markets on Wednesday: 10 Factors to Watch

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- The Sensex closed at 73,034, up 1,109 points, and the Nifty ended at 22,722, up about 359 points on Tuesday.
  • •- The rally added around Rs 5 lakh crore to total market capitalisation, which now stands at Rs 470 lakh crore.
  • •- US markets were little changed overnight, with chip stocks under renewed selling pressure and bank results mixed.
  • •- HCL Tech, TCS and Infosys led value turnover on the NSE, while Vodafone Idea topped volume charts.
  • •- Analysts expect markets to stay range-bound as inflation sits near the upper end of the RBI's tolerance band.

Indian equity benchmarks staged their sharpest rebound in weeks on Tuesday, and the drivers behind that move — an IT earnings relief rally, a finance minister's capital gains tax remarks and steadier bonds — will set the tone for Wednesday's session.

The Sensex jumped 1,109 points to close at 73,034, while the Nifty climbed around 359 points to end at 22,722. The gains added approximately Rs 5 lakh crore to the total market capitalisation of listed companies, pushing it to Rs 470 lakh crore. Broader markets joined the rally, with the Nifty Midcap 100 rising more than 1 per cent and the Nifty Smallcap 100 closing with marginal gains.

Vinod Nair, Head of Research at Geojit Investments, read the session as an earnings-and-stability story. "Indian equities advanced as stronger-than-expected technology earnings boosted confidence in corporate profit growth and lifted benchmark-heavy large-cap stocks. Rupee stability and easing bond yields further supported sentiment," he said, while cautioning that with inflation near the upper end of the RBI's tolerance band and geopolitical uncertainty keeping energy markets volatile, policymakers are likely to remain cautious — potentially keeping the market range-bound.

Wall Street offered little direction overnight. At mid-morning in New York, the Dow Jones Industrial Average was up 9 points at 51,859.69, the S&P 500 was down 0.08 per cent at 7,800.86, and the Nasdaq Composite was 0.12 per cent lower at 27,213.38, as chip stocks faced renewed selling pressure and investors digested mixed quarterly results from major US banks.

Trading activity on Tuesday was concentrated in the IT heavyweights. HCL Tech led NSE value turnover at Rs 2,818 crore, followed by TCS at Rs 2,474 crore and Infosys at Rs 2,283 crore, with RBL Bank, Paytm, BSE and HDFC Bank rounding out the most active list. In volume terms, Vodafone Idea dominated with 24.19 crore shares traded, followed by Suzlon Energy, Yes Bank, Pine Labs, Bandhan Bank, JP Power and MMTC.

Breadth data captured a market where the rally was real but selective. Of the 3,706 stocks that traded on the NSE on Tuesday, 2,128 advanced, 1,418 declined and 98 were unchanged — a solidly positive tilt, though one that also left a long tail of stocks under selling pressure. RBL Bank, Sterlite Tech, Aegis Logistics, RR Kabel, Aether Industries, ICICI Pru Life and TD Power Systems saw significant selling, while Maruti Suzuki, Natco Pharma, Tube Investments, Max Healthcare and Fortis Healthcare were among those touching 52-week lows.

On the buying side, Bandhan Bank, Birlasoft, HCL Tech, Elecon Engineering, KPIT Technologies, TCS and Shriram Finance witnessed strong buying interest, and PVR Inox, PNB Housing and PTC Industries were among those hitting 52-week highs.

Wednesday's calendar will test whether Tuesday's enthusiasm holds. The Q2 earnings stream continues, and the primary market stays in focus with the Jio Platforms IPO expected to open next week and investors watching for its formal price band. Bond yields, which eased on Tuesday, and the rupee's trajectory after two days of sharp gains will also be on the radar, as will crude, with Brent holding above $100 a barrel.

The September retail inflation print of 5.54 per cent remains the macro anchor. With the reading at a two-year high and close to the central bank's upper tolerance band, every incoming data point on prices — from food to fuel — will be read through the lens of the RBI's December policy meeting. Until that picture clears, rallies like Tuesday's are likely to be traded rather than trusted, with range-bound conditions the base case among strategists.

The IT rally that powered Tuesday's session had fundamental fuel. HCL Tech jumped 8 per cent after reporting results that beat Street expectations on profitability, and TCS added 6 per cent, lifting the Nifty IT index 5 per cent in its best session in months — even though the sector gauge remains down sharply for the year. For Wednesday, the question is follow-through: whether the earnings relief broadens to mid-tier IT names or fades as profit-takers re-emerge.

Currency and bond markets are quietly supporting equities as well. The rupee has jumped 177 paise over two sessions against the dollar after the Reserve Bank of India's forceful measures to defend the currency, and bond yields eased on Tuesday after last week's grind to three-year highs. That combination — a steadier rupee and calmer yields — reduces two of the market's biggest macro headwinds and explains why financial stocks joined the IT-led rally.

For retail investors, the actionable signal from Tuesday's data is selective participation. Advances outnumbered declines by roughly three to two, but 52-week lows in quality names like Maruti Suzuki and Max Healthcare show the weakness beneath the surface is real. Strategists advise sticking to sectors with visible earnings momentum — IT, private banks, and selective real estate — rather than chasing the broadest part of the market.

GLOSSARY: - Market capitalisation: The total value of all listed companies, calculated as share price multiplied by shares outstanding. - Value turnover: The rupee amount of trading in a stock during a session, indicating institutional interest. - Tolerance band: The RBI's allowable inflation range around its 4% target, with 6% as the upper ceiling. - Range-bound: A market condition where indices oscillate within a band without a clear directional trend. - Breadth: The ratio of advancing stocks to declining ones, showing how widespread a market move is.
