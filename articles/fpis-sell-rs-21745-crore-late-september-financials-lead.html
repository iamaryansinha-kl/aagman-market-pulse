FPIs Sell Rs 21,745 Crore in Late September; Financials Lead Exits

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/FPIs sold Rs 21,745 crore of Indian equities between September 16-30, roughly 54% more than the Rs 14,116 crore sold in the first half of the month
  • 02/Financials led the exits at about Rs 6,943 crore in H2, followed by oil & gas at Rs 4,469 crore and autos at Rs 3,542 crore, with telecom, metals and capital goods also sold
  • 03/Full-month FPI equity sales hit Rs 35,861 crore in September; analysts cite valuation pressure, profit-taking and positioning ahead of the Jio Platforms IPO

Foreign portfolio investors sold Rs 21,745 crore of Indian equities between September 16 and September 30, provisional exchange data show. The pace of selling accelerated sharply in the second half of the month.

The late-September outflow was roughly 54% larger than the Rs 14,116 crore sold in the first half. Total FPI equity sales for September reached approximately Rs 35,861 crore.

Financials bore the brunt of the exit, as is typical when foreigners cut India exposure. The sector recorded outflows of about Rs 6,943 crore in the second half, compared with Rs 6,204 crore in the first half of September.

Oil and gas followed with Rs 4,469 crore sold in H2 versus Rs 2,385 crore in H1. Autos came next at Rs 3,542 crore, up from Rs 2,670 crore earlier in the month.

Telecom, metals, consumer durables and capital goods also saw selling during the period. In telecom, investors booked profits after a significant rerating of the sector over recent months, locking in gains.

Analysts say positioning ahead of the Jio Platforms IPO may have influenced telecom flows. Some investors likely trimmed Bharti Airtel exposure to deploy capital into the upcoming Jio issue.

"The selling reflects a combination of portfolio rebalancing, profit-taking and a greater focus on valuations relative to earnings growth. Financials, which account for a large share of foreign portfolios, tend to see significant selling when overseas investors reduce their India exposure" - Vishad Turakhia, CEO, Equirus Securities.

The macro backdrop adds pressure: the rupee trades near record lows and $26.3 billion exited EM equities globally in September. The RBI also raised the repo rate to 5.5% on October 7. Yet primary markets are booming, with a record Rs 90,462 crore raised via IPOs in the September quarter even as foreigners exit secondary-market stocks.

FINANCIALS LED WITH ABOUT RS 6,943 CRORE SOLD IN THE SECOND HALF OF SEPTEMBER, VERSUS RS 6,204 CRORE IN THE FIRST HALF. FINANCIALS CARRY THE LARGEST WEIGHT IN FOREIGN PORTFOLIOS, SO THEY TEND TO SEE THE HEAVIEST SELLING WHEN OVERSEAS INVESTORS REDUCE THEIR INDIA EXPOSURE, ACCORDING TO VISHAD TURAKHIA, CEO OF EQUIRUS SECURITIES. WHAT IS DRIVING THE SELLING IN TELECOM STOCKS?
TELECOM SAW PROFIT-TAKING AFTER A SIGNIFICANT RERATING OF THE SECTOR, WITH THE STOCKS HAVING RUN UP SHARPLY OVER RECENT MONTHS. ANALYSTS SAY THE UPCOMING JIO PLATFORMS IPO MAY HAVE INFLUENCED POSITIONING, WITH INVESTORS POSSIBLY CUTTING BHARTI AIRTEL EXPOSURE TO DEPLOY CAPITAL INTO THE JIO ISSUE WHEN IT OPENS.
HOW DOES THE FPI SELLING SIT AGAINST THE BROADER MARKET BACKDROP?
THE RUPEE IS TRADING NEAR RECORD LOWS AND $26.3 BILLION EXITED EMERGING MARKET EQUITIES GLOBALLY IN SEPTEMBER, PUTTING BROAD PRESSURE ON ASIAN AND EMERGING MARKETS. THE RBI ALSO RAISED THE REPO RATE TO 5.5% ON OCTOBER 7. DESPITE THE FOREIGN SELLING, INDIA'S PRIMARY MARKETS ARE STRONG: COMPANIES RAISED A RECORD RS 90,462 CRORE THROUGH IPOS IN THE SEPTEMBER QUARTER. GLOSSARY: FPI (Foreign Portfolio Investor): Overseas investors — pension funds, hedge funds, asset managers — who buy Indian stocks and bonds without taking controlling stakes. Profit-taking: Selling a stock that has risen to lock in gains. It is not necessarily a negative view on the company. Rerating: When investors collectively agree to pay a higher multiple for a sector's earnings, pushing valuations up. Telecom rerated on tariff-hike expectations. Outflow: Money leaving a market. Sustained FPI outflows put pressure on stock prices and the rupee.
