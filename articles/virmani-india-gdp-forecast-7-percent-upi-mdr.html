Virmani Holds India GDP Forecast at 7%, Backs UPI MDR Shift

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/NITI Aayog Member Arvind Virmani has retained his India GDP growth forecast at 7% for the full year, with an uncertainty range of plus or minus 1%
  • 02/Virmani said he would not revise his full-year estimate on the basis of quarterly data, even after India recorded 7.8% growth in the first quarter of FY27
  • 03/Separately, he backed a gradual shift towards a cost-based merchant discount rate for UPI, arguing that the initial subsidy has created a new public good

NITI Aayog Member Arvind Virmani has retained his India GDP growth forecast at 7% for the year. He maintained a wide uncertainty range of plus or minus 1 percentage point around that number.

Virmani spoke to ANI on the sidelines of the Kautilya Economic Conclave. His forecast is a personal assessment, not the official government projection.

"My forecast right now for the whole year is still the same. 7% plus or minus 1%," Virmani said.

The NITI Aayog Member had earlier raised his growth forecast from 6.5% to 7%. At the same time, he widened the uncertainty range attached to the projection.

He stressed that uncertainty is an inherent feature of macroeconomic conditions. Forecasts can be knocked off course by developments that were not anticipated when the projections were made.

"Uncertainty is a fact of macroeconomic life," he said.

Virmani said he reassessed his forecast about a month ago. He reviewed the outlook in light of unresolved trade issues and other global developments.

After that review, he decided to retain the 7% projection. The wide range reflects the unresolved trade environment and evolving global factors.

India recorded 7.8% GDP growth in the first quarter of FY27. That expansion came in stronger than many economists and analysts had expected.

The strong print triggered a round of forecast upgrades across the analyst community. Virmani was not among those raising their numbers.

"A lot of people saw this 7.8 and suddenly started raising their forecasts. But I don't do that. I have a forecast, I have a range of uncertainty," he said.

Virmani said he would not revise his full-year estimate solely on the basis of quarterly GDP data. He works off a full-year number with an explicit band around it, rather than chasing individual prints.

His comments come as economists and analysts reassess India's growth outlook. The stronger-than-expected first-quarter expansion has sharpened that debate.

External factors remain a source of uncertainty in that reassessment. Trade developments in particular are still unresolved.

On digital payments, Virmani turned to the Merchant Discount Rate on UPI transactions. He argued that the time has come to move the system gradually towards a cost-based model.

He said the initial subsidy on UPI played an important role. In his view, it expanded the payments network into what he described as a new public good.

"A good subsidy creates a new public good at minimum cost," Virmani said.

Virmani framed the transition as a gradual one. He suggested retaining support for transactions below a specified threshold while withdrawing it above that level.

In his reading, that structure represents a transition away from subsidies rather than an abrupt removal of support. No threshold amount or implementation timeline has been specified.

A move towards a cost-based system would address one persistent complaint. Competing international payment companies have raised concerns over whether UPI receives subsidised treatment.

Virmani acknowledged the question of whether an MDR could affect UPI transaction volumes. His view is that transactions will continue to grow over time.

There could, however, be a temporary impact if additional costs are passed on to users or businesses. He said the adjustment would need to work its way through the system.

"Eventually you have to pass the thing through into the system," he said.

He added that economists generally prefer direct transfers over product-based subsidies. That preference underpins his argument for reforming the UPI subsidy structure rather than preserving it indefinitely.

Taken together, the two positions sketch out a consistent philosophy. Virmani favours clear-eyed estimates with explicit uncertainty bands on growth, and gradual, cost-reflective pricing on payments.

The GDP forecast retains its 7% midpoint with the widest band he has attached to it. The UPI position, meanwhile, signals that the debate over MDR is moving from whether to how.

IS THE 7% GDP FORECAST THE OFFICIAL GOVERNMENT PROJECTION?
NO. THE 7% FIGURE IS VIRMANI'S PERSONAL FORECAST AS A MEMBER OF NITI AAYOG. HE HAD EARLIER RAISED IT FROM 6.5% TO 7% WHILE SIMULTANEOUSLY WIDENING THE UNCERTAINTY RANGE AROUND IT.
WILL VIRMANI REVISE HIS FORECAST AFTER THE 7.8% FIRST-QUARTER GDP PRINT?
NO. VIRMANI SAID HE WOULD NOT REVISE HIS FULL-YEAR ESTIMATE SOLELY ON THE BASIS OF QUARTERLY GDP DATA, DESPITE INDIA RECORDING 7.8% GROWTH IN THE FIRST QUARTER OF FY27. HE SAID OTHER ANALYSTS RAISED FORECASTS AFTER THE PRINT, BUT HE WORKS OFF A FULL-YEAR NUMBER WITH AN UNCERTAINTY RANGE.
WHAT DID VIRMANI SAY ABOUT UPI MERCHANT DISCOUNT RATE?
VIRMANI BACKED A GRADUAL SHIFT TOWARDS A COST-BASED UPI MERCHANT DISCOUNT RATE. HE ARGUED THE INITIAL SUBSIDY EXPANDED UPI INTO A NEW PUBLIC GOOD AND THAT SUPPORT COULD BE RETAINED BELOW A SPECIFIED THRESHOLD WHILE BEING WITHDRAWN ABOVE IT. NO THRESHOLD AMOUNT OR IMPLEMENTATION DATE HAS BEEN SPECIFIED.
COULD A COST-BASED MDR SLOW UPI TRANSACTION GROWTH?
VIRMANI SAID UPI TRANSACTIONS WOULD CONTINUE TO GROW, ALTHOUGH THERE COULD BE A TEMPORARY IMPACT IF ADDITIONAL COSTS WERE PASSED ON TO USERS OR BUSINESSES. A COST-BASED MODEL WOULD ALSO ADDRESS CONCERNS FROM COMPETING INTERNATIONAL PAYMENT COMPANIES ABOUT SUBSIDISED TREATMENT OF UPI.
