Bitcoin Crashes to Two-Week Low at $76,711 as Liquidations Hit $550M

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Bitcoin crashed to $76,711 on May 18, its lowest since May 1, as US-Iran war fears spooked traders
  • 02/Nearly $550 million in bullish positions were liquidated within four hours during early Asia trading
  • 03/US spot Bitcoin ETFs saw over $1 billion in outflows last week; $77,800 support has been broken

Bitcoin dropped to $76,711 on Monday, May 18, its lowest level in more than two weeks. The slide began during early Asia trading as macro risks from the US-Iran war intensified.

The drop triggered massive liquidations. Coinglass data shows nearly $550 million in bullish bets were unwound between 6:30 a.m. and 10:30 a.m. Singapore time.

President Donald Trump warned that the "clock is ticking" for Iran to reach a deal. Oil prices climbed and bond yields surged as the Strait of Hormuz remained closed.

US-listed spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds suffered more than $1 billion in outflows last week. It was the first time ETF withdrawals crossed that mark since late January.

Bitcoin fell through key support at $77,800, accelerating the selloff. Traders bought roughly $38 million in put options at the $77,500 strike for May 18 expiry, Deribit data show.

"Bitcoin's pullback is a macro story," said Rachael Lucas, analyst at BTC Markets. "Risk appetite has repriced, and Bitcoin is moving with it."

Sean McNulty, Asia-Pacific derivatives lead at FalconX, said the drop "appears to have triggered a stop run compounded by lingering downside hedging from last week."

Structural support remains between $76,000 and $76,800, Lucas noted. A daily close above $80,000 would signal that selling pressure is finally exhausting.
