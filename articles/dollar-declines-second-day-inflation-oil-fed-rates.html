Dollar Slips for Second Day on Easing Inflation, Oil Price Drop

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- The US Dollar Index (DXY) fell to 101.37 on June 26, down 0.06%, marking a second consecutive daily decline.
  • •- Headline PCE inflation rose 4.1% year-on-year in May, broadly in line with forecasts, reducing pressure for immediate Fed action.
  • •- Despite the two-day pullback, the greenback remains on track for a weekly gain of approximately 0.3%, supported by a hawkish Fed tone earlier in the month.

The US Dollar Index (DXY) eased for a second consecutive session on Friday, June 26, 2026, settling at 101.37 after opening at 101.50 and trading in a range of 101.05 to 101.57. The modest 0.06% decline reflected a cautious repricing of Federal Reserve policy expectations following the release of Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) inflation data.

Headline PCE inflation accelerated to 4.1% year-on-year in May, matching consensus forecasts, while the monthly reading rose 0.4%. The in-line report led investors to slightly scale back expectations for aggressive Fed tightening, though markets still price in three rate hikes for 2026. The probability of a first move in September stands at approximately 62%, according to CME FedWatch data.

The dollar's losses were most pronounced against the euro and the Swiss franc, reflecting a modest recovery in European currencies as US Treasury yields paused their recent climb. The EUR/USD pair gained ground as traders reassessed the pace of monetary divergence between the Federal Reserve and the European Central Bank.

Newly appointed Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh reaffirmed the central bank's commitment to bringing inflation under control, easing concerns that political pressure from the White House might prompt premature rate cuts. The Fed also raised its 2026 PCE inflation projections during its latest policy update, reinforcing a hawkish tilt that had driven the DXY to a 52-week high of 101.80 earlier in June.

Falling crude oil prices provided additional relief on the inflation front. Brent crude declined to $73.57 per barrel on June 26, down sharply from $82.21 at the start of the week, as tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz normalized and geopolitical tensions eased. West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude traded near $70.07. The slide in energy costs reduces near-term inflationary pressure, giving the Fed more room to calibrate its tightening cycle.

Despite the two-day retreat, the dollar remains firmly higher on a weekly basis, up roughly 0.3%. The greenback has gained 2.49% over the past month and 3.10% year-to-date, underpinned by widening US interest rate differentials and persistent safe-haven demand amid global geopolitical uncertainty.

The DXY's 52-week range of 95.55 to 101.80 highlights the magnitude of the dollar's recovery since early 2026. Analysts at major banks note that while near-term technical conditions suggest potential consolidation, the broader trend remains constructive for the greenback as long as US economic data continues to outpace peers.

For Indian investors and importers, the dollar's relative stability near 101 offers a window to hedge foreign currency exposures. The rupee's performance against a basket of currencies will be closely tied to how aggressively the Fed moves relative to the Reserve Bank of India in the coming quarters.
