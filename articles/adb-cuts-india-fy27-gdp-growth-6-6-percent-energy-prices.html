ADB Cuts India's FY27 GDP Growth Aim to 6.6% on Higher Energy Prices

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

The Asian Development Bank has revised its outlook for India.

The Manila-based lender has cut India's FY27 GDP growth forecast to 6.6%, down from its earlier projection. The revision reflects the impact of higher energy prices on the economy, which has increased input costs across manufacturing and services sectors.

The ADB's outlook is now more conservative than the Indian government's own estimates. New Delhi has projected growth in the range of 6.5-7% for the fiscal year, though independent analysts have been trimming forecasts amid global headwinds.

Higher energy prices are the primary drag. Crude oil has surged past $80 per barrel following renewed US strikes on Iran, pushing up fuel costs and transportation expenses. India imports more than 80% of its oil requirements, making it highly vulnerable to global price spikes.

The ADB noted that inflation pressures could persist if energy prices remain elevated. The Reserve Bank of India has been maintaining a tight monetary stance to bring inflation back to its 4% target, and the ADB expects rates to remain restrictive for longer than previously anticipated.

Despite the downgrade, India remains the fastest-growing large economy in Asia. The ADB's outlook for China is considerably lower, and developed economies in the region are projected to grow at less than half India's pace.

The report also flagged risks from the global trade environment. New tariffs and trade restrictions could disrupt India's export-oriented sectors, particularly textiles, pharmaceuticals, and information technology services.
