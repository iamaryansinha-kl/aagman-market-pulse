Industrial Warehousing Leasing Hits Record 59.1 Mn Sq Ft: Savills

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Industrial and warehousing leasing across India's top 24 cities hit a record 59.1 million sq ft in January-September, up 7 per cent from 55.3 million sq ft a year earlier.
  • 02/Fresh supply grew far faster, rising 25 per cent annually to 68.9 million sq ft, signalling developers are building ahead of demand.
  • 03/Tier-II and III cities such as Jaipur and Surat now drive nearly one-fifth of total absorption; watch absorption and rents in 2026 as new supply gets tested.

Industrial and warehousing leasing in India hit a record 59.1 million sq ft during January-September. The figure marks a 7 per cent rise from 55.3 million sq ft leased in the same period last year, according to Savills India.

Demand was propelled by light manufacturing, third-party logistics and e-commerce companies. Third-party logistics, or 3PL, operators manage warehousing and transportation for other businesses.

Fresh supply responded even faster than demand. New completions rose 25 per cent annually to 68.9 million sq ft during the nine months, pointing to a developer buildout running well ahead of current absorption.

The top eight tier-I cities drove the bulk of activity. Ahmedabad, Bengaluru, Chennai, Hyderabad, Kolkata, Mumbai, Delhi-NCR and Pune together saw leasing rise to 47.2 million sq ft from 44 million sq ft a year ago.

Tier-II and III cities accounted for 11.9 million sq ft, up from 11.3 million sq ft in the year-ago period. The tracked smaller hubs include Jaipur, Surat, Indore, Coimbatore, Lucknow, Nagpur, Kochi, Guwahati, Bhubaneswar, Patna, Hosur, Rajpura, Vizag, Hubli, Belgaum and Anantapur.

Savills expects the sector to witness steady growth in 2026. "India's industrial and logistics sector is expected to witness steady growth in 2026, supported by manufacturing expansion and sustained demand from 3PL, FMCG/FMCD, retail, and e-commerce occupiers," the consultancy said. Rising consumption, supply chain realignment and growing activity in Tier-II and III cities would continue to drive demand, it predicted.

Mukesh Choudhary, Managing Director of realty firm Accuspace, said demand for quality industrial and logistics spaces is growing. "While Tier-I hubs continue to lead, Tier-II and III markets like Jaipur are carving out a distinct and critical position, driving nearly one fifth of the country's total absorption," he said.

Choudhary said his company is witnessing a structural shift as e-commerce firms, 3PL providers and manufacturing units look for high-grade regional hubs to optimise, de-risk and decentralise their supply chains. New parks are being developed across the country to serve this demand.

Lalit Parihar, MD of realty firm Aaiji Group, pointed to strength in Gujarat. Demand for industrial and logistics space is robust across the state's major cities, including Ahmedabad, Surat and Dholera, he said.

The numbers set up a clear test for 2026. Leasing grew 7 per cent while supply grew 25 per cent, so absorption of the new 68.9 million sq ft pipeline and the trend in rents will determine whether the record pace holds.

The geography of demand is shifting as much as its volume. Tier-II and III cities — from Hosur and Coimbatore in the south to Jaipur, Rajpura and Dholera in the north and west — now account for roughly a fifth of national absorption.

That diffusion reflects how occupiers think about risk. Concentrating inventory in one mega-warehouse near a metro invites disruption; a lattice of regional hubs keeps goods closer to consumers and shortens last-mile delivery.

Third-party logistics providers sit at the centre of the demand mix. As manufacturers and retailers outsource distribution, 3PL firms sign the long leases that fill large-format parks — making them the sector's most watched tenant category.

E-commerce remains the other engine. Quick-commerce dark stores and fulfilment centres count as industrial and warehousing absorption in Savills' methodology, and their buildout cycle has been relentless through 2026.

The supply response, though, is the number to watch. Developers added 68.9 million sq ft in nine months — a 25% jump against 7% leasing growth — betting that 2026 demand will absorb what 2025 built.

If absorption keeps lagging supply, rents soften and developers slow land buys. If manufacturing-led demand catches up, the current buildout looks prescient. Either way, 2026 is the test year Savills is pointing at.

Gujarat's cluster illustrates the regional bet. Ahmedabad, Surat and Dholera — the latter a planned greenfield industrial city — are drawing a disproportionate share of new park development, helped by port access and state incentives.

The consumption backdrop supports the optimists. Rising household consumption and supply-chain realignment — companies diversifying away from single-country sourcing — are structural, multi-year drivers rather than cyclical ones.

For investors in listed developers and real-estate investment vehicles, the leasing record is the demand-side evidence that logistics is the most institutionally accepted real-asset class in India today.

GLOSSARY: Absorption: The amount of space leased or occupied by tenants over a period, net of vacated space. Grade-A (high-grade) facility: A modern warehouse meeting institutional standards on ceiling height, flooring, fire safety and automation. 3PL (third-party logistics): A company that manages warehousing and distribution for other businesses under contract. Supply chain realignment: The restructuring of sourcing and distribution networks — in India's case partly driven by companies diversifying beyond China. Last-mile delivery: The final leg of a shipment from a hub to the end consumer, typically the costliest per kilometre.
