Kotak Mahindra Bank Q2 Profit Rises 29% to Rs 4,212 Crore

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Standalone profit rose 29% YoY to Rs 4,212 crore, while consolidated PAT grew 26% to Rs 5,645 crore, up 3% sequentially.
  • •- Net interest income rose 16% to Rs 8,459 crore, but net interest margin fell to 4.31% from 4.54% a year earlier on FCNR(B) deposit mobilisation.
  • •- Asset quality improved: gross NPA fell to 1.04% and net NPA to 0.24%, while credit cost nearly halved to 0.35% from 0.79%.
  • •- Net advances grew 25% to Rs 5.77 lakh crore and deposits 23% to Rs 6.51 lakh crore, with the CASA ratio dipping to 38.2% on FCNR(B) inflows.

In a quarter when the Reserve Bank of India is tightening, bond yields are at three-decade extremes and funding costs are squeezing lenders from every side, Kotak Mahindra Bank delivered the kind of numbers that remind you why it trades at a premium. Standalone profit for the September quarter rose 29% year-on-year to Rs 4,212 crore, up 2% from the preceding quarter; on a consolidated basis, profit rose 26% to Rs 5,645 crore, 3% higher sequentially — a figure that includes the contribution of its subsidiaries in insurance, asset management, securities and alternate assets, which together supply roughly a third of consolidated earnings. The engine was net interest income, which climbed 16% to Rs 8,459 crore — growth achieved, notably, while much of the banking system is fighting margin compression.

The one soft spot was margin. Net interest margin came in at 4.31%, down from 4.54% a year earlier, a decline the bank attributed to the mobilisation of FCNR(B) deposits during the quarter. Those foreign currency non-resident deposits are a cheap-now, costly-later way to shore up the liability side: they bulk up the balance sheet quickly but carry a structural drag on margin while they sit there. Excluding the effect, the picture looks more like the Kotak of old — and the cost of funds actually fell to 4.52% from 4.70% a year ago, a number most competitors wrestling with deposit pricing would trade for in a heartbeat. The CASA ratio declined to 38.2% from 42.3% on the same FCNR(B) inflows; strip those out and it stood at 41.8%, still lower year-on-year but far less alarming.

On growth, the bank barely paused. Net advances expanded 25% year-on-year to Rs 5,77,295 crore, customer assets grew 23% to Rs 6,26,801 crore, and total deposits rose 23% to Rs 6,51,491 crore — deposit growth that comfortably outpaces the system and funds the loan book without strain. It also settles the question that has hung over private banks all year: whether deposit competition would force lenders to choose between margin and growth. Kotak's answer this quarter is that it chose both — funding a 25% loan book with 23% deposit growth while cutting its cost of funds by 18 basis points is precisely the combination the market has been sceptical any bank could deliver in a tightening cycle. Operating expenses rose 14% to Rs 5,271 crore, but operating profit grew faster at 21% to Rs 6,382 crore, meaning the cost machine is still earning its keep. Provisions fell 15% year-on-year to Rs 803 crore, and credit cost nearly halved to 0.35% from 0.79% — in an environment where the RBI has been warning about macro risks, Kotak is provisioning less because its book is genuinely cleaner, not because it is being optimistic.

Asset quality was the quiet triumph of the quarter. Gross non-performing assets fell to 1.04% and net NPAs to 0.24% as of September 30, 2026 — levels most of the sector can only approach, achieved while the loan book was growing at 25%. The provision coverage remains deep, and the bank's Basel III capital adequacy of 21.9% gives it both a fortress balance sheet and the firepower to keep growing while others retrench. That capital ratio is worth pausing on: in a system where the RBI's tightening is designed precisely to find out who is over-leveraged, Kotak is running nearly double the regulatory minimum, which is why its management can talk about growth while peers talk about defence. Analysts reading the print described it as evidence of strong operational efficiency and strategic deposit mobilisation, with the focus on asset quality and cost management expected to support growth in coming quarters. In a week when investors were dumping financials along with everything else, Kotak's numbers made the case that the sell-off is a macro trade, not a verdict on every bank.

That distinction matters for how you read this earnings season, and for what it says about the rest of the banking results still to come this month. The macro is genuinely hostile — crude near six-month highs, a hawkish central bank, ten straight weeks of foreign selling — and no lender is immune to funding costs. But Kotak's quarter shows the dispersion inside the sector: balance sheets that were built conservatively are now compounding while the tide goes out, and margins diluted by choice (FCNR(B) deposits are, after all, a deliberate funding strategy) are very different from margins eroded by competition. The contrast with the broader market's mood is stark, too: on a Friday when the Sensex shed over 600 points and midcaps sank, a 29% profit rise with sub-0.4% credit cost reads almost defiantly calm. The bank did not guide to any change in that posture; it did not need to. A 29% profit rise, credit cost below 0.4% and 25% loan growth in a tightening cycle is its own statement.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT WERE KOTAK MAHINDRA BANK'S Q2 NUMBERS?
Standalone PAT rose 29% YoY to Rs 4,212 crore and consolidated PAT rose 26% to Rs 5,645 crore. Net interest income grew 16% to Rs 8,459 crore.
WHY DID THE BANK'S NET INTEREST MARGIN FALL?
NIM declined to 4.31% from 4.54% a year earlier, primarily due to mobilisation of FCNR(B) deposits during the quarter; excluding these, the CASA ratio was 41.8% versus the reported 38.2%.
HOW DID ASSET QUALITY PERFORM?
Gross NPA improved to 1.04% and net NPA to 0.24%, with credit cost nearly halving to 0.35% from 0.79% and provisions down 15% at Rs 803 crore. GLOSSARY: FCNR(B) DEPOSITS — Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank) deposits, dollar-denominated funds raised from overseas Indians that bolster a bank's foreign currency liquidity but dilute rupee margins. CASA RATIO — The share of current and savings account deposits in total deposits; a higher ratio means cheaper funding and better margins. CREDIT COST — Provisions for bad loans expressed as a percentage of average advances; a key measure of lending risk. NIM — Net interest margin, the difference between what a bank earns on loans and pays on deposits, relative to its interest-earning assets.
