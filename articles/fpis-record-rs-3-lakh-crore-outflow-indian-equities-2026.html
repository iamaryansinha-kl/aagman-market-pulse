FPIs Pull Record Rs 3 Lakh Crore From Indian Equities

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/FPIs sold Rs 3.05 lakh crore of Indian equities in the first nine months of 2026, the first time secondary-market outflows have crossed the Rs 3 lakh crore mark
  • 02/The selling tops the Rs 2.4 lakh crore withdrawn in all of 2025 and Rs 1.21 lakh crore in 2024, with three months of the year still remaining
  • 03/Motilal Oswal's Gautam Duggad sees no meaningful turnaround until global yields, the US dollar and crude oil prices cool; FPIs were sellers in six of the nine months

Foreign portfolio investors have pulled out more than Rs 3 lakh crore from Indian equities in the first nine months of 2026. NSDL data shows sales of over Rs 3.05 lakh crore, the first time outflows from the secondary market have crossed the Rs 3 lakh crore mark.

The pace eclipses every previous annual outflow record, and it comes with three months still left in the year. FPIs withdrew Rs 2.4 lakh crore from equities in all of 2025 and Rs 1.21 lakh crore in 2024.

The exit has not been limited to stocks. Including selling in government bonds under the Fully Accessible Route (FAR), FPIs have pulled out close to Rs 3.58 lakh crore from both asset classes combined.

The sustained selling kept Indian benchmarks under pressure through 2026. The Sensex is down nearly 15.5% and the Nifty nearly 14% in local currency terms; in dollar terms, both benchmarks have fallen more than 20%.

The drivers are external. Rising US bond yields, a stronger dollar and shifting expectations around global interest rates have reduced the relative attractiveness of emerging-market equities.

Artificial intelligence has sharpened the rotation. A boom in AI-centric markets such as Taiwan and South Korea prompted reallocation, and FPIs were net sellers in six of the nine months of 2026.

The brief respites did not hold. July and August brought combined inflows of Rs 46,180 crore, but foreign investors turned sellers again in September, offloading around Rs 33,000 crore.

September's renewed selling came amid the simmering West Asia conflict, a spike in US bond yields, a weakening rupee and a revival in appetite for South Korea and Taiwan markets.

Bond flows flipped just as quickly. FAR inflows surged in June after the government announced measures to attract foreign capital and on expectations that Bloomberg would include Indian bonds in its flagship Global Aggregate Index.

When Bloomberg deferred the inclusion, inflows started to taper from mid-July and FPIs turned sellers. Cumulative FAR inflows stood at Rs 49,355 crore in June and July, against outflows of Rs 11,439 crore in August and September, CCIL data shows.

"Rising US bond yields, a stronger dollar and shifting expectations around global interest rates have reduced the relative attractiveness of emerging-market equities," said Gautam Duggad, MD and head of sales, Institutional Equities at Motilal Oswal Financial Services.

Duggad flagged crude as an added headwind for India. "Elevated crude oil prices could also weigh on India's import bill and corporate margins, adding to concerns over earnings," he said.

He does not see the tide turning soon. "We do not expect a meaningful turnaround in FPI flows until there is greater clarity or respite on global yields, interest rates, the US dollar, and cooling of crude oil prices," Duggad said.

The selling, however, is not spread evenly across the market. Shankar Sharma, founder of Gquant and First Global, said the mood among a few FPIs he had recently met was "extremely negative" on India overall, with interest mainly in select smaller companies.

"They will continue selling. That's going to be the pattern. So they will largely keep exiting large caps, and selectively buy small and mid-cap stocks," Sharma said.
