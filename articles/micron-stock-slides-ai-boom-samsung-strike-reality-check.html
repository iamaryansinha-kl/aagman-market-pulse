Micron Stock Slides as AI Boom Faces Reality Check From Samsung Strike Risk

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Micron Technology shares declined sharply as investors grew cautious over AI-driven semiconductor valuations and supply chain risks
  • 02/Samsung faces a potential 18-day strike by 45,000 union workers starting May 21, threatening global DRAM and HBM memory supply
  • 03/Micron trades at $803.63 with P/E of 37.94x, nearly double its 5-year median of 20.72x, while insiders sold $52.4 million in shares over the past three months

Micron Technology shares have pulled back as the AI-driven rally faces a reality check. Investors are questioning whether semiconductor valuations have run too far ahead of fundamentals.

The stock trades at $803.63, more than eightfold higher than a year ago. Its market capitalization recently crossed $900 billion for the first time before the latest slide.

Micron's trailing P/E ratio stands at 37.94x. That compares to a 5-year median of 20.72x, suggesting the stock is priced at a significant premium to historical levels.

Insiders have sold $52.4 million worth of shares in the past three months. This selling activity raises questions about confidence in the current valuation.

A major risk is brewing at Samsung Electronics, the world's largest memory chip maker. Approximately 45,000 unionized workers plan an 18-day strike starting May 21.

Samsung produces roughly one-third of global DRAM and is a critical supplier of high-bandwidth memory chips. An April one-day walkout cut foundry output by 58% and memory fabrication by 18%.

Samsung and SK Hynix control roughly two-thirds of global DRAM and an even larger share of HBM. Together with Micron, they form an oligopoly with only three manufacturers worldwide.

The memory market is already tight. Apple reportedly accepted a 100% price increase from Samsung for iPhone 17 memory components earlier this year.

A prolonged Samsung strike could benefit Micron by tightening supply further. However, it also underscores how vulnerable the AI supply chain is to single points of failure.
