SOPA Flags Edible Oil Non-Standard Packs as 'Deliberately Misleading' Consumers

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/SOPA has called non-standard edible oil pack sizes "deliberately misleading" and urged the central government to intervene immediately.
  • 02/One edible oil brand advertises 19 different pack sizes, with some packs differing by only 25 or 50 grams despite appearing identical.
  • 03/SOPA wants packaging standardised but says companies should get sufficient time to modify production systems and use existing materials.

The Soybean Processors Association of India (SOPA) has slammed non-standard edible oil pack sizes as "deliberately misleading." The Indore-based industry body wrote to the Secretary of the Union Consumer Affairs Department on April 29, 2026.

SOPA Executive Director D N Pathak said the letter urges immediate government action. Five national edible oil organisations had previously made a joint presentation on the same issue.

"The government, with good intentions, removed standard quantity restrictions and mandated per-unit pricing. However, some manufacturers are misusing this exemption," the letter stated. It called for a regulatory framework that prevents "abuse or exploitation."

SOPA cited a brand that advertises oil in 19 different pack sizes. Many appear identical in size but differ by as little as 25 or 50 grams, confusing consumers.

"A consumer is often presented with two seemingly identical pouches, one containing 880 ml and the other 910 ml. The 880 ml pouch appears cheaper, but the actual price per liter is higher," the letter explained. Per-unit prices are written in paise with decimals, such as 24.72 paise per millilitre.

"This argument is baseless. The average retail consumer does not calculate the price per millilitre or per gram," SOPA said, rejecting claims that per-unit pricing makes standardisation unnecessary.

Even compliant companies are now forced to sell non-standard sizes to remain competitive, SOPA noted. The body urged prompt government action while asking for sufficient transition time for manufacturers.
