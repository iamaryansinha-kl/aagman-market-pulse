CoinSwitch Turns Profitable in FY26; Revenue Climbs 150% to Rs 324 Cr

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/CoinSwitch achieved profitability at the adjusted EBITDA level in FY26, with revenue climbing 150% YoY to Rs 324.19 crore
  • 02/Adjusted EBITDA margin improved to 27% while operating costs rose only 37%, showing clear operating leverage after the crypto winter
  • 03/The platform now serves over 2 crore users across 450+ digital assets; watch its diversification into mutual funds and fixed income

CoinSwitch, one of India's largest crypto investing platforms, has turned profitable. The Bengaluru-based company achieved profitability at the adjusted EBITDA level in FY26, the financial year ended March 2026.

Revenue climbed 150% year-on-year to Rs 324.19 crore in FY26. That compares with roughly Rs 130 crore in FY25, based on the reported growth rate.

The margin story is sharper still. Adjusted EBITDA rose more than 50-fold to Rs 86.38 crore, taking the adjusted EBITDA margin to 27%. Operating costs grew just 37% against 150% revenue growth, delivering clear operating leverage.

CONTEXT: The turnaround follows a brutal stretch for Indian crypto exchanges. From April 2022, India imposed a 30% tax on crypto gains plus a 1% TDS on every transaction, with no loss offsetting allowed. Domestic exchange volumes collapsed from FY23 onwards, compounding the damage from the 2022 market downturn.

The 1% TDS was particularly damaging for high-frequency traders. It taxed gross transaction value regardless of whether a trade was profitable, pushing many active traders to offshore platforms or out of the market entirely.

Leading Indian exchanges survived the squeeze through a familiar playbook: deep cost cuts, headcount reductions, a push into compliance, and diversification beyond pure crypto trading fees.

The recovery has been aided by the global crypto market's 2025-26 upswing. Bitcoin's run over the past year revived retail trading activity, with BTC trading near $83,900 in late September 2026 and Ethereum around $2,680.

Higher token prices typically lift exchange economics in two ways. Existing users trade more actively, and dormant accounts reactivate as retail interest returns.

CoinSwitch has also been busy building beyond crypto. The company has diversified into mutual funds and fixed-income offerings on its wealth platform, seeking to reduce dependence on crypto transaction volumes.

The platform now serves over 2 crore users and supports more than 450 digital assets. It has focused on expanding its active user base and educating newcomers about crypto investing.

The FY25 base was depressed by that downturn, which partly flatters the 150% growth print. Even so, delivering a 27% adjusted EBITDA margin in a market still carrying a 30% gains tax and 1% TDS points to genuine cost discipline.

The company is backed by Andreessen Horowitz (a16z), Accel, Tiger Global, Sequoia Capital India (now Peak XV Partners) and Ribbit Capital. It has raised over $300 million to date.

Its investor list reads like a who's who of global venture capital. That backing gave CoinSwitch the capital cushion to absorb years of losses that smaller rivals could not sustain.

WazirX, once among India's largest exchanges, suffered a major hack in 2024 and underwent a painful restructuring. Its troubles redistributed users and volumes toward better-capitalised rivals, including CoinSwitch and CoinDCX.

IMPACT: CoinSwitch's profitability is the first hard validation of the diversification thesis for Indian crypto platforms. A profitable, scaled exchange strengthens the case for potential IPO ambitions among India's crypto unicorns.

Any future softening of India's crypto tax regime would be a direct earnings lever. Lower friction on trades could lift volumes further, given how much activity the 2022 rules pushed offshore.

A friendlier global regulatory shift toward formal crypto frameworks could further revive Indian volumes. At the same time, the competitive shakeout is real — the downturn appears to have cemented a structure where only the largest, best-capitalised players survive.

The key metric to watch in FY27 is whether profitability holds when crypto prices stabilise. Sustained adjusted EBITDA margins above 20% would confirm the model works through a market cycle, not just a rally.

Investors should note the caveat: adjusted EBITDA profitability is not net profit. The company has not disclosed a net profit figure for FY26, and the 30% crypto tax regime plus 1% TDS remain in force in India.
