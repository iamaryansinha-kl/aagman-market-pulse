NBFC Credit Growth Hits 15.8% in August as Gold Loans Surge 69%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/NBFC credit grew 15.8% YoY to ₹60.6 lakh crore at end-August 2026, accelerating from 14.9% in July, RBI data released Tuesday shows
  • 02/Gold loans surged 69.1% to ₹3.65 lakh crore and consumer durable loans jumped 56.4%, pointing to stressed rural credit demand and festive consumption
  • 03/The RBI MPC announces its rate decision on October 7; strong credit growth gives the central bank room to hike the repo rate for the first time since February 2023

Credit at non-banking financial companies grew 15.8% year-on-year to ₹60.6 lakh crore at the end of August 2026, according to Reserve Bank of India data released on Tuesday.

Growth stood at 14.9% at the end of July. The latest reading confirms a clear re-acceleration in NBFC lending after a sluggish stretch through 2025.

The pick-up was powered by two segments: gold loans and consumer durable loans. Both grew at multiples of the overall book.

Gold loans outstanding surged 69.1% year-on-year to ₹3.65 lakh crore. In the year-ago period, the same segment was growing at 46.8%.

Consumer durable loans jumped 56.4% to ₹77,784 crore. A year earlier, growth in this segment was just 19.6%.

Consumer durable credit is a reliable festive-season proxy. The August surge suggests retailers are financing everything from smartphones to refrigerators ahead of Diwali.

The gold-loan spike cuts two ways. It reflects strong demand for small-ticket, fast-disbursal credit. It also signals stress in rural balance sheets.

Retail inflation printed at 4.82% in August, and crude oil is holding above $100 a barrel. Household budgets are stretched, and gold loans often become the bridge.

These loans typically run for 6 to 12 months against pledged jewellery. Borrowers use them for farm inputs, medical bills and debt repayment.

Aggregate retail loans at NBFCs grew 22% year-on-year to ₹26.5 lakh crore. That compares with 13.6% growth in the year-ago period.

Retail lending now accounts for roughly 44% of the total NBFC lending universe. It is the single biggest driver of the sector's balance-sheet expansion.

Housing loans, the largest chunk of NBFC retail credit, grew 12.1% to ₹8.62 lakh crore. A year earlier, the segment had expanded just 3.8%.

The housing recovery matters. It suggests rate-sensitive retail demand is reviving after two years of tepid growth, led by end-user buyers rather than speculators.

Lending to the services sector moderated to 16.2% from 24% a year earlier. The deceleration was concentrated in trade and transport-operator segments.

Commercial real estate lending, however, remained robust. Developers continue to lean on NBFC balance sheets as bank credit to the segment stays selective.

Credit to industry grew 8.4% year-on-year, marginally higher than the 8.3% recorded in the year-ago period. Corporate borrowing from NBFCs remains subdued.

Agriculture and allied activities credit grew 17.4%, a sharp jump from 5.1% a year earlier. Rural lending is the clear swing factor in the latest data.

NBFCs are still growing slower than banks. Commercial bank credit expanded more than 19% year-on-year in July, but the gap is narrowing as NBFCs push into retail niches.

The numbers land hours before the RBI's Monetary Policy Committee announces its decision on October 7. Governor Sanjay Malhotra's six-member panel has held the repo rate at 5.25% for four straight meetings.

Markets widely expect a 25 basis point hike to 5.50%. That would be the RBI's first rate increase since February 2023, ending a run of easing that began with 125 basis points of cuts in 2025.

About 60% of economists polled by Reuters expect the October hike. Inflation has run above the RBI's 4% target for three consecutive months.

External pressure strengthens the case for tightening. The rupee has weakened past 96 per dollar, and foreign investors have pulled roughly $26 billion from Indian equities in 2026.

Strong credit growth alongside Q1 GDP expansion of roughly 8% gives the MPC room to act without choking demand. Tuesday's NBFC data feeds directly into that calculus.

"We expect the RBI to start the hiking process in October. Inflation is tracking higher than the RBI's forecast for the current quarter, and the rise is fairly broad-based," said Abhishek Upadhyay, co-head of economics and fixed income research at ICICI Securities Primary Dealership.

For NBFCs, faster loan growth is good for fee income and balance-sheet expansion. The composition of that growth is the concern.

A book tilting toward gold loans and consumer durables is more exposed to asset-quality shocks than one anchored in housing. Gold-loan losses stay low while prices rise, but a sharp correction in gold would lift loan-to-value ratios overnight.

Higher policy rates would also squeeze margins. NBFC funding costs reset quickly, while gold and durable loans are priced largely at fixed rates, so yields adjust slower than costs.

Investors will watch the RBI's commentary on consumption credit as closely as the rate call itself. Any hint of macro-prudential concern on unsecured or gold-backed lending could trigger a re-rating across NBFC stocks.

For now, the August data hands the MPC a clean growth story. Credit is flowing, retail demand is accelerating, and the festive quarter has begun.

GLOSSARY: NBFC: A non-banking financial company. It lends like a bank but cannot accept demand deposits such as current and savings accounts. YOY (YEAR-ON-YEAR): Growth measured against the same month or period of the previous year. GOLD LOAN: A secured short-tenure loan, usually 6-12 months, where the borrower pledges gold jewellery as collateral. CONSUMER DURABLE LOAN: Short-tenure financing for household goods such as smartphones, appliances and electronics. REPO RATE: The rate at which the RBI lends money to banks. It is the benchmark that drives most lending and deposit rates in the economy. MPC: The Monetary Policy Committee, the six-member panel headed by the RBI Governor that votes on the repo rate. LOAN-TO-VALUE (LTV) RATIO: The loan amount expressed as a percentage of the pledged collateral's value. A higher LTV means higher risk for the lender.
