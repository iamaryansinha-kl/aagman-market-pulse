Sugar Prices Hold Below Rs 60 per kg for Fourth Straight Day as Festive Demand Builds

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Average retail sugar price held at Rs 59.57 per kg on Monday, below Rs 60 for the fourth straight day
  • 02/Centre allowed sugar imports on August 21 and tightened stockholding norms for bulk users and dealers
  • 03/Government lowered the 2025-26 production estimate to 306 lakh tonnes from 343 lakh tonnes; annual demand is 280-285 lakh tonnes

The average all-India retail price of sugar remained below Rs 60 per kg for the fourth straight day on Monday, following a series of government measures to rein in sweetener prices ahead of the festive season.

The average retail price stood at Rs 59.57 per kg on Monday, marginally up from Rs 58.99 per kg the previous day, according to Consumer Affairs Ministry data. Prices dropped below the Rs 60 level on Friday.

The average remains higher than the Rs 58.23 per kg recorded on August 21, when the Centre first allowed sugar imports and took other steps to cool prices. The maximum retail price remained firm at Rs 85 per kg, while the minimum was Rs 40 per kg.

On August 21, the government allowed sugar imports and further tightened stockholding norms for bulk users and dealers. It has accused sugar mills of "jacking up" prices, maintaining that the country has adequate stocks.

The government lowered its production estimate for the 2025-26 marketing year to 306 lakh tonnes from an earlier forecast of 343 lakh tonnes. Annual domestic demand is pegged at 280-285 lakh tonnes.

Among major producing states, Maharashtra's average retail price fell to Rs 58.22 per kg from Rs 59 per kg, while Uttar Pradesh eased to Rs 57.88 per kg from Rs 59.64 per kg on August 21.

Madhya Pradesh recorded a sharper fall, with prices dropping to Rs 56.57 per kg from Rs 62.36 per kg. West Bengal saw a marginal dip to Rs 60.43 per kg from Rs 60.95 per kg.

In the southern states, Andhra Pradesh's average retail price rose to Rs 55.56 per kg from Rs 49.58 per kg on August 21, while Kerala inched up to Rs 57.8 per kg from Rs 56.75 per kg. Tamil Nadu saw a marginal decline.

India's sugar export ban, in place since May, will stay until end-September as the government prioritises domestic supply. Sugar prices hit a record high in August, contributing to retail inflation.

Festive demand is expected to keep prices elevated in the coming weeks despite the government's measures and adequate stock position.
