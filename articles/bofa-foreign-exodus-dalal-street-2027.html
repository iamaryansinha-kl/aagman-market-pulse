BofA Warns Foreign Investor Exodus From Dalal Street May Continue Into 2027

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Bank of America predicts foreign investor outflows from Indian equities may persist through 2027 and possibly 2028
  • 02/Earnings downgrades and rupee weakness are key factors keeping foreign portfolio investors away from Dalal Street
  • 03/Other Asian markets are attracting investor capital as Indian stocks continue to underperform global benchmarks

Bank of America has warned that the foreign investor exodus from Indian equities may extend into 2027 and possibly 2028. The firm does not expect a meaningful return of foreign portfolio investors before that timeline.

Indian stocks have been underperforming global peers in recent months. This weak relative performance has made other Asian markets more attractive to international capital.

Earnings downgrades across several sectors have weighed on investor sentiment. Foreign investors are reassessing growth expectations for Indian corporates.

A weakening Indian rupee has added to the concerns of overseas investors. Currency depreciation erodes dollar-denominated returns for foreign funds.

Capital is rotating toward other Asian economies that offer better near-term risk-reward profiles. This shift in allocation has intensified selling pressure on Dalal Street.

The sustained outflow trend poses a challenge for domestic market liquidity. Indian equities have historically relied heavily on foreign institutional participation.

Analysts caution that a reversal in foreign sentiment would require stronger earnings delivery and currency stabilization. Until then, domestic flows may need to offset the foreign exodus.
