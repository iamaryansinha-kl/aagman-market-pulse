Sensex Jumps 1,200 Points on IT Gains, Cheaper Oil, Firmer Rupee

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- The Sensex climbed over 1,200 points to 74,010 by late morning, with the Nifty up 300 points above 22,950.
  • •- IT stocks rallied for a third straight session, while TCS, Infosys, Tech Mahindra, Bharti Airtel, ICICI Bank and ITC led the gains.
  • •- Brent crude fell $1.76 to $104 a barrel, and the rupee opened 0.2% stronger at 94.92.
  • •- Smallcaps diverged, with the Smallcap 250 falling up to 0.8% even as the benchmark surged.
  • •- Thursday marked the Sensex weekly F&O expiry, which amplified intraday swings.

Indian equities roared back on Thursday, with the Sensex surging more than 1,200 points to 74,010 by 11.50 am and the Nifty climbing over 300 points to trade above 22,950 — a rebound that put the benchmark indices back near record territory after Wednesday's decline, even as the broader market quietly told a different story.

The rally's leadership was unmistakable, and heavily institutional. IT stocks extended gains for a third straight session on the back of improved earnings — HCLTech's results set the tone — and TCS, Infosys, HCLTech and Tech Mahindra powered the index. Bharti Airtel, ICICI Bank, Axis Bank and ITC rounded out the gainers. On the losing side sat a very different roster: Bharat Electronics, Adani Ports, Trent, Power Grid, State Bank of India, L&T and NTPC — a mix of defence-linked, infrastructure and PSU names that have been recent retail favourites.

That split is the key detail to hold onto. While the Sensex surged, the Nifty Smallcap 250 index fell as much as 0.8 per cent, with MTAR Technologies, Netweb Technologies and CEAT plunging up to 5 per cent. The midcap index managed a modest 0.3 per cent gain. India VIX, the fear gauge, rose over 1 per cent even as headline indices rallied — a classic sign that the move is concentrated, not broad, and that options desks are paying up for protection beneath the surface.

Four forces converged on the day. First, the global AI trade cracked overnight: hot US consumer inflation data for September revived Federal Reserve rate-hike expectations, Wall Street slid on losses in AI-linked stocks, and Asia opened lower — MSCI's Asia-Pacific ex-Japan index fell 1.2 per cent, South Korea's KOSPI slumped 5.6 per cent, and Japan's Nikkei dropped 1.3 per cent. For India, the chip selloff was read as a positive: VK Vijayakumar, chief investment strategist at Geojit Investments, argued that foreign investors who have been selling Indian stocks to buy AI names "will halt this trend and under favourable conditions might even reverse it" — a flows argument that treats India's recent underperformance as collateral damage rather than fundamental deterioration.

Second, oil retreated. Brent crude futures fell $1.76, or 1.75 per cent, to $104 a barrel, and WTI dropped $1.02 to $94.07, as markets weighed Hormuz disruption risks against US efforts to ease sanctions on vessels transporting petroleum. For an economy running an energy-shock current account deficit, every dollar off crude is a direct import-bill reprieve — and Thursday's fall compounded a decline that had already taken the edge off the week's inflation scare.

Third, the rupee opened 0.2 per cent stronger at 94.92 against the dollar, extending a four-session recovery from last week's panic levels, as the dollar retreated from an 18-month peak. The currency's rebound, engineered by the RBI's new oil-company dollar window and tighter derivatives rules, has removed the single biggest overhang on foreign investor sentiment this month.

Fourth, expiry mechanics. October 15 marked the weekly futures and options expiry for the Sensex, a day when traders square off or roll positions and short-covering can exaggerate intraday moves in either direction. Technically, the setup now points higher: Anand James, chief market strategist at Geojit, sees a path toward 23,200-23,600 on the Nifty, with the 22,550-22,787 band the level to hold; a slip below 22,550 would expose 22,070.

Beneath all of this sits a policy rumour with market-moving potential. Indications that the finance ministry might reconsider the taxation of long-term capital gains in the coming Budget — the July 2024 hike in LTCG and STCG rates that foreign investors blame for accelerating their exit — "have the potential to keep the market resilient," Vijayakumar said. It is the second time in a week that capital-gains relief has surfaced as a talking point, and on a day when flows are the market's dominant question, even the possibility of a tax olive branch is worth points.

What is different about this rally, compared with the grinding rebounds of the past month, is its quality-versus-breadth trade: the leaders are liquid, institutional favourites — the very stocks foreign investors would return to first — rather than the retail-heavy smallcaps that carried previous sessions. HCLTech's results anchor the IT story, and with financial results from the big banks due imminently, the market has near-term catalysts on both legs of the leadership. Vijayakumar's read is that good financial-sector numbers "can impart resilience to the market" through the earnings season.

The honest summary of Thursday's session: a powerful, leadership-narrow rally on a confluence of oil, rupee, expiry and a global rotation out of AI — exactly the kind of move that looks decisive at noon and gets tested at the close. Whether the smallcap divergence resolves upward or the benchmark gives back its gains will tell traders which story the market actually believes in.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHY DID THE SENSEX RALLY OVER 1,200 POINTS?
Gains were driven by a third straight session of IT buying after HCLTech's results, a $1.76 drop in Brent crude, a stronger rupee at 94.92, and short-covering on the weekly F&O expiry.
WHICH STOCKS LED THE GAINS?
TCS, Infosys, Tech Mahindra, HCLTech, Bharti Airtel, ICICI Bank, Axis Bank and ITC; laggards included BEL, Adani Ports, SBI, L&T, Power Grid and NTPC.
HOW DID THE BROADER MARKET PERFORM?
Narrowly. The Nifty Smallcap 250 fell up to 0.8%, several smallcaps dropped 5%, and India VIX rose over 1% even as benchmarks surged.
WHAT COULD SUSTAIN THE RALLY?
Analysts point to a reversal of FII selling if the AI trade unwinds, possible LTCG tax relief in the Budget, oil staying near $104, and the rupee holding its recovery. GLOSSARY: - F&O expiry: The weekly settlement of futures and options contracts that forces position unwinding or rollover. - India VIX: An index measuring expected market volatility; rising VIX signals demand for protection. - LTCG: Long-term capital gains tax on investments held beyond a set period. - MSCI ex-Japan: A benchmark of Asian equities excluding Japan. - Short-covering: Traders buying back borrowed positions to close them, which pushes prices up.
