India Needs Up to Rs 172 Trillion Infrastructure Investment by FY31: NaBFID-BCG Report

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

India's infrastructure financing challenge has a number: Rs 168-172 trillion. That is what the country needs to invest through FY31, according to a joint report by the National Bank for Financing Infrastructure and Development and Boston Consulting Group, and a large slice of it is not yet in a financeable state.

The report, titled Channelizing Domestic and Global Capital for Infrastructure Financing, breaks the requirement into stages. Of the total, Rs 38-40 trillion has been financed, Rs 31-32 trillion awaits financial close, and Rs 21-22 trillion is classified as stalled. That leaves Rs 78-80 trillion yet to translate into an announced project pipeline at all.

The longer horizon is bigger still. Financing India's long-term infrastructure demand of Rs 680-770 trillion through 2047 depends on building bankable projects and matching them to the right capital pools, the report says. The mix is the problem: only 34-36% of the FY26-47 requirement sits in sectors with proven, bankable financing models. Sectors accounting for 55-57% of the requirement, including metro rail, water supply, irrigation and new rail lines, deliver wide socio-economic gains but lack standalone financial viability.

Greenfield projects make up 80-85% of the forward pipeline, while urban infrastructure represents nearly half of the demand through 2047, a shift from the highways-and-power-heavy mix that private capital knows how to fund.

The gap is narrower than the headline suggests. Existing capital pools can cover most of the FY26-31 demand, leaving an annual residual gap of Rs 2-3 trillion, and domestic institutions hold Rs 2-3 trillion of undeployed capacity within current regulatory ceilings. The report outlines three scalable routes to close it: partial credit enhancement to tap institutional bond markets, Infrastructure Investment Trusts to recycle operational assets, and alternative investment funds alongside private credit. Global alternative infrastructure assets under management reached $1.8 trillion in 2025.

NaBFID MD and CEO Rajkiran Rai G framed the stakes directly: Viksit Bharat 2047 will require infrastructure to precede growth, and government capex must be complemented by private sector capital. BCG's Ashish Garg stressed that viable commercial models and enforceable payment structures will be critical as the sector mix shifts.

The message to policymakers is clear. The money exists, but projects in metros, water and irrigation need revenue models before capital will follow.
