India Near-Term Outlook Clouded by Supply Risks, Weather: RBI Report

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/RBI monthly state of economy report flags supply side pressures and weather uncertainties clouding India's near-term economic outlook
  • 02/April CPI inflation rose to 3.5% while WPI hit 8.3%, a 42-month high, indicating incipient price pressures in the economy
  • 03/Domestic demand remains strong with industrial and services activity resilient; external sector challenges cushioned by policy buffers

India's near-term economic outlook faces supply side pressures and weather-related uncertainties. The Reserve Bank of India flagged these risks in its monthly state of economy report.

The April Consumer Price Index rose to 3.5% from 3.4% in March. Food inflation drove the increase while core inflation remained steady.

The Wholesale Price Index inflation jumped to 8.3% in April from 3.9% in March. This marks a 42-month high and signals incipient price pressures building in the economy.

Financial conditions, crude oil prices and capital flows pose challenges to the external sector. The merchandise trade deficit widened in April over March on rising crude oil and gold imports.

Robust services exports and positive net FDI flows provide support. Foreign exchange reserves and proactive policy measures cushion the economy against external headwinds.

Domestic demand continues to drive growth. Most listed private non-financial companies showed quarter-on-quarter improvement in business performance in the fourth quarter.

Summer sowing progressed rapidly with above-normal pre-monsoon rainfall. However, above-normal minimum temperatures and unseasonal rains pose risks to remaining rabi crop harvesting.

"Although headline inflation remains firmly within the tolerance band, the pass through to domestic prices needs to be monitored," RBI researchers guided by deputy governor Poonam Gupta said.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT RISKS HAS THE RBI IDENTIFIED FOR INDIA'S NEAR-TERM ECONOMIC OUTLOOK?
THE RBI MONTHLY STATE OF ECONOMY REPORT FLAGS SUPPLY SIDE PRESSURES AND WEATHER-RELATED UNCERTAINTIES AS KEY RISKS. THE POTENTIAL SECOND ROUND EFFECT OF WEST ASIA GEOPOLITICAL TENSIONS ON DOMESTIC PRICES ALSO NEEDS CAREFUL MONITORING.
WHAT WERE THE KEY INFLATION NUMBERS FOR APRIL 2026?
THE CONSUMER PRICE INDEX (CPI) ROSE TO 3.5% IN APRIL FROM 3.4% IN MARCH, DRIVEN BY FOOD INFLATION. THE WHOLESALE PRICE INDEX (WPI) SURGED TO 8.3% FROM 3.9%, MARKING A 42-MONTH HIGH.
HOW IS THE EXTERNAL SECTOR PERFORMING?
FINANCIAL CONDITIONS, CRUDE OIL PRICES AND CAPITAL FLOWS CONTINUE TO POSE CHALLENGES. HOWEVER, ROBUST SERVICES EXPORTS, POSITIVE NET FDI FLOWS, FOREX RESERVES AND PROACTIVE POLICY MEASURES ARE CUSHIONING THE ECONOMY AGAINST EXTERNAL HEADWINDS.
WHAT IS THE STATUS OF DOMESTIC DEMAND AND INDUSTRIAL ACTIVITY?
DOMESTIC DEMAND REMAINS THE KEY DRIVER OF GROWTH. INDUSTRIAL AND SERVICES ACTIVITY STAYED STRONG IN MANY SEGMENTS, WITH MOST LISTED PRIVATE NON-FINANCIAL COMPANIES SHOWING QUARTER-ON-QUARTER IMPROVEMENT IN Q4.
WHAT ARE THE RISKS TO THE AGRICULTURE SECTOR?
SUMMER SOWING HAS MADE RAPID PROGRESS SUPPORTED BY ABOVE-NORMAL PRE-MONSOON RAINFALL. HOWEVER, ABOVE-NORMAL MINIMUM TEMPERATURES AND UNSSEASONAL RAINS IN SOME PARTS POSE RISKS TO HARVESTING OF REMAINING RABI CROPS.
