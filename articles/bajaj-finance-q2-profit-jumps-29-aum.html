Bajaj Finance Q2 Profit Jumps 29% to Rs 6,367 Crore; AUM Up 26%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Net profit rose 29% YoY to Rs 6,367 crore from Rs 4,948 crore.
  • •- Net interest income grew 24% to Rs 13,335 crore; net total income rose 23% to Rs 15,974 crore.
  • •- Assets under management expanded 26% to Rs 5.85 lakh crore, adding Rs 37,680 crore in the quarter alone.
  • •- The lender booked 13.45 million new loans and added 4.42 million customers, taking the franchise to 128.85 million.
  • •- A Rs 337 crore prudent macro provision dented profit; excluding it, PBT grew 34% and credit cost was 1.32%.
  • •- Loan losses and provisions were Rs 2,166 crore including the buffer, down 12% excluding it.

Bajaj Finance reported on Monday the kind of quarter that settles arguments rather than starting them. Net profit for the July-September period rose 29 per cent year-on-year to Rs 6,367 crore, net interest income grew 24 per cent to Rs 13,335 crore, and assets under management expanded 26 per cent to Rs 5.85 lakh crore — a franchise now adding, in a single quarter, more AUM (Rs 37,680 crore) than most listed NBFCs hold in total. For a stock that has spent 2026 being punished by the same rotation that hit every other private financial, the numbers were a reminder that the operating engine and the market's mood have decoupled. The September quarter is seasonally Bajaj Finance's strongest — festival-season disbursements peak and collections benefit from year-end business flows — so a portion of the growth was expected; what was not fully priced was the combination of that seasonality with a falling credit-cost ratio and the deliberate, voluntary absorption of a macro provision in the period.

The detail beneath the headline is where the quarter earns its keep. Net total income rose 23 per cent to Rs 15,974 crore, and pre-provisioning operating profit grew at the same 23 per cent pace to Rs 10,699 crore. Operating costs as a share of net total income ticked down to 33 per cent from 33.1 per cent — a marginal number that matters enormously at this kind of scale, because Bajaj Finance's structural advantage has always been distribution economics: 13.45 million new loans booked in the quarter, 4.42 million customers added, and a base of 128.85 million that keeps cross-sell costs falling as a proportion of every rupee lent. Fee and other income, at roughly Rs 2,600 crore implied by the gap between net total income and net interest income, remains the quiet second engine that differentiates the franchise from pure spread lenders.

The one discretionary line — and the one that will get the most attention in conference calls — is a prudent management and macroeconomic provision of Rs 337 crore. Including it, loan losses and provisions came to Rs 2,166 crore; excluding it, they fell 12 per cent year-on-year to Rs 1,829 crore, and the annualised credit-cost ratio dropped to 1.32 per cent from 1.88 per cent a year ago. Reported profit before tax grew 29 per cent to Rs 8,537 crore; had the buffer not been taken, it would have grown 34 per cent to Rs 8,874 crore, with net profit of Rs 6,619 crore. The conservatism reads as positioning: Bajaj Finance is choosing to fund its own caution about the macro cycle — higher rates, softer consumption in pockets — out of its best quarter, rather than be forced into it later.

It is worth pausing on what 128.85 million customers means in the context of Indian finance. That customer count is larger than the population of most countries in the world, and it grows by roughly the size of a mid-sized Indian city every ninety days. The quarterly addition of 4.42 million customers at a time when unsecured-lending norms have tightened and competitors have pulled back suggests Bajaj Finance is consolidating the very segment — small-ticket, high-frequency consumer credit — that regulators spent last year flagging as overheated. The company appears to have taken the regulatory tightening as an invitation to gain share, using its cost advantage and data-led underwriting to keep growing where thinner-franchise lenders have retreated. Whether that proves prescient or merely contrarian will be visible in the credit-cost line over the next four quarters — which is exactly why management chose this quarter to pre-fund caution.

The context of the print is the day's other market signal. On the same Monday that PSU banks rallied on results and private lenders sold off, Bajaj Finance — the archetypal private consumption lender — delivered its cleanest quarter in some time and remains down sharply for the year. The divergence is not about these numbers; it is about what investors are willing to pay for growth when the rate cycle has turned. Bond yields near multi-year highs make fixed income a genuine competitor for the savings that fuel NBFC balance sheets, and every quarter of RBI tightening tightens that arithmetic further. Yet the deposit-and-lending machine keeps compounding: 24 per cent NII growth with a falling cost ratio is, in the lending business, the closest thing to proof that scale still works. At 26 per cent AUM growth with falling credit costs and improving cost ratios, the franchise is doing everything a lender can do; whether the multiple follows is the market's problem, not the company's. The Rs 337 crore provision suggests management is under no illusions that the path is smooth — but building buffers from a position of strength is precisely what separates cyclical lenders from durable ones.
