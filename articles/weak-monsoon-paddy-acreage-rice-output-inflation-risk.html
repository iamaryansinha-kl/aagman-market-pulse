Weak Monsoon Threatens Paddy Acreage, Rice Output as June Rainfall Hits 39.8% Deficit

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/June 2026 is the driest in over a decade with monsoon rainfall 39.8% below the long-term average, the fifth driest since 1901
  • 02/Paddy sowing drops to 2.57 million hectares vs 3.44 million hectares last year; total Kharif sowing down 23% to 18.27 million hectares
  • 03/FCI holds 81.75 million tonnes of foodgrain stocks as of 1 May, well above the 21 million tonne buffer norm, cushioning immediate supply risks

India's southwest monsoon has delivered the driest June in over a decade. Rainfall was 39.8% below the long-term average, making it the fifth driest since record-keeping began in 1901.

The India Meteorological Department has predicted below-normal rains in July at 94% of the long-term average. July is the peak sowing month for Kharif paddy, a water-intensive crop.

Paddy sowing has already slowed sharply. As of 26 June, paddy covered 2.57 million hectares, down from 3.44 million hectares a year earlier. This is a decline of roughly 25% year-on-year.

Total Kharif sowing across all crops stood at 18.27 million hectares, compared with 23.64 million hectares last year. Oilseeds saw the sharpest drop, falling 1.94 million hectares to 1.7 million hectares.

Cotton sowing also remained subdued at 2.96 million hectares, a decrease of 1.57 million hectares. Pulses sowing fell by 653,000 hectares to 1.49 million hectares.

"Based on initial feedback, not only is acreage likely to decline, but production is also expected to fall," said Jagjit Singh Dallewal, president of Bhartiya Kisan Union (Ekta Sidhupur). The union represents over 200,000 farmers in Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh.

Farmers face higher input costs as water-intensive paddy requires more diesel for irrigation. Many are uncertain whether they can recover these additional expenses, Dallewal added.

A senior ICAR scientist said farmers are being advised to adopt short-duration paddy varieties. These mature in under 120 days compared with 135-140 days for conventional varieties. Direct seeded rice, which requires less water, is also being recommended.

"Given below-normal rainfall forecast in July, irrigation costs are likely to rise due to heat stress," said Sudhir Panwar, farm policy expert and former member of Uttar Pradesh Planning Commission. Farmers may shift to less water-intensive crops instead of paddy.

"More than half of Kharif sowing is typically covered in July," said Aditi Nayar, chief economist at Icra Ltd. Below-normal rainfall in July would adversely impact Kharif crop output.

India enters the season with comfortable inventories. Central pool foodgrain stocks stood at 81.75 million tonnes as of 1 May 2026, against a buffer norm of 21 million tonnes. Rice stocks were at 38.95 million tonnes and wheat at 42.8 million tonnes.

HOW MUCH HAS PADDY SOWING DECLINED?
AS OF 26 JUNE, PADDY WAS SOWN OVER 2.57 MILLION HECTARES, DOWN FROM 3.44 MILLION HECTARES A YEAR EARLIER. THIS REPRESENTS A DECLINE OF APPROXIMATELY 25% YEAR-ON-YEAR.
WHAT ARE THE GOVERNMENT'S FOODGRAIN STOCKS?
AS OF 1 MAY 2026, CENTRAL POOL FOODGRAIN STOCKS STOOD AT 81.75 MILLION TONNES, WELL ABOVE THE BUFFER NORM OF 21 MILLION TONNES. THIS INCLUDES 38.95 MILLION TONNES OF RICE AND 42.8 MILLION TONNES OF WHEAT.
WHAT ADAPTIVE MEASURES ARE BEING RECOMMENDED FOR FARMERS?
FARMERS ARE BEING ADVISED TO ADOPT SHORT-DURATION PADDY VARIETIES THAT MATURE IN UNDER 120 DAYS (VS 135-140 DAYS FOR CONVENTIONAL VARIETIES) AND DIRECT SEEDED RICE (DSR), WHICH REQUIRES LESS WATER AND REDUCES LABOUR COSTS.
