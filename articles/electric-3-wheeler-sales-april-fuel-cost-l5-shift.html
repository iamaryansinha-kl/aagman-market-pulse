Electric 3-Wheeler Sales Steady at 61,260 in April; Fuel Costs Push Fleet Shift to L5

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Electric 3-wheeler registrations rose 1% month-on-month to 61,260 units in April, with the L5 segment climbing 4% to 25,307 units.
  • 02/CNG prices stayed elevated at ₹77-91.5/kg across metros, while electric 3-wheelers cost just ₹1.4 per km to run, sharpening the total cost of ownership gap.
  • 03/Mahindra Last Mile Mobility, Bajaj Auto and TVS Motor Co together controlled 88% of the L5 market, while smaller OEMs lost share, pointing to further consolidation.

India's electric three-wheeler market held ground in April, with Vahan data showing registrations at 61,260 units, a modest 1% uptick from March. The stability masks a sharp structural tilt: the higher-speed L5 segment expanded nearly 4% to 25,307 units, while the L3 category slipped 1% to 35,953 units, signalling a move toward lithium-ion powered, high-payload vehicles.

Fuel cost anxiety is driving the shift. CNG prices remained elevated at ₹77 per kg in Delhi, ₹81 in Mumbai, ₹91.5 in Chennai and ₹90 in Bengaluru as of early May. Against this, electric three-wheelers offer a predictable running cost of roughly ₹1.4 per kilometre, making them the cheaper option for high-utilisation fleet use.

The gains are concentrating among legacy players. Mahindra Last Mile Mobility and Bajaj Auto retained the top two slots, while TVS Motor Co strengthened its hold on third place. The three combined commanded nearly 88% of the L5 market, leaving smaller OEMs such as Omega Seiki Mobility grappling with volume declines as distribution and service networks become critical.

Uday Narang, Founder and CEO of Omega Seiki Mobility, acknowledged the squeeze but framed it positively. "There may be dips for a few OEMs, including us, but what is encouraging is the move towards higher-speed vehicles—that is a positive for the industry," he said, adding that logistics operators now demand payload capacities of 1 to 1.5 tonnes.

Fleet electrification is moving from pilot to scale. Amazon is shifting toward larger L5 cargo vehicles, while Flipkart has committed to deploying 25,000 EVs by 2030. Blinkit has achieved high electrification levels in key hubs, and Swiggy is pushing charging-as-a-service models to support a fully electric fleet. Quick commerce and food delivery are the primary growth engines, with short delivery cycles favouring compact, high-utilisation electric three-wheelers.

Amit Bhat, India Managing Director at the International Council on Clean Transportation, said large platforms have moved beyond early adoption targets and now face structural hurdles. "While electrification of last-mile fleets is well underway, constraints around charging infrastructure, vehicle financing and high utilisation requirements are becoming the key bottlenecks to scaling towards 100% electrification," he noted. This is driving a shift toward fleet ownership and leasing models, as individual delivery partners struggle with upfront costs and range concerns.

The broader three-wheeler market is formalising around the L5 standard. Unlike L3 vehicles used largely in low-cost, semi-urban applications, L5 variants offer higher payload and are increasingly replacing petrol and CNG fleets in B2B logistics. The segment's 60% share of the overall three-wheeler market in April, against a 24% share for CNG, underlines how rapidly the balance is tilting.
