India's Seafood Exports Hit Record ₹72,325.82 Crore in FY26 on Shrimp Surge

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/India's seafood exports reached a record ₹72,325.82 crore ($8.28 billion) in FY26, with volumes hitting 19.32 lakh tonnes according to MPEDA provisional data
  • 02/Frozen shrimp remained the primary growth driver at ₹47,973.13 crore ($5.51 billion), accounting for over two-thirds of total export earnings with 6.35% value growth
  • 03/While US shipments declined 14.5% in value due to reciprocal tariffs, China, EU and Southeast Asia offset the drop with 22.7%, 37.9% and 36.1% value growth respectively

India's seafood exports touched a record ₹72,325.82 crore ($8.28 billion) in FY26. Volumes reached 19.32 lakh tonnes, according to provisional data from the Marine Products Export Development Authority (MPEDA).

Frozen shrimp continued to dominate as the primary growth driver. It contributed ₹47,973.13 crore ($5.51 billion), accounting for over two-thirds of total export earnings.

Shrimp shipments grew 4.6 per cent in volume and 6.35 per cent in value year-on-year. The category has remained India's flagship seafood product for over a decade.

The United States retained its position as the largest export destination. However, shipments to the US declined sharply by 19.8 per cent in volume and 14.5 per cent in value, totaling $2.32 billion.

The US slowdown reflected the impact of reciprocal tariffs imposed during the year. Indian shrimp faced effective tariff rates of nearly 58 per cent post-August 2025, compared to 13-14 per cent for competitors Ecuador and Indonesia.

China emerged as a key growth market, with exports rising 22.7 per cent in value and 20.1 per cent in volume. It remained the second-largest destination for Indian seafood.

The European Union posted the strongest gains among major markets. Exports to the EU surged 37.9 per cent in value and 35.2 per cent in volume, driven by improved compliance with traceability requirements.

Southeast Asia also recorded significant expansion, with growth of over 36.1 per cent in value and 28.2 per cent in volume. Vietnam doubled as a re-export hub for Indian shrimp.

Exports to Japan grew 6.55 per cent in value. West Asia showed a marginal decline due to regional turmoil during the latter part of the financial year.

On the product side, frozen fish, squid, cuttlefish, dried items and live products showed positive momentum. Chilled products were the only segment to decline during the year.

Among ports, Vizag, JNPT, Kochi, Kolkata and Chennai together accounted for nearly 64 per cent of total export value. This concentration highlights the importance of coastal infrastructure.

The FY26 numbers represent a significant jump from ₹62,408 crore ($7.45 billion) in FY25. The sector has nearly doubled from ₹30,213 crore in 2013-14, reflecting an average annual growth of 7 per cent over 11 years.

Market diversification has become a strategic priority for India. The government is promoting high-value species including tuna, seabass, cobia, mud crab and seaweed to reduce dependence on shrimp.
