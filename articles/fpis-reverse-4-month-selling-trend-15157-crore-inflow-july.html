FPIs Reverse 4-Month Selling Trend with Rs 15,157 Crore Inflow in July

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

Foreign investors are back.

Foreign portfolio investors have reversed a four-month selling trend with a net inflow of Rs 15,157 crore in July, providing a much-needed boost to Indian equity markets. The return of foreign capital has lifted sentiment and helped the Nifty and Sensex hold near record levels after months of pressure from consistent FPI selling.

The four-month selling streak was significant. From March through June, FPIs had been net sellers in Indian equities, pulled back by a combination of factors: elevated valuations, geopolitical tensions in the Middle East, concerns about RBI monetary policy, and the relative attractiveness of other emerging markets. The consistent outflows had weighed on large-cap stocks and kept the market range-bound.

The July reversal reflects improved global sentiment. Easing inflation data in the US and Europe has raised hopes that major central banks may begin cutting interest rates sooner than expected. Lower rates in developed markets reduce the yield advantage of holding dollars and euros, making emerging markets like India relatively more attractive.

The inflow has been broad-based across sectors. Financials, technology, and consumer discretionary stocks have seen the highest FPI buying, reflecting foreign investors' preference for large-cap, liquid names with earnings visibility. Mid and small-cap stocks have also benefited, though to a lesser extent.

The timing is critical for the market. July marks the beginning of the September quarter earnings season, and FPI positioning ahead of results can influence stock performance. Strong foreign inflows provide a cushion against any earnings disappointments and support the market's premium valuation.

Domestic institutional investors have been absorbing the selling. During the four months of FPI outflows, domestic mutual funds and insurance companies were net buyers, providing stability. Now that FPIs have turned buyers, the combined buying pressure from domestic and foreign investors could drive the market higher.
