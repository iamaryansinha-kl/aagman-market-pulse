JP Morgan Report: Global Earnings Revisions Lift Equities Despite Stagflation Fears

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/JP Morgan report pushes back against stagflation fears, says aggregate earnings projections for 2026 are rising across US and most global markets
  • 02/Europe's median 2026 earnings growth estimate is around 8% versus headline 18%; semiconductors, mining and industrials seen delivering robust results
  • 03/Brent crude near $100/bbl remains compatible with further earnings upside unless geopolitical tensions escalate sharply, report says

Global equity markets may find support from resilient corporate earnings despite stagflation concerns, according to a JP Morgan report dated May 3, 2026.

The report challenges the bearish narrative around slowing growth and tighter liquidity. It states that stagflation fears may be overstated relative to underlying fundamentals.

Aggregate earnings projections for 2026 continue to move upward across regions. This momentum is not limited to the US but is broad-based across most markets.

The Energy sector has seen strong upgrades on elevated oil prices. However, positive revisions are spreading to several other sectors beyond energy alone.

Brent crude averaging around USD 100 per barrel would still support further earnings upside. Significant geopolitical escalation would be needed to trigger widespread downgrades.

In Europe, headline 2026 earnings growth projections appear elevated at around 18%. The report clarifies this is largely due to base effects in Consumer Discretionary.

A more realistic median estimate for Europe is around 8%. This leaves room for potential upside surprises if economic activity remains stable.

Semiconductors, mining and industrials are expected to deliver robust Q1 results. Consumer discretionary may stay under pressure in the near term.

"We still think Semis, Mining and Industrials results will look positive. Consumer Discretionary is likely to be under pressure, but better numbers could be coming in 2H, China dataflow is looking stronger," the report noted.

Markets had entered oversold territory following the March sell-off. Investors who turned bearish may need to rebuild positions, lending further momentum to a rebound.
