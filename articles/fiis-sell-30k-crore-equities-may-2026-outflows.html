FIIs Sell Rs 30K Crore in May; 2026 Outflows Reach Rs 2.22 Lakh Crore

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/FIIs remain net sellers for the third consecutive month in May 2026, offloading Rs 30,374 crore worth of domestic equities so far
  • 02/Total FII outflows in 2026 have swelled to Rs 2.22 lakh crore, making March the worst month with Rs 1.17 lakh crore sold
  • 03/DIIs emerged as strong counter-buyers at Rs 6,003.53 crore on Friday; market outlook hinges on US-Iran tensions and crude oil prices

Foreign institutional investors (FIIs) have sold Indian equities worth Rs 30,374 crore so far in May 2026. They have now been net sellers for three successive months.

The total FII exodus from Indian markets in 2026 has reached Rs 2.22 lakh crore. This sustained selling pressure reflects deepening global risk aversion.

March 2026 was the worst month, witnessing outflows of Rs 1,17,775 crore. April followed with another Rs 60,847 crore in selling.

On Friday alone, FIIs dumped shares worth Rs 4,440.47 crore. Domestic institutional investors (DIIs) absorbed this selling by buying Rs 6,003.53 crore worth of shares.

The benchmark Nifty gained 64.60 points or 0.27% to close at 23,719.30. The BSE Sensex settled at 75,415.35, up 231.99 points or 0.31%.

Pabitro Mukherjee, Associate Vice President - Research at Bajaj Broking, said investor sentiment remains cautious. "Global uncertainty and macroeconomic headwinds led to cautious trading activity across the markets," he said.

Geopolitical tensions, elevated crude oil prices, and a weaker Rupee have kept foreign investors on edge. A sharp rise in bond yields, driven by inflation concerns and prolonged higher interest rates, has added to the pressure.

In 2025, FIIs withdrew Rs 1,66,286 crore from Indian markets. Trade deal delays and premium valuations weighed heavily on sentiment throughout the year.

Bajaj Broking expects institutional activity to be driven by global developments going forward. The US-Iran negotiation progress will remain a critical factor for geopolitical stability and crude oil price volatility.
