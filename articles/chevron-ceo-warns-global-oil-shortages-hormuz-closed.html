Chevron CEO Warns Global Oil Shortages Starting as Hormuz Stays Shut

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Chevron CEO Mike Wirth warned on May 4 that physical oil shortages will begin appearing worldwide as the Strait of Hormuz remains closed
  • 02/The strait handles roughly 20% of global crude supply; Asia will be hit first, then Europe, with economies forced to slow
  • 03/Brent crude trades at $113.77 and WTI at $105.06; Chevron stock is up 21% YTD as the crisis deepens

Chevron Chairman and CEO Mike Wirth warned on Monday that physical oil shortages will begin appearing around the world. The Strait of Hormuz, through which 20% of global crude passes, remains closed due to the U.S.-Israeli conflict with Iran.

"We will start to see physical shortages," Wirth said at a Milken Institute discussion. He noted that surplus supply in commercial markets, shadow fleet tankers, and national strategic reserves are being absorbed.

Asia is most heavily dependent on Gulf oil production and refineries. Europe will be affected next. The United States, a net crude exporter, will feel the impact later.

Wirth described the overall effect as "potentially as big as in the 1970s." Two major supply disruptions in that decade caused fuel rationing and long lines at pumps.

The warning comes as Brent crude trades at $113.77 per barrel and WTI at $105.06. Oil prices have surged roughly 60% since the conflict began on February 28, 2026.

The crisis has already claimed a casualty in the airline sector. Spirit Airlines went out of business over the weekend as jet fuel costs surged amid tighter supplies.

Europe is reporting a 105.7% year-over-year rise in jet fuel prices. Airlines have cancelled numerous flights due to severe shortages.

Chevron itself has benefited from the price spike. The company reported Q1 2026 adjusted earnings of $1.41 per share, crushing Wall Street's 95-cent forecast. Upstream earnings rose 4% YoY to $3.9 billion.

"Demand needs to move to meet supply," Wirth said. "Economies are going to have to slow."
