FPIs Dump ₹13,944 Crore in Holiday-Shortened Week as Equity Selloff Deepens

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/FPIs withdrew ₹13,944 crore in a four-day trading week ending May 1, with equities bearing the brunt of the exit, per NSDL data.
  • 02/The selling extends a brutal 2026 trend: total outflows have reached ₹1.92 lakh crore in four months, already exceeding all of 2025's ₹1.66 lakh crore.
  • 03/Watch crude oil prices, US-Iran talks, and the April CPI print for any sign of FPI sentiment reversal.

Foreign Portfolio Investors pulled out ₹13,944 crore from Indian markets in the holiday-shortened trading week ending May 1, with equities absorbing the bulk of the damage, according to data from the National Securities Depository Limited. Markets were closed on May 1 for Labour Day, compressing the week's activity into four sessions and amplifying the daily selling intensity.

The outflow adds to a relentless foreign exit that has now pushed total FPI withdrawals to ₹1.92 lakh crore in the first four months of 2026. This four-month figure has already eclipsed the ₹1.66 lakh crore that left Indian markets across the entire calendar year 2025.

Equities were the primary casualty, continuing a pattern seen throughout April when FPIs offloaded ₹60,847 crore from stocks. The financial services sector, a traditional foreign favourite, has accounted for a disproportionate share of the selling.

Dr V K Vijayakumar, Chief Investment Strategist at Geojit Investments, noted earlier in April that "FPIs continued selling" through the month, taking the total sell figure for April through mid-month to ₹44,929 crore. By month-end, that figure had swelled to ₹60,847 crore.

Himanshu Srivastava, Principal Manager Research at Morningstar Investment Research India, attributed the sustained exit to "escalating geopolitical tensions" in West Asia that have pushed crude oil prices higher and revived global inflation concerns. This has reduced expectations of near-term rate cuts and kept bond yields elevated.

Vaqar Javed Khan, Senior Analyst at Angel One, called it a "textbook risk-off reaction" to US-Iran tensions. With crude above USD 100, the rupee weakening past 92, and the Nifty trading near 21 times earnings, India's valuations have looked expensive to short-horizon foreign allocators.

Despite the foreign exodus, domestic institutional investors have provided a cushion. DII buying of roughly ₹1.7 lakh crore year-to-date has absorbed much of the FPI selling, while monthly SIP inflows above ₹25,000 crore have sustained retail participation.

Analysts say a durable reversal in FPI flows would require three conditions: WTI crude falling below USD 90, US 10-year yields dropping under 4.5%, and tangible de-escalation in West Asia. India's April CPI print, due mid-May, is the next domestic trigger that could shift the rate-cut narrative.
