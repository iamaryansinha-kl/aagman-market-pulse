Gold, Silver Futures Rally 7% as Government Hikes Import Duty to 15%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Gold futures for June delivery jump ₹9,723 (6.34%) to ₹1,63,165 per 10 grams on the MCX after the duty hike
  • 02/Silver futures for July delivery soar ₹19,439 (6.97%) to ₹2,98,501 per kg as duty rises from 6% to 15%
  • 03/The move effective May 13 aims to curb bullion imports amid record gold imports of $71.98 billion in FY2026

Domestic gold and silver prices surged after the government hiked import duties on precious metals to 15% from 6%. The Finance Ministry notification raised the social welfare surcharge and agriculture infrastructure cess effective May 13, 2026.

On the Multi Commodity Exchange (MCX), gold futures for June delivery appreciated by ₹9,723 to ₹1,63,165 per 10 grams. This marks a 6.34% single-session rally.

Silver witnessed an even sharper jump. The most-traded July contract gained ₹19,439, or 6.97%, to settle at ₹2,98,501 per kilogram on the MCX.

The duty hike reverses a move from July 24, 2024, when the Centre had slashed customs duty on gold from 15% to 6%. The reversal comes amid a spiralling import bill due to the ongoing West Asia crisis.

On Sunday, Prime Minister Narendra Modi called for curbs on gold purchases alongside broader austerity measures to conserve foreign exchange. Traders said domestic bullion prices jumped immediately after the duty notification.

India's gold imports surged over 24% to an all-time high of $71.98 billion in 2025-26. However, volume terms saw a 4.76% dip to 721.03 tonnes, indicating higher global prices.

In international markets, gains remained comparatively muted. Comex gold futures for June rose just $21.40 (0.46%) to $4,708.10 per ounce, while silver advanced 2.12% to $87.40 per ounce.

Market participants said the sharp divergence between domestic and global prices reflected the immediate impact of higher import duties. The hike could moderate imports in the near term as the government looks to contain pressure on the current account deficit amid elevated crude oil prices and geopolitical tensions.

THE MOVE AIMS TO CURB PRECIOUS METALS IMPORTS AMID A RISING IMPORT BILL DRIVEN BY THE ONGOING WEST ASIA CRISIS. THE GOVERNMENT SEEKS TO CONTAIN PRESSURE ON THE CURRENT ACCOUNT DEFICIT, ESPECIALLY WITH ELEVATED CRUDE OIL PRICES AND GEOPOLITICAL TENSIONS.
