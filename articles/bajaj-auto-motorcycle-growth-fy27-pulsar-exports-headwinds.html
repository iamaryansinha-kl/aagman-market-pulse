Bajaj Auto Warns Motorcycle Growth Could Slow to 7-9% in FY27 as Pulsar, Exports Face Headwinds

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Bajaj Auto cautions India's motorcycle industry growth could slow to 7-9% in FY27 from over 20% in recent quarters amid inflationary and geopolitical headwinds
  • 02/FY26 revenue rose 17% YoY to ₹58,732 crore, EBITDA up 19% to ₹12,019 crore, PAT up 21% to ₹9,825 crore
  • 03/The company is betting on premium Pulsar motorcycles, export diversification and electric vehicles to sustain momentum despite broader market slowdown

Bajaj Auto Limited (NSE: BAJAJ-AUTO) has warned that India's motorcycle industry growth could moderate sharply to 7-9% in FY27. This is down from over 20% levels seen in recent quarters.

Executive Director Rakesh Sharma made the comments after the company's FY26 results on Wednesday, May 6, 2026. He pointed to rising fuel prices, metal inflation, supply-chain disruptions and West Asia tensions as key pressures.

"It's a considerable slowdown," Sharma said. He noted that softer consumer sentiment was already visible in April retail trends and could continue in the coming months.

For FY26, Bajaj Auto delivered its highest-ever revenue of ₹58,732 crore, up 17% year-on-year. EBITDA rose 19% to ₹12,019 crore and profit after tax climbed 21% to ₹9,825 crore.

Total volumes crossed 51 lakh units in FY26, the highest ever for the company. Exports crossed 6 lakh units for the second consecutive quarter, operating at around 2 lakh units per month.

Sharma highlighted the 150cc-plus motorcycle category as the company's biggest growth driver. He said Bajaj gained market share across regions in H2 FY26 through product upgrades introduced since October.

"In every single state of the country, the 150-plus segment performance has been better than industry growth," Sharma said. More product interventions are planned through FY27.

Export recovery is becoming geographically diversified. Nigeria remains at roughly half of its FY22 peak, but Latin America and other emerging markets are expanding sharply.

"Latin America has been fantastic, not just from a volume point of view, but also from revenue as well as margins," Sharma noted. The region increasingly buys higher-end Pulsar models.

Input-cost inflation of 3-5% is expected due to higher metal prices. Some pressure has been offset through April price hikes and favourable currency movements.

Sharma indicated that EVs now account for more than 20% of Bajaj Auto's domestic revenues. He suggested the impact of subsidy reduction is becoming less important in consumer decision-making.

KTM turnaround efforts could take around 18 months, involving sourcing changes, manufacturing optimisation and cost rationalisation. Bajaj is ramping up KTM production in India with newer models being localised.
