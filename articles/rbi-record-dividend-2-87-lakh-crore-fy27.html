RBI Declares Record Rs 2.87 Lakh Crore Dividend for FY27, Below Estimate

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/RBI declares record surplus transfer of Rs 2.87 lakh crore to government for FY27, up from Rs 2.69 lakh crore last year
  • 02/Payout falls short of Rs 3.16 lakh crore budget estimate; 10-year bond yield up 50 bps, rupee down 7% on war-driven energy costs
  • 03/CRB maintained at 6.5% of balance sheet with Rs 1.09 lakh crore transfer; forex gains and gold surge powered the record dividend

The Reserve Bank of India on Friday declared a record surplus transfer of Rs 2.87 lakh crore to the government for FY27. The payout, while record-breaking, fell short of the Rs 3.16 lakh crore budgeted in the Union Budget.

RBI's balance sheet expanded by 20.61% to Rs 91.97 lakh crore as of March 31, 2026. Gross income rose 26.42% year-on-year, while pre-provision expenditure climbed 27.60%.

The Central Board approved transferring Rs 1,09,379.64 crore to the Contingent Risk Buffer for FY26. This compares with Rs 44,861.70 crore transferred in the previous year.

The dividend arrives as rising crude prices inflate India's import bill and widen the current account deficit. The rupee has weakened nearly 7% this year, while the 10-year bond yield climbed about 50 basis points to 7.10%.

A sharp 10% fall in the US dollar and a 60% surge in gold prices during FY26 boosted RBI's accounting profitability. The central bank also purchased roughly Rs 9 lakh crore of bonds to inject liquidity.

Economists polled by Reuters said the windfall would not prevent India from missing its 4.3% fiscal deficit target. However, it provides a critical buffer as the Iran conflict drives energy costs higher.

"The government is not here to earn from the RBI," said Anil Bhansali, head of treasury at Finrex Treasury Advisors. "At present, they have no other alternative to generate extra revenue."

"While the RBI dividend has provided the Finance Ministry with a robust cushion, there have been serious efforts to consolidate the government's balance sheet," said Aditya Vyas, chief economist at STCI Primary Dealer.

The surplus transfer follows the revised Economic Capital Framework, which mandates the CRB stay between 4.5% and 7.5% of total balance sheet. The final payout reflects the delicate balance between state coffers and central bank resilience.
