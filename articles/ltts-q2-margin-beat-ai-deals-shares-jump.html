LTTS Soars 11% on Margin Beat, AI Deal Wins

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- LTTS shares surged nearly 11% to cross Rs 4,000 after a stronger-than-expected September quarter.
  • •- EBIT margin expanded 60 basis points sequentially to 16.3%, reaching the mid-16% target earlier than planned.
  • •- Revenue rose 3.4% sequentially to Rs 3,040 crore; adjusted profit rose 19.7% year-on-year to Rs 380 crore.
  • •- Large deal total contract value was about $203 million, with more than half AI-related.
  • •- AI-led revenue reached $120 million annualised, roughly 9.5% of quarterly revenue.
  • •- Brokerages still stayed cautious: Motilal Oswal and Nuvama hold Neutral/Hold, and Nomura kept Neutral.

The market has been waiting for an Indian IT-services company to show AI converting from presentation slides into purchase orders, and on Wednesday morning L&T Technology Services became the clearest exhibit yet. Shares of the engineering R&D specialist surged nearly 11% to cross the Rs 4,000 mark after a September quarter that beat on the metric investors care most about right now: margins. EBIT margin expanded 60 basis points sequentially to 16.3%, reaching the company's mid-16% target earlier than expected — against analyst forecasts of 15.6% from both Motilal Oswal and Nomura. Revenue rose 3.4% sequentially to Rs 3,040 crore, dollar revenue grew 2.7% in constant currency to $317 million, and adjusted profit rose 19.7% year-on-year to Rs 380 crore.

The deal book supplied the narrative. LTTS secured large deals worth about $203 million in total contract value during the quarter, with more than half AI-related and nearly half linked to the sustainability business; management is targeting a $200 million quarterly large-deal run rate with a pipeline up more than 25% from a year earlier. AI-led revenue has reached about $120 million annualised — roughly 9.5% of quarterly revenue — and the company has trained more than 16,000 engineers, about 67% of its workforce, in AI, filing enough patents during the quarter to take its total to 1,808. Partnerships with Nvidia, Cognite, Anthropic and Databricks, a new engineering intelligence lab with Nvidia, and a large AI-led engagement with a global telecom equipment provider ramping during the quarter round out the evidence that the AI pipeline is operational rather than promotional. Management believes it is nine to twelve months ahead of peers in Engineering Intelligence.

The vertical mix tells a more textured story. Sustainability remained the main growth engine at roughly 38% of revenue, growing 4.9% sequentially with an EBIT margin near 29.5% — the company's most profitable business — powered by data centres, energy transition, robotics and plant engineering. Mobility grew 2.3% on North American strength even as European automotive stayed weak amid OEM restructuring, with recovery expected over nine to twelve months. The technology vertical slipped 0.9% sequentially: tech infrastructure and semiconductors performed strongly, helped by the AI-led telecom deal, but a MedTech programme ramp-down and delayed follow-on work dragged the segment, and management expects technology growth to stay muted in Q3 with recovery from Q4.

Management's confidence that the second half of FY27 will be stronger than the first — backed by conversion of previously won large deals, an improving order backlog and engagement ramp-ups — is what the market chose to believe, alongside the long-term aspiration of 13-15% revenue growth over three to five years with 16-17% EBIT margins. The test arrives next quarter: wage hikes are scheduled for Q3 with an expected 50-80 basis-point margin impact, and management's claim that mid-16% margins can hold through that headwind, on mix and AI productivity, will be the first verification point of the new narrative. Delivery on that claim would move the AI story from investor enthusiasm to audited arithmetic, which is where sustainable re-ratings actually begin.

Notably, the brokerages did not chase the rally. Motilal Oswal held Neutral while raising its target to Rs 3,850 from Rs 3,400, judging the ER&D industry's macro recovery — weak autos and technology spend — as slower than the stock now prices. Nuvama held Hold at a Rs 3,450 target, noting the stock trades at about 22 times estimated FY28 earnings, and Nomura stayed Neutral at Rs 3,500. The gap between a stock crossing Rs 4,000 and target prices clustering Rs 3,450-3,850 is the market pricing faster conversion of AI deal wins into revenue than the analysts will underwrite; the quarter gave the bulls real numbers for the first time, and the next two quarters will determine whether those targets follow the stock or the stock follows the targets.

The quarter also lands in a contested industry context. India's engineering R&D services sector has spent two years digesting weak demand from European automotive restructuring and soft technology spending, and LTTS's own technology vertical — still shrinking sequentially — shows the recovery is uneven. What distinguishes the company's position is that its fastest-growing, highest-margin business is the one aligned with the two biggest global capex themes: AI infrastructure and the energy transition. With the Nvidia lab, Anthropic and Cognite partnerships, and a telecom infrastructure deal already ramping, LTTS is attempting to move up the value chain from engineering services vendor to engineering intelligence platform — a repositioning whose economics, if the 29.5% sustainability margin is any guide, are substantially richer than the traditional ER&D model it would partly replace. For investors, that makes LTTS less a bet on the ER&D cycle turning than a bet on a specific company escaping that cycle's gravity — a thesis Wednesday's 11% move shows a growing audience is willing to underwrite, analyst target prices notwithstanding.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHY DID LTTS SHARES JUMP 11%?
The September quarter beat expectations on EBIT margin, which hit 16.3% versus forecasts of 15.6%, reaching the company's mid-16% target early, alongside strong AI-led deal wins.
HOW BIG WERE THE AI DEALS?
Large deal wins totalled about $203 million in contract value, more than half AI-related; AI-led revenue is now $120 million annualised, about 9.5% of quarterly revenue.
WHAT ARE THE RISKS TO THE MARGIN STORY?
Q3 wage hikes are expected to cut margins by 50-80 basis points; management says mix and AI productivity should hold margins at mid-16% levels.
WHAT DO BROKERAGES THINK?
Cautious — Motilal Oswal and Nomura hold Neutral, Nuvama holds Hold, with targets of Rs 3,450-3,850, below where the stock traded after the rally. GLOSSARY: - EBIT margin: Operating profit as a percentage of revenue. - Constant currency: Growth measured excluding exchange-rate movements. - Total contract value: The full value of a deal signed over its contractual term. - Ramp-up: The period during which a newly won engagement builds to full revenue contribution.
