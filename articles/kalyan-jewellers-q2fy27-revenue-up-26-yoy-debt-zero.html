Kalyan Jewellers Q2FY27: Revenue Up 26% YoY, Non-GML Debt at Zero

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Kalyan Jewellers India (NSE: KALYANKJIL) reported Q2FY27 consolidated revenue growth of over 26% YoY, led by India operations up 27%
  • 02/Same-store sales grew approximately 20%, Candere jumped 64% YoY, international revenue rose 18%, and non-GML debt fell to zero
  • 03/Watch the Akshaya Thanga Maligai regional-brand rollout in Tamil Nadu, where the state's biggest season starts October 11

Kalyan Jewellers India Ltd (NSE: KALYANKJIL) shares rose 2% to Rs 560 on the BSE on Wednesday. The gain followed the company's provisional business update for Q2FY27, which showed consolidated revenue growth of more than 26% year-on-year.

India operations drove the quarter, with revenue up nearly 27% YoY despite a hard base. All nine days of Navratri fell in Q2 last year, making the year-ago comparison unusually strong. Same-store sales growth still came in at approximately 20%.

International revenue rose 18% in Q2FY27. The Middle East grew 12% entirely through same-store sales despite regional tensions, and international markets contributed nearly 11% of consolidated revenue.

The Candere lifestyle jewellery brand was the standout, with revenue up 64% YoY. Kalyan also launched 12 showrooms in India (net 11), nine Candere stores, and one UK showroom during the quarter ended September 30, 2026.

The quarter's boldest move was Akshaya Thanga Maligai (ATM), Kalyan's first regional brand, launched in Chennai. Four more franchised ATM showrooms are planned this financial year, two each in Q3 and Q4.

"We are highly encouraged by the way the brand has been received and the strong revenue traction over the first 40 plus days of operation, with the biggest season for Tamil Nadu slated to start from October 11," the company said in its business update.

The balance sheet cleaned up as well. Kalyan completed the sale of one non-core real estate parcel worth approximately Rs 86 crore and expects to sell a second, valued at roughly Rs 16 crore, in Q3. More significantly, non-GML (gold metal loan) debt came down to zero during the quarter — clearing all costlier non-inventory borrowings while keeping the cheap GML funding route intact.

The update sharpened a same-day contrast with Titan Company, which reported jewellery revenue growth of about 21% for the quarter. Kalyan outgrew its larger peer on same-store sales and cut debt to zero, yet still trades at a fraction of Titan's valuation multiple.

The gap cuts both ways. The stock has slipped nearly 7% over the past month even as the business accelerates, and gold price swings plus Middle East exposure remain live variables heading into the festive season.

Management is betting on that season anyway. "We are upbeat about the upcoming festive and wedding season across the country and are fully ready with fresh collections, campaigns and new showroom launches," the company said. At Rs 560, Kalyan carries a market capitalisation above Rs 56,610 crore, with the stock up over 13% in 2026, 112% in three years, and 586% in five years.
