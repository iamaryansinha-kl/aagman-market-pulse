India's August Exports Jump 26.1% to $43.8 Billion, Trade Deficit Narrows to $26.9 Billion

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- 01/ Exports grew 26.1% YoY to $43.8 billion in August, accelerating from 19.6% in July.
  • •- 02/ Imports rose 14.1% to $70.7 billion, slowing from 17.5% in July.
  • •- 03/ The merchandise trade deficit narrowed to $26.9 billion from $32 billion in July.
  • •- 04/ Shipments to the US rose 21.8%; gold imports plunged 57.7%.
  • •- 05/ Crisil expects the current account deficit to widen to 1.5% of GDP this fiscal.

## Exports accelerate to 26.1%

India's merchandise exports rose 26.1% year-on-year to $43.8 billion in August 2026, sharply accelerating from 19.6% growth in July. The gain was broad-based: core exports excluding oil and gems and jewellery grew 22.7%, up from 14.9% in July.

Oil exports remained robust, growing 63.3%. Gems and jewellery exports returned to growth, rising 0.7% after a 2.7% contraction in July.

## US demand leads, West Asia drags

Shipments to the United States rose 21.8% year-on-year, accelerating from 12.8% growth in July. Exports to Malaysia, Singapore and Japan also gathered pace.

Exports to the UAE and Saudi Arabia declined 26.1% and 17.5% respectively, reflecting renewed disruptions in West Asia.

Agricultural exports gained momentum on higher shipments of rice and marine products, while meat, dairy and poultry exports continued to post double-digit growth.

## Imports slow, deficit narrows

Imports grew 14.1% year-on-year to $70.7 billion in August, down from 17.5% growth in July. The moderation was driven by a 34.8% contraction in gems and jewellery imports, with gold imports plunging 57.7%.

Oil imports accelerated to 25.8% growth from 17.6%, largely on a low statistical base.

The combination of faster exports and slower imports pulled the merchandise trade deficit down to $26.9 billion from $32 billion in July and $27.2 billion a year earlier. This is the narrowest deficit since March, when West Asia supply disruptions distorted trade flows.

## Services cushion and the outlook

Preliminary estimates show a services trade surplus of $17.5 billion in August, marginally lower than $17.6 billion in July but well above $15.6 billion a year earlier.

The external sector still faces pressure. Crisil Intelligence expects crude oil to average $88-93 per barrel this fiscal, versus $70.3 last fiscal, and sees the current account deficit widening to 1.5% of GDP from 0.6%.

A sustained crude spike could push the CAD outlook higher, even as August's data signal a strong start for merchandise exports and a meaningful moderation in the trade gap.
