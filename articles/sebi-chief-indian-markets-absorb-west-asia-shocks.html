SEBI Chief: Indian Markets Can Absorb West Asia Crisis Shocks

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/SEBI Chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey says Indian markets have the capacity to absorb different types of shocks from the West Asia crisis
  • 02/Foreign portfolio investors have pulled out funds since September 2024, but domestic investors have retained confidence
  • 03/West Asia conflict has disrupted oil supply chains, creating inflationary risks and spillover effects globally

SEBI Chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey said Indian markets can absorb different types of shocks. He made the statement on Monday in Bhubaneswar.

Pandey was speaking at the Regional Investors Seminar for Awareness. He addressed concerns about rising volatility in financial markets.

The West Asia conflict has disrupted oil supply chains worldwide. Oil prices have been affected across global markets.

"All the economies have been affected by this and obviously, there are inflationary risks," Pandey said. He warned that spillover effects and second-order effects will follow.

Pandey admitted there have been foreign portfolio investment outflows since September 2024. However, he noted that domestic investors have "retained their confidence."

"Ups and downs in the market are quite natural because globally they are interconnected," the SEBI chairman said. He emphasized that markets resume normal trajectory once crises end.

The Nifty 50 was trading at 23,649.95 during Monday's session. It had gained 6.46 points.

The West Asia crisis has raised concerns about energy security and inflation in India. Oil imports form a significant portion of India's trade deficit.
