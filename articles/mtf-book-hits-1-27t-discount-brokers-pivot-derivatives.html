MTF Book Hits ₹1.27T as Discount Brokers Pivot from Derivatives

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/India's margin trading facility book reached a record ₹1.27 trillion in May 2026, surging 65% year-on-year according to CareEdge Ratings
  • 02/Discount brokers Groww and Angel One are doubling down on MTF after SEBI curbs slashed derivatives revenue, charging ~15% annual interest
  • 03/SEBI proposed new MTF rules on 18 June to allow NCD funding, but nearly half the industry's book is backed by illiquid non-F&O stocks

India's margin trading facility book hit a record ₹1.27 trillion in May 2026. It rose 65% year-on-year and 11% month-on-month, according to CareEdge Ratings.

Discount brokers are pivoting to MTF after SEBI's derivatives crackdown dented their biggest revenue stream. Groww and Angel One derived 55% and 47% of Q4 FY26 revenue from equity derivatives respectively.

Groww's MTF book surged nearly five-fold year-on-year to ₹2,814 crore in Q4 FY26. Angel One's average client funding book rose 60% to ₹5,850 crore from ₹3,650 crore a year earlier.

Both brokers charge approximately 15% annual interest on MTF loans. Groww also levies a brokerage fee of 0.1% on the order value.

MTF contribution to revenue remains modest at 7-7.5% for both firms. However, it generates recurring interest income unlike volatile derivatives brokerage fees.

"Regulatory changes have structurally capped the derivatives revenue model," said Nilesh Sharma, executive director and president at SAMCO Securities. "The discount broking industry has reached a saturation point."

Nearly half of the industry's MTF book is backed by relatively illiquid non-F&O stocks. This could amplify selling pressure during sharp market corrections, warned Karthick Jonagadla of Quantace Research.

SEBI proposed allowing brokers to fund MTF via listed non-convertible debentures in a June 18 consultation paper. It also plans to raise the minimum net worth threshold for MTF-offering brokers to ₹5 crore from ₹3 crore.
