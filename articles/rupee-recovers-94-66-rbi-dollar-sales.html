Rupee Recovers to 94.66 as RBI-Backed Dollar Sales Curb Losses

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/The Indian rupee recovered from an intraday low of 94.91 to close at 94.66 against the US dollar on June 25, 2026
  • 02/State-run banks sold dollars in the market, likely acting on behalf of the RBI, to prevent further depreciation
  • 03/The dollar index surged to 101.6, a 13-month high, as markets priced in potential US Federal Reserve rate hikes later this year

The Indian rupee closed at 94.66 against the US dollar on Wednesday, June 25, 2026. It recovered from an intraday low of 94.91 touched earlier in the session.

The previous close was 94.73 per dollar. The rupee gained approximately 0.07% for the trading session.

State-run banks intervened in the forex market by selling dollars. Traders said the sales were likely conducted on behalf of the Reserve Bank of India to curb further depreciation.

The dollar index (DXY) strengthened to 101.6 on Wednesday. This marked its highest level in 13 months amid shifting expectations around US monetary policy.

Markets are increasingly pricing in the possibility that the Federal Reserve may raise interest rates later this year. This has boosted demand for the greenback across global currency markets.

The rupee has faced sustained pressure in recent months due to a combination of factors. These include foreign portfolio investor outflows, high FDI repatriation, and broad dollar strength.

The RBI has been actively defending the currency. On June 5, 2026, it announced measures including full subsidy on FX hedging costs for FCNR(B) deposits and removal of taxes on capital gains for foreign investors in government securities.

Analysts expect the rupee to remain vulnerable if the Fed maintains a hawkish stance. RBI intervention will likely continue to smooth volatility and prevent disorderly moves.
