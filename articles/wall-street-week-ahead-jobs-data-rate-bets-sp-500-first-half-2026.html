Wall Street Week Ahead: Jobs Data, Rate Bets In Focus As S&P 500 Rises 7% In 2026

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/June U.S. jobs report due Thursday with payrolls expected at 110,000 after 172,000 in May
  • 02/Fed funds futures signal better-than-even odds of a rate hike by September meeting
  • 03/Nasdaq Composite fell over 4% this week as semiconductor stocks see volatile swings

The S&P 500 has gained more than 7% so far in 2026. Major U.S. equity indexes are set to close out a solid first half of the year.

But equities have had a rougher time in June. The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite fell more than 4% on the week.

High-flying semiconductor shares have seen huge moves this week. The Philadelphia SE Semiconductor Index has soared 85% since the market's late-March low.

The monthly jobs report due on Thursday is the next key catalyst. Economists polled by Reuters expect June payrolls to rise by 110,000 jobs.

"If we do get a really good jobs number, my guess is the market's not going to treat that as good news," said Doug Huber, deputy chief investment officer at Wealth Enhancement. "It's going to treat it as the economy's hot."

The Federal Reserve's latest meeting revealed policymakers were laser-focused on containing inflation. Data on Thursday showed inflation breaking above 4% for the first time in three years.

"The Fed is very finely balanced," said Brad Conger, chief investment officer at Hirtle & Co. "If jobs are strong, interest rates could go back up, and that challenges the market."

Fed funds futures indicate better-than-even odds of a hike by the central bank's September meeting. This marks a reversal from January when investors expected rate cuts by year-end.

Oil has dropped to around $70 a barrel from $100 a month ago. Energy prices eased amid a ceasefire in the Middle East.

Nike is scheduled to report earnings next week. Second-quarter reporting season ramps up later in July.
