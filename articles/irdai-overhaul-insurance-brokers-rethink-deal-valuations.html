IRDAI Overhaul May Force Insurance Brokers to Rethink Deal Valuations

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

A consultation paper from the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India has put a question mark over some of the most-watched transactions in the insurance distribution space.

The proposed overhaul could force investors to reassess how they value insurance brokerage businesses. At stake are live deals, including Alliance Insurance Brokers' planned stake sale and the proposed IPO of the consolidated InsuranceDekho-RenewBuy entity.

The regulator's core concern is arithmetic. IRDAI data shows broker-sourced premium grew 37% between FY23 and FY25. Commissions, however, surged 173% over the same period. Average commission rates climbed from 8.5% to 17%.

That divergence sits at the heart of the proposal. Prescriptive commission limits, based on product complexity, distribution channel, and the effort involved in selling insurance, would cap the payouts that have fuelled broker economics.

The Numbers Behind the Freeze

Alliance Insurance Brokers has already moved on a partial exit. The firm has roped in O3 Capital to sell a one-third stake to a strategic investor, according to a person familiar with the matter. Whether that process can hold its valuation assumptions under the proposed regime is now an open question.

The InsuranceDekho-RenewBuy combination is further along. The two businesses consolidated last month and have been working on an initial public offering of roughly Rs 3,000 crore. Both deals could be affected, depending on how much of their book is exposed to retail distribution.

Industry estimates suggest the impact could be severe for some players. Some brokers could see revenue decline 60-70% if the proposals are implemented in their current form, industry representatives said. The final impact will depend on each firm's business mix.

The economics have changed, said a senior insurance broker at a large broking firm. Ongoing IPO and strategic sale plans would have to be reassessed once greater clarity emerges on the final regulations.

The buyer and seller will have to decide, the broker added. Whoever is buying, they do not know what valuation they can put to the business.

Why Valuations Are Hard to Lock In

The uncertainty is structural, not temporary. IRDAI has proposed prescriptive commission limits that vary across products, lines of business, and distribution channels. It has also proposed lower expense-of-management limits and greater scrutiny of distribution costs.

That makes the future revenue and margin profile of a brokerage difficult to model. An executive at a broking company said the consultation paper has left the industry uncertain about the extent of the impact, as commission limits will vary across products, lines of business, and distribution channels.

Buyers typically value distribution businesses on forward commission income. With the commission structure itself under review, the multiple applied to that income becomes a matter of judgment rather than convention.

Until the final commission structure is known, investors may find it difficult to estimate future revenue, margins, and valuations, industry executives said.

The Consolidation Argument

Not everyone loses under the proposed regime. Higher compliance costs and lower payouts could accelerate consolidation among smaller brokers, experts said. Firms with scale, diversified product mixes, and direct corporate relationships are better positioned to absorb fixed compliance costs.

That could reshape the competitive map. A market where average commission rates nearly doubled in two years has attracted new entrants and aggressive capital. Capping those payouts would favour incumbents with structural advantages over smaller, commission-driven distributors.

What Happens Next

The regulator has invited stakeholder comments until October 25. That timeline gives dealmakers a defined but narrow window to assess exposure and respond.

For now, the live transactions will likely proceed on extended timelines, with valuation conversations anchored to the final regulation rather than current run-rates. Companies with heavy retail distribution exposure face the largest repricing risk. Those with commercial and reinsurance books may see limited impact.

The consultation period effectively freezes the market's ability to price these assets. Until IRDAI finalises the structure, every insurance brokerage deal in the pipeline carries a regulatory discount.

UNTIL THEN, INVESTORS CANNOT RELIABLY MODEL FUTURE REVENUE, MARGINS, OR VALUATIONS FOR BROKERAGE BUSINESSES. GLOSSARY: IRDAI: The Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India. The sector regulator that frames rules for insurers, brokers, and distributors. COMMISSION RATE: The percentage of premium paid to a broker or agent as compensation for sourcing a policy. Average broker commission rates rose from 8.5% to 17% between FY23 and FY25. EXPENSE OF MANAGEMENT: The total operating cost of an insurer or intermediary, including commissions. IRDAI sets limits on this ratio to protect policyholder funds. CONSULTATION PAPER: A formal document issued by a regulator outlining proposed rule changes and inviting stakeholder feedback before finalisation. STAKE SALE: The sale of an ownership stake in a company by existing shareholders, often to a strategic or financial investor. IPO: Initial Public Offering. The first sale of a company's shares to public investors, priced using forward revenue and earnings estimates.
