Nithin Kamath Warns RBI Dovish Stance May Not Last Amid Inflation Risks

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •• Nithin Kamath says RBI may reverse its dovish stance sooner than markets expect due to persistent inflation pressures.
  • •• Core inflation remains sticky even as headline CPI dropped to 2.1% in June 2025, the lowest since January 2019.
  • •• RBI has cut repo rate by 100 bps since February 2025 to 5.5%, but markets may be pricing in too much easing.

Zerodha co-founder and CEO Nithin Kamath has warned that the Reserve Bank of India may not maintain its current dovish stance for long.

In a recent analysis, Kamath described the macroeconomic environment as an "unholy mess" where inflation remains persistent despite headline numbers looking benign.

Headline CPI inflation fell to just 2.1% in June 2025, marking the lowest reading since January 2019 when it touched 1.97%.

However, core inflation has started ticking upward, suggesting underlying price pressures remain entrenched in the economy.

The RBI has responded to slowing growth by cutting the repo rate by 100 basis points since February 2025.

The benchmark lending rate now stands at 5.5%, down from 6.5% where it had been held since February 2023.

Kamath argues that markets may be misreading the inflation trajectory and pricing in more rate cuts than the RBI can deliver.

Food inflation, while currently muted, remains a wildcard given India's dependence on monsoon patterns and global commodity swings.

The RBI's inflation target band is 4% with a tolerance of plus or minus 2 percentage points on either side.

With headline CPI below the target mid-point but core inflation rising, the central bank faces a difficult balancing act.

Kamath's warning comes at a time when equity markets have rallied on expectations of sustained monetary easing.

If the RBI pivots back to a neutral or hawkish stance, rate-sensitive sectors like banking, real estate, and auto could face headwinds.

Investors should watch the RBI's next monetary policy statement closely for any shift in forward guidance.
