Karur Vysya Bank Q2 Profit Jumps 37% to Record Rs 788 Crore

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Q2 net profit rose 37% YoY to Rs 788 crore, the bank's highest-ever quarterly profit.
  • •- Net interest income grew 19% to Rs 1,497.3 crore.
  • •- Net interest margin improved 28 bps YoY to 4.34%.
  • •- Gross NPA was 0.69%, net NPA 0.18%; ROA 2.10%, ROE 20.47%.
  • •- Advances grew 20.35%, deposits 15.96%; total business reached Rs 2.40 lakh crore.
  • •- Shares rallied as much as 5.51% to a fresh 52-week high.

Mid-sized private banks keep producing the cleanest numbers of this earnings season. Karur Vysya Bank on Friday reported a 37 per cent year-on-year jump in September-quarter net profit to Rs 788 crore — its highest-ever quarterly profit — on the back of 19 per cent net interest income growth, and the market rewarded it immediately: the stock rallied as much as 5.51 per cent to an intraday high of Rs 364.70, touching a fresh 52-week high of Rs 365.90 during the session.

The profit engine is margin expansion layered on loan growth. Net interest income — the difference between interest earned and interest paid — rose 19 per cent to Rs 1,497.3 crore from Rs 1,261 crore a year earlier, while net interest margin improved 28 basis points year-on-year to 4.34 per cent, a level most larger private lenders would trade for. Fee and commission income added another leg, rising 11 per cent, and the cost-to-income ratio came in at a lean 40.53 per cent, meaning the bank converts a large share of every rupee of revenue into operating profit before provisions.

The balance sheet grew in the balanced way management has been promising. Total business increased 17.96 per cent year-on-year and 5.48 per cent sequentially to Rs 2,39,712 crore. Deposits grew 15.96 per cent to Rs 1,28,123 crore, and advances grew 20.35 per cent to Rs 1,11,589 crore, keeping the loan book expanding faster than funding — the composition that supports margins. The retail, agriculture and MSME segment, which anchors Karur Vysya's franchise in Tamil Nadu, grew 20 per cent year-on-year. Current and savings account deposits rose 15.41 per cent, taking the CASA ratio to 27.52 per cent.

Asset quality, the metric where this bank has historically differentiated itself, remained exceptional. Gross non-performing assets stood at 0.69 per cent and net NPAs at 0.18 per cent — levels that make most of the banking sector look sloppy by comparison. Return on assets came in at 2.10 per cent for the quarter and return on equity at 20.47 per cent, meaning the bank earns roughly twice the sector's typical return on its assets. Managing Director and CEO Ramesh Babu B said key performance indicators were broadly in line with earlier guidance, and signalled the same recipe for the coming quarters: calibrated growth, operational efficiency, customer centricity and disciplined risk management.

The market context explains the 5.5 per cent pop. The shares have gained around 60 per cent over the past year, taking market capitalisation to roughly Rs 34,123 crore, and technical indicators show the stock trading above all eight simple moving averages. A 37 per cent profit print with 20 per cent loan growth and 0.69 per cent gross NPAs gives momentum investors exactly the combination they are paying for, and the 52-week high suggests the market sees no near-term ceiling.

Karur Vysya's quarter is also a data point in the broader regional-bank story that this earnings season keeps reinforcing: the well-run mid-tier lenders are outgrowing the giants. Where the largest private banks struggle to move deposit and loan growth much beyond the mid-teens, focused franchises like Karur Vysya — with deep Tamil Nadu roots, a granular RAM book and an unglamorous discipline on costs and credit — are compounding at 18-20 per cent without breaking their asset-quality models. The NIM of 4.34 per cent, in particular, reflects a funding and pricing discipline that scale usually dilutes.

The watch-items are the usual ones for a bank at this stage of its cycle: whether RAM-heavy growth can stay this clean through a full credit cycle, whether the 11 per cent fee-income growth accelerates as the franchise deepens, and whether management — mindful, in the CEO's words, of the evolving operating environment — can resist the temptation that has undone many a well-run mid-cap lender: chasing growth into segments it does not know. On Friday's evidence, the discipline is holding, and the market is pricing in more of the same.

For the broader market, the print lands the same week as the results of much larger private peers and, against them, holds its own: a 4.34 per cent NIM and 0.69 per cent gross NPAs would be considered exceptional at any size. In a season where investors have rewarded quality over scale — and punished any whiff of asset-quality slippage — Karur Vysya delivered the clean version of the mid-tier banking story, and the 52-week high says the rerating is not finished.

The franchise itself remains a study in focus: a century-old Tamil Nadu bank that never tried to be a national giant, survived the bad-loan cycle that felled stronger names, and now compounds quietly at twenty per cent while larger rivals wrestle with margin math. Fridays like this one are when that patience converts into market value, and when a mid-tier lender's quiet compounding starts looking like the best business model in Indian banking.

GLOSSARY: - Net interest margin (NIM): Interest income minus interest expense as a share of interest-earning assets — the core profitability gauge for a bank. - CASA ratio: The share of deposits in current and savings accounts, which are cheaper for banks to fund. - RAM segment: Retail, agriculture and MSME lending — typically granular, higher-yielding loans. - ROA/ROE: Return on assets and return on equity — measures of how profitably a bank uses its balance sheet and shareholders' capital.
