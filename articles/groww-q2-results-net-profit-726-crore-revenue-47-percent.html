Groww Q2 Net Profit Surges 54% to Rs 726 Crore, Revenue Jumps 47%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Groww parent Billionbrains Garage Ventures reported consolidated net profit of Rs 725.60 crore for Q2 FY27, up 54% year-on-year from Rs 471 crore.
  • •- Revenue from operations rose 47% to Rs 1,501 crore from Rs 1,019 crore a year earlier.
  • •- EBITDA rose 1% sequentially to Rs 968 crore, with margins improving 60 basis points to 64.5%.
  • •- The platform added 11 lakh new transacting users, driven by primary market activity, and captured a 22% share of IPO allotments.
  • •- Equity derivatives' contribution to income fell 12.2 percentage points due to the new client-level activity framework.
  • •- Wealth assets crossed Rs 10,000 crore, while Groww AMC's assets rose 145% year-on-year to Rs 7,179 crore.

Groww parent Billionbrains Garage Ventures on Wednesday reported a second quarter that underscored the discount broker's transformation from a stock-trading app into a broad financial services franchise: consolidated net profit of Rs 725.60 crore, up 54 per cent from Rs 471 crore a year earlier, on revenue from operations that jumped 47 per cent to Rs 1,501 crore.

Profit attributable to the owners of the company came in at Rs 725.60 crore, though the sequential picture was flatter — net profit dipped 1 per cent quarter-on-quarter, reflecting a softer derivatives market. EBITDA gained 1 per cent sequentially to Rs 968 crore from Rs 960 crore in the first quarter, with margins improving 60 basis points to 64.5 per cent from 63.9 per cent. Consolidated expenses rose 29 per cent year-on-year, but with profit growing twice as fast, the PAT margin expanded 3 percentage points to 47 per cent.

The market's reaction was muted: shares traded about half a per cent lower at Rs 193 apiece on the BSE after the results, a reminder that at a 47 per cent net margin, expectations for this stock are already written in bold.

The quarter's real story is the user engine. Groww added 11 lakh new transacting users, driven primarily by primary market activity — about 42 lakh users applied for equity IPOs through the platform, giving Groww a 22 per cent share of total allotments. That statistic captures the structural shift in Indian capital markets better than any other this earnings season: the IPO boom is not just feeding underwriters, it is feeding the platforms that retail investors use to reach it.

There was one clear drag. The contribution of equity derivatives to total income fell 12.2 percentage points year-on-year, reflecting market adjustments to the new client-level activity framework, known as CAS. Equity derivatives revenue declined 14.3 per cent quarter-on-quarter, which the company attributed largely to CAS but noted was in line with the broader industry's decline. Management expects the contribution to recover once the recommendations in Sebi's consultation paper — which proposes delinking derivatives settlement from CAS closing prices — take effect.

The cost line also carried interesting signals. Expenses came in higher on an uptick in tech costs driven by more trading days, one-time reimbursements to clients for abnormal trades, and inflation in computing costs that the company attributed partly to artificial intelligence demand. Groww expects that inflation-driven component to remain part of its operational baseline run rate — a small but telling admission that the AI boom is now a cost line for consumer fintech platforms, not just a theme for semiconductor stocks.

Beyond broking, the newer businesses scaled meaningfully. Wealth assets crossed Rs 10,000 crore, and Groww AMC's assets under management rose 145 per cent year-on-year to Rs 7,179 crore from Rs 2,928 crore, with the share of actively managed assets increasing to 57 per cent from 47 per cent. AMC revenue grew 131 per cent year-on-year, though the unit still reported a small net loss of Rs 8.3 crore as it invests for scale.

Taken together, the quarter sketches the company's strategic bet clearly: broking margins fund a march into wealth and asset management, while IPO-driven user acquisition replaces derivatives as the growth engine. With the Jio IPO set to open next week and the primary market pipeline at record levels, that bet has a powerful tailwind. The derivatives question remains open — but for now, the platform that captured 22 per cent of India's IPO allotments is proving it can monetize the flood of first-time investors better than anyone else in the market.

The competitive frame matters here. Groww has spent three years trading places with Zerodha at the top of India's discount broking league table by active clients, and its decision to list earlier this year turned its quarterly results into a public scorecard for the entire industry. Where its unlisted rival discloses little, Groww's filings now offer the clearest window into how India's retail investing boom converts into revenue — and this quarter's answer is: through IPOs, wealth and lending-adjacent products, with derivatives no longer the centre of gravity.

The CAS framework deserves explanation, because it is reshaping every broker's P&L. The client-level activity framework, introduced by Sebi to curb speculative churn in equity derivatives, ties margins and position limits to genuine client activity, squeezing the high-frequency retail options churn that had swollen broker revenues over the past two years. Groww's 14.3 per cent quarter-on-quarter derivatives revenue decline mirrors what the rest of the industry has reported; the company's hope — shared by the market — is that Sebi's consultation paper delinking derivatives settlement from CAS closing prices will restore some activity without reopening the speculative floodgates.

There is also a quiet milestone embedded in the AMC numbers. Groww's asset management arm, barely three years old, now manages Rs 7,179 crore with an actively managed share of 57 per cent — meaning it is winning with stock-picking funds rather than index clones. The unit's Rs 8.3 crore loss is the cost of distribution and talent build-out; the 131 per cent revenue growth suggests the path to profitability is more a question of when than if.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT WERE GROWW'S Q2 NUMBERS?
Consolidated net profit rose 54% year-on-year to Rs 725.60 crore, while revenue from operations grew 47% to Rs 1,501 crore. EBITDA was Rs 968 crore with a 64.5% margin.
WHY DID DERIVATIVES INCOME FALL?
The contribution of equity derivatives to total income fell 12.2 percentage points due to market adjustments to the new client-level activity framework (CAS). Groww expects recovery once Sebi's consultation paper recommendations take effect.
HOW MANY NEW USERS DID GROWW ADD?
11 lakh new transacting users in the quarter, driven by primary market activity. Around 42 lakh users applied for IPOs through the platform, a 22% share of total allotments.
HOW ARE THE NEWER BUSINESSES PERFORMING?
Wealth assets crossed Rs 10,000 crore and Groww AMC's AUM rose 145% year-on-year to Rs 7,179 crore, with AMC revenue up 131%, though the unit reported a small net loss of Rs 8.3 crore. GLOSSARY: - New transacting user (NTU): A customer who executes at least one transaction on the platform during the period. - Client-level activity framework (CAS): A Sebi-mandated structure tying derivative positions and margins to individual client activity. - AUM: Assets under management, the total value of investments a fund manager administers. - Margin trading facility (MTF): Broker-funded leverage allowing clients to trade with borrowed money. - PAT margin: Net profit as a percentage of revenue.
