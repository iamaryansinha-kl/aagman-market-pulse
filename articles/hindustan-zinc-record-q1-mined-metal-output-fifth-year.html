Hindustan Zinc Reports Record Q1 Mined Metal Output for Fifth Straight Year

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Hindustan Zinc reports record Q1 mined metal output of 268,000 kt, up 1% YoY for fifth consecutive year
  • 02/Saleable metal production up 4% to 260,000 kt; refined zinc at 213,000 kt; silver stable at 149 tonnes
  • 03/Signs MoU for green hydrogen adoption; shares fell 16% in June amid global silver price volatility

Hindustan Zinc, a Vedanta Group company, announced its business update for the first quarter ended June 30, 2026. The company achieved another record production milestone, driven primarily by better ore grades.

The company reported its highest-ever first-quarter mined metal production for the fifth consecutive year. Output rose 1% year-on-year to 268,000 kt in Q1FY27 from 265,000 kt in the corresponding quarter last year.

Saleable metal production increased 4% year-on-year to 260,000 kt. This was supported by capacity unlocked through debottlenecking initiatives at Chanderiya and Dariba. The 160 ktpa roaster at Debari also contributed, partially offset by planned maintenance activities.

Refined zinc production rose to 213,000 kt during the quarter. Refined lead production declined 2% year-on-year to 47,000 kt, as per the company's regulatory filing.

Silver production remained largely stable at 149 tonnes, or 4.8 million ounces. This was flat compared with the year-ago period. Wind power generation stood at 133 million units, marginally lower than 134 million units reported in Q1FY26.

In the March quarter, mined metal production stood at 315,000 kt. Saleable metal production was 282,000 kt. Silver production came in at 176 tonnes in that quarter.

On June 22, the company signed a memorandum of understanding with Advantek Associates and Aero Eagle Automobiles. This is to explore the adoption of green hydrogen and other clean energy solutions across its operations.

The company said this initiative forms part of its strategy to develop low-carbon mining operations. It aligns with the company's target of achieving net-zero emissions by 2050 or earlier.

For the quarter ended March 2026, the company reported a 67.6% year-on-year jump in net profit to ₹5,033 crore. Revenue rose 43.8% to ₹12,692 crore. This was supported by higher metal prices, improved realisations, and strong operational performance.

The company's shares witnessed a sharp correction in June, falling 16%. This marked their second-biggest monthly decline in 2026. The drop was amid a sustained decline in global silver prices.

The correction erased much of the stock's gains for the year. Its year-to-date return now stands at 14%, a sharp slowdown from the 38% gain recorded in 2025.

Hindustan Zinc is particularly sensitive to fluctuations in silver prices. Silver contributes significantly to the company's revenue and profitability.

Earlier this month, Vedanta confirmed that officials from the Enforcement Directorate had visited some of its offices. This was part of a FEMA-related investigation, which also weighed on investor sentiment.

Media reports also suggested that the government is considering selling up to a 2% stake in Hindustan Zinc. The government had previously divested a 1.6% stake in November, raising around ₹3,500 crore. Those shares were sold at ₹505 apiece.
