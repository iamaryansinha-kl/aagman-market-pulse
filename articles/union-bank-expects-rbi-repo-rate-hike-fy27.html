Union Bank Expects RBI Repo Rate to Reach 5.75-6% in FY27

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Union Bank of India expects the RBI to raise the repo rate by 25 basis points at the October 2026 MPC meeting, with one or two further hikes in FY27 taking the benchmark to 5.75-6.00%.
  • 02/The 5-year G-Sec yield rose 45 basis points in September versus 24 basis points for the 10-year, narrowing the spread from 36 to 16 basis points.
  • 03/Watch the RBI's hawkish guidance and continued liquidity absorption through OMO sales and VRRR auctions in the months ahead.

Union Bank of India expects the Reserve Bank of India to raise the repo rate by 25 basis points at the October monetary policy review. The bank's report, dated October 4, sees one or two further hikes in FY27 taking the benchmark rate to 5.75-6.00%.

The call comes just ahead of the RBI's Monetary Policy Committee meeting, scheduled for October 5-7, 2026. According to the report, inflation risks persist, and the bank also expects hawkish guidance that signals continued vigilance on prices.

The expectation frames the October decision as the start of a measured tightening cycle rather than a one-off move. Union Bank's economists see the benchmark rate settling in a 5.75-6.00% band by the end of the current fiscal year.

Indian government bonds, meanwhile, remained under pressure through September. High crude oil prices and rising global yields strengthened expectations of RBI rate hikes during the month.

Domestic factors added to the strain. Higher bond supply, including RBI open market operation (OMO) sales and increased long-tenor issuance in the second-half borrowing calendar, kept yields elevated despite lower overall government borrowing.

The pressure showed up clearly across the yield curve. The 5-year G-Sec yield rose 45 basis points in September, compared with a 24 basis point rise for the 10-year.

As a result, the gap between the two maturities narrowed sharply. The 10-year minus 5-year spread compressed from 36 basis points to 16 basis points over the course of the month.

Borrowing numbers explain part of the supply worry. The government plans gross dated G-Sec borrowing of Rs 7.86 lakh crore in H2FY27.

The gap between budgeted and projected gross borrowing stands at around Rs 1.1 lakh crore. Even so, the report notes that net borrowing remains broadly unchanged.

The reason is an aggressive switch programme. Switch auctions have reduced redemptions, deferring repayments and lowering redemption requirements and refinancing needs in FY27.

Supply will also come from other channels. Higher T-bill issuance and moderately higher state borrowing are expected to add to market supply in the coming months.

On the liquidity side, the RBI has been steadily draining the surplus from the banking system. It is absorbing the FCNR(B)-led surplus through variable rate reverse repo (VRRR) auctions, OMO sales and foreign exchange swaps.

The pace of absorption picked up in September. OMO sales totalled Rs 1 lakh crore across three auctions during the month.

The impact on system liquidity was substantial. The banking system surplus shrank to Rs 4.85 lakh crore as of September 30, from Rs 11.16 lakh crore on September 6.

That is a drawdown of more than half in under four weeks. It reflects the central bank's effort to mop up excess funds flowing from FCNR(B) deposit inflows.

Despite the tightening of liquidity, a broad-based cash reserve ratio hike appears less preferred. The report points to the RBI's earlier exemption of eligible FCNR(B) deposits from reserve requirements as the reason.

The risks to bond yields lean to the upside, according to the report. The 10-year G-Sec yield could face further upward pressure if policy tightening coincides with persistently elevated crude oil prices, rising global yields and subdued auction demand.

Taken together, the report sketches a challenging backdrop for Indian government bonds in the second half of the fiscal year. Tighter policy, heavy supply and draining liquidity are the three forces Union Bank says investors should track.

All of this remains the bank's expectation rather than a confirmed policy path. The MPC's decision at its October 5-7 meeting, and the tone of its guidance, will determine how much of this call plays out.
