Adani Power Lines Up Land & SPVs for Nuclear Push, Guides ₹25,000 Crore FY27 Capex

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Adani Power Ltd incorporates Adani Atomic Energy Ltd in February 2026 and Coastal-Maha Atomic Energy Ltd in April 2026 to enter nuclear power.
  • 02/CEO S B Khyalia says the company is identifying sites and seeking approvals so it can "move fast" once government rules are notified.
  • 03/Watch for nuclear policy clarity from the Centre and the ₹25,000 crore to ₹33,000 crore annual capex ramp through FY28.

Adani Power Ltd, India's largest private sector thermal power producer with over 18 GW of installed capacity, is building a deliberate early-stage pipeline in nuclear energy. The company is aggregating land, incorporating special purpose vehicles, and seeking regulatory approvals ahead of formal policy rules.

"So far the government of India has not notified the rules, so we are only getting ourselves ready. As and when the rules come, it will give us clarity about the size, capacity etc. We are preparing ourselves to move fast," said S B Khyalia, Chief Executive Officer of Adani Power Ltd, during an investor call earlier this week.

Adani Atomic Energy Ltd (AAEL) was incorporated in February 2026 as the company's vehicle into nuclear power. In April 2026, Coastal-Maha Atomic Energy Ltd (CMAEL), a step-down wholly owned subsidiary, was set up to undertake generation, transmission and distribution of nuclear energy.

Importantly, Khyalia clarified that Adani Power is not repurposing existing thermal assets for nuclear. "In our case these are new sites and will not be existing thermal sites," he said, signalling a greenfield approach.

The nuclear push runs parallel to a heavy capex cycle. Adani Power has guided for ₹25,000 crore in capital expenditure in FY27, with spending expected to rise to over ₹33,000 crore in FY28. The company is adding 1,320 MW at its Korba plant, with the first unit expected between June and September 2026.

Project execution is at advanced stages across key plants. Mahan Phase-II (1,600 MW) stands at 86% completion, Raipur Phase-II (1,600 MW) at 54%, and Raigarh Phase-II (1,600 MW) at 47% as of March 31, 2026.

However, geopolitical issues affecting labour and LPG availability have delayed the Mahan project. The first unit is now expected in Q4 FY27 or Q1 FY28, with the second unit six months later. Consequently, around 1.3 GW is expected in FY27, with 1.6 GW pushed to FY28.

Beyond India, Adani Power recently incorporated an SPV in Bhutan for a 520 MW hydro power plant. Khyalia said the company will evaluate international projects in thermal, hydro and transmission.
