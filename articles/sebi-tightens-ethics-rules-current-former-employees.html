Sebi Tightens Ethics Rules for Current and Former Employees

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

Sebi is closing the revolving door.

The Securities and Exchange Board of India has tightened ethics rules for its current and former employees, imposing stricter restrictions on post-employment activities, conflicts of interest, and the use of insider information. The new rules are designed to prevent regulatory capture — the phenomenon where former officials leverage their connections and knowledge for private gain — and to maintain public confidence in the integrity of the regulatory process.

The revolving door is a global problem. In financial markets around the world, former regulators routinely join the firms they once oversaw, bringing with them deep knowledge of regulatory processes, personal relationships with current officials, and the ability to navigate compliance requirements more effectively than outsiders. This creates an uneven playing field and raises concerns about whether regulation is being designed to benefit the industry rather than protect investors.

Sebi's new rules impose cooling-off periods. Former employees will now face extended restrictions on joining entities they regulated or investigated during their tenure. The cooling-off periods are designed to reduce the incentive for regulatory decisions to be influenced by the prospect of future employment and to ensure that officials make decisions based on public interest rather than personal career advancement.

Confidentiality rules are being strengthened. Former Sebi employees will face stricter prohibitions on sharing confidential information obtained during their tenure. This includes non-public information about investigations, enforcement actions, and policy deliberations. The rules aim to prevent former officials from monetising their access to sensitive regulatory intelligence.

Current employees face enhanced disclosure requirements. Sebi staff will now need to disclose their financial holdings, investments, and relationships with regulated entities more extensively. The disclosures are designed to identify potential conflicts of interest before they influence regulatory decisions and to create a paper trail that can be audited if questions arise.

The move comes amid broader scrutiny of regulatory independence. In recent years, there have been concerns about the relationship between Sebi and the entities it regulates, including allegations of regulatory forbearance, delayed enforcement actions, and inconsistent application of rules. While these concerns are not unique to India, the tightening of ethics rules is a step toward addressing them.
