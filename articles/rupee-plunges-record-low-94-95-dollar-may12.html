Rupee Plunges to Record Low of 94.95 Per Dollar on May 12

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Indian rupee plunged to a record low of 94.95 against the US dollar on May 12, 2026
  • 02/The rupee depreciation was driven by sustained foreign fund outflows, rising crude oil prices, and West Asia geopolitical tensions
  • 03/The Reserve Bank of India is expected to intervene to stabilize the currency amid macroeconomic concerns

The Indian rupee plunged to a record low of 94.95 against the US dollar on May 12, 2026. This marked a fresh all-time low for the domestic currency amid sustained selling pressure.

The rupee depreciation was driven by multiple factors. Foreign Institutional Investors offloaded equities worth ₹8,437.56 crore on Monday, May 11. Unabated foreign fund outflows pressured the currency lower.

Brent crude, the global oil benchmark, traded higher at $105.2 per barrel. India imports over 80% of its crude oil requirements. Rising oil prices widen the current account deficit and put additional pressure on the rupee.

West Asia geopolitical tensions also weighed on sentiment. U.S. President Donald Trump said the ceasefire with Iran was at its "weakest" on May 11. He rejected Tehran's peace proposal as "totally unacceptable."

The broader markets reflected this stress. The BSE Sensex fell 525 points to 75,489 in early trade on May 12. The NSE Nifty dropped 164 points to 23,651.

Analysts expect the Reserve Bank of India to intervene in the forex market to stabilize the currency. The RBI has been actively managing rupee volatility through dollar sales and forward market operations.

The rupee weakness also impacts import costs and inflation. A weaker currency makes imported goods more expensive, potentially affecting the RBI's inflation management efforts.
