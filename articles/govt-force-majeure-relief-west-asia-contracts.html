Govt Allows Force Majeure Relief for Contracts Hit by West Asia Disruptions

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Centre recognises West Asia situation as Force Majeure under procurement rules via April 29 memorandum
  • 02/Contractual obligations due on or after February 28, 2026 may be extended by two to four months without penalties
  • 03/Relief applies only to parties not in default as of February 27, 2026; drone sector among key beneficiaries

The Centre has recognised the ongoing West Asia situation as a Force Majeure event. An Office Memorandum issued by the Ministry of Finance's Department of Expenditure on April 29, 2026 allows extension of contractual timelines.

Contractual obligations due on or after February 28, 2026 may be extended by two to four months. No penalties or additional costs will be imposed on affected suppliers and contractors.

The extension period will be determined by procuring entities on a case-by-case basis. They will examine the admissibility of Force Majeure claims before granting relief.

Delays arising from Force Majeure events should not attract liquidated damages. This applies where suppliers, contractors, consultants, or service providers are not at fault.

However, Force Majeure invocation is valid only for parties not in default as of February 27, 2026. Relief applies solely to non-performance directly linked to West Asia disruptions.

The clarification follows representations by major industry bodies to the Finance Ministry. Supply chain disruptions had particularly affected the drone sector.

"The government's decision to permit invocation of Force Majeure in light of the ongoing West Asia situation is a timely step for the drone and defence manufacturing ecosystem." - Smit Shah, President, Drone Federation India.

The industry has faced component shortages, logistics delays and tighter export controls over recent months. The move provides predictability for manufacturers and supports efforts to build a self-reliant drone ecosystem.

WHICH GOVERNMENT DEPARTMENT ISSUED THE FORCE MAJEURE CLARIFICATION?
THE MINISTRY OF FINANCE'S DEPARTMENT OF EXPENDITURE ISSUED AN OFFICE MEMORANDUM ON APRIL 29, 2026, ALLOWING PROCURING ENTITIES TO INVOKE FORCE MAJEURE FOR CONTRACTS IMPACTED BY WEST ASIA DISRUPTIONS.
WHO IS ELIGIBLE FOR THE FORCE MAJEURE RELIEF?
FORCE MAJEURE INVOCATION IS VALID ONLY WHERE PARTIES TO THE CONTRACT WERE NOT ALREADY IN DEFAULT OF THEIR OBLIGATIONS AS OF FEBRUARY 27, 2026. RELIEF APPLIES ONLY TO NON-PERFORMANCE DIRECTLY ATTRIBUTABLE TO WEST ASIA DISRUPTIONS.
WHICH SECTORS ARE EXPECTED TO BENEFIT FROM THIS RELIEF?
THE DRONE SECTOR IS AMONG THE KEY BENEFICIARIES. INDUSTRY BODIES HAD REPRESENTED TO THE MINISTRY OF FINANCE REGARDING SUPPLY CHAIN DISRUPTIONS, COMPONENT SHORTAGES, AND LOGISTICS DELAYS AFFECTING GOVERNMENT CONTRACTS.
HOW WILL THE EXTENSION PERIOD BE DETERMINED?
THE EXTENSION PERIOD WILL BE DETERMINED BY PROCURING ENTITIES ON A CASE-BY-CASE BASIS AFTER EXAMINING THE ADMISSIBILITY OF FORCE MAJEURE CLAIMS. DELAYS SHOULD NOT ATTRACT LIQUIDATED DAMAGES WHERE SUPPLIERS ARE NOT AT FAULT.
