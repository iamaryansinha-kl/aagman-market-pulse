El Nino Strength Uncertain in 2026, Australian Weather Bureau Warns

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Australian Bureau of Meteorology says El Nino forecasts range from weak-to-moderate to potentially strong event in 2026
  • 02/Nino-3.4 index stands at 0.23°C as of April 26, with 0.5°C warming over the past fortnight
  • 03/Models predict El Nino thresholds reached between May and July, with impacts on global agriculture and commodity markets

The Australian Bureau of Meteorology warns uncertainty prevails over El Nino strength in 2026. Forecasts range from weak-to-moderate to potentially strong.

The outlook depends on warming in the central tropical Pacific, measured by the relative Nino3.4 index. All models forecast continued warming in coming months.

The latest Nino-3.4 index value for the week ending April 26, 2026, is 0.23°C. This sits within the ENSO-neutral range of minus 0.80 to plus 0.80 degrees Celsius.

The index has warmed 0.5°C over the past fortnight. Sub-surface warming indicates sea surface temperatures will likely rise further in coming weeks.

Model forecasts vary on timing. Some suggest El Nino development as early as May, while others show slower warming with thresholds not reached until July.

Sea surface temperature forecasts for May through July indicate warmer-than-average waters around much of Australia. The Tasman Sea could see temperatures up to 2°C above average.

"A strong El Nino signal in the Nino3.4 region doesn't necessarily mean strong impacts. ENSO is only one of many factors that can influence seasonal weather," the Bureau said.

The 30-day Southern Oscillation Index stands at minus 10.6 as of April 26. The 60-day and 90-day values are plus 2.5 and plus 6.2, respectively.
