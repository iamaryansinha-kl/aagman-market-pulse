Unilever CEO Sees Indian Market Competition Shortage Boosting Volume Growth

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Unilever CEO Fernando Fernández says supply chain disruptions and rising crude oil prices are constraining local Indian competitors
  • 02/HUL reported 6% volume growth in the March quarter, with highest-ever share in laundry powders in India during Q1/2026
  • 03/India remains Unilever's second-largest market with 12-14% of global sales; quick commerce sales doubled in FY26

Unilever PLC CEO Fernando Fernández says supply chain disruptions and rising crude oil prices are creating a shortage of local competition in India. This constraint may support the company's volume growth in home care categories.

"We are seeing some shortage in some local players, particularly in India and Southeast Asia," said Fernández during the earnings call. The shortage can support volumes and make pricing pass-through easier.

Hindustan Unilever Ltd (HUL) (NSE: HINDUNILVR) reported 6 per cent volume growth in the March quarter. Chief Financial Officer Srinivas Phatak termed the performance as of "very high order."

In Q1/2026, Unilever achieved its highest-ever share in laundry powders in India. The company is also sharply increasing its position in the fast-growing liquid detergent segment.

"Classically, in home care, inflation works in our favour," said Phatak. HUL's diverse portfolio allows it to cater to different price points while smaller players face supply and cash constraints.

India contributes 12-14 per cent to Unilever's global sales. It remains the company's second-largest market by revenue after the United States.

HUL has doubled its quick commerce sales in FY26. E-commerce turnover grew 25 per cent as the company invests in omni-channel capabilities.

The company has expanded its general trade outlet coverage by 200,000 stores, reaching 2.3 million outlets. Unilever reported 12.6 billion euros in Q1/2026 turnover with 2.9 per cent global volume growth.

HINDUSTAN UNILEVER LTD (HUL) REPORTED 6 PER CENT VOLUME GROWTH IN THE MARCH QUARTER. CHIEF FINANCIAL OFFICER SRINIVAS PHATAK TERMED THE PERFORMANCE AS OF A "VERY HIGH ORDER."

INDIA CONTRIBUTES APPROXIMATELY 12-14 PER CENT TO UNILEVER'S TOTAL GLOBAL SALES. IT IS THE COMPANY'S SECOND-LARGEST MARKET BY REVENUE AFTER THE UNITED STATES.
