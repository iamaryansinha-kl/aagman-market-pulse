EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Brent crude rose 81 cents to $105.83 and WTI gained 97 cents to $99.23, reversing a 5.6% drop triggered by Trump's final-stage Iran talk comments
  • 02/The US withdrew a record 10 million barrels from its Strategic Petroleum Reserve last week, while commercial crude inventories fell 7.9 million barrels to 445 million
  • 03/Iran announced a new Persian Gulf Strait Authority and a controlled maritime zone, keeping the Strait of Hormuz largely closed despite the April ceasefire

Brent crude futures rose 81 cents, or 0.77%, to $105.83 a barrel early Thursday. US West Texas Intermediate gained 97 cents, or 0.99%, to $99.23. Both benchmarks had plunged over 5.6% on Wednesday after President Trump said Iran negotiations were in final stages. He also threatened further attacks if Tehran refused a peace deal. Iran warned against more strikes and unveiled a new Persian Gulf Strait Authority. It declared a controlled maritime zone over the Strait of Hormuz, which carried about 20% of global oil and LNG shipments before the war started on February 28. Traffic through Hormuz remains heavily restricted despite an April ceasefire. The US has blockaded Iran's coastline, keeping most energy shipments blocked. The US Energy Information Administration reported a record draw of nearly 10 million barrels from the Strategic Petroleum Reserve last week. Commercial crude inventories fell 7.9 million barrels to 445 million, far exceeding the 2.9 million-barrel decline analysts had expected. Gasoline inventories fell by 1.5 million barrels, while distillate stocks rose by 372,000 barrels. The mixed refined product picture suggests uneven demand recovery. "The sharp drop in oil prices appears to be pricing in the possibility of a breakthrough in the talks," said Yang An, analyst at Haitong Futures. "However, if Trump insists on making no concessions to Iran, an agreement seems unlikely, and the final outcome of the negotiations could reverse sharply." "The drawdown in oil inventories will make it difficult for oil prices to remain low," said Mingyu Gao, chief researcher for energy and chemicals at China Futures. "With the Strait of Hormuz blocked, global refined-product and onshore crude inventories are expected to fall below their lowest levels for this time of year in the past five years by late May and late June."

HOW MUCH DID US OIL INVENTORIES FALL?
THE US ENERGY INFORMATION ADMINISTRATION REPORTED A RECORD WITHDRAWAL OF NEARLY 10 MILLION BARRELS FROM THE STRATEGIC PETROLEUM RESERVE. COMMERCIAL CRUDE INVENTORIES FELL BY 7.9 MILLION BARRELS TO 445 MILLION, VERSUS ANALYST EXPECTATIONS OF A 2.9 MILLION-BARREL DRAW.
WHAT IS THE STATUS OF THE STRAIT OF HORMUZ?
THE STRAIT OF HORMUZ REMAINS LARGELY CLOSED DESPITE AN APRIL CEASEFIRE. IRAN ANNOUNCED A NEW PERSIAN GULF STRAIT AUTHORITY AND A CONTROLLED MARITIME ZONE. THE STRAIT PREVIOUSLY CARRIED ABOUT 20% OF GLOBAL OIL AND LNG SHIPMENTS.
WHAT DID PRESIDENT TRUMP SAY ABOUT IRAN NEGOTIATIONS?
TRUMP SAID NEGOTIATIONS WITH IRAN WERE IN THEIR FINAL STAGES BUT THREATENED FURTHER ATTACKS IF TEHRAN DID NOT AGREE TO A PEACE DEAL. ANALYSTS WARN THAT WITHOUT CONCESSIONS FROM BOTH SIDES, A BREAKTHROUGH REMAINS UNLIKELY.
WHAT IS THE NEAR-TERM OUTLOOK FOR OIL PRICES?
ANALYSTS AT CHINA FUTURES EXPECT GLOBAL REFINED-PRODUCT AND ONSHORE CRUDE INVENTORIES TO FALL BELOW FIVE-YEAR LOWS BY LATE MAY AND LATE JUNE DUE TO THE BLOCKED STRAIT. THE RECORD INVENTORY DRAWS SUGGEST SUPPLY TIGHTNESS WILL SUPPORT PRICES EVEN IF TALKS PROGRESS.
