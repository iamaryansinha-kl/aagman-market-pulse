Syngene Q4 FY26 Profit Drops 16% to ₹153 Cr on Exceptional Charges

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Syngene International Q4 FY26 PAT dropped 16% YoY to ₹153 crore, weighed down by ₹25 crore in exceptional employee termination costs
  • 02/Revenue grew 2% to ₹1,037 crore; full-year revenue rose 3% with 25% EBITDA margin, in line with revised guidance
  • 03/Leadership changes ahead: Kiran Mazumdar-Shaw becomes Executive Chairperson; Siddharth Mittal takes over as MD & CEO from July 1

Syngene International Limited (NSE: SYNGENE) reported a 16% year-on-year decline in profit after tax to ₹153 crore for Q4 FY26. The company recognised ₹25 crore as exceptional expenses, net of tax, linked to employee termination benefits.

Revenue for the quarter stood at ₹1,037 crore, up 2% from the year-ago period. The modest growth reflects the specific impact from a single large-molecule biologics client.

For the full year FY26, revenue from operations grew 3%. The company delivered an EBITDA margin of 25%, meeting its revised annual guidance.

Operating margin excluding other income contracted to 29.27% in Q4 FY26, down from 33.75% in Q4 FY25. PAT margin fell to 14.27% from 18.01% a year earlier.

"The overall numbers reflect the specific impact from a single large-molecule biologics client, with the underlying business showing steady momentum," said Managing Director and CEO Peter Bains. Full-year performance was in line with revised guidance, he added.

Kiran Mazumdar-Shaw has transitioned from Non-Executive Chairperson to Executive Chairperson effective April 1, 2026, for a five-year term. Siddharth Mittal will succeed Peter Bains as Managing Director and CEO from July 1, 2026, joining from Biocon Limited.

The company holds a net cash position with zero long-term debt. It operates 2.2 million sq ft of facilities with over 6,000 scientists across its integrated research and manufacturing network.

Syngene continued investing in new capabilities during the year. Focus areas include peptides, antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs), and AI-driven digital platforms aimed at enhancing speed and productivity for global pharma clients.
