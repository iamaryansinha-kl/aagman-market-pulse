US PCE Inflation Hits 4.1% in May, First Above 4% Since 2023; Fed September Hike Odds at 80%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/US PCE inflation jumped to 4.1% in May, the highest in three years and first reading above 4% since April 2023.
  • 02/Core PCE inflation rose to 3.4% YoY; services inflation outpaced goods inflation, complicating the Fed's path back to 2%.
  • 03/CME FedWatch tool shows roughly 80% probability of a rate hike at the September 15-16 FOMC meeting.

US inflation accelerated further in May, breaking above 4% for the first time in three years. The personal consumption expenditures (PCE) price index surged 4.1% in the 12 months through May, the Commerce Department's Bureau of Economic Analysis reported on Thursday.

The April reading was unrevised at 3.8%. Economists polled by Reuters had forecast the 4.1% advance. The PCE price index climbed 0.4% over the month, matching April's increase.

Energy prices drove the headline jump. The Middle East conflict had boosted oil prices, pushing gasoline higher after Tehran took control of the Strait of Hormuz. On Thursday, oil prices fell to pre-war levels after the United States and Iran signed a preliminary peace deal.

"PCE price inflation remains too high and will keep the Fed on hold and mulling a potential rate hike at upcoming meetings," said Scott Anderson, chief U.S. economist at BMO Capital Markets. "Services inflation was even higher than goods inflation last month and will not be easily tamed by falling energy prices."

Excluding volatile food and energy components, core PCE inflation increased 3.4% year-on-year in May after rising 3.3% in April. Core PCE advanced 0.3% on a monthly basis, unchanged from the prior month.

The Federal Reserve tracks PCE inflation for its 2% target. The central bank last week held its benchmark overnight interest rate in the 3.50%-3.75% range. Updated quarterly projections showed policymakers expected to raise borrowing costs this year.

Financial markets are now pricing in aggressive tightening. CME Group's FedWatch tool showed roughly an 80% chance that the Fed will raise rates at the September 15-16 meeting. Both headline and core PCE inflation were last below 2% in early 2021.

Consumer spending remained resilient despite the inflation shock. Household outlays jumped 0.7% in May after rising 0.4% in April, supported by larger tax refunds and a stock market rally. Consumption accounts for more than two-thirds of economic activity.

Business investment also strengthened. Non-defense capital goods orders excluding aircraft, a proxy for business spending, increased 1.6% in May after declining 0.7% in April. GDP growth estimates for the second quarter run as high as a 3.0% annualized rate, up from 2.1% in Q1.
