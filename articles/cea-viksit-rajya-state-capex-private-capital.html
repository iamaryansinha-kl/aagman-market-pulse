Viksit Bharat Depends on Viksit Rajya: CEA Calls for More Private Capital, Higher State Capex

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

India's development push will be decided in the states, not just in Delhi. Chief Economic Adviser V Anantha Nageswaran told a conference of state finance ministers and officials in New Delhi that Viksit Bharat depends on Viksit Rajya, calling for significantly greater private-capital participation and higher state-level capital expenditure.

The message came at the closing session of a two-day Conference on Financing India's Journey towards Viksit Bharat, which brought together Union and state finance ministers, senior officials, economists, bankers and industry representatives.

Nageswaran outlined three priorities for states. First, create an enabling environment for private investment by ensuring land, power and logistics availability backed by effective single-window clearances. Second, improve the quality of investment proposals, with stronger project-preparation pipelines and credible reports that can access domestic and multilateral financing. Third, strengthen states' own capital expenditure despite fiscal constraints, including directing credit towards underserved districts with growth potential.

He endorsed a proposal by former Maharashtra additional chief secretary Sudhir Shrivava to raise state capital outlay from about 2.4% of GSDP currently to 3% by 2031-32.

The savings math is stark. In his keynote, 15th Finance Commission chairman N K Singh said India's gross domestic savings rate of around 34% of GDP needs to rise towards 38-40% to sustain the 7-8% growth that Viksit Bharat requires. Singh also called for state-wise debt sustainability assessments and greater fiscal transparency, including accounting for off-budget borrowings, guarantees, arrears and borrowings through state-owned entities. He suggested states use data analytics, artificial intelligence and machine learning with existing tax databases to identify compliance gaps and broaden the effective tax base.

The second day of the conference covered financing agricultural transformation, the energy transition, battery storage and pumped storage, carbon capture and a carbon-credit framework, alongside sessions on technology and measuring progress at the GSDP level. SBI chairman Challa Sreenivasulu Setty addressed a session on banking-sector sources of finance.

Working groups involving states will now take the discussions forward with sector-specific financing requirements and actionable recommendations. Nageswaran proposed making the conference an annual fixture, framing India's task as dual: sustaining the long-term 2047 journey while accelerating resource mobilisation over the next five years, when global financing opportunities remain available.

The framing matters. With central government capex already elevated, the marginal investment dollar increasingly has to come from states and private balance sheets, and the CEA's emphasis on project quality suggests Delhi sees preparation capacity, not funding, as the binding constraint.
