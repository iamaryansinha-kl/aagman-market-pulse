India Fertiliser Subsidy Bill May Jump 20% on West Asia Crisis

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Fertiliser ministry official Aparna Sharma said on April 27 that India's subsidy bill may rise 20% in FY2027 due to higher import costs from the West Asia crisis.
  • 02/India placed orders for a record 2.5 million metric tons of urea at $935-$959 per ton, nearly double the $508-$512 paid just two months ago.
  • 03/Peak fertiliser demand hits in June-July during the kharif sowing season, potentially tightening global supply further and raising future costs.

India's fertiliser subsidy bill is expected to rise by around 20% this financial year. Fertiliser ministry official Aparna Sharma announced the estimate on April 27, 2026. The increase is driven by surging global prices triggered by the West Asia crisis.

India's fertiliser subsidy for FY2026 was estimated at ₹1.87 trillion ($19.85 billion). A 20% increase would push the FY2027 bill above ₹2.2 trillion. This puts pressure on the government's budget allocation for the sector.

India, the world's largest urea importer, has placed orders for a record 2.5 million metric tons. The purchase price ranges from $935 to $959 per ton. This is nearly double the $508-$512 per ton paid just two months ago.

The record purchase accounts for roughly a quarter of India's annual urea imports of about 10 million metric tons. West Asia supplies roughly half of India's DAP and urea imports. Saudi Arabia is the largest DAP supplier and Oman is the biggest urea supplier.

Rising import costs will increase subsidies to fertiliser companies. These firms sell crop nutrients to farmers below market prices. The gap between market price and the subsidised rate is borne by the government.

CRISIL Ratings warned that supply chain disruptions could raise the government's subsidy burden by ₹200-250 billion. ICRA Research noted that the FY2027 budget estimates fertiliser subsidies at ₹1.7 lakh crore. Actual spending may exceed this figure significantly.

India holds 18 million metric tons of total fertiliser reserves against 13 million MT last year. Urea stocks stand at around 6 million MT. Demand will surge in June and July during the kharif sowing season for rice, corn, cotton and oilseeds.

The Iran conflict has disrupted global shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. Prime Minister Narendra Modi flagged the risks in Parliament on March 23, warning that trade routes and essential supplies are being affected.
