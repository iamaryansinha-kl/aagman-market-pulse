Oil Firms Lose ₹14/Litre on Petrol, ₹18 on Diesel as Crude Hits $125

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Oil marketing companies are losing ₹14 per litre on petrol and ₹18 per litre on diesel as retail pump prices stay capped while crude has surged to $120-125 per barrel
  • 02/Icra projects LPG under-recoveries of ₹80,000 crore in FY2027 and fertiliser subsidy to balloon to ₹2.05-2.25 lakh crore, above the budgeted ₹1.71 lakh crore
  • 03/Strait of Hormuz supply disruptions are driving the crisis; any relief depends on easing geopolitical tensions and normalisation of global supply chains

Indian oil marketing companies are selling petrol at a loss of ₹14 per litre and diesel at ₹18 per litre. Retail pump prices remain capped even as global crude has surged past $120 per barrel.

The losses stem from the gap between frozen retail fuel rates and elevated international crude prices. Two months ago, crude traded at $70-72 per barrel before the West Asia crisis escalated.

Icra estimates LPG under-recoveries could reach ₹80,000 crore in FY2027 if current trends persist. The fertiliser subsidy burden is projected to rise to ₹2.05-2.25 lakh crore, well above the budgeted ₹1.71 lakh crore.

Supply disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz have tightened fuel availability. The strait handles around 20 per cent of global oil and LNG trade.

"The stable pump prices for auto fuels amid elevated crude oil prices are impacting the profitability of the oil marketing companies," said Prashant Vasisht, Senior Vice President and Co-Group Head at Icra.

Urea pool prices have risen to about $19 per MMBtu in April 2026 from $13 before the crisis. Fertiliser players face sharp cost escalation from higher sulphur, ammonia and natural gas prices.

Icra's outlook on the crude oil refining segment remains stable. However, the outlook on fuel retailing, fertiliser, basic chemicals and petrochemicals remains negative.

Chemical and polymer prices have surged amid disrupted trade flows and higher fuel costs. Icra expects demand to normalise once inventory buildup subsides.

City gas distributors face margin pressure from rising gas prices and currency depreciation. CNG margins are expected to weaken as cost increases are only partially passed on.

EY has warned that India's GDP growth may slip to 6 per cent if the Indian crude basket averages $120 per barrel in FY2027.
