India-US Sign Critical Minerals Pact to Cut China Dependence

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/India and the US signed a bilateral critical minerals and rare earths agreement on May 26, 2026, during the Quad Foreign Ministers' meeting in Delhi
  • 02/China controls 90% of global rare earth processing and 94% of magnet production, leaving supply chains exposed to export controls
  • 03/India holds 6-8% of global rare earth reserves but produces under 1% of output, with a Rs 7,280 crore magnet manufacturing plan underway

India and the United States signed a critical minerals and rare earths agreement on May 26, 2026. The pact was inked during the Quad Foreign Ministers' meeting in Delhi. EAM S Jaishankar and US Secretary of State Marco Rubio were present.

The framework covers mining, processing, recycling and related investment across the full supply chain. It aims to secure resources for semiconductors, electric vehicles, clean energy and defence technologies.

China controls roughly 70% of global rare earth mining and 90% of processing capacity. It also dominates 94% of permanent magnet manufacturing. This concentration has triggered alarm after Beijing imposed export controls on multiple elements.

India holds an estimated 6-8% of global rare earth reserves at roughly 6.9 million tonnes. The country produces less than 1% of total global output. It currently imports 60-80% by value of rare earth permanent magnets from China.

The groundwork for this deal was laid on February 4 at the Critical Minerals Forum in Washington. India signed on to the Pax Silica initiative later that month. US Ambassador Sergio Gor flagged a "big announcement" at the India Today Conclave on March 13.

India's Budget 2026-27 allocated Rs 7,280 crore for rare earth permanent magnet manufacturing. It targets 6,000 metric tonnes of annual capacity. Dedicated rare earth corridors are planned in Odisha, Kerala, Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu.

"We are two countries with strategic interests in ensuring reliable long-term access to critical minerals," said Marco Rubio. The agreement signals deepening India-US cooperation on supply chain security amid rising geopolitical competition.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT IS THE INDIA-US CRITICAL MINERALS AGREEMENT SIGNED ON MAY 26, 2026?
INDIA AND THE UNITED STATES SIGNED A BILATERAL FRAMEWORK ON CRITICAL MINERALS AND RARE EARTHS TO SECURE SUPPLY CHAINS FOR MINING, PROCESSING, RECYCLING AND INVESTMENT. THE PACT WAS SIGNED DURING THE QUAD FOREIGN MINISTERS' MEETING IN DELHI.
WHY IS THIS AGREEMENT IMPORTANT GIVEN CHINA'S ROLE IN RARE EARTHS?
CHINA CONTROLS APPROXIMATELY 70% OF GLOBAL RARE EARTH MINING, 90% OF PROCESSING CAPACITY AND 94% OF PERMANENT MAGNET MANUFACTURING. BEIJING HAS USED EXPORT CONTROLS AS LEVERAGE, EXPOSING GLOBAL SUPPLY CHAINS FOR SEMICONDUCTORS, EVS AND DEFENCE.
WHAT RARE EARTH RESERVES DOES INDIA HOLD?
INDIA HOLDS AN ESTIMATED 6-8% OF GLOBAL RARE EARTH RESERVES, WITH ABOUT 6.9 MILLION TONNES OF RARE EARTH OXIDES FROM 13.15 MILLION TONNES OF MONAZITE DEPOSITS ACROSS KERALA, TAMIL NADU, ODISHA, ANDHRA PRADESH AND OTHER STATES.
WHAT DOMESTIC STEPS HAS INDIA TAKEN TO REDUCE RARE EARTH IMPORT DEPENDENCE?
THE UNION BUDGET 2026-27 ALLOCATED RS 7,280 CRORE FOR RARE EARTH PERMANENT MAGNET MANUFACTURING WITH A TARGET OF 6,000 METRIC TONNES ANNUAL CAPACITY. DEDICATED RARE EARTH CORRIDORS ARE PLANNED IN ODISHA, KERALA, ANDHRA PRADESH AND TAMIL NADU.
WHAT DOES THE PACT COVER BEYOND MINING?
THE AGREEMENT COVERS THE ENTIRE SUPPLY CHAIN INCLUDING MINING, PROCESSING, RECYCLING AND RELATED INVESTMENT. IT TARGETS RESOURCES FOR SEMICONDUCTORS, ELECTRIC VEHICLES, CLEAN ENERGY SYSTEMS AND ADVANCED DEFENCE TECHNOLOGIES.
