Korea's Kospi Hits 8% Lower Circuit, Trading Halted as Tech Selloff Deepens

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

South Korea's stock market has hit the emergency brakes.

The Kospi index plunged 8% in a single session, triggering the lower circuit breaker and halting trading for the first time in years, as panic selling in technology stocks turned a correction into a full-blown market crisis. The index, which had already fallen 25% from its June peak, is now in uncharted territory, with investors unsure where the bottom lies.

The 8% circuit breaker is a rare event. South Korean stock exchanges have circuit breakers that halt trading when the market moves beyond a certain threshold in either direction. An 8% decline triggering the lower circuit suggests that the selling was so intense and so widespread that it overwhelmed the market's ability to function normally. Trading halts are designed to give investors time to calm down and reassess, but they can also deepen panic when they resume.

Semiconductor stocks are at the epicentre. SK Hynix, which had debuted on US markets with strong gains just days earlier, has seen its shares collapse as the AI demand narrative unravels. Samsung Electronics, the world's largest memory chip maker, has also been hammered, with its shares falling sharply alongside the broader tech sector. The two companies together account for a significant portion of the Kospi's market capitalisation, so their decline drags the entire index down.

The AI demand narrative is being questioned. The story that had driven the semiconductor boom — that AI infrastructure buildout would create insatiable demand for memory chips, GPUs, and high-bandwidth memory — is now facing its first real test. Analysts are questioning whether the spending plans of hyperscalers like Google, Microsoft, Amazon, and Meta are sustainable, and whether the supply buildout is running ahead of actual demand. The market is voting with its feet, and the vote is decidedly negative.

Global investors are in full retreat. Foreign portfolio investors, who had been major buyers of Korean stocks during the AI boom, are now selling aggressively. The $46 billion exodus from emerging market equities in June is partly driven by the Korean selloff, and the circuit breaker suggests that the outflows are accelerating, not slowing. The stronger won, which reduces returns for dollar-based investors, is adding to the pressure.

The spillover effects are global. The Korean semiconductor rout is affecting Taiwan, Japan, and the US technology sector. NVIDIA, AMD, and other AI chipmakers have seen their shares decline as the market reassesses the demand picture. The rout is also affecting Indian IT services companies, which had benefited from AI-related spending, though the Indian market has so far been more resilient than its Asian peers.
