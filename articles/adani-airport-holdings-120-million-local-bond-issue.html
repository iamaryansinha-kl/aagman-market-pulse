Adani Airport Holdings Raises $120 Million via 8.96% Local Bond Issue

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Adani Airport Holdings Ltd raised $120 million, about Rs 1,000 crore, through a three-year domestic bond issue carrying an 8.96% annual coupon, payable quarterly.
  • 02/Proceeds will fund capital expenditure across Adani's airport network; the issue follows an earlier plan for a bond of up to Rs 1,500 crore to refinance debt and fund expansion.
  • 03/Watch the pace of group deleveraging and the FY27 traffic ramp at Navi Mumbai, where daily passengers are targeted to rise to around 50,000 by end-2026.

Adani Airport Holdings Ltd (AAHL) has raised $120 million, roughly Rs 1,000 crore, through a local bond issue. The bonds have a three-year tenor with a coupon rate of 8.96% per annum, payable quarterly.

The company said proceeds will be deployed towards capital expenditure across Adani's airport network. The funds will also support its broader growth plans, according to the issue disclosure.

CONTEXT: This is a debt funding transaction, not an equity event. The Adani group has been steadily returning to domestic bond markets through 2025-26 as group-level deleveraging progressed, and AAHL's issue fits that pattern of tapping local credit rather than issuing fresh equity.

The issue follows an earlier reported plan from September 2026. Adani Airport Holdings was then said to be planning a domestic bond issue of up to Rs 1,500 crore, aimed at refinancing debt and supporting airport expansion.

The final issue size of roughly Rs 1,000 crore comes in below that upper limit. That suggests the company chose to calibrate the raise to near-term funding needs rather than max out the announced programme.

Indian companies are increasingly turning to the local bond market as bank credit remains expensive. For large infrastructure borrowers like AAHL, the listed corporate bond route offers pricing discovery and a broader investor base than term loans.

AAHL already has listed debt on its books. One example is a 9.95% senior secured exchange-listed bond due June 12, 2028.

On that June 2028 instrument, AAHL's leverage stood at a debt/EBITDA of around 3.93x in FY26. That ratio gives bond investors a reference point for how the new 8.96% paper stacks up against existing obligations.

The new coupon of 8.96% is noticeably below the 9.95% on the 2028 bond. For a comparable credit, that gap points to either improved pricing conditions in the domestic bond market or a modestly better credit perception as deleveraging advances.

AAHL operates India's largest private airport portfolio. Its network spans Mumbai's Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj International Airport, Delhi, Ahmedabad, Lucknow, Mangaluru, Jaipur, Guwahati and Thiruvananthapuram.

The operating base behind that portfolio is substantial. AAHL handled around 95 million passengers in FY26, and brokerage Jefferies expects traffic to climb to roughly 150 million by FY31, implying a 9% CAGR driven largely by the phased ramp-up of Navi Mumbai International Airport.

Mumbai airport, India's second-busiest, recorded over 13.6 million passengers in Q1 of FY26. The airport is capacity constrained, with a phased terminal refurbishment planned over the next three to five years to lift capacity from around 55 million to around 60 million passengers.

Navi Mumbai International Airport, commissioned in December 2025, is the growth lever. It currently handles around 20,000 passengers a day and nearly 150 flight movements, with management targeting roughly 50,000 daily passengers by the end of 2026, and international operations beginning July 15, 2026.

Across its network, AAHL handles about 23% of India's passenger traffic, 21% of air traffic movements and 29% of air cargo. That scale underpins the cash flows servicing its debt.

IMPACT: For fixed-income investors, the issue extends AAHL's maturity ladder at a sub-9% coupon, cheaper than its outstanding 9.95% paper, while quarterly interest payments suit yield-focused accounts. For the broader market, a successful placement by a flagship Adani borrower reinforces the reopening of domestic bond access for the group after the deleveraging phase of 2024-25, though leverage of 3.93x debt/EBITDA and the execution risk of Navi Mumbai's ramp remain the variables to monitor.
