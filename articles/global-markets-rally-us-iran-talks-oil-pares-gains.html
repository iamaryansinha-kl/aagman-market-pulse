Global Markets Rally as US-Iran Talks Resume, Oil Pares Gains

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/S&P 500 futures climb 0.5% and Nasdaq 100 futures rise 0.6% as US and Iran agree to halt strikes and resume talks in Qatar this week
  • 02/Brent crude pares gains to trade around $72.10 after jumping as much as 1.9% to over $73 amid Middle East conflict escalation since Thursday
  • 03/South Korea's Kospi falls 1.6% ahead of major growth strategy unveiling; Samsung and SK Group may invest over $1.3 trillion in the next 10 years

US equity-index futures climbed after reports that the US and Iran backed away from fresh conflict escalation. Both sides agreed to halt strikes and meet in Qatar this week.

S&P 500 Index contracts rose 0.5%. Nasdaq 100 futures gained 0.6%. The gains came after Axios reported the ceasefire plan, citing an unidentified US official.

Asian shares slipped 0.2% in early trading. South Korea's Kospi Index, the world's best-performing major gauge this year, fell 1.6%. Investors focused on Monday's growth strategy unveiling.

Brent crude jumped as much as 1.9% to over $73 per barrel. It later pared gains to trade around $72.10. The Middle East conflict had intensified since Thursday.

Iran struck a container ship, a Qatari oil vessel, and military bases in Kuwait and Bahrain. The US launched multiple retaliatory strikes. Tensions remain high despite the Qatar talks.

"The stock market seems to believe that President Trump has no choice but to make concessions as the midterm elections approach," said Shoji Hirakawa, chief global strategist at Tokai Tokyo Intelligence Lab.

Treasury futures edged lower with Australian and New Zealand bonds. Higher oil prices rekindled inflation concerns. The dollar held steady against major peers, while gold dropped 0.8% to $4,055 an ounce.

Samsung Electronics and SK Group are set to announce major investment plans. Their combined investments may total over $1.3 trillion in the next 10 years, Korea Economic Daily reported.
