Crude At $65, Discretionary Boom: Helios CEO Irani's Market Calls

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Helios Mutual Fund CEO Dinshaw Irani predicts crude oil falling to $65 per barrel by year-end, creating a structural surplus for India
  • 02/Irani backs discretionary consumption and digital-first companies over FMCG, calling Indian IT valuations a trap at mid-teens PE multiples
  • 03/RBI and government policy moves could attract $70-90 billion in foreign flows, stabilizing the rupee and boosting equity markets

Dinshaw Irani, CEO of Helios Mutual Fund, expects crude oil prices to fall to around $65 per barrel by the end of 2026. India paid roughly the same average per barrel last year.

The US is adding 5 million barrels per day to global supply. Iran is adding another 3 million, creating an 8 million barrel daily surplus against a previous 14 million deficit through the Strait of Hormuz.

India imports roughly 5 million barrels of oil daily. The price drop would ease both the current account deficit and pressure on the rupee.

Irani points to RBI and government policy moves as catalysts for rupee stability. FCNR(B) deposit incentives, removal of withholding tax on G-Sec interest, and capital gains exemptions on government bonds could draw $70-90 billion in additional foreign flows.

"All this has made the rupee fairly stable, which is what international investors are looking at," Irani told ET Now.

On equities, Irani is avoiding FMCG. He calls valuations too stretched for a category delivering only high-single to low-double digit growth. "Just because per capita incomes go up doesn't mean we'll take extra baths a day," he said.

Discretionary consumption is Irani's preferred bet. He is bullish on digital-first companies targeting Gen Z and Gen Alpha consumers, who make up over two-thirds of India's workforce.

Irani also sees opportunity in retail, consumer-facing NBFCs, wealth management, hospitality, and quality healthcare. India has fewer than 70,000 quality hospital rooms, signaling structural undersupply.

Irani is wary of Indian IT stocks, comparing them unfavorably to Cognizant at 6-8x PE in the US. He questions why Indian IT should command mid-teens multiples when the US is being more prudent.

On private banks, Helios is holding but not adding aggressively. The FCNR(B) relief has eased liability-side pressure, but PSU banks remain formidable competitors on the asset side.
