DP World Scales Up India-UAE Trade with 1,500 Trucks Daily After Hormuz Blockade

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/DP World has scaled inland cargo flows to 1,500 trucks per day from Khor Fakkan and Fujairah ports as the Strait of Hormuz remains blocked.
  • 02/India-UAE bilateral trade reached approximately $100 billion in FY2025, making UAE India's largest West Asia trading partner.
  • 03/Exporters face high freight rates and erratic shipping; SCI is considering feeder services to Khor Fakkan to ease costs.

DP World has ramped up inland transport to 1,500 trucks per day carrying bonded cargo. The trucks move goods from Khor Fakkan and Fujairah ports in the UAE to their final destinations. The scaling follows the ongoing blockade of the Strait of Hormuz.

"Our co-ordinated response across connected gateways is enabling cargo to move through multi modal corridors," said Ahmad Yousef Al-Hassan, CEO and managing director, DP World GCC. "We have prioritised transport of food, pharmaceuticals and other critical items."

The company has deployed additional cold chain infrastructure including a reefer pit stop. This maintains desired temperatures for refrigerated units during transit. Coordination with customs and regional authorities is ensuring supply chain resilience.

Shipping lines are now operating services to alternate ports like Khor Fakkan, Fujairah, Sohar and Salalah. From these ports goods are carried inland to Jebel Ali which remains the main UAE hub for customs clearance and distribution.

UAE is India's largest trading partner in the West Asia region. Annual bilateral trade volume stood at around $100 billion in FY 2025. Trade has surged following the signing of the Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement in 2022.

While alternate supply routes have been charted exporters are complaining of high freight rates and erratic shipping services. India's commerce ministry is working with exporters to streamline trade flows.

Earlier this month commerce minister Piyush Goyal held discussion with UAE's minister for foreign trade Thani bin Ahmed Al Zeyoudi. Both sides stressed the need for co-ordinated efforts for smooth trade operations.

"We have made a representation to the government about the challenges," said Ajay Sahai, Director General and CEO of Federation of Indian Export Organisations. "Shipping Corporation of India is considering feeder services to Khor Fakkan in the UAE and that could help in lowering freight rates for our exporters."

Maersk, world's second largest container line, said it has adjusted its network. It has introduced alternate gateway ports like Khor Fakkan, Salalah, Sohar, Jeddah and Aqaba.

"This has ensured continuity of business for reefers and essential commodities," said Bhavik Mota, Maersk's Director (regional ocean management, Indian sub-continent, Middle East and Africa). "Leveraging on our intermodal capabilities we have also ensured connectivity into Jebel Ali via Khor Fakkan."
