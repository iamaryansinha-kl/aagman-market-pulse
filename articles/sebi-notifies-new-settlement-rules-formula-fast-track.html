Sebi Settlement Rules 2026: New Formula, Rs 10 Lakh Fast-Track Route

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Sebi has notified new settlement rules dated October 6, 2026, with a revised formula that separates the settlement amount from disgorgement of wrongful gains
  • 02/The base amount is linked to the minimum penalty prescribed for the violation and adjusted for stage, gravity, aggravating and mitigating factors, and legal costs
  • 03/A fast-track route covers cases with settlement amounts up to Rs 10 lakh, moving them directly from the internal committee to a panel of whole-time members

The Securities and Exchange Board of India has notified new settlement regulations. A notification dated October 6, 2026 introduces a revised formula for calculating settlement amounts and a fast-track route for small cases.

The headline change is structural. Settlement terms will now comprise three distinct components: the settlement amount, disgorgement of wrongful gains wherever applicable, and remedial and regulatory terms.

The third component was earlier referred to as non-monetary terms. The new framework renames it and places it on a formal footing alongside the two monetary components.

Until now, wrongful gains were baked into the settlement calculation. They were then effectively counted again while computing settlement terms, meaning an entity could pay twice for the same gain.

The new rules end that double counting. Wrongful gains, loss avoided or loss caused to investors will no longer be included while determining the base amount. Where quantified, such amounts will be disgorged separately on top of the settlement amount.

The settlement amount itself will be built on a base amount. This base amount is linked to the minimum penalty prescribed for the violation under securities laws.

The base amount will then be adjusted using six factors. These relate to the stage of proceedings, regulatory action, gravity of the violation, aggravating factors, mitigating factors and legal costs.

The formula gives both sides a clearer starting point for negotiations. An entity can estimate exposure from the prescribed penalty, while Sebi can scale the amount up or down based on conduct and cooperation.

The framework also introduces two fast-track settlement routes. These are violation-based fast-track settlement and monetary threshold-based fast-track settlement.

Under the monetary threshold route, cases where the settlement amount does not exceed Rs 10 lakh get a shorter path. They move directly from the internal committee to a panel of whole-time members.

Under the violation-based route, Sebi will issue a notice offering the entity an opportunity to settle. The entity can settle by paying the amount specified in the notice.

For market plumbing, the routes matter most to smaller cases. Entities facing minor violations get speed and predictability instead of the usual internal-committee grind.

Investors, meanwhile, get cleaner disgorgement. Because wrongful gains are now stripped out of the base amount and recovered separately, the recovery of ill-gotten gains is more transparent.

Sebi also stands to benefit. Faster resolution of small cases frees up enforcement bandwidth for more serious violations, easing the load on its docket.

Put together, the notification rewires how settlement terms are computed and how small cases are processed. The changes apply to settlement proceedings initiated under the new regulations.

The double-counting fix is the quiet heart of the reform. Under the old practice, an entity's wrongful gain was baked into the settlement amount and then effectively charged again — paying twice for the same money.

Separating disgorgement changes the incentives. An entity that made no wrongful gain now faces a formula-driven settlement; one that profited disgorges that profit on top, but cannot be charged for it twice inside the base amount.

The penalty-linkage gives the formula an anchor. Because the base amount starts from the minimum penalty prescribed in securities law, the settlement cannot drift below what the statute itself considers the floor for the violation.

The adjustment factors then make the settlement path-dependent. Settling early, before regulatory action hardens, weighs differently from settling after proceedings advance — a structure that quietly rewards cooperation.

For the regulator, the fast-track routes address a docket problem. Securities enforcement has historically ground slowly; cases under Rs 10 lakh jumping straight to whole-time members removes a whole class of small matters from the queue.

The violation-based route is the more novel instrument. By specifying the settlement amount in the notice itself, Sebi converts settlement from a negotiation into an offer — accept the number, or litigate.

Entities facing minor disclosure lapses are the likely users of both routes. The framework gives them a predictable exit price; the market gets faster resolution of routine matters.

What the framework does not do is cap accountability for serious violations. Disgorgement remains separate and uncapped by the formula, and remedial and regulatory terms can still accompany any settlement.

The October 6 notification lands amid a broader Sebi effort to make enforcement faster and more predictable. Settlement, in this design, becomes a tool that clears small cases quickly while keeping large ones expensive.

For market participants, the practical takeaway is paperwork discipline. With stage-of-proceedings as a pricing factor, the decision to settle is itself worth making early — delay now has a quoted cost.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT HAS SEBI NOTIFIED IN THE NEW SETTLEMENT RULES?
SEBI HAS NOTIFIED NEW SETTLEMENT REGULATIONS DATED OCTOBER 6, 2026. THE RULES PROVIDE A REVISED FORMULA FOR CALCULATING SETTLEMENT AMOUNTS, SEPARATE DISGORGEMENT OF WRONGFUL GAINS, AND A FAST-TRACK ROUTE FOR CASES INVOLVING SETTLEMENT AMOUNTS OF UP TO RS 10 LAKH.
WHAT ARE THE COMPONENTS OF SETTLEMENT TERMS UNDER THE NEW FRAMEWORK?
SETTLEMENT TERMS WILL NOW COMPRISE THREE COMPONENTS: THE SETTLEMENT AMOUNT, DISGORGEMENT OF WRONGFUL GAINS WHEREVER APPLICABLE, AND REMEDIAL AND REGULATORY TERMS, WHICH WERE EARLIER REFERRED TO AS NON-MONETARY TERMS.
HOW WILL THE SETTLEMENT AMOUNT BE CALCULATED UNDER THE NEW FORMULA?
THE SETTLEMENT AMOUNT WILL BE BASED ON A BASE AMOUNT LINKED TO THE MINIMUM PENALTY PRESCRIBED FOR THE VIOLATION UNDER SECURITIES LAWS. THE BASE AMOUNT WILL BE ADJUSTED USING FACTORS RELATING TO THE STAGE OF PROCEEDINGS, REGULATORY ACTION, GRAVITY OF THE VIOLATION, AGGRAVATING FACTORS, MITIGATING FACTORS AND LEGAL COSTS.
WHAT IS THE FAST-TRACK SETTLEMENT ROUTE AND WHO CAN USE IT?
THE FAST-TRACK ROUTE HAS TWO TYPES. UNDER THE MONETARY THRESHOLD ROUTE, CASES WHERE THE SETTLEMENT AMOUNT DOES NOT EXCEED RS 10 LAKH MOVE DIRECTLY FROM THE INTERNAL COMMITTEE TO A PANEL OF WHOLE-TIME MEMBERS. UNDER THE VIOLATION-BASED ROUTE, SEBI ISSUES A NOTICE OFFERING THE ENTITY AN OPPORTUNITY TO SETTLE BY PAYING THE AMOUNT SPECIFIED.
HOW DOES THE NEW FRAMEWORK REMOVE DOUBLE COUNTING OF WRONGFUL GAINS?
WRONGFUL GAINS, LOSS AVOIDED OR LOSS CAUSED TO INVESTORS WILL NO LONGER BE INCLUDED WHILE DETERMINING THE BASE AMOUNT. SUCH AMOUNTS, WHERE QUANTIFIED, WILL BE DISGORGED SEPARATELY, ENDING THE EXISTING PRACTICE WHERE THEY WERE EFFECTIVELY COUNTED AGAIN WHILE COMPUTING SETTLEMENT TERMS. GLOSSARY: Settlement: A negotiated resolution where a violating entity pays terms instead of facing full adjudication. Disgorgement: Repayment of profits made — or losses avoided — through a violation. Base amount: The starting figure for calculating a settlement, here linked to the minimum statutory penalty. Whole-time members: Full-time Sebi board members who decide on regulatory matters, including settlements. Internal committee: Sebi's first-level body that evaluates and recommends settlement terms. Non-monetary terms: Obligations imposed in a settlement beyond payment, now called remedial and regulatory terms.
