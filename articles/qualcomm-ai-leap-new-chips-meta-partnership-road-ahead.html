Qualcomm's AI Leap: New Chips, Meta Partnership & The Road Ahead

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Qualcomm has unveiled its Dragonfly C1000 AI data center chip family, with Meta signing on as the first major hyperscale customer for deployment beginning late 2028.
  • 02/The company targets $5 billion in data center revenue by fiscal 2027 and $15 billion annually by 2029, while non-smartphone revenues are projected to nearly double to $40 billion by decade's end.
  • 03/Qualcomm faces fierce competition from Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, and Amazon in the AI infrastructure market, but has acquired AI software startup Modular to strengthen its ecosystem.

Qualcomm has unveiled a new family of AI data center chips and systems, marking its most ambitious expansion beyond the smartphone industry. The centerpiece is the Dragonfly C1000 CPU, built on Qualcomm's Oryon architecture and optimized for AI inference workloads.

Meta has signed on as the first major hyperscale customer for Qualcomm's new AI data center processors. The social media giant plans to begin deploying these chips in its AI infrastructure starting late 2028. Qualcomm has also secured commitments from two additional unnamed hyperscale customers.

The company has set aggressive financial targets for its data center business. Qualcomm expects to generate approximately $5 billion in revenue by fiscal 2027 and grow that to $15 billion annually by 2029. Management believes this segment could become a multi-billion-dollar business by the end of the decade.

Qualcomm's broader diversification strategy aims to nearly double non-smartphone revenues to $40 billion by the end of the decade. This reflects management's confidence that AI infrastructure represents the company's biggest long-term growth opportunity outside mobile devices.

The Dragonfly C1000 is designed to deliver high performance while using smartphone-inspired memory technologies. Qualcomm says this approach can reduce costs and improve power efficiency for cloud providers running AI inference workloads.

Qualcomm is entering one of the technology industry's most competitive markets. It will face established players including Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Marvell, Amazon, and Google, all of which are investing heavily in AI infrastructure and custom silicon solutions.

To strengthen its competitive position, Qualcomm has acquired AI software startup Modular. The acquisition will help developers run AI models efficiently across different chip architectures without rewriting software, addressing the ecosystem challenge posed by Nvidia's dominant CUDA platform.

Investors responded positively to Qualcomm's AI roadmap announcement. The company's higher revenue targets, major customer wins, and expanding product portfolio boosted confidence in its long-term growth strategy. The announcement also lifted sentiment across semiconductor stocks broadly.
