GST Collections Hit All-Time High of ₹2.42 Lakh Crore in April 2026

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/GST collections in April 2026 surged to an all-time high of ₹2.42 lakh crore, marking an 8.7% increase over the ₹2.33 lakh crore collected in April 2025.
  • 02/The record was achieved without compensation cess; import-linked revenue jumped 25.8% while domestic collections grew a modest 4.3%.
  • 03/Analysts warn of a sequential dip ahead as year-end recalibration sets in, with the 57th GST Council meeting due to address inverted duty concerns.

GST collections in April 2026 touched an all-time high of ₹2.42 lakh crore. This marks the highest monthly mop-up since the tax was introduced in 2017.

The figure represents an 8.7% year-on-year increase over the ₹2.33 lakh crore collected in April 2025. Notably, this record was achieved without any compensation cess.

Collections in April reflect goods consumed and services availed during March 2026. Strong imports and year-end stock clearance drove the record revenue.

Data from the GST portal showed domestic collections at over ₹1.85 lakh crore, up 4.3% year-on-year. Gross import revenue crossed ₹57,000 crore, surging 25.8% from last year.

Total refunds rose 19.3%, bringing net collections to approximately ₹2.11 lakh crore. Net collection growth stood at 7.3% compared to April 2025.

"In an increasingly dynamic global landscape, we must critically re-examine our policy frameworks to further incentivize domestic manufacturing and ensure 'Make in India' keeps pace with global supply chain shifts," said Saurabh Agarwal, Tax Partner at EY India.

Vivek Jalan, Partner at Tax Connect, flagged a 54% spike in refunds under the inverted duty structure. He noted this accumulation has raised business expenses, a concern flagged for the upcoming 57th GST Council meeting.

"As we transition into the new fiscal year, we should anticipate a stabilization in the coming months, with collections likely seeing a sequential dip," Agarwal cautioned.

Manoj Mishra, Partner at Grant Thornton Bharat, pointed to the dominance of Maharashtra, Karnataka, Gujarat and Uttar Pradesh. "India's GST performance mirrors its macro story, the fastest-growing major economy at 6.5%," he said.
