Tata Sons Shortlists Vinod Kannan, Nipun Aggarwal for Air India CEO

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/The CEO search for India's second-largest airline has narrowed to two candidates: Singapore Airlines executive Vinod Kannan and Air India's commercial head Nipun Aggarwal.
  • 02/Campbell Wilson resigned on April 7, 2026, after four years at the helm, but Air India still recorded a ₹8,033 crore loss for the nine months ending December 31, 2024.
  • 03/The winner will take charge ahead of a critical 2027 inflection point when deliveries from Air India's nearly 600-aircraft orderbook begin in earnest.

Tata Sons is close to naming a new chief executive for Air India, with Singapore Airlines executive Vinod Kannan and Air India commercial head Nipun Aggarwal emerging as the two finalists, two sources with direct knowledge said.

Campbell Wilson announced his resignation on April 7, 2026, after four years as CEO. His term was due to end in 2027, but he is serving a six-month notice period while the board finds his successor.

Tata Sons owns roughly 74.9% of Air India, while Singapore Airlines holds a 25.1% stake following the November 2024 merger of Vistara into the flag carrier.

Kannan joined Singapore Airlines in 2001 and served as CEO of Vistara from January 1, 2022, through its merger into Air India. Aggarwal joined Air India in January 2022 after Tata took control and previously worked at Bank of America Merrill Lynch.

The appointment comes at a volatile moment. Air India reported a loss of ₹8,033 crore on revenue of ₹56,367 crore for the nine months ending December 31, 2024.

For the full fiscal year 2024-25, revenue rose roughly 14% year-on-year to about ₹61,000 crore, and the group achieved its first positive operating profit (EBITDAR) since privatization.

Tata Sons and Singapore Airlines injected ₹9,558 crore into Air India during FY24-25, with the most recent tranche of ₹4,306 crore arriving in March 2025. The airline is now seeking an additional ₹100 billion for system upgrades.

Wilson's tenure left a modernized foundation with 100 new aircraft added and legacy narrowbody refits completed. The incoming CEO must now manage deliveries from a nearly 600-aircraft orderbook starting in 2027 amid ongoing geopolitical and financial headwinds.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHO ARE THE TWO FINALISTS FOR AIR INDIA CEO?
VINOD KANNAN, CURRENTLY SENIOR VICE PRESIDENT OF SALES AND MARKETING AT SINGAPORE AIRLINES AND FORMER CEO OF VISTARA, AND NIPUN AGGARWAL, AIR INDIA'S COMMERCIAL HEAD WHO JOINED IN JANUARY 2022.
WHY DID CAMPBELL WILSON RESIGN?
WILSON, WHO BECAME CEO AFTER TATA SONS PRIVATIZED AIR INDIA IN JANUARY 2022, RESIGNED ON APRIL 7, 2026. HIS TERM WAS DUE TO END IN 2027, BUT HE STEPPED DOWN AMID PERSISTENT LOSSES, REGULATORY SCRUTINY AFTER THE JUNE 2025 CRASH, AND GEOPOLITICAL DISRUPTIONS.
HOW MUCH DOES SINGAPORE AIRLINES OWN OF AIR INDIA?
SINGAPORE AIRLINES HOLDS A 25.1% STAKE IN AIR INDIA, WITH THE REMAINING 74.9% OWNED BY TATA SONS. SIA ACQUIRED THIS STAKE FOLLOWING THE NOVEMBER 2024 MERGER OF VISTARA INTO AIR INDIA.
WHAT IS THE FINANCIAL CONDITION OF AIR INDIA?
FOR THE NINE MONTHS ENDING DECEMBER 31, 2024, AIR INDIA POSTED A LOSS OF ₹8,033 CRORE ON REVENUE OF ₹56,367 CRORE. FOR THE FULL FY24-25, REVENUE ROSE ~14% YOY TO ₹61,000 CRORE, AND THE GROUP ACHIEVED POSITIVE EBITDAR FOR THE FIRST TIME SINCE PRIVATIZATION.
HOW MUCH CAPITAL HAS BEEN INJECTED INTO AIR INDIA?
TATA SONS AND SINGAPORE AIRLINES INJECTED ₹9,558 CRORE DURING FY24-25, WITH THE MOST RECENT TRANCHE IN MARCH 2025 TOTALING ₹4,306 CRORE. THE AIRLINE IS NOW SEEKING AN ADDITIONAL ₹100 BILLION ($1.14 BILLION) FOR SYSTEM AND ENGINEERING UPGRADES.
