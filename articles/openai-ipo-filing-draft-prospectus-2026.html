OpenAI IPO Filing: ChatGPT Maker Files Draft Prospectus in Days, WSJ Says

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/OpenAI is preparing to confidentially file a draft IPO prospectus with regulators as early as Friday, May 22, 2026, per WSJ report
  • 02/Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley are lead underwriters; the ChatGPT maker is racing rival Anthropic to be first generative AI startup to go public
  • 03/Company valued at $500 billion in October 2025 secondary; targeting IPO as soon as September 2026 amid $100 billion pre-IPO funding push

OpenAI is preparing to file for an initial public offering in the coming days or weeks. The Wall Street Journal reported the development on May 20, 2026, citing people familiar with the matter.

The ChatGPT maker is working with bankers at Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley. The firms are helping draft a confidential IPO prospectus for regulatory filing.

The filing could happen as early as Friday, May 22, 2026. OpenAI did not officially confirm the report. Reuters could not independently verify the timeline.

OpenAI is targeting a public listing as soon as September 2026. The move positions the company to beat rival Anthropic in the race to become the first generative AI startup to go public.

The company was valued at approximately $500 billion in an October 2025 secondary offering. It is now seeking over $100 billion in pre-IPO private funding at an estimated $830 billion valuation.

Major backers include Microsoft, Amazon, SoftBank, and Nvidia. The company is not expected to reach profitability until roughly 2030 despite rapid revenue growth.

CFO Sarah Friar has been preparing OpenAI to "look and feel and act like a public company." The company recently hired a chief accounting officer and an investor relations lead.

The IPO push follows an April 2026 WSJ report that OpenAI missed internal revenue and user targets. The company disputed the report, saying 2026 growth remains "well within plan."

An OpenAI IPO would rank among the largest technology listings in history. The filing comes as the company navigates an ongoing lawsuit from co-founder Elon Musk seeking up to $134 billion in damages.
