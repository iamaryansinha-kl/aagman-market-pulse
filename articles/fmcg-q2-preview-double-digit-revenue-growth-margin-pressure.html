FMCG September Quarter Preview: Double-Digit Growth, Margin Squeeze

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/India's FMCG companies are expected to post double-digit revenue growth in the September quarter, driven by a combination of volume gains and calibrated price increases.
  • 02/Rising costs of sugar, edible oils, packaging materials and freight are likely to keep gross margins under pressure across the sector despite steady demand and improving rural consumption.
  • 03/Key monitorables this earnings season: rural and urban demand trends, raw material inflation, and whether pricing actions and operating leverage can protect profitability at each company.

India's FMCG companies are heading into the September quarter earnings season with a split story. Revenue is expected to grow in double digits, but the margin line is where the quarter will be won or lost.

Growth is being powered by volume gains and calibrated price increases. Steady demand trends and improving rural consumption are supporting topline expansion across the sector.

The pressure is coming from the cost side. Sugar, edible oils, packaging materials and freight costs are all rising, keeping gross margins under strain and limiting profitability despite healthy demand.

Hindustan Unilever Ltd (NSE: HINDUNILVR) is expected to post low double-digit revenue growth. Home care and beauty and wellbeing will lead, with personal care and foods steady. Edible oil, packaging and food commodity inflation will weigh on gross margins despite pricing actions.

Nestle India Ltd (NESTLEIND) is expected to deliver another strong quarter with revenue growth of around 20%. Confectionery and beverages are likely to lead, powered by premiumisation and quick commerce channels. Cost efficiencies and operating leverage should boost margins despite input inflation.

Asian Paints Ltd (NSE: ASIANPAINT) is expected to post double-digit revenue growth. Mid-single-digit volume growth in decorative paints and better pricing are the drivers, but crude-linked raw material, packaging and freight cost inflation will keep margins under pressure.

Britannia Industries Ltd (NSE: BRITANNIA) is expected to deliver strong double-digit revenue growth. Sugar and other commodity inflation will pressure gross margins, partly offset by sourcing efficiencies and operating leverage. Elevated employee costs and higher advertising spends may weigh on overall margins.

Colgate-Palmolive India Ltd (NSE: COLPAL) is likely to report double-digit revenue growth. The drivers are strong volume expansion, pricing gains and premiumisation, supported by a favourable base, stronger execution and investment in premium brands and new launches.

Dabur India Ltd (NSE: DABUR) is expected to see revenue growth led by its domestic business. Home and personal care and foods and beverages are the key momentum drivers. Watch demand trends, rural expansion, international performance, and geopolitical and commodity uncertainties.

Varun Beverages Ltd (NSE: VBL) is likely to deliver a strong quarter with revenue growth of over 20%. Robust domestic demand, healthy volume expansion and international traction are the drivers, supported by expanded capacities, an extended summer season and deeper geographic penetration.

Two cross-cutting themes stand out across these previews. Premiumisation and quick commerce are accelerating growth for companies like Nestle and Colgate, while rural consumption is steadily improving across the sector.

Demand trends still need close monitoring at paint and discretionary names. Asian Paints' preview flags soft consumption across key markets, a reminder that the demand recovery remains uneven.

The tension heading into results is clear. On one side sit steady demand and improving rural consumption; on the other sit sticky input costs for sugar, edible oils, packaging and freight. Pricing actions and operating leverage will determine which force wins at each company.

The split screen — healthy topline, squeezed profitability — mirrors what the RBI's own sector surveys have been signalling: demand is holding up, including a gradual improvement in rural consumption, but companies lack the pricing power to fully pass through what inputs are costing them.

The cost basket differs by company, which is why the margin stories diverge. Food-linked inputs are the pressure point for HUL, Nestle and Britannia — edible oils, sugar, dairy and food commodities — while Asian Paints is exposed to crude-linked raw materials plus packaging and freight. Varun Beverages and Nestle have the friendlier setup, where cost efficiencies and operating leverage are expected to expand margins even as input costs rise.

Quick commerce keeps appearing as a quiet growth driver — Nestle's growth is explicitly tied to premiumisation and the quick-commerce channel, a reminder that India's e-grocery boom has become material to how packaged food companies sell.

For investors, the quarter's verdict will rest less on the revenue line than on management commentary around three questions: whether rural demand recovery is broadening beyond staples, how much more pricing action the market can absorb, and when input-cost inflation — particularly crude-linked and soft-commodity baskets — starts to flatten. The Q2 numbers, due over the coming weeks, will be the first big test of whether volume-led growth can keep carrying the sector while margins recover.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT KIND OF REVENUE GROWTH IS EXPECTED FROM INDIA'S FMCG COMPANIES IN THE SEPTEMBER QUARTER?
FMCG COMPANIES ARE EXPECTED TO DELIVER DOUBLE-DIGIT REVENUE GROWTH IN THE SEPTEMBER QUARTER, DRIVEN BY VOLUME GAINS AND CALIBRATED PRICE INCREASES. NESTLE INDIA AND VARUN BEVERAGES COULD POST GROWTH OF AROUND 20% OR MORE.
WHAT IS PRESSURING FMCG COMPANY MARGINS THIS QUARTER?
RISING COSTS OF RAW MATERIALS INCLUDING SUGAR, EDIBLE OILS, PACKAGING MATERIALS AND FREIGHT ARE KEEPING GROSS MARGINS UNDER PRESSURE ACROSS THE SECTOR, LIMITING PROFITABILITY DESPITE STEADY DEMAND TRENDS.
WHAT IS THE EXPECTED SEPTEMBER QUARTER OUTLOOK FOR HINDUSTAN UNILEVER?
HUL IS EXPECTED TO POST LOW DOUBLE-DIGIT REVENUE GROWTH, SUPPORTED BY HOME CARE AND BEAUTY AND WELLBEING SEGMENTS, WITH PERSONAL CARE AND FOODS STEADY. EDIBLE OILS, PACKAGING MATERIALS AND FOOD COMMODITY INFLATION WILL WEIGH ON GROSS MARGINS.
WHICH FMCG COMPANIES ARE EXPECTED TO POST THE STRONGEST GROWTH?
NESTLE INDIA IS EXPECTED TO DELIVER AROUND 20% REVENUE GROWTH, AND VARUN BEVERAGES IS LIKELY TO POST OVER 20% GROWTH, DRIVEN BY VOLUME EXPANSION, PREMIUMISATION, QUICK COMMERCE AND AN EXTENDED SUMMER SEASON.
WHAT SHOULD INVESTORS WATCH DURING THE FMCG EARNINGS SEASON?
KEY MONITORABLES INCLUDE RURAL AND URBAN DEMAND TRENDS, RAW MATERIAL INFLATION, COMPETITIVE INTENSITY, INTERNATIONAL PERFORMANCE AT DABUR AND VARUN BEVERAGES, AND WHETHER PRICING ACTIONS AND OPERATING LEVERAGE CAN OFFSET STICKY INPUT COSTS. GLOSSARY: Operating leverage: The way fixed costs spread over higher sales volumes, lifting profit margins as revenue grows. Gross margin: Revenue minus the direct cost of goods sold, expressed as a share of revenue. Premiumisation: The shift of consumers toward higher-priced, premium product variants within a category. Quick commerce: Grocery and essentials delivery within minutes through app-based platforms. Reverse charge: A GST mechanism where the buyer, not the seller, accounts for the tax.
