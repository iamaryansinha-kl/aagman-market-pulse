FPI Ownership Hits 14-Year Low at 14.7% on ₹2.18 Lakh Crore Selloff

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/FPI ownership of Indian equities falls to 14.7%, the lowest since June 2012, as sustained selling continues through 2026
  • 02/DIIs now hold 18.9% surpassing FPIs for the first time since December 2024; total FPI outflows reached ₹2.18 lakh crore via exchanges YTD
  • 03/Analysts warn India is losing EM allocations to Korea and Taiwan amid AI-driven tech rallies and currency depreciation concerns

FPI ownership of Indian equities has dropped to 14.7 per cent. This is the lowest level since June 2012 according to JM Financials' April 2026 tracker.

Domestic Institutional Investors now hold 18.9 per cent of Indian equities. They have overtaken FPIs for the first time since December 2024.

FPIs were net sellers in four of five trading sessions this week ended May 8, 2026. Aggregate equity outflows through exchanges and primary markets stood at ₹14,207.20 crore.

Monday saw outflows of ₹8,035.69 crore through stock exchanges. Thursday recorded the steepest single-day selling at ₹5,697.61 crore. Friday's outflows narrowed sharply to just ₹69.31 crore.

Dr V K Vijayakumar, Chief Investment Strategist at Geojit Investments, said total FPI sales through exchanges in 2026 now stand at ₹2,18,540 crore. Primary market investments have held up at ₹12,340 crore.

"At a time when Korea received nearly $4 billion and Taiwan around $5.5 billion in flows, India is still not getting its due share of emerging market allocations" - N ArunaGiri, CEO of TrustLine Holdings.

Vijayakumar pointed to the AI-driven technology rally in South Korea and Taiwan as a key diversion. Currency depreciation and earnings growth concerns in India have also driven foreign outflows.

Even amid selling, FPIs have invested in power, construction and capital goods. ArunaGiri cautioned that without meaningful FPI allocation increases, large caps will remain underperformers relative to the SMID segment.
