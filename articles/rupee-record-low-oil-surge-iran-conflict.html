Rupee Hits Record Close at 94.8450 as Oil Surges Near $115 Amid Iran Conflict

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/The rupee closed at a record low of 94.8450 per dollar, down 0.3%, as Brent crude surged over 3% to nearly $115 per barrel
  • 02/State-run banks likely acting for the RBI sold dollars to limit losses, while the Philippine peso and Indonesian rupiah also hit record lows
  • 03/DBS analysts warn of higher-for-longer rates and inflation; Fed funds futures show no policy changes expected until late 2027

The Indian rupee closed at 94.8450 per US dollar on Wednesday. This marks a record closing low, down 0.3% for the session.

Brent crude surged more than 3% to nearly $115 per barrel. The spike follows stalled efforts to resolve the Iran conflict.

U.S. President Donald Trump has instructed aides to prepare for an extended blockade of Iran. The Wall Street Journal reported this late Tuesday.

Dollar sales by state-run banks, likely acting on behalf of the RBI, helped limit the rupee's losses. Traders noted this intervention.

Other oil-sensitive Asian currencies also suffered. The Philippine peso and Indonesian rupiah both hit record lows on Wednesday.

"Around these levels last month the RBI had stepped in heavily with both dollar sales and regulatory measures, at least one of those could be used again," a trader at a state-run bank said.

"We think it makes sense to brace for a higher-for-longer rates world, amidst what looks to be higher-for-longer inflation," analysts at DBS said in a note.

Fed funds futures are pricing in no policy changes by the U.S. Federal Reserve until late 2027. The CME Group's FedWatch tool shows this.

THE PHILIPPINE PESO AND INDONESIAN RUPIAH BOTH HIT RECORD LOWS ON WEDNESDAY. THESE OIL-SENSITIVE CURRENCIES ARE ALSO UNDER PRESSURE FROM SURGING CRUDE PRICES AND GEOPOLITICAL TENSIONS.
