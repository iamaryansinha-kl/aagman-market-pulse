Adani Power Q2 Profit Zooms 134% to Rs 6,919 Crore on Demand

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Consolidated net profit rose 134% year-on-year to Rs 6,919 crore, from Rs 2,953 crore a year earlier.
  • •- Revenue jumped 45% to Rs 19,439 crore; EBITDA climbed 60% to Rs 8,231 crore, with margins at 42.3%.
  • •- Power sales volumes rose 11.5% to 26.4 billion units, riding India's surging electricity demand.
  • •- The company is advancing 23,720 MW of new thermal capacity across four projects.

India's power demand boom is showing up in the bottom line. Adani Power reported a 134% year-on-year jump in consolidated net profit to Rs 6,919 crore for the second quarter of FY27, against Rs 2,953 crore in the year-ago period, with revenue from operations up 45% at Rs 19,439 crore. EBITDA rose 60% to Rs 8,231 crore from Rs 5,150 crore, and the EBITDA margin expanded 400 basis points to 42.3%. The engine behind it all: India consumed 11.5% more electricity in the quarter than it did a year ago.

The demand numbers explain the profit. India's power demand rose to 502 billion units in Q2 FY27, driven by rapid economic growth and a weak monsoon that crimped hydropower — forcing the grid to lean harder on thermal plants. Generation overall rose 11% to 536 billion units, and within that, coal- and lignite-based power supplied 66% of total supply, up from 65% a year earlier, with coal-fired plants alone accounting for 73% of the incremental power generated in the quarter. The aggregate plant load factor of India's coal fleet rose to 68% from 62%. When the grid runs that hot, generators with spare capacity print money: fixed costs spread over more units sold, and merchant realisations firm up as buyers compete for scarce megawatts. Adani Power's consolidated sales volume rose 11.5% to 26.4 billion units — moving almost exactly in line with national demand, which is what a baseload-heavy fleet is designed to do.

CEO S B Khyalia framed the quarter as both an operational milestone and a strategic vindication. The company, he said, continues to make progress in profitability and project execution, focused on timely supply of reliable power for India's growing baseload requirements — and then made the statement that will get the most attention: "Geopolitical developments and climate-related uncertainties have further highlighted the importance of domestic coal-based power in meeting India's long-term electricity needs." It is a pointed line, arriving on a day when Brent crude sits near $114 a barrel after Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia and evacuation of Aramco's Dhahran headquarters. India's energy-import anxiety and its coal-power profits are, increasingly, the same story.

The national backdrop gives the claim substance. Power generation rose 11% to 536 billion units even as demand hit 502 billion units, meaning the system ran flat out to keep up — and still leaned on imports-sensitive fuel. For policymakers juggling energy security against transition commitments, quarters like this are ammunition for the pragmatic middle path: keep building renewables at record pace, but stop apologising for the coal fleet that keeps the lights on when the monsoon underdelivers. Adani Power's results are, in effect, the financial expression of that policy equilibrium — and of a grid where demand growth has once again outrun every other plan on the table.

The pipeline is substantial — and its sequencing tells you what management expects demand to do. Adani Power is advancing a capacity expansion programme to add 23,720 MW of new thermal capacity, roughly doubling the scale of the business over the coming years. At the 1,320 MW Korba Phase-II supercritical project, the first 660 MW unit has been synchronised with the grid and is expected to be commissioned shortly, with the second unit due in Q4 FY27. The 1,600 MW Mahan Phase-II ultra-supercritical project is on track for full commissioning in early FY28. Further out, the 1,600 MW Raipur Phase-II and 1,600 MW Raigarh Phase-II ultra-supercritical projects have each crossed 65% progress in under 30 months since work began, with commissioning scheduled for FY29. Add it up and the company is building capacity roughly equal to its current operating fleet — a bet that the demand curve it just rode to a 134% profit surge keeps bending upward through the decade.

The market's reaction was muted in the extreme — Adani Power's stock slipped about 1.75% to Rs 182 in Friday's broad selloff — but the quarter itself marks a clean demonstration of the operating leverage in the model: when national demand grows at double digits and the coal PLF rises six percentage points, a generator with scale captures disproportionate economics. The 42.3% EBITDA margin, up 400 basis points, is the number institutional investors will circle first. It is also worth noting what did not change this quarter: nothing about the result required exceptional execution or a one-off gain — it is simply what the P&L of a large thermal fleet looks like when a growing economy, a weak monsoon and a geopolitical energy shock all push in the same direction. The strategic question — how much of that margin survives the energy transition the company is also citing as a reason to build more coal — is one for a longer piece; Friday's numbers say the transition remains, for now and for the shareholders who own this quarter, extremely profitable to wait upon.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT WERE ADANI POWER'S Q2 NUMBERS?
Net profit of Rs 6,919 crore, up 134% from Rs 2,953 crore a year earlier. Revenue rose 45% to Rs 19,439 crore and EBITDA climbed 60% to Rs 8,231 crore, with the margin at 42.3% versus 38.3% last year. Power sales volumes grew 11.5% to 26.4 billion units.
WHAT DROVE THE SURGE?
India's power demand rose 11.5% to 502 billion units in the quarter, and with weak monsoon hurting hydro output, coal's share of generation rose. Adani Power's sales volumes grew 11.5% to 26.4 billion units and its EBITDA margin expanded 400 basis points.
WHAT IS THE EXPANSION PLAN?
23,720 MW of new thermal capacity. Korba Phase-II's first 660 MW unit is synchronised and due shortly, Mahan Phase-II is due early FY28, and Raipur/Raigarh Phase-II are at 65%+ progress for FY29.
WHY IS COAL'S ROLE GROWING IF INDIA IS ADDING RENEWABLES?
Because demand is growing faster than clean capacity can be built, and this quarter exposed the gap: a weak monsoon cut hydropower output just as demand rose 11.5%, so coal filled 73% of the incremental generation. Until storage and renewables scale further, every heatwave, weak monsoon or demand spike reasserts coal's role — which is exactly the argument the company made in its commentary. GLOSSARY: BILLION UNITS (BU) — A measure of electricity; one billion units equals one billion kilowatt-hours. India consumed 502 BU in Q2 FY27. PLANT LOAD FACTOR (PLF) — The average output of a power plant against its maximum capability; India's coal fleet ran at 68% in the quarter, up from 62%. SUPERCRITICAL / ULTRA-SUPERCRITICAL — Modern, more efficient coal-plant designs that burn less coal per unit of power; the backbone of India's new thermal builds. BASELOAD — The minimum level of electricity demand the grid must meet around the clock; plants that supply it run continuously, unlike peak-load plants.
