Brent Crude at $108.60, WTI at $102.12 as Hormuz Tensions Persist

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/July Brent crude futures hit $108.60 (+0.40%) and June WTI at $102.12 (+0.17%) on May 4, 2026, despite Trump's Hormuz vessel release plan
  • 02/OPEC+ agreed to raise June output by 188,000 bpd on May 3, but ING analysts say 55% from Gulf producers won't materialize amid Hormuz disruptions
  • 03/May MCX crude futures traded at ₹9,732 (+0.69%); investors should watch if Hormuz reopens and whether OPEC+ actually delivers the supply boost

Crude oil futures traded higher on Monday, May 4, 2026. July Brent futures hit $108.60, up 0.40 per cent. June WTI crude stood at $102.12, gaining 0.17 per cent.

May crude oil futures on the MCX traded at ₹9,732 against the previous close of ₹9,665. That is a gain of 0.69 per cent. June MCX futures were at ₹9,302, up 0.96 per cent.

US President Donald Trump announced Project Freedom to guide stranded vessels out of the Strait of Hormuz. He called it a humanitarian gesture for neutral countries caught in the Middle East dispute.

OPEC+ held a virtual meeting on May 3 to review market conditions. Seven countries decided to raise output by 188,000 barrels per day in June 2026. This marks the first meeting since the UAE exited the group.

Warren Patterson, Head of Commodities Strategy at ING Think, and colleague Ewa Manthey said the production increase is unlikely to happen. About 55 per cent was expected from Persian Gulf producers. That supply won't move amid ongoing Hormuz disruptions.

Traders are weighing Trump's Hormuz relief against OPEC+ supply plans. The Strait handles roughly one-fifth of global oil shipments. Any sustained blockage keeps upside risk alive.

Investors should monitor whether ships actually exit Hormuz safely. They should also track if OPEC+ members fill the promised 188,000 bpd hike. Supply reality often diverges from quota announcements.
