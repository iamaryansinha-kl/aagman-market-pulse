Extreme Heat Events Threaten Global Agri-Food Systems, New Study Warns

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/A new study warns that intensifying extreme heat events are raising serious risks to global agri-food systems
  • 02/Rising temperatures threaten crop yields, disrupt supply chains, and increase food price volatility across major producing regions
  • 03/The findings add urgency to climate adaptation investments in agriculture, irrigation, and resilient crop varieties

A new study has warned that intensifying extreme heat events pose mounting risks to agri-food systems worldwide.

The study was published on April 22, 2026. It links rising temperature anomalies to measurable damage across food production chains.

Extreme heat directly reduces crop yields. Wheat, rice, and maize output drops sharply when temperatures exceed optimal growing thresholds.

Heat stress also disrupts supply chains. Storage, transport, and logistics networks face spoilage and efficiency losses during prolonged hot spells.

Food price volatility tends to spike after major heat events. The 2022 European drought and 2023 Indian heatwave both triggered regional inflation in staples.

The study emphasizes that frequency and intensity of heat events are both increasing. This is not a one-off risk but a structural threat to food security.

Major producing regions including the Indo-Gangetic Plain, US Midwest, and Brazilian Cerrado are particularly exposed. These zones feed billions of people.

Adaptation investments in irrigation, heat-resistant seeds, and early warning systems are critical. The study calls for faster deployment of climate-resilient agriculture.
