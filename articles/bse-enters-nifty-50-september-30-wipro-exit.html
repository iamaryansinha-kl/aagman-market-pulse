BSE Enters Nifty 50 From September 30: Wipro Exits After Three Decades

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/BSE Ltd (NSE: BSE) joins the Nifty 50 effective September 30, 2026, replacing Wipro (NSE: WIPRO) after a three-decade run in the benchmark
  • 02/Nuvama estimates roughly $741 million of passive inflows into BSE and about $246 million of outflows from Wipro as index funds rebalance
  • 03/Today, September 29, is the last trading day under the old composition; funds tracking Nifty 50, Nifty 100, Nifty 500 and Nifty50 Equal Weight all adjust

The change takes effect tomorrow. BSE Ltd enters the Nifty 50 index on September 30, 2026, replacing Wipro Ltd, after the semi-annual review by NSE Indices.

NSE Indices announced the decision on August 10, 2026. The reshuffle applies after the close of trading today, September 29.

The Nifty 50 is India's most tracked benchmark. Its 50 constituents represent roughly 53.73% of NSE's free-float market capitalisation, as of March 30, 2026.

BSE qualified on the numbers. Its six-month average free-float market capitalisation stands at Rs 1,40,879 crore.

Wipro's comparable figure is Rs 55,930 crore. That makes BSE's free-float market cap roughly 2.5 times Wipro's.

The inclusion rule requires an eligible stock's free-float market cap to be at least 1.5 times that of the smallest existing Nifty 50 constituent. BSE cleared that bar comfortably.

Free-float market cap counts only shares available for public trading. It excludes promoter holdings, which is why the free-float figure matters more than total market cap for index purposes.

NSE Indices runs this review every six months. The Index Maintenance Sub-Committee (Equity) applies a fixed methodology, which keeps the selection mechanical rather than discretionary.

Two other companies were eligible but missed out. TVS Motor Company had an average free-float market cap of Rs 84,566 crore, and Divi's Laboratories stood at Rs 82,930 crore.

Neither was selected because the threshold test failed further down the index. After Wipro's removal, the two lowest-ranked constituents are HDFC Life at Rs 65,666 crore and Tata Consumer Products at Rs 73,054 crore.

TVS Motor's market cap is not 1.5 times HDFC Life's. So the entry slot went to BSE, the only stock that cleared every test.

Eligibility for the Nifty 50 also requires futures and options trading on NSE. Stocks without an F&O segment cannot enter the index, regardless of size.

That is a hard filter. Several large Indian companies sit outside the benchmark purely because they lack an active derivatives segment.

Wipro's exit ends a long run. The IT major has been part of the Nifty 50 for roughly three decades, surviving multiple index reviews through the years.

Wipro is not leaving the NSE index family. It moves to the Nifty Next 50, which tracks the 50 companies ranked just below the benchmark.

The flow math is what matters for traders tomorrow. Nuvama estimates around $741 million of passive buying in BSE as index funds and ETFs adjust their portfolios.

That buying is mandatory, not discretionary. Passive funds must replicate the index, whatever the price.

Domestic index funds and ETFs form the bulk of this money. India-linked foreign index products add to the total.

On the other side, Wipro could see roughly $246 million of passive selling. That is the mechanical outflow from funds that no longer need to hold the stock.

Passive flows of this size tend to concentrate at the open. Index-tracking funds typically execute their rebalancing on the effective date itself.

The market has already priced in part of this move. On August 10, BSE shares rose 4% to close at Rs 3,596.80 on the announcement day.

Wipro fell 1.29% to Rs 185.11 the same day. Front-running by active funds often starts the moment a semi-annual review outcome becomes clear.

The Nifty 50 swap is only the headline. The Nifty 100 adds five names: BSE, Hitachi Energy India, Polycab India, Vedanta Aluminium Metal, and Vodafone Idea.

The Nifty 100 removes five: Indian Hotels, Lodha Developers, REC, Shree Cement, and United Spirits.

The Nifty Next 50 mirrors part of this shuffle. It adds Hitachi Energy, Polycab, Vedanta Aluminium, Vodafone Idea, and the incoming Wipro.

The Nifty Next 50 drops Indian Hotels, Lodha Developers, REC, Shree Cement, and United Spirits.

The Nifty 500 sees a wider turnover. Twenty-seven stocks enter and twenty-seven exit, including additions like Aether Industries, Avanti Feeds, Azad Engineering, Bharat Coking Coal, CleanMax, Embassy REIT, and Fractal Analytics.

Embassy REIT's entry is notable. It marks a further broadening of NSE equity indices beyond plain-vanilla operating companies.

Funds tracking the Nifty 100, Nifty 500 and Nifty Next 100 all rebalance on the same date. The combined turnover across these products adds up to a busy session for dealers.

The same swap applies to the Nifty50 Equal Weight index. Any fund or ETF tracking that variant will mirror the BSE-for-Wipro change.

For BSE, the inclusion is a milestone. The exchange operator joins the very benchmark run by its larger rival, NSE.

The timing adds a layer of irony. NSE itself only recently listed on the bourses, and BSE's entry into the Nifty 50 comes just weeks after its rival became a listed stock.

Beyond flows, index membership improves a stock's visibility. It enters model portfolios, stock screeners, and institutional watchlists that filter strictly for Nifty 50 names.

Liquidity typically deepens after inclusion. Average daily traded volumes in BSE shares have historically risen after each step up the NSE index ladder.

For index investors, the practical impact shows up in fund portfolios. Every Nifty 50 index fund and ETF will hold BSE from tomorrow and will have sold its Wipro stake.

Investors should separate the flow story from fundamentals. Index inclusion changes demand for the stock, not the underlying business.

That cuts both ways. BSE's passive buying tailwind can fade once rebalancing ends, and Wipro's selling pressure can ease once index funds finish exiting.

What to watch tomorrow: BSE's opening price and volumes in the first hour, and whether Wipro's decline extends beyond the estimated outflow amount. Active traders often fade the flow-driven move after the initial spike.

The September review was announced well in advance. Much of the flow effect may already be in the price, which softens the impact for anyone chasing tomorrow's open.

This is index mechanics, not a verdict on either company. BSE's entry reflects its swollen valuation and turnover; Wipro's exit reflects the IT sector's derating over recent years.
