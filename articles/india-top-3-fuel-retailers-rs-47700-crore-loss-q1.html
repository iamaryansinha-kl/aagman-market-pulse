India's Top 3 Fuel Retailers May Report Rs 47,700 Crore Loss in Q1

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

India's fuel retailers are facing a massive hit.

The country's top three public sector oil marketing companies — Indian Oil, Bharat Petroleum, and Hindustan Petroleum — may report a combined loss of Rs 47,700 crore in the June quarter. The losses stem from selling petrol and diesel at prices below their procurement and refining costs.

The losses reflect a pricing freeze that lasted through most of the quarter. Despite crude oil prices rising above $80 per barrel, retail fuel prices in India were not adjusted upwards. The retailers absorbed the difference, selling fuel at a loss to consumers.

Indian Oil is expected to bear the largest share of the losses as the biggest player. BPCL and HPCL will also report significant red ink. All three companies had reported profits in the previous quarter when crude prices were lower and margins were healthy.

The government typically compensates oil marketing companies for losses on fuel sales. However, the mechanism is often slow and partial. Retailers may have to wait months for reimbursement, straining their cash flows and balance sheets.

The Q1 losses will be reflected in upcoming earnings announcements. Investors have already been selling shares of the oil marketing companies, anticipating weak results. BPCL and HPCL shares have underperformed the broader market in recent weeks.

The episode highlights the challenge of administered fuel pricing in India. While keeping retail prices stable helps control inflation, it imposes heavy costs on public sector retailers and ultimately on government finances.
