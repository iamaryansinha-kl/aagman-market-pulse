SkyHop Aviation Gets AOC, India's First Scheduled Seaplane Operator

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/SkyHop Aviation has secured its Air Operator Certificate from DGCA, becoming India's first dedicated scheduled seaplane operator on April 27, 2026.
  • 02/The Avani Singh-led venture will begin with a 19-seater De Havilland Canada DHC-6 Twin Otter aircraft connecting five Lakshadweep islands to the mainland.
  • 03/India currently has zero operational seaplane services after SpiceJet's 2019-20 Gujarat service shut during Covid; industry estimates demand for 50-100 aircraft over the next five years.

SkyHop Aviation has received its Air Operator Certificate from the Directorate General of Civil Aviation, becoming India's first dedicated scheduled seaplane operator effective April 27, 2026. The company was founded by Avani Singh, daughter of SpiceJet Chairman Ajay Singh, and was incorporated on November 29, 2018 with a paid-up capital of ₹5.00 crore.

Operations will begin with a 19-seater De Havilland Canada DHC-6 Twin Otter aircraft modified with floats for water landings. In the first phase, SkyHop will connect five islands in Lakshadweep with each other and the mainland, while also evaluating routes in the Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Andhra Pradesh, and the North-East.

The venture secured its No Objection Certificate and security clearance in June 2024, signed its first aircraft lease on February 4, 2026, and completed a successful test flight at Ganga Barrage in Rishikesh on April 6, 2026.

India currently has zero operational seaplane services. The previous attempt came in 2019-20 when SpiceJet operated flights between Sabarmati riverfront and the Statue of Unity in Gujarat, a service Prime Minister Narendra Modi flew on before it was discontinued during the Covid-19 pandemic.

Only around 170 of India's nearly 4,000 cities currently have air connectivity, leaving significant room for regional aviation expansion. Globally, seaplanes serve island and tourism-heavy regions with the Maldives alone operating approximately 100 seaplane aircraft as a benchmark for India's nascent market.

The Centre supports the segment through the UDAN 5.5 scheme, which allows operators to develop routes across 80 identified water bodies. Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman announced incentives for indigenising seaplane manufacturing and a viability gap funding scheme to support commercial operations.

Industry estimates suggest India needs 20-25 water aerodromes and more than 50 seaplane routes over the next five years, with a projected aircraft requirement of 50-100 seaplanes. "Receiving the AOC is an important milestone for us and the result of consistent effort over the past year," said Avani Singh, Founder and CEO, SkyHop Aviation.

Several state governments have expressed interest in water aerodrome development. However, the segment faces challenges including infrastructure build-out, maintenance costs, and regulatory complexity unique to water-based aircraft operations.
