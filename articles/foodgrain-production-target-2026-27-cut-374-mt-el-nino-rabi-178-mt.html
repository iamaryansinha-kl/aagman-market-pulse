Foodgrain Target 2026-27 Cut to 373.93 MT Amid El Nino Fears

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Government sets 2026-27 foodgrain production target at 373.93 MT, 2.63 MT below the 2025-26 record estimate of 376.56 MT
  • 02/Rabi (winter-sown) foodgrain target for 2026-27 fixed at 178 MT as Centre prepares for possible El Nino-linked crop losses
  • 03/Centre assures states of adequate fertilisers, certified seeds and funds; watch monsoon rainfall June-September and FCI buffer stocks

The Union government on Tuesday, September 29, 2026 set a foodgrain production target of 373.93 million tonnes (MT) for the 2026-27 crop year (July-June). The target is 2.63 MT below the record 2025-26 output estimate of 376.56 MT.

The downgrade is the first meaningful reduction after two years of record harvests. It signals that New Delhi is bracing for weather stress rather than betting on continued output growth.

The rabi, or winter-sown, foodgrain target for 2026-27 has been fixed at 178 MT. Rabi crops — primarily wheat, gram and mustard — are planted from October and harvested from March.

The Centre has assured states of adequate availability of fertilisers, certified seeds and financial resources for the rabi season. The commitment is part of stepped-up preparedness for possible El Nino-linked crop losses.

CONTEXT: El Nino, the warming of equatorial Pacific waters, historically weakens the south-west monsoon over India. Academic studies of past El Nino years show kharif foodgrain output falling 8-12% on average, with stress sometimes extending into rabi through lower reservoir and soil-moisture levels.

Global agencies including Copernicus and the FAO have flagged El Nino conditions persisting through 2026-27. Below-average rainfall is considered likely in parts of India during June-September 2026.

India is the world's second-largest producer of rice and wheat. The scale of the harvest makes even a 2.63 MT trim significant for procurement, stocks and prices.

The 2025-26 crop year was a record. Third Advance Estimates released on May 27, 2026 put output at 376.56 MT, up 5.3% from 357.73 MT in 2024-25.

Rice production in 2025-26 was estimated at a record 154.02 MT, while wheat output was pegged at 120.65 MT. Maize hit a new high of 55.09 MT.

It helps to understand what this number is. Production targets are aspirational planning figures set ahead of the season, while actual output estimates come later from the government's Crop Weather Watch and advance estimate releases.

The kharif-rabi split offers some cushion. Around half of India's foodgrain area is under irrigation, which partially insulates rabi crops from a weak monsoon.

IMPACT: For investors, the first transmission channel is food inflation. A monsoon shortfall typically shows up in food prices with a 3-6 month lag, and food carries roughly a 46% weight in the CPI basket.

Lower production targets also imply tighter procurement by state agencies. That raises the political stakes around minimum support prices and puts buffer-stock management by the Food Corporation of India under scrutiny.

The government's assurance on fertiliser and seed supply is a signal for agri-input demand. It also matters for FMCG and rural-demand stocks, where farm incomes drive consumption.

Agriculture Minister Shivraj Singh Chouhan has credited improved seed quality, climate-resilient varieties and 339 new crop varieties released by ICAR in 2025-26 for the recent output surge.

The risk is two-sided. A normal or better-than-feared monsoon could still deliver output near the 2025-26 record, making the 373.93 MT target look conservative.

Conversely, a strong El Nino could push realised output well below target. That scenario would pressure FCI buffer stocks and invite tighter export policy on rice, wheat and sugar.
