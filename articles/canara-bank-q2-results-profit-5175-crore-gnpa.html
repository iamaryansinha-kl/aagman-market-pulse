Canara Q2 Profit Up 8.4% to Rs 5,175 Crore as Bad Loans Shrink

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Canara Bank reported Q2 net profit of Rs 5,175 crore, up 8.4% from Rs 4,774 crore a year earlier.
  • •- Net interest income rose 20.4% to Rs 11,005 crore; net interest margin improved to 2.55% from 2.52%.
  • •- Gross NPAs fell to 1.33% from 1.57% a quarter ago and 2.35% a year ago.
  • •- Provisions dropped to Rs 1,782 crore from Rs 2,354 crore in the year-ago quarter.
  • •- Global advances grew 19.5% to Rs 13.75 lakh crore, outpacing deposit growth of 13.13%.
  • •- Deposits were buoyed by $5.8 billion of FCNR-B inflows through the RBI's dedicated window.
  • •- Non-interest income fell 18% to Rs 5,772 crore, capping overall earnings growth.

Canara Bank on Tuesday delivered the kind of quarter India's public sector banks have been striving for: double-digit net interest income growth, sharply cleaner asset quality, and loan growth strong enough to outrun deposits — offset only by a weak treasury performance that kept the bottom line to a single-digit gain.

Net profit for the July-September quarter came in at Rs 5,175 crore, up 8.4 per cent from the Rs 4,774 crore reported a year earlier. The engine was core income: net interest income rose 20.4 per cent to Rs 11,005 crore, and the net interest margin improved to 2.55 per cent from 2.52 per cent in the preceding quarter — a modest expansion, but achieved while the system's funding costs are climbing and the central bank is tightening policy.

The asset quality story was the quarter's standout. Gross non-performing assets fell to 1.33 per cent at the end of September, from 1.57 per cent three months earlier and 2.35 per cent a year ago — a decline of more than a percentage point on the year that few large lenders have matched. With the loan book cleaner, provisions dropped to Rs 1,782 crore from Rs 2,354 crore in the year-ago quarter, directly padding the profit line.

Growth is running well ahead of the system. Global advances rose 19.5 per cent year on year to Rs 13.75 lakh crore, outpacing global deposit growth of 13.13 per cent to Rs 16.97 lakh crore. Managing director Brajesh Kumar Singh said the bank is working to narrow that gap and expects high advance growth to sustain, backed by festive-season demand.

One curiosity in the numbers: a substantial slice of the deposit growth arrived from overseas. Canara's books absorbed $5.8 billion — about Rs 53,000 crore — through foreign currency non-resident deposits under the Reserve Bank of India's dedicated window. The FCNR-B inflows flattered the deposit base, though they also signal how Indian banks are collectively helping the RBI defend the rupee by mobilising dollar deposits from the diaspora rather than drawing down reserves.

The drag came from the fee-and-markets side. Non-interest income fell 18 per cent to Rs 5,772 crore, and treasury income was just Rs 499 crore — down 68.5 per cent from a year earlier, when bond-market gains had flattered bank P&Ls. Total income rose 6 per cent to Rs 40,931 crore, and operating profit eked out a 2 per cent gain to Rs 8,758 crore. In a quarter when government bond yields spiked to eighteen-month highs, Canara's treasury book was a cost centre rather than a contributor — a pattern likely to repeat across PSU banks this results season.

The composition of the quarter tells a clear story about where PSU bank economics are heading. Pre-provision profitability increasingly rests on the lending spread — volume growth times margin — rather than on treasury windfalls or fee income. That makes Canara's 20 per cent NII growth and 19.5 per cent advance growth the metrics that matter, and its weak non-interest income an accepted trade-off. On the asset quality front, a 1.33 per cent GNPA ratio puts the bank comfortably among the cleaner large PSU lenders and lowers the provision burden that has historically consumed earnings.

The market liked what it saw: the stock closed up 3.59 per cent at Rs 124.76 on Tuesday ahead of the results. The open questions are the ones the whole sector faces — whether 19.5 per cent loan growth survives a tightening cycle intact, whether deposit pricing pressure compresses the margin as FCNR-driven flows roll off, and whether the RBI's rate hikes, delivered to fight imported inflation, cool the very credit demand currently powering the advance book.

For now, Canara's quarter reads as the system in miniature: strong lending engines, cleaner balance sheets, and a treasury line at the mercy of a bond market that has turned hostile. The banks that can keep NII compounding at 20 per cent through that transition will justify the re-rating; Canara's first half suggests it intends to be one of them.

Two nuances deserve attention beneath the headline. First, the margin level: 2.55 per cent, while improving, remains well below what private sector peers earn — a structural gap reflecting the bank's legacy low-yield book and PSU funding costs. Growth is doing the heavy lifting that pricing cannot. Second, the deposit-advances arithmetic: lending at 19.5 per cent against deposit growth of 13.13 per cent is sustainable only while the bank can keep widening its liability franchise or while windows like FCNR-B keep supplying dollars; persistently outgrowing deposits eventually forces either slower credit or costlier money.

The festive-season guidance is the near-term tell. Singh's comment that advance growth will sustain on festive demand puts a marker down for the third quarter, when consumer credit typically peaks. If Canara can hold loan growth near 19 per cent through the RBI's tightening cycle while keeping GNPA below 1.5 per cent, the earnings trajectory will largely take care of itself — regardless of what the treasury desk books in a volatile bond market.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT WERE CANARA BANK'S Q2 NUMBERS?
Net profit rose 8.4% to Rs 5,175 crore, net interest income grew 20.4% to Rs 11,005 crore, and NIM improved to 2.55% from 2.52%.
HOW DID ASSET QUALITY PERFORM?
Gross NPAs fell to 1.33% from 1.57% in the prior quarter and 2.35% a year ago; provisions dropped to Rs 1,782 crore from Rs 2,354 crore.
WHAT DROVE DEPOSIT GROWTH?
Global deposits rose 13.13% to Rs 16.97 lakh crore, including $5.8 billion (about Rs 53,000 crore) of FCNR-B inflows through the RBI's dedicated window.
WHAT WAS THE WEAK SPOT?
Non-interest income fell 18% to Rs 5,772 crore and treasury income collapsed 68.5% to Rs 499 crore as bond yields rose sharply. GLOSSARY: - Net interest income: Interest earned on loans minus interest paid on deposits. - GNPA: Gross non-performing assets — loans where borrowers have stopped paying; lower is better. - Provisions: Money set aside against potential loan losses. - FCNR-B: Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank) deposits, dollar deposits from Indians overseas. - Treasury income: Profits from trading and valuing a bank's bond and securities book.
