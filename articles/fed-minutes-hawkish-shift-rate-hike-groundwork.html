Fed Minutes Signal Hawkish Shift: Majority Ready to Lay Groundwork for Rate Hike

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Majority of Fed policymakers at April 28-29 meeting said tightening may be needed if inflation stays above 2% target, driven by Iran war pressures
  • 02/Four officials dissented at the meeting, the most since 1992; Governor Miran favored a cut while three opposed easing-bias language
  • 03/Incoming Fed Chair Kevin Warsh faces an increasingly hawkish committee; 2-year Treasury yield surged above 4.10% on policy repricing

The Federal Reserve minutes from the April 28-29 meeting reveal a sharply hawkish shift. A growing majority said policy tightening may be needed if inflation persists above the 2% target.

The meeting, the last chaired by Jerome Powell, saw four policymakers dissent. That marks the most dissents at a Fed meeting since 1992.

Governor Stephen Miran dissented in favor of a rate cut. Three other officials opposed keeping language that suggested a future easing bias.

The hawkish drift stems from inflation pressures aggravated by the U.S.-Israel-led war against Iran. The nearly three-month conflict has driven oil prices up over 50%.

Many participants wanted to remove the post-meeting statement language suggesting an easing bias. They see price pressures widening beyond energy into broader goods and services.

The FOMC held the policy rate at 3.50% to 3.75%. Kevin Warsh will be sworn in as the new Fed chair amid this divided landscape.

The 2-year U.S. Treasury yield jumped from below 3.40% on February 27 to above 4.10% Tuesday. Markets have repriced sharply away from rate-cut expectations.

A Reuters poll showed fewer than 50% of economists now expect a rate cut by December. That is down from two-thirds just one month earlier.
