Jane Street vs Sebi at SAT: 'Regulator Changed the Goal Posts'

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Jane Street's counsel told SAT the regulator "changed the goal posts" after the firm explained its trading strategy in a 12-page note on August 30, 2024
  • 02/The firm paid Sebi's deposit demand before any hearing, calling it an ex-parte order — the largest of its kind issued by the regulator
  • 03/Sebi received 42 complaints but refused to disclose the complainants' identities; Jane Street called that position "untenable"

Jane Street hit back at Sebi's allegations at the Securities Appellate Tribunal on Thursday. The firm's counsel argued the regulator "changed the goal posts" after the fact, and that this is the heart of its defence.

The world's most profitable trading firm is mounting a two-pronged attack on the Sebi order. One prong is procedural — an ex-parte deposit order it calls the largest the regulator has ever issued. The other is substantive — its strategy was disclosed in writing long before the order came.

"Why on earth would I want to adopt dilatory tactics? My money is at stake. My reputation is on the line," senior counsel Darius Khambata told the tribunal. The firm wants a quick resolution, he said, and has no reason to delay.

On substance, the defence is blunt. "Largeness of trading doesn't mean manipulation. Why is Sebi scared of large trading?" Khambata asked the bench, pressing the argument that scale alone is not evidence of wrongdoing.

The "changed goal posts" argument rests on a document trail. Jane Street submitted a detailed 12-page note on August 30, 2024, explaining its trading strategy — before the exchange and investor surveillance department reports were prepared. The firm also held multiple calls with NSE and Sebi officials.

"These are global practices of hedging... we have nothing to hide," Khambata said. The implication is clear: the regulator knew the strategy, and the rules moved only afterwards.

On procedure, Jane Street paid the money Sebi sought without being heard, describing the direction as an ex-parte order. It has also halted its trading activity in India. The firm argued the deposit order was the largest of its kind issued by Sebi, and that any order with civil consequences is adjudication — meaning a fair hearing is a constitutional right.

Then there are the complainants. Sebi received 42 complaints in the matter but has refused to disclose their identities. "This is untenable," Khambata said, questioning what the secrecy suggests about how the probe began. He also objected to Sebi masking counterparty details in documents shared with the firm, asking: "Don't change the rule post facto. What is so secret about the communication between Sebi and NSE? That's part of my defence."

Sebi, for its part, has previously labelled Jane Street's legal tactics dilatory and is contesting the firm's document demands — a tussle covered in earlier hearings. What to watch next: whether the SAT bench engages with the August 2024 disclosure timeline, and how it treats the constitutional fair-hearing argument.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT DID JANE STREET ARGUE AT THE SAT HEARING ON THURSDAY?
JANE STREET'S COUNSEL DARIUS KHAMBATA TOLD THE SECURITIES APPELLATE TRIBUNAL THAT SEBI "CHANGED THE GOAL POSTS" AFTER THE FACT. THE FIRM ARGUED ITS TRADING STRATEGY WAS DISCLOSED IN WRITING TO SEBI AND NSE IN A 12-PAGE NOTE DATED AUGUST 30, 2024 — MONTHS BEFORE THE SURVEILLANCE REPORTS WERE PREPARED.
DID JANE STREET PAY THE DEPOSIT SEBI DEMANDED?
YES. THE FIRM DEPOSITED THE MONEY SEBI SOUGHT EVEN WITHOUT BEING HEARD, DESCRIBING THE DIRECTION AS AN EX-PARTE ORDER. IT HAS ALSO HALTED ITS TRADING ACTIVITY IN INDIA. JANE STREET ARGUES THE DEPOSIT ORDER WAS THE LARGEST OF ITS KIND ISSUED BY SEBI.
WHAT IS JANE STREET'S DEFENCE ON ITS TRADING STRATEGY?
THE FIRM'S CORE ARGUMENT IS THAT "LARGENESS OF TRADING DOESN'T MEAN MANIPULATION." IT SAYS ITS ACTIVITY REFLECTS GLOBAL PRACTICES OF HEDGING, THAT IT HAS "NOTHING TO HIDE," AND THAT IT EXPLAINED THE STRATEGY TO NSE AND SEBI OFFICIALS IN WRITING AND ON MULTIPLE CALLS BEFORE ANY ACTION WAS TAKEN.
HOW MANY COMPLAINTS DID SEBI RECEIVE AGAINST JANE STREET?
SEBI RECEIVED 42 COMPLAINTS IN THE MATTER. JANE STREET HAS QUESTIONED WHY THE REGULATOR REFUSED TO DISCLOSE THE COMPLAINANTS' IDENTITIES, CALLING THAT POSITION "UNTENABLE." THE FIRM HAS ALSO OBJECTED TO SEBI MASKING COUNTERPARTY DETAILS IN DOCUMENTS SHARED WITH IT.
WHY DOES JANE STREET SAY A FAIR HEARING IS ITS CONSTITUTIONAL RIGHT?
THE FIRM ARGUES THAT ANY ORDER WITH CIVIL CONSEQUENCES — SUCH AS THE DEPOSIT DIRECTION — AMOUNTS TO ADJUDICATION. UNDER THAT VIEW, NATURAL JUSTICE REQUIRES A HEARING BEFORE SUCH AN ORDER IS PASSED, AND PASSING IT EX-PARTE DENIES A CONSTITUTIONALLY PROTECTED RIGHT. GLOSSARY: Ex-parte order: A regulatory or judicial order passed without hearing the other side first. Jane Street says Sebi's deposit direction was ex-parte — issued before the firm got a hearing. Securities Appellate Tribunal (SAT): The specialised tribunal that hears appeals against Sebi orders. Jane Street's challenge to the regulator is being argued here. Hedging: A trading strategy that offsets risk by taking opposite positions in related instruments. Jane Street says its strategy was standard global hedging practice, disclosed to NSE and Sebi in 2024.
