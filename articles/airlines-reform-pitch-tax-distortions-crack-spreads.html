Airlines Flag Tax Distortions And Crack Spreads In Reform Pitch Beyond Fuel Pricing

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Airlines expand reform demands beyond fuel pricing to include GST structure, refinery margin discipline, and fuel taxation framework changes
  • 02/Industry flags 18% GST on premium-class tickets as threatening viability of long-haul international routes where premium cabins drive revenues
  • 03/Crack spreads surged during March 2026 crude spike, pushing ATF prices higher beyond global oil benchmark increases

India's airline industry has expanded its reform pitch beyond immediate fuel price relief to demand structural changes in taxation and pricing frameworks. The move comes after the Centre rolled out temporary measures to cushion carriers against rising jet fuel costs driven by the ongoing West Asia crisis.

In a formal representation to the Centre, airlines have flagged the Goods and Services Tax structure on air travel as a major concern. The increase in tax on premium-class tickets from 12 per cent to 18 per cent could affect the viability of long-haul international routes, where premium cabins contribute a significant share of revenues. Industry insiders warn that rising input costs combined with higher taxation on premium fares could weaken demand in revenue-critical segments and influence capacity deployment on key global sectors.

Airlines have also reiterated the need for discipline in refinery margins, or crack spreads, during periods of volatility. Industry estimates show these margins rose sharply during the March 2026 crude surge, pushing aviation turbine fuel prices higher beyond what global oil benchmark increases alone would justify. A senior airline executive told BusinessLine that refining margin spikes go beyond cost recovery and are difficult for carriers to absorb, noting the need for greater pricing predictability.

The industry has proposed a mechanism to smoothen fuel price shocks by deferring incremental costs over 12 months. The proposal is designed to ease liquidity pressure during sudden spikes while allowing oil marketing companies to recover full costs over time. This comes even as the Centre has already limited the cost pass-through of fuel price increases for domestic scheduled flights.

India's fuel taxation framework remains outside the GST regime, making such levies a non-creditable cost for airlines. Industry insiders describe this as effectively turning fuel taxes into a dead cost. At present, fuel accounts for up to 40 per cent of airline operating costs in India, significantly above global averages. The Centre is examining longer-term options such as bringing ATF under the GST framework, which industry estimates suggest could lower overall costs by up to 10 per cent.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT TAX REFORMS ARE AIRLINES DEMANDING BEYOND FUEL PRICING RELIEF?
AIRLINES ARE DEMANDING STRUCTURAL CHANGES INCLUDING BRINGING AVIATION TURBINE FUEL UNDER THE GST REGIME, REDUCING THE 18 PER CENT GST ON PREMIUM-CLASS TICKETS BACK TO 12 PER CENT, AND IMPOSING DISCIPLINE ON REFINERY CRACK SPREADS DURING VOLATILITY PERIODS.
WHY IS THE GST HIKE ON PREMIUM TICKETS A CONCERN FOR AIRLINES?
THE INCREASE FROM 12 PER CENT TO 18 PER CENT GST ON PREMIUM-CLASS TICKETS THREATENS THE VIABILITY OF LONG-HAUL INTERNATIONAL ROUTES WHERE PREMIUM CABINS CONTRIBUTE A SIGNIFICANT SHARE OF REVENUES. HIGHER TAXATION COULD WEAKEN DEMAND IN THESE REVENUE-CRITICAL SEGMENTS.
WHAT ARE CRACK SPREADS AND WHY DO AIRLINES WANT THEM CONTROLLED?
CRACK SPREADS ARE REFINERY MARGINS THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN CRUDE OIL PRICES AND REFINED PRODUCT PRICES. AIRLINES SAY THESE MARGINS SPIKED SHARPLY DURING THE MARCH 2026 CRUDE SURGE, PUSHING ATF PRICES HIGHER BEYOND WHAT GLOBAL OIL BENCHMARK INCREASES ALONE WOULD JUSTIFY.
WHAT IS THE PROPOSED 12-MONTH COST DEFERRAL MECHANISM?
AIRLINES HAVE PROPOSED DEFERRING INCREMENTAL FUEL COSTS OVER 12 MONTHS TO EASE LIQUIDITY PRESSURE DURING SUDDEN PRICE SPIKES WHILE ALLOWING OIL MARKETING COMPANIES TO RECOVER FULL COSTS OVER TIME. THE MECHANISM MANAGES TIMING RATHER THAN AVOIDING COSTS.
HOW MUCH OF AIRLINE OPERATING COSTS GO TO FUEL IN INDIA?
FUEL ACCOUNTS FOR UP TO 40 PER CENT OF AIRLINE OPERATING COSTS IN INDIA, WHICH IS SIGNIFICANTLY ABOVE GLOBAL AVERAGES. INDUSTRY ESTIMATES SUGGEST THAT RATIONALISING FUEL TAXATION COULD LOWER OVERALL COSTS BY UP TO 10 PER CENT.
