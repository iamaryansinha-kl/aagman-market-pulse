Nifty IT Jumps 4%: Infosys, TCS Surge Up To 5% After 30% H1 Crash

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Nifty IT index surged over 4% to 26,781 on July 2, 2026, with Infosys and TCS jumping up to 5%
  • 02/The IT index had crashed 30% in H1CY26 versus a 9% decline in Nifty 50, and lost 29% over one year
  • 03/Experts remain cautious; a sustained rerating needs proof of demand recovery and earnings stabilization

The Nifty IT index surged over 4% to 26,781 around 10:10 am on July 2, 2026. Infosys, TCS, Coforge, Mphasis, HCL Tech, and Persistent Systems jumped up to 5% during the session.

The IT index hit an over three-year low of 25,699 just a day earlier on July 1. The sharp bounce came after a brutal 30% crash in the first half of calendar year 2026.

Over the last one year, the IT index has lost 29%. Some individual stocks have crashed up to 40% from their peaks.

By contrast, the benchmark Nifty 50 was up just 0.40% at 24,096. The IT pack clearly outpaced the broader market on Thursday.

Ajit Mishra, SVP of Research at Religare Broking, called the move a technical bounce. "I don't see value in the sector at this juncture. I doubt the rally can sustain unless we hear encouraging commentary from management during the upcoming earnings season," Mishra said.

Brokerage Motilal Oswal Financial Services echoed caution. It said a sustained rerating is possible only after evidence that demand is improving and revenue growth is stabilizing.

Rishabh Nahar, Partner and Fund Manager at Qode Advisors, said IT is a "show me" sector right now. "The core worry is structural: agentic AI tools are seen eating into application development, testing and maintenance work, with some estimates of a 10-12% revenue hit over the next 3-4 years," Nahar said.

Investors will closely watch enterprise spending trends and AI-related revenue during the upcoming Q1FY27 earnings season. Management guidance will be the key trigger for the next leg of the move.

For now, experts suggest staying on the sidelines unless companies prove that the 1-2% growth phase is behind them. Selective bottom-up bets in well-capitalized, diversified players may be considered, but a sector-wide call is premature.
