RBI Services Survey: Q3FY27 Demand Positive, Margins Subdued

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/RBI Services and Infrastructure Outlook Survey shows services demand outlook positive for Q3FY27, but business optimism moderated versus the previous quarter
  • 02/Services firms expect cost pressures to ease moderately, yet optimism on selling prices and profit margins remains relatively low
  • 03/Services companies indicated spare capacity of 8.7% in Q1FY27; the overall outlook stays optimistic through Q1FY28

India's services sector is expected to maintain a positive demand outlook in Q3FY27. However, business optimism has moderated compared with the previous quarter, according to the Reserve Bank of India's latest Services and Infrastructure Outlook Survey.

The survey is a quarterly RBI exercise covering services and infrastructure companies. It captures how firms assess the quarter just ended and what they expect on demand, costs, prices, employment and margins in the quarters ahead.

During Q2FY27, services sector companies reported an improvement in overall business conditions. Turnover and full-time employment also strengthened during the quarter, the RBI said.

The recovery came with a catch. "Firms reported increased cost pressures with some increase in selling prices, although profit margin sentiment remained subdued," the central bank noted.

That combination matters. Costs rose faster than prices, which means firms absorbed part of the squeeze. Subdued margin sentiment signals pricing power is weak even as volumes grow.

For Q3FY27, services firms remained optimistic about demand conditions. Their level of optimism, however, moderated compared with the previous quarter.

Cost pressures are expected to ease moderately in the coming quarter. "Firms expressed relatively lower optimism regarding selling prices and profit margins," the survey said.

The forward view stretches further out. "Respondents' outlook remains optimistic till Q1:2027-28," the RBI said, adding that input cost pressures are likely to sustain with expectations on selling prices remaining stable in the ensuing quarters.

In plain terms, services companies see demand holding up for at least the next three to four quarters. What they do not see is the ability to raise prices enough to rebuild margins.

On the supply side, services sector firms indicated they had the capacity to deliver 8.7 per cent more services during Q1FY27 using their existing resources. That is a significant buffer.

The spare-capacity number suggests demand can grow without fresh investment. It also implies firms have room to expand output quickly if orders accelerate.

Infrastructure companies told a similar story with a twist. During Q2FY27, they remained optimistic about overall business conditions, employment and turnover, and reported some easing in cost pressures.

Sentiment on selling prices and profit margins among infrastructure firms moderated, though. Their Q3FY27 expectations stayed confident on business conditions and turnover, while price and margin expectations weakened.

Looking ahead to Q4FY27 and Q1FY28, infrastructure firms remain optimistic on the overall business situation and their own turnover. "They also expect rise in input costs with concomitant rise in selling prices," the survey said.

That last point sets infrastructure apart from services. Infrastructure firms expect to pass higher input costs through to customers. Services firms, by contrast, expect selling prices to stay stable.

The macro read is one of growth without pricing power. Services demand is holding up and employment is expanding, but margins are the pressure point. It is a relevant signal for the Q2 earnings season now underway.

The split in the numbers is what makes this survey worth reading closely. On the demand side, the picture is intact: firms saw business conditions, turnover and full-time employment all improve through Q2FY27, and they expect demand conditions to stay positive through the current quarter. On the pricing side, the picture darkens: costs climbed, companies passed only some of that through to selling prices, and sentiment on profit margins stayed subdued.

That combination — volumes growing while pricing power weakens — is the classic shape of a margin squeeze. It is also consistent with the cost backdrop Indian services firms have been navigating: sticky input costs meeting customers who are price-sensitive. The survey's forward guidance captures the bind neatly. Input cost pressures are likely to sustain through the coming quarters, while expectations are for selling prices to remain stable. In plain terms, firms expect their costs to keep rising and their prices to stay put.

The capacity number adds a quieter but important signal. Services firms told the RBI they could deliver 8.7 per cent more output with their existing resources. That spare capacity means the sector can absorb stronger demand without heavy new investment — good for growth — but it also hints that firms have not been running hot enough to justify the capital spending that would tighten supply and restore pricing power.

For markets, the survey lands in the middle of the Q2 earnings season, which kicked off with TCS last week and brings HCL Technologies on Monday and Wipro later in the week. Services-sector commentary on demand and margins will be one of the threads investors test management commentary against.

The infrastructure half of the survey tells a related story with one key difference. Infrastructure companies were optimistic about business conditions, employment and turnover in Q2FY27, saw some easing in cost pressures, but saw sentiment on selling prices and profit margins moderate. Looking to Q3FY27, their confidence in conditions and turnover held up, while price and margin expectations weakened further.

Where the two sectors diverge is in what comes next. Infrastructure firms expect input costs to rise — and selling prices to rise along with them. That pass-through assumption is the sector's cushion, and it stands in contrast to services firms, who see stable prices ahead even as their own costs persist.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT DOES THE RBI SERVICES AND INFRASTRUCTURE OUTLOOK SURVEY SHOW FOR Q3FY27?
THE SURVEY SHOWS THAT SERVICES SECTOR FIRMS EXPECT A POSITIVE DEMAND OUTLOOK IN Q3FY27, ALTHOUGH THE LEVEL OF BUSINESS OPTIMISM HAS MODERATED COMPARED WITH THE PREVIOUS QUARTER. FIRMS ANTICIPATE SOME EASING IN COST PRESSURES, BUT THEY REMAIN LESS OPTIMISTIC ABOUT SELLING PRICES AND PROFIT MARGINS.
HOW DID SERVICES COMPANIES PERFORM IN Q2FY27?
SERVICES SECTOR COMPANIES REPORTED AN IMPROVEMENT IN OVERALL BUSINESS CONDITIONS, TURNOVER AND FULL-TIME EMPLOYMENT DURING Q2FY27. HOWEVER, FIRMS ALSO REPORTED INCREASED COST PRESSURES WITH SOME INCREASE IN SELLING PRICES, WHILE PROFIT MARGIN SENTIMENT REMAINED SUBDUED.
HOW MUCH SPARE CAPACITY DO SERVICES FIRMS HAVE?
SERVICES SECTOR FIRMS INDICATED THEY HAD THE CAPACITY TO DELIVER 8.7 PER CENT MORE SERVICES DURING Q1FY27 USING THEIR EXISTING RESOURCES. THIS POINTS TO DEMAND RESILIENCE WITH HEADROOM TO GROW OUTPUT WITHOUT FRESH INVESTMENT.
WHAT IS THE RBI'S FORWARD OUTLOOK BEYOND Q3FY27?
RESPONDENTS' OUTLOOK REMAINS OPTIMISTIC TILL Q1:2027-28. INPUT COST PRESSURES ARE LIKELY TO SUSTAIN, WITH EXPECTATIONS ON SELLING PRICES TO REMAIN STABLE IN THE ENSUING QUARTERS, COVERING Q4 2026-27 AND Q1 2027-28.
HOW DO INFRASTRUCTURE COMPANIES' EXPECTATIONS DIFFER FROM SERVICES FIRMS?
LIKE SERVICES FIRMS, INFRASTRUCTURE COMPANIES REMAIN OPTIMISTIC ABOUT OVERALL BUSINESS CONDITIONS AND TURNOVER FOR Q4FY27 AND Q1FY28. THE DIFFERENCE IS ON PRICING: INFRASTRUCTURE FIRMS EXPECT A RISE IN INPUT COSTS WITH A CONCOMITANT RISE IN SELLING PRICES, WHILE SERVICES FIRMS EXPECT SELLING PRICES TO REMAIN STABLE. GLOSSARY: Services and Infrastructure Outlook Survey: A quarterly Reserve Bank of India survey asking services and infrastructure companies about current business conditions and their expectations for the coming quarters. Profit margin sentiment: Survey respondents' assessment of how their profit margins are faring or will fare, distinct from actual reported profits. Spare capacity: The additional output a company says it can produce using its existing staff, equipment and facilities. Pass-through: The extent to which a business raises its selling prices in response to rising input costs. Selling prices: The prices at which firms sell their goods or services, as distinct from the input costs they pay.
