IMF Raises India's FY27 Growth Forecast To 7% From 6.5% In August

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/The IMF has raised India's GDP growth forecast for FY27 to 7%, up from 6.5% in its August forecast.
  • 02/The upgrade follows Q1FY27 growth of 7.8% and mirrors the RBI's own raise to 7.1% from 6.7% this month.
  • 03/The stronger growth outlook coexists with 5.54% inflation, a weaker rupee and a wider current account deficit.

The International Monetary Fund has lifted India's GDP growth forecast for the financial year 2026-27 to 7 per cent. The new number, reported on October 13, is up from the 6.5 per cent the fund pencilled in during August.

The upgrade is the second major endorsement of India's growth momentum this month. On October 1, the Reserve Bank of India raised its own FY27 projection to 7.1 per cent from 6.7 per cent.

The RBI pointed to robust activity indicators as the reason for its more optimistic view. The economy grew 7.8 per cent in the April-June quarter, the strongest pace in several quarters.

The IMF's revision suggests the fund sees that momentum as sustainable rather than a one-quarter spike. A full country breakdown is expected when the fund publishes its next detailed World Economic Outlook.

Growth of 7 per cent would keep India firmly the world's fastest-growing major economy. China, by comparison, is growing in the mid-single digits amid property sector stress and weak consumption.

The upgrade also lands in a tricky domestic macro environment. Retail inflation rose to 5.54 per cent in September, a two-year high and the fourth straight month above the RBI's 4 per cent target.

Food prices, an energy pass-through from the West Asia conflict and a deficient monsoon are all feeding price pressures. The RBI's own Q3FY27 inflation projection stands at 6 per cent.

That combination — strong growth, sticky inflation — is why the RBI held the repo rate at 5.5 per cent at its October review. Rate-cut expectations that dominated the first half of the year have been pushed out.

Some economists now see the risk of hikes. Oxford Economics expects two 25-basis-point increases, and Kotak Mahindra has flagged a possible 50-basis-point move if inflation breaches tolerance levels.

The external balance is also under pressure. The current account deficit widened to $14.4 billion in the June quarter from $9.7 billion in the March quarter, RBI data showed.

A $100-plus Brent crude on the West Asia conflict has bloated India's oil import bill. The rupee has weakened more than 6 per cent this year, prompting the RBI to roll out a series of support measures since the start of the week.

Since October 10, the central bank has tightened cash reserve norms, restricted rupee non-deliverable forwards to hedging only, and opened a special dollar window for state-run oil companies.

The rupee rallied 1 per cent on Monday after that package, its sharpest single-day gain in months. Bond yields, however, climbed to a three-year high of 7.37 per cent as liquidity tightened.

For policymakers, the IMF's endorsement strengthens the case that India can absorb the oil shock without derailing growth. The fund's revision echoes rating agencies and brokerages that have nudged their India forecasts higher over the past quarter.

The composition of growth matters as much as the headline. Government capital spending and services exports have led so far, while private manufacturing investment is still patchy outside a few sectors.

The IMF's endorsement follows similar nudges from private forecasters. Several brokerages now pencil in 7 per cent or higher for FY27, arguing that the investment cycle has finally turned.

Monetary policy space remains constrained. Households expect inflation of 9.1 per cent a year ahead, according to the RBI's September survey, leaving the central bank little room to support growth directly.

Rural demand, too, remains uneven. A deficient monsoon has clouded the kharif harvest, which is one reason food inflation refuses to cool.

For markets, the 7 per cent forecast is a medium-term positive. It supports earnings growth assumptions for FY27, even as near-term volatility from oil, rates and foreign investor flows persists.

The key risk to the outlook is a prolonged energy shock. If Brent stays above $100 through the winter, the inflation-growth trade-off will get harder and the RBI's next move may not be the cut markets once hoped for.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT DID THE IMF CHANGE IN ITS INDIA FORECAST?
THE IMF RAISED INDIA'S GDP GROWTH FORECAST FOR FY27 (2026-27) TO 7 PER CENT, UP FROM 6.5 PER CENT IN ITS AUGUST FORECAST, ACCORDING TO REPORTS ON OCTOBER 13, 2026.
WHAT IS THE RBI'S GROWTH PROJECTION?
THE RBI RAISED ITS FY27 PROJECTION TO 7.1 PER CENT FROM 6.7 PER CENT AT ITS OCTOBER 1 POLICY REVIEW. INDIA GREW 7.8 PER CENT IN Q1FY27.
WHY DID THE RBI NOT CUT RATES IF GROWTH IS STRONG?
BECAUSE INFLATION IS RISING — SEPTEMBER CPI HIT 5.54 PER CENT, A TWO-YEAR HIGH AND THE FOURTH STRAIGHT MONTH ABOVE THE 4 PER CENT TARGET. THE RBI HELD THE REPO RATE AT 5.5 PER CENT.
HOW IS THE EXTERNAL SECTOR HOLDING UP?
MIXED. THE CURRENT ACCOUNT DEFICIT WIDENED TO $14.4 BILLION IN Q2FY27 FROM $9.7 BILLION IN Q1FY27, AND THE RUPEE HAS FALLEN OVER 6 PER CENT THIS YEAR ON HIGH OIL PRICES.
WHAT ARE THE MAIN RISKS TO THE 7% GROWTH FORECAST?
A PROLONGED OIL SHOCK ABOVE $100 BRENT, STICKY FOOD INFLATION FROM A DEFICIENT MONSOON, AND TIGHTER GLOBAL FINANCIAL CONDITIONS THAT COULD FORCE THE RBI TO RAISE RATES INSTEAD OF CUTTING THEM. GLOSSARY: GDP growth forecast: An estimate of how much an economy's total output will expand over a given period. Fiscal year (FY): India's accounting year running April to March; FY27 means April 2026 to March 2027. Repo rate: The rate at which the RBI lends to banks, the benchmark for all interest rates in the economy. Current account deficit (CAD): The gap between a country's foreign exchange earnings and spending on trade and transfers. Tolerance band: The RBI's allowed inflation range of 2-6% around its 4% target. World Economic Outlook: The IMF's flagship publication with growth forecasts for the global economy and member countries.
