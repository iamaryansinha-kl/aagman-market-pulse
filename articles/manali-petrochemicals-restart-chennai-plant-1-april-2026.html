Manali Petrochemicals to Restart Chennai Plant-1 After 5-Week Shutdown

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Manali Petrochemicals Ltd announces restart of Plant-1 at Manali, Chennai on April 20, 2026, revoking the force majeure declared on March 11
  • 02/The 5-week shutdown was caused by a government directive forcing sole supplier CPCL to halt propylene supply and prioritize LPG production
  • 03/Plant-2 remained operational throughout using inventory; full production resumption and financial impact remain key watchpoints

Manali Petrochemicals Ltd (NSE: MANALIPETC) has announced the restart of operations at Plant-1 in Manali, Chennai. The company revoked its force majeure declaration in a stock exchange filing dated April 20, 2026.

Plant-1 was shut down on March 12, 2026 after Chennai Petroleum Corporation Ltd (CPCL) completely stopped supplying propylene. CPCL cited a directive from the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas (MoPNG) to prioritize Liquefied Petroleum Gas (LPG) production.

Propylene is the sole and indispensable feedstock for MPL's manufacturing operations. The company sources it exclusively from CPCL, making the supply cut a critical operational disruption.

During the 5-week shutdown, Plant-2 continued to operate using available feedstock inventory. The company did not quantify the financial impact, stating the force majeure was an ongoing event.

The government directive was issued amid geopolitical disruptions to international crude oil and petrochemical supply chains. CPCL was mandated to direct crude oil processing exclusively toward LPG production for the duration of the order.

MPL manufactures propylene oxide (PO), propylene glycol (PG), and polyols. It is India's only domestic producer of propylene glycol and the largest producer of propylene oxide.

The stock closed at ₹55.16 on April 20, 2026, with a market capitalization of ₹9.49 billion. Shares had fallen to ₹39.38 on March 30, 2026 from ₹49.99 on March 11, but recovered 40% ahead of the restart announcement.

Competitors including Tamil Nadu Petro Products Ltd, Supreme Petrochem Ltd, and Gujarat Petrosynthese Ltd also faced similar supply disruptions. TN Petro Products declared force majeure on its PO plant on March 17, 2026.

The restart eliminates the immediate operational risk, but investors should monitor whether propylene supply remains stable and how the 5-week production loss affects Q1 FY2027 earnings.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHEN DID MANALI PETROCHEMICALS RESTART PLANT-1?
MANALI PETROCHEMICALS LTD ANNOUNCED THE RESTART OF PLANT-1 AT MANALI, CHENNAI ON APRIL 20, 2026. THE COMPANY REVOKED ITS FORCE MAJEURE DECLARATION IN A FILING TO THE BSE AND NSE ON THE SAME DATE.
WHY WAS PLANT-1 SHUT DOWN IN MARCH 2026?
PLANT-1 WAS SHUT DOWN ON MARCH 12, 2026 AFTER CHENNAI PETROLEUM CORPORATION LTD (CPCL) COMPLETELY STOPPED SUPPLYING PROPYLENE. THE HALT FOLLOWED A DIRECTIVE FROM THE MINISTRY OF PETROLEUM AND NATURAL GAS (MOPNG) DIRECTING CPCL TO PRIORITIZE LPG PRODUCTION OVER DOWNSTREAM PETROCHEMICAL DERIVATIVES.
HOW LONG WAS THE SHUTDOWN AND WHAT WAS THE IMPACT ON OPERATIONS?
THE SHUTDOWN LASTED APPROXIMATELY 5 WEEKS FROM MARCH 12 TO APRIL 20, 2026. DURING THIS PERIOD, PLANT-2 CONTINUED OPERATING USING EXISTING FEEDSTOCK INVENTORY. THE COMPANY TERMINATED THE EVENT AS FORCE MAJEURE AND STATED THE FINANCIAL IMPACT COULD NOT BE ESTIMATED AT THAT TIME.
WHAT PRODUCTS DOES MANALI PETROCHEMICALS MANUFACTURE AND WHAT IS ITS MARKET POSITION?
MPL MANUFACTURES PROPYLENE OXIDE (PO), PROPYLENE GLYCOL (PG), AND POLYOLS. IT IS INDIA'S ONLY DOMESTIC PRODUCER OF PROPYLENE GLYCOL AND THE LARGEST PRODUCER OF PROPYLENE OXIDE. THESE PRODUCTS ARE USED IN POLYURETHANE, PHARMACEUTICALS, CONSTRUCTION MATERIALS, AND SPECIALTY CHEMICALS.
HOW DID THE STOCK PERFORM DURING AND AFTER THE SHUTDOWN?
MANALIPETC SHARES FELL FROM ₹49.99 ON MARCH 11, 2026 TO A LOW OF ₹39.38 ON MARCH 30, 2026 — A DECLINE OF 21.2%. THE STOCK THEN RECOVERED TO ₹55.16 BY APRIL 20, 2026, GAINING 40% FROM THE MARCH LOW. THE COMPANY'S MARKET CAPITALIZATION STANDS AT ₹9.49 BILLION.
