LTIMindtree Q2: AI Revenue Up 47%, Profit Flat, Rs 25 Dividend

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Revenue rose 13.6% YoY to Rs 11,807.6 crore; EBIT rose 11.5% to Rs 1,838.1 crore.
  • •- PAT was flat at Rs 1,379.9 crore, down 0.1% YoY; dollar profit fell 7.9% to $144 million.
  • •- Enterprise AI and Business AI revenue combined grew 47% YoY.
  • •- Client metrics improved across every size band, including a third $100 million-plus client.
  • •- An interim dividend of Rs 25 per share was declared.
  • •- The stock rose 3.66% to Rs 4,390 but remains down 28% this year.

The mid-tier IT majors are producing the season's most instructive split: revenue engines running hot, profit engines running cold. LTIMindtree on Friday reported September-quarter revenue growth of 13.6 per cent to Rs 11,807.6 crore and EBIT growth of 11.5 per cent to Rs 1,838.1 crore — and yet profit after tax was essentially unchanged at Rs 1,379.9 crore, down 0.1 per cent from a year earlier. In dollar terms the picture was starker still: net profit fell 7.9 per cent year-on-year to $144 million.

The headline explanation is the one the whole sector is living with: AI-shaped revenue is arriving faster than AI-shaped margins. LTM's combined Enterprise AI and Business AI revenue grew 47 per cent year-on-year, the kind of growth rate that would have been implausible for a services firm five years ago. But building and delivering those engagements requires expensive talent, fresh delivery tooling and the reinvestment treadmill that every IT company now runs on. Revenue up 13.6 per cent, profit flat — that is what the AI transition looks like in a P&L before the operating leverage kicks in.

The client metrics suggest the growth is broad rather than concentrated. LTM ended the quarter with 729 active clients after adding 20 new logos, and the pyramid strengthened at every level: clients generating more than $5 million in revenue rose 14 to 172, the $10 million-plus band expanded by 16 to 109, the $20 million-plus band added six to reach 51, and both the $50 million-plus and $100 million-plus categories gained one each, taking the totals to 15 and three. Notably, the newest $100 million-plus client is a financial services name — a vertical where LTM has been investing deliberately. CEO and managing director Venu Lambu credited the company's AI-centric strategy and technology-domain convergence for the performance, adding a financial services client to its top revenue band and declaring the company "well positioned for stronger growth ahead."

The operational numbers tell a tighter story. Headcount stood at 88,835, trailing-12-month attrition was 13.3 per cent, and utilisation excluding trainees ran at 86.4 per cent — a firm utilisation level that, combined with flat profit, again points to wage and delivery costs absorbing what pricing could not. The board declared an interim dividend of Rs 25 per share on a Re 1 face value, payable within 30 days to shareholders on record as of October 23 — a generous payout ratio that softens the profit optics and signals confidence in cash generation.

Context is everything with this print. LTM's stock closed Friday's session up 3.66 per cent at Rs 4,390, but it remains down 28.17 per cent for the year, with a market capitalisation of roughly Rs 1,30,216 crore. The year-to-date decline reflects an earlier derating of mid-cap IT rather than company-specific damage, and Friday's reaction — a rally on a flat-profit quarter — suggests the market is rewarding the AI revenue growth and taking the margin question as a timing issue. The same pattern showed up in results from larger and smaller peers this week: bookings and AI-led demand are strong, currency is a headwind, and the margin recovery is always one quarter away.

The forward question for LTM is whether the 47 per cent AI growth rate compounds into the kind of $50-100 million engagements that reshape a services firm's mix, or remains a fast-growing slice within a portfolio still anchored to traditional work. The third $100 million client helps the bull case; flat profit despite 13.6 per cent revenue growth helps the bear's. Friday's dividend, at least, gives shareholders something certain while they wait for the margins to follow the momentum.

It is also worth locating LTM within the season's broader IT pattern. The larger peers that reported earlier in the week showed the same basic trade: solid revenue growth, AI-driven demand, and margins squeezed between reinvestment and currency. What differentiates the mid-tier names like LTM is the mix shift speed — smaller legacy books convert faster — which is precisely why the AI growth number, 47 per cent, is the single most important figure in this print. If that rate holds for even two more quarters, the composition of LTM's revenue will have changed more than any traditional peer's; if it decelerates sharply, the flat profit starts to look less like a transition cost and more like the new normal. The stock's 28 per cent year-to-date decline suggests the market has been pricing the latter scenario; Friday's rally suggests the print nudged some investors toward the former.

CEO Venu Lambu's confidence about "stronger growth ahead" will be tested against the same headwinds every Indian IT firm faces: a strong rupee by recent standards, a US market that remains the destination for most of its revenue, and client budgets that keep shifting toward AI while total spending stays constrained. LTM's answer — technology-domain convergence, deeper financial-services penetration, and a dividend while it waits — is a reasonable one. The next two quarters will show whether reasonable is enough.

WHAT WERE LTIMINDTREE'S Q2 HEADLINES?
Revenue rose 13.6% to Rs 11,807.6 crore, PAT was flat at Rs 1,379.9 crore, and combined AI revenue grew 47% YoY.
WHY WAS PROFIT FLAT DESPITE STRONG REVENUE?
Heavy reinvestment in AI delivery capability, talent costs and currency effects absorbed the operating leverage; dollar profit fell 7.9% to $144 million.
WHAT ABOUT THE DIVIDEND?
An interim dividend of Rs 25 per share (face value Re 1) was declared, record date October 23.
HOW DID CLIENT METRICS MOVE?
729 active clients, 20 new additions; $5mn+ clients at 172, $10mn+ at 109, and a third client entering the $100mn+ band.
