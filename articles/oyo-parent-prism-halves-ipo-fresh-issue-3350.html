OYO Parent PRISM Halves IPO Fresh Issue to Rs 3,350 Crore

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- PRISM plans to nearly halve the fresh issue in its IPO to up to Rs 3,350 crore from about Rs 6,650 crore.
  • •- The cut follows continued strong cash generation and an improving balance sheet, reducing the capital the company needs.
  • •- The original plan earmarked roughly Rs 5,000 crore of the raise for debt repayment.
  • •- Most proceeds under the revised structure will still go toward repaying or prepaying borrowings.
  • •- Moody's revised PRISM's outlook to positive while reaffirming its B2 corporate family rating.
  • •- OYO's EBITDA more than doubled to Rs 2,630 crore in FY26, and Moody's expects roughly Rs 3,000 crore over the next 12-18 months.

A company cutting its IPO in half is usually a story about desperation. OYO parent PRISM's decision to shrink the fresh issue of its proposed public offering to up to Rs 3,350 crore — from the roughly Rs 6,650 crore originally envisaged — is a story about the opposite: cash generation strong enough that the company no longer needs the money it once thought it did. People familiar with the matter told PTI on Tuesday that the reduction follows continued robust cash flow and an improving balance-sheet position, which together lowered the capital requirement compared with what was planned. The logic, as one of them put it, is to "raise what the business needs while keeping the capital structure efficient" — with the residual debt to be repaid over the next two to three years from operating cash flow rather than from a supersized primary raise. It is a framing that would have been unthinkable in the IPO discussions of three years ago, when OYO's cash burn was the central question any prospective investor asked first.

The revised sizing marks a striking turn for a company whose India IPO narrative has been defined by its balance sheet. When OYO first planned to raise up to Rs 6,650 crore, around Rs 5,000 crore of that was earmarked for debt repayment — the number was the story, because the debt was the story. Under the restructured issue, most of the proceeds are still proposed for repayment or prepayment, in part or in full, of certain borrowings; the destination has not changed, only the vehicle's size. What changed in between is the income statement: for FY26, OYO's EBITDA more than doubled to Rs 2,630 crore from Rs 1,080 crore in FY25, a scale-up that makes a mockery of the old framing of OYO as a cash-burning startup and quietly converts the IPO from a rescue into a rationalisation.

The credit market has already adjusted its lens. Moody's Ratings this week revised PRISM's outlook to positive from stable while reaffirming the company's B2 corporate family rating, explaining that it expects OYO's EBITDA to grow to around Rs 3,000 crore ($315 million) over the next 12 to 18 months as the company continues to scale and add new storefronts to its platform. A positive outlook on a B2 rating is not investment grade, and PRISM's structure — the rating sits at the Holdco while the operating business carries the growth — remains leveraged by any standard. But direction matters more than level at this stage of the credit story: EBITDA more than doubling in a single year, with a ratings agency explicitly modelling the next leg higher, is the trajectory that turns distressed-debt narratives into refinancing narratives. For IPO subscribers weighing whether a hotel-aggregator deserves their capital, a Moody's outlook upgrade arriving in the same week as the issue restructure is the cleanest external validation the company could have ordered.

Ownership anchors the story in familiar hands. Ritesh Agarwal, who founded OYO in 2013, holds 30.52 per cent of the company, while SVF India Holdings (Cayman) Ltd, the SoftBank Group subsidiary, remains the largest shareholder at 40.04 per cent. The revised issue follows PRISM's earlier filing of its Updated Draft Red Herring Prospectus, putting the offering in the late stages of preparation amid what may be the most crowded primary-market window in Indian history — Jio Platforms opens tomorrow at a scale that absorbs institutional attention the way a black hole absorbs light, and a queue of mid-caps including Paras Healthcare are pricing alongside it. Choosing to shrink rather than compete for the marginal rupee of primary demand is either prudent timing or telling modesty, and the EBITDA arithmetic supports the former. A smaller fresh issue also means less dilution for Agarwal and SoftBank at a price the market now has less power to negotiate down.

For the broader startup-to-listing pipeline, the PRISM decision offers a template worth watching. The first wave of Indian tech listings was criticised for raising more than businesses needed and then hunting for uses of capital; a company that raises half its planned issue on the strength of its own cash generation, and commits to retiring the rest of its debt from operations over two to three years, inverts that critique entirely. The IPO becomes not an ATM but a milestone — the moment a founder-heavy, SoftBank-anchored cap table goes public with a balance sheet that no longer requires the public's money to survive.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
HOW MUCH IS PRISM CUTTING ITS IPO?
The fresh issue is being reduced to up to Rs 3,350 crore from about Rs 6,650 crore, sources said, because strong cash generation has lowered the funding requirement.
WHAT WILL THE PROCEEDS BE USED FOR?
Most proceeds will go toward repayment or prepayment of borrowings; residual debt is planned to be repaid over two to three years from business cash flows.
WHAT DID MOODY'S SAY?
Moody's revised PRISM's outlook to positive while reaffirming the B2 corporate family rating, expecting OYO's EBITDA to reach around Rs 3,000 crore in 12-18 months.
WHO OWNS PRISM?
Founder Ritesh Agarwal holds 30.52 per cent; SoftBank's SVF India Holdings is the largest shareholder at 40.04 per cent. GLOSSARY: - Fresh issue: New shares created in an IPO whose proceeds go to the company, as distinct from an offer for sale by existing holders. - Corporate family rating: A credit agency's rating of a company's overall ability to repay debt. - EBITDA: Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation, a measure of operating profitability. - Holdco: A holding company whose main assets are stakes in operating businesses rather than operations of its own.
