FPI Selling Tops Rs 52,000 Crore in October, Nifty Defies Exodus

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- October FPI selling reached about Rs 52,100 crore, exceeding September's Rs 35,861 crore with two weeks left.
  • •- A 15-session selling streak that began September 24 withdrew roughly Rs 84,000 crore before Friday's small buy.
  • •- Calendar-2026 outflows have now crossed Rs 3 lakh crore.
  • •- The Nifty gained 2.8% to 23,146 and the Sensex 3.1% on the week, led by an 11.7% IT surge.
  • •- US 10-year yields near 5.3% and Brent's run to $110 drove the exit; the rupee still recovered 1.9% to 95.07.
  • •- Domestic institutional buying absorbed the foreign supply — the week's defining divergence.

The foreign sell-off now has a number large enough to be its own narrative. Foreign portfolio investors sold a net Rs 7,934 crore of Indian equities in the week ended October 16, taking October's cumulative outflows to approximately Rs 52,100 crore — already ahead of September's full-month Rs 35,861 crore, with two trading weeks still on the calendar. Settled data shows selling on four of five reporting days; provisional data, which runs a day ahead, put the week's gross figure closer to Rs 8,990 crore. Friday's provisional purchases of Rs 1,166 crore ended a 15-session selling streak that had withdrawn roughly Rs 84,000 crore since September 24. For calendar 2026, foreign investors have now pulled more than Rs 3 lakh crore from Indian equities.

The market's response is the part of this story that will be studied longer than the outflow itself. The Nifty gained 2.8 per cent during the week to close at 23,146, and the Sensex added 3.1 per cent — the benchmarks' strongest week in approximately six months, delivered in the middle of a record foreign exodus. The divergence has a name: domestic institutional buying, which absorbed the foreign supply session after session and turned the flow data into a lagging indicator of sentiment rather than a leading one. India has spent a decade building the systematic investment plan machinery that now performs this function; last week was its clearest live demonstration, as the SIP bid met a distressed seller and calmly took the other side.

The technology sector did the rest. The Nifty IT index surged 11.7 per cent over the week on encouraging September-quarter earnings and revived sentiment around AI-related demand, providing the returns that kept domestic money confident while foreign money left. That a single sector's rally could offset a Rs 52,000 crore exit says something about both the concentration of the index and the depth of conviction currently riding on the IT trade — the same trade whose long-run risks the chief economic adviser was cataloguing days earlier, and whose short-run earnings have carried the market regardless.

The selling itself has familiar drivers, each tightening at once. The US 10-year Treasury yield climbed to around 5.3 per cent after September inflation printed at 3.7 per cent against a 3.6 per cent expectation, having earlier approached 5.365 per cent — within sight of a 24-year high. The dollar index hit an 18-month high, and market-implied odds of a Federal Reserve rate increase at the October 28 meeting jumped from about 22 per cent to above 40 per cent. Higher yields raise the return on the risk-free asset every emerging-market equity competes against, and the arithmetic has been unforgiving all year. Crude added the second pressure: Brent opened the week at $104.95 and touched $110 by Friday, a combination that threatens India's inflation outlook, current account and corporate margins simultaneously — the kind of triple burden that historically sends foreign money to the exits and, this month, did.

The one chart that did not follow the script is the rupee. It recovered about 1.9 per cent during the week to 95.07 against the dollar, helped by the Reserve Bank of India's post-October 7 measures — a repo rate raised 25 basis points to 5.50 per cent, a stance shifted to calibrated tightening, and a dedicated dollar window for public-sector oil marketing companies that took a visible buyer out of the spot market. The RBI's intervention arithmetic has been expensive, with reserves having fallen sharply through the defence, but the currency's weekly recovery amid a record equity outflow is evidence that the defence is functioning as designed: foreign investors can leave, and the currency does not have to carry the entire exit.

For the coming fortnight, the flow question resolves into two dates. The October 28 Fed decision will either validate the rate-hike pricing that is driving the exits or unwind it; and the Jio Platforms subscription window opening October 21 will offer the market a domestic absorption test of an entirely different scale — if the primary market can digest the country's largest-ever IPO while foreigners sell, the narrative of Indian liquidity as a counterweight to global cycles graduates from claim to fact. Until then, the Rs 52,100 crore headline should be read alongside the index level that rose while it accumulated. Both are true. Only one of them is priced.

There is also a seasonality to the streak that the Rs 3 lakh crore calendar-year total conceals. The selling did not begin with October's oil spike or the Fed's hawkish turn — it began in earnest through the middle of the year and has persisted through every rally, which means the exits are less a trade against India than a trade against the asset class. Emerging-market funds are redeeming, and India's liquidity makes it the easiest large market to leave; the streak is therefore a compliment of a kind, the market depth that lets $10 billion depart without disorder being the same depth that invited the allocation in the first place. The composition, though, has shifted in ways the aggregate hides: selling has concentrated in the financials and energy-linked names most exposed to the oil-import bill, while the IT trade that led the rally absorbed far less foreign supply. Foreign money is not leaving India uniformly; it is leaving the India that buys crude and financing the India that sells software — a rotation by exit door that the weekly net figure will never show.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
HOW MUCH HAVE FPIS SOLD IN OCTOBER?
About Rs 52,100 crore as of October 16, surpassing September's full-month outflow of Rs 35,861 crore, with two weeks of October still remaining.
WHY ARE FOREIGN INVESTORS SELLING?
US 10-year Treasury yields near 5.3%, a Fed rate-hike probability that jumped above 40% for October 28, a strong dollar, and Brent crude's rise to $110 have all pressured emerging-market allocations.
WHY DID THE MARKET RISE DESPITE THE SELLING?
Domestic institutional buying absorbed the foreign outflows, and the Nifty IT index's 11.7% weekly surge drove benchmark gains of 2.8-3.1%.
WHAT ENDED THE SELLING STREAK?
Provisional data showed FPIs turned net buyers on Friday, October 16, ending a 15-session selling streak that had withdrawn roughly Rs 84,000 crore. GLOSSARY: - FPI: Foreign portfolio investor — overseas funds investing in Indian stocks and bonds. - Settled data: Trade flows confirmed after settlement, typically lagging a day behind provisional figures. - SIP bid: The steady monthly inflow from India's systematic investment plans, a structural domestic buyer. - Dollar window: A dedicated RBI facility supplying dollars to specific buyers, reducing spot-market pressure.
