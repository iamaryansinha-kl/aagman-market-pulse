BPCL Profit Falls 21% to Rs 4,863 Crore on Cost Surge

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Profit attributable to owners fell 21.45% year-on-year to Rs 4,863.05 crore, from Rs 6,191.49 crore a year earlier.
  • •- Revenue from operations surged 29.78% to Rs 1,57,817.93 crore, but total expenses rose faster, up 32.54% to Rs 1,51,932.8 crore.
  • •- Sequentially, the quarter was a sharp turnaround from a Rs 3,962.13 crore loss in Q1, with EBITDA of Rs 8,971 crore versus a Rs 4,077 crore loss.
  • •- The board approved a wholly-owned subsidiary for the proposed greenfield refinery and petrochemical complex at Ramayapatnam, Andhra Pradesh.

The arithmetic of oil marketing in India rarely forgives: sell more fuel, book more revenue, and still watch the profit line shrink because the margin per litre is not yours to set. Bharat Petroleum Corporation on Friday reported a 21.45% year-on-year decline in profit attributable to owners at Rs 4,863.05 crore for the September quarter, from Rs 6,191.49 crore a year earlier — even as revenue from operations surged 29.78% to Rs 1,57,817.93 crore. The culprit sits in the expense lines: total expenses grew even faster than revenue, rising 32.54% to Rs 1,51,932.8 crore, leaving a little over Rs 5,885 crore between income and outflow where a year ago the same gap was comfortably wider.

The sequential picture tells the more flattering story, and it matters. In the June quarter — when crude volatility and inventory swings battered every state-run refiner — BPCL posted a loss of Rs 3,962.13 crore. The September quarter swung decisively back: EBITDA came in at Rs 8,971 crore, against a Rs 4,077 crore loss in the preceding quarter, with an EBITDA margin of 6.02%. Year-on-year profit is down, but the company has, in effect, printed half a year's worth of recovery in ninety days. Anyone reading only the headline decline would miss that the quarterly trajectory points the other way.

Oil marketing economics have been punishing for the state-run trio through FY26: administered pricing, crude swings booked through inventory, and the political impossibility of passing costs to consumers in a high-inflation environment.

Why did expenses outrun revenue by enough to shrink profit a fifth? The company's filing does not break out every line, but the structure of the business explains the shape of the result. BPCL buys crude at global prices, refines it, and sells the products into a domestic market where pump prices respond to political as much as commercial logic; when the cost side moves faster than the revenue side — whether through pricier crude, weaker product cracks, or the working-capital drag of holding more expensive inventory — the squeeze shows up exactly here, in a quarter where revenue grew almost 30% and profit still fell. Revenue quality, not revenue quantity, is the metric that matters for an OMC, and this quarter's revenue was high-volume, thin-margin business.

Which is precisely why the board's other Friday decision may matter more than the earnings themselves. BPCL's directors approved the incorporation of a wholly-owned subsidiary to serve as the dedicated project company for the proposed greenfield refinery and petrochemical complex at Ramayapatnam in Andhra Pradesh, with initial authorised share capital of up to Rs 4,900 crore. The entity will house the project's legal and governance structure and support lender engagement and potential partnerships while detailed feasibility reports are finalised; the financial investment decision will be taken separately by the competent authorities at the appropriate stage, and the incorporation itself remains subject to government and regulatory approvals.

The strategic logic, though, is clear enough to state plainly. India's fuel demand is still growing, but the growth is slowing as electrification bites into the two-wheeler and urban fleets that drive petrol consumption; petrochemicals, by contrast, is a demand pool India currently meets largely through imports. A coastal refinery-cum-petrochemical complex converts BPCL from a pure fuel retailer — squeezed between global crude and political pump prices — into a manufacturer of higher-value products with export potential, which is the only structural answer to the margin problem this quarter once again exposed. Whether the project economics survive feasibility, approval and financing review is a question for later years; Friday's incorporation approval is the first formal step, and the market's muted reaction to the stock suggests investors are waiting for much more than a project company on paper. It is also expensive, slow and approval-heavy — the kind of capital commitment that will be scrutinised at every milestone for a company whose shares are down nearly 26% this year, trading at Rs 281.95 after a 1.74% Friday fall and well off the 52-week high of Rs 391.65. At roughly Rs 1.22 lakh crore of market capitalisation, the market already values the country's second-largest fuel retailer less like a growth business and more like a regulated utility with occasional, entirely predictable margin accidents. Friday's quarter gave the market a serviceable set of numbers and a big strategic headline; what it did not give was evidence that the margin mathematics of the core business have structurally improved. For a shareholder, that distinction is the whole story: the quarterly turnaround validates the recovery, but only sustained marketing margins — not one quarter of EBITDA — will re-rate a stock that has spent 2026 de-rating. That proof, if it comes, will be the story of the next several quarters, and the Ramayapatnam subsidiary, once its feasibility reports land, will be where investors look for it.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHY DID BPCL'S PROFIT FALL DESPITE STRONG REVENUE?
Total expenses rose 32.54% year-on-year, outpacing 29.78% revenue growth and compressing margins; profit attributable to owners fell to Rs 4,863.05 crore from Rs 6,191.49 crore.
HOW DID THE QUARTER COMPARE TO Q1?
It was a sharp sequential turnaround: EBITDA of Rs 8,971 crore against a Rs 4,077 crore loss in Q1, swinging from a Rs 3,962.13 crore net loss to a Rs 4,863 crore profit.
WHAT IS THE ANDHRA PRADESH REFINERY DECISION?
The board approved a wholly-owned subsidiary with authorised capital up to Rs 4,900 crore to serve as the project company for the greenfield refinery and petrochemical complex at Ramayapatnam; final investment approval comes later. GLOSSARY: OIL MARKETING COMPANY (OMC) — A state-controlled retailer of petrol, diesel and aviation fuel whose selling prices are politically influenced while input costs float with global crude. EBITDA — Earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation; a cleaner read of operating performance than net profit for capital-heavy refiners. INVENTORY SWING — The accounting gain or loss a refiner books when crude prices move between purchase and sale; it can turn a good operating quarter into a reported loss. GREENFIELD REFINERY — A new refinery built from scratch, as opposed to expanding an existing one; Ramayapatnam is BPCL's proposed coastal complex in Andhra Pradesh.
