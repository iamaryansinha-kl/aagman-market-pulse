Christopher Wood Warns of AI Fatigue; Jefferies Turns to India as Alternative

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

One of Wall Street's most prominent strategists is shifting focus from AI to India.

Christopher Wood, Jefferies' global equity strategist known for his influential Greed & Fear newsletter, has warned of AI fatigue and is turning to India as an alternative investment destination. The shift reflects a broader reassessment of technology-driven market narratives that have dominated global equity flows for the past two years.

Wood has been a vocal advocate of AI-related investments. His newsletters have extensively covered the AI theme, recommending exposure to semiconductor companies, cloud providers, and software firms riding the generative AI wave. But his latest commentary suggests that the AI trade may be becoming overcrowded and that the easy gains have already been made.

India represents a different kind of growth story. Unlike AI, which is a technology cycle that could peak or fade, India's growth is driven by structural factors: demographics, urbanisation, financialisation of savings, and manufacturing relocation from China. These trends are longer-lasting and less dependent on the hype cycle that drives technology valuations.

Wood's pivot is significant for global fund flows. When a strategist of his stature shifts recommendation, it can influence where billions of dollars in institutional capital are allocated. A rotation from AI-focused US technology stocks to India could accelerate the trend of foreign portfolio investors increasing their India exposure.

The timing is notable. Indian markets have already seen record foreign inflows in 2026, with the Nifty hitting fresh highs. Wood's endorsement could add further momentum, particularly from global investors who have been underweight India relative to the country's growing share of global GDP.

India is not without risks. Valuations are elevated, the rupee has been volatile, and geopolitical tensions in the Middle East are affecting oil prices and trade flows. But Wood appears to be betting that these are shorter-term concerns compared to the structural growth opportunity.
