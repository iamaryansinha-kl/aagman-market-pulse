IRCON Marks 50 Years with ₹23,801 Cr Order Book, Eyes High-Speed Rail and Global Expansion

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/IRCON International Limited celebrated its golden jubilee on April 28, 2026, marking 50 years since its 1976 incorporation as Indian Railway Construction Company.
  • 02/The Navratna PSU holds an order book of ₹23,801 crore as of December 31, 2025, with 75% from railways and 18% from roads and highways.
  • 03/Watch FY26 revenue guidance of ₹10,000-11,000 crore, competitive bidding margin pressure, and CMD Hari Mohan Gupta's June 30, 2026 retirement.

Ircon International Limited (NSE: IRCON) celebrated its golden jubilee on April 28, 2026, completing five decades since its incorporation on the same date in 1976 as Indian Railway Construction Company Private Limited. The Ministry of Railways-backed Navratna PSU has grown from a railway-focused contractor into a diversified infrastructure giant spanning 24 countries.

The company commands an order book of ₹23,801 crore as of December 31, 2025, up from ₹20,347 crore at March-end 2025. Railway projects dominate at 75%, followed by roads and highways at 18%, with the balance spread across airports, power transmission, and urban transit. Infomerics Ratings affirmed IRCON's IVR AAA/Stable rating in March 2026, citing low counterparty risk and government-backed contracts.

Revenue has come under pressure. FY25 total operating income fell 14% year-on-year to ₹10,193 crore from ₹11,950 crore in FY24. Profit after tax dropped to ₹737.59 crore in FY25 versus ₹862.90 crore in FY24. The EBITDA margin excluding other income contracted to 4.70% in FY25 from 6.39% in FY24, as competitive bidding replaced the earlier nomination-based order system.

The competitive transition began in 2021 and now accounts for roughly 61% of the outstanding order book. New projects are being won at thinner margins, and the company has seen a 0.5% to 1% margin compression on fresh contracts. Two projects have incurred losses, contributing to lower EBITDA and PAT margins in recent quarters.

Despite top-line softness, liquidity remains strong. IRCON held cash and equivalents of ₹1,977.85 crore on a standalone basis as of March 2025, with zero long-term debt. The interest service coverage ratio stood at 64.66x in FY25. The company relies on internal accruals, project advances, and mobilisation funding rather than borrowed capital.

In Q2 FY26, revenue came in at ₹2,112 crore with PAT of ₹137 crore. The first half saw order inflows exceed ₹4,000 crore, and management expects a similar quantum in H2. Full-year revenue guidance is set at ₹10,000-11,000 crore, roughly flat compared to FY25. The order book as of September 30, 2025 stood at ₹23,865 crore, providing medium-term revenue visibility.

International exposure accounts for approximately 9% of the order book, spanning Bangladesh, Nepal, Sri Lanka, Malaysia, and Algeria. Foreign exchange earnings contributed roughly ₹20 crore in FY26, mainly from the Bangladesh project. International margins typically exceed domestic ones, offering a partial hedge against competitive pressure at home.

Future growth leans on high-speed rail, dedicated freight corridors, NCRTC metro projects, and NHAI highway contracts. IRCON has also entered solar energy and mass rapid transit systems. The company's execution record includes over 405 completed domestic projects and 130 international projects as of December 2025.

Leadership transition looms. Chairman and Managing Director Hari Mohan Gupta, a 1988 civil engineering graduate from the University of Roorkee, is set to retire on June 30, 2026. He took charge after selection by the Public Enterprises Selection Board in December 2023. Director (Finance) Ragini Advani, a qualified chartered accountant, Director (Projects) Rajesh Naik, and Director (Works) Ajit Kumar Mishra form the core management team.

The Government of India holds 65.17% of IRCON's equity as of December 2025, down from higher historical levels but well above the 51% floor that rating agencies monitor. Any further divestment beyond this threshold would trigger a rating downgrade under Infomerics' sensitivity framework.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHEN WAS IRCON INCORPORATED AND WHAT WAS ITS ORIGINAL NAME?
IRCON WAS INCORPORATED ON APRIL 28, 1976, AS INDIAN RAILWAY CONSTRUCTION COMPANY PRIVATE LIMITED. IT LATER BECAME A PUBLIC LIMITED COMPANY AND WAS RENAMED IRCON INTERNATIONAL LIMITED IN 2003.
WHAT IS IRCON'S CURRENT ORDER BOOK AND HOW IS IT DISTRIBUTED ACROSS SEGMENTS?
AS OF DECEMBER 31, 2025, IRCON'S ORDER BOOK STOOD AT ₹23,801 CRORE. RAILWAY PROJECTS ACCOUNT FOR 75%, ROADS AND HIGHWAYS 18%, AND THE REMAINDER IS SPLIT ACROSS AIRPORTS, POWER, AND OTHER INFRASTRUCTURE SEGMENTS. ABOUT 91% IS DOMESTIC AND 9% INTERNATIONAL.
HOW HAS COMPETITIVE BIDDING AFFECTED IRCON'S PROFITABILITY?
SINCE 2021, IRCON HAS SHIFTED FROM NOMINATION-BASED TO COMPETITIVE BIDDING. THIS HAS SQUEEZED EBITDA MARGINS TO 4.70% IN FY25 FROM 6.39% IN FY24. THE PAT MARGIN HELD RELATIVELY STEADY AT 6.91% IN FY25 VERSUS 6.97% IN FY24, BUT NEW PROJECTS ARE BEING WON AT 0.5% TO 1% LOWER MARGINS.
WHAT IS IRCON'S FY26 REVENUE GUIDANCE AND ORDER INFLOW OUTLOOK?
MANAGEMENT HAS GUIDED FOR FY26 OPERATING REVENUE OF ₹10,000-11,000 CRORE. ORDER INFLOWS EXCEEDED ₹4,000 CRORE IN H1 FY26, WITH A SIMILAR RANGE EXPECTED IN H2. PAT MARGINS ARE EXPECTED TO STABILISE AT 6% TO 7% GOING FORWARD.
WHAT ARE THE KEY RISKS AND CATALYSTS FOR IRCON IN THE NEAR TERM?
KEY RISKS INCLUDE COMPETITIVE BIDDING PRESSURE, MARGIN EROSION ON NEW PROJECTS, AND LEADERSHIP TRANSITION AS CMD HARI MOHAN GUPTA RETIRES ON JUNE 30, 2026. CATALYSTS INCLUDE HIGH-SPEED RAIL AND NHAI PROJECTS, A STRONG ₹23,801 CRORE ORDER BOOK, ZERO LONG-TERM DEBT, AND CASH RESERVES OF NEARLY ₹2,000 CRORE.
