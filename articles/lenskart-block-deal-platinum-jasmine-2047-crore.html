Lenskart Block Deal in the Works as ADIA-Backed Trust Eyes Rs 2,047 Crore Stake Sale

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

Lenskart Solutions is set for another large secondary sale. Platinum Jasmine A 2018 Trust, an Abu Dhabi Investment Authority-backed investment vehicle, is looking to offload about a 1.7% holding in the eyewear retailer through a block deal estimated at Rs 2,047.40 crore, with the shares reportedly priced around Rs 682.45 apiece.

The trust has been a steady seller since the stock listed. As of March 2026, Platinum Jasmine held about 12.08% of Lenskart, making it the company's second-largest public shareholder. In June, it sold a 2.3% stake through a block deal worth Rs 1,960 crore at a floor price of Rs 486 per share, a transaction that was absorbed by a broad set of global and domestic institutions.

The contrast between the two prices tells the story. At roughly Rs 682 in the proposed deal against Rs 486 in June, the implied pricing is up around 40% in a little over three months, reflecting the rally in the stock since its debut and the confidence of buyers in the Peyush Bansal-led company's growth trajectory.

The June sale had set a template for how these exits get absorbed. Goldman Sachs Bank Europe emerged as the largest buyer then, purchasing 1.37 crore shares worth Rs 701.47 crore, followed by Societe Generale-ODI at Rs 698.44 crore, with Fidelity, Manulife, WhiteOak Capital, Quant Mutual Fund, Mirae Asset, Kotak Funds, ICICI Prudential Mutual Fund and others participating.

Lenskart's operating numbers support the demand. For the March quarter, revenue rose 45.6% year-on-year to Rs 2,515.7 crore and EBITDA surged 84% to Rs 538.4 crore, with margin improving to 21.4% from 16.9%. India business topline grew 33% in FY26, led by 21% same-store sales growth, while the international business grew 30%.

The share sales also reflect the normal rhythm of post-listing monetisation. Lenskart's six-month post-listing lock-in expired in May 2026, unlocking nearly 104.7 crore shares, or around 60% of total equity, and early backers including SoftBank have been trimming positions since.

The block deal window, if executed, will test appetite for new-age consumer stocks at premium valuations. The stock has held up through successive exits this year, suggesting institutional demand continues to outpace the supply from early investors.
