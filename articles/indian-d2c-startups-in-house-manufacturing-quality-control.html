Indian D2C Startups Pivot to In-House Manufacturing for Quality Control Edge

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/D2C startups are bringing manufacturing in-house to control quality, inventory, and margins
  • 02/Eat Better Co invested ₹10 crore for 50,000 sq ft facility; LittleBox cut lead times from 60 days to 14 days
  • 03/Quick commerce growth is accelerating the shift as brands need faster replenishment cycles

A growing number of direct-to-customer startups in India are setting up their own manufacturing facilities. They are moving away from third-party manufacturers to gain control over product quality, inventory levels, and margins.

Startups following this approach include snacking brand Eat Better Co, fashion company LittleBox India, travel gear seller Assembly Luggage, and skincare startup Minimalist.

"Owning manufacturing gives you some sort of proprietariness. You can also iterate much faster on products and R&D if you own a manufacturing facility," said Harmanpreet Singh, founder and managing partner at Prath Ventures.

Eat Better Co started with a home kitchen in 2020. It invested about ₹10 crore this year to set up a 50,000 sq ft manufacturing facility in Jaipur. This gives it a lead time of 2-3 days compared with 30-45 days with third-party manufacturers.

"The only way to ensure quality throughout the process was by owning the entire manufacturing," said Shaurya Kanoria, founder of Eat Better Co. "If we wanted to innovate on recipes, control quality and maintain consistency while scaling, we had to be at the centre of manufacturing."

LittleBox invested about ₹7 crore over the past four years to set up its manufacturing facility in Noida. "With a third-party manufacturer, the average lead time is 60-75 days. In our case, it is just 14 days," said founder Partha Kakati.

Experts said owning manufacturing can improve profitability. Startups retain the margin that would have gone to third parties. About 80-90% of D2C brands still depend on China or third-party manufacturers.

"Today, one of the questions investors ask is whether a brand can eventually own manufacturing," said Keshav Agarwal, founder of Daylight Capital. Reducing dependence on third parties is increasingly seen as a competitive advantage.

However, not everyone agrees. "For a large share of consumer brands, owning a factory can actually be a liability at the seed-to-Series A stage," said Adarsh Menon, partner at Fireside Ventures. "It can lock up capital and reduce flexibility."

What is shifting the calculus is quick commerce. "As more sales move to 10-30-minute delivery, the brands that win are the ones who can replenish fast and protect margins," Menon added.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHY ARE D2C STARTUPS SHIFTING TO IN-HOUSE MANUFACTURING?
STARTUPS WANT GREATER CONTROL OVER PRODUCT QUALITY, INVENTORY LEVELS, AND MARGINS. OWNING FACTORIES ENABLES FASTER PRODUCT INNOVATION, SHORTER LEAD TIMES, AND REDUCED DEPENDENCE ON THIRD PARTIES.
WHICH STARTUPS HAVE SET UP THEIR OWN MANUFACTURING?
EAT BETTER CO (SNACKS, JAIPUR FACILITY), LITTLEBOX INDIA (FASHION, NOIDA), ASSEMBLY LUGGAGE (TRAVEL GEAR), AND MINIMALIST (SKINCARE) ARE AMONG THE STARTUPS THAT HAVE INVESTED IN IN-HOUSE MANUFACTURING.
HOW MUCH HAVE THESE STARTUPS INVESTED IN MANUFACTURING?
EAT BETTER CO INVESTED ABOUT ₹10 CRORE FOR A 50,000 SQ FT FACILITY IN JAIPUR. LITTLEBOX INVESTED ABOUT ₹7 CRORE OVER FOUR YEARS FOR ITS NOIDA FACILITY.
WHAT IMPACT DOES IN-HOUSE MANUFACTURING HAVE ON LEAD TIMES?
LITTLEBOX CUT LEAD TIMES FROM 60-75 DAYS WITH THIRD-PARTY MANUFACTURERS TO JUST 14 DAYS. EAT BETTER CO ACHIEVED 2-3 DAY LEAD TIMES COMPARED WITH 30-45 DAYS PREVIOUSLY.
WHAT ROLE IS QUICK COMMERCE PLAYING IN THIS SHIFT?
QUICK COMMERCE IS PUSHING MORE FOUNDERS TOWARD OWNING THEIR SUPPLY CHAIN. BRANDS THAT CAN REPLENISH FAST AND PROTECT MARGINS AGAINST RISING PLATFORM COMMISSIONS ARE BETTER POSITIONED TO WIN IN THE 10-30 MINUTE DELIVERY MODEL.
