Federal Bank Q2 Profit Jumps 45% to Record Rs 1,386 Crore

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Record quarterly net profit of Rs 1,386 crore, up 45% YoY from Rs 955 crore.
  • •- Net interest income rose 26% to Rs 3,153 crore; NIM improved 26 bps to 3.32%.
  • •- Advances grew 22% to Rs 2.98 lakh crore; deposits rose 19% to Rs 3.43 lakh crore.
  • •- Gross NPAs fell 51 bps to 1.32%; net NPAs dropped to 0.17% from 0.48%.
  • •- NRE deposits including FCNR rose 27% to Rs 1.09 lakh crore.
  • •- MD KVS Manian flagged confidence on margins despite the FCNR cost impact.

While the big private banks have been busy reporting respectable, unspectacular quarters, Federal Bank has delivered the kind of print that forces a re-rating conversation. The Kerala-headquartered lender on Friday reported its highest-ever quarterly net profit of Rs 1,386 crore for the September quarter, up 45 per cent from Rs 955 crore a year earlier, driven by a 26 per cent jump in net interest income to Rs 3,153 crore and a striking improvement in asset quality.

The margin story is the quarter's technical highlight. Net interest margin improved 26 basis points year-on-year to 3.32 per cent, even as the bank absorbed the cost of the RBI's special FCNR deposit window — the same diaspora-mobilisation programme behind this month's record reserves and capital-account surplus. Federal Bank raised Rs 14,922 crore of FCNR deposits under the window during the period and had Rs 9,438 crore of loans against FCNR deposits at end-September. Managing director and CEO KVS Manian was direct about the trade-off: "The FCNR has some impact on the NIMs, we are quite confident of maintaining our trajectory on NIMs. So, we do not see it significantly impacting our NIMs." In plainer terms: the deposits cost more than domestic money, but the volume they brought — NRE deposits including FCNR rose 27 per cent to Rs 1.09 lakh crore — transformed the liability base.

Growth was broad across the book. Net advances rose 22 per cent year-on-year to Rs 2.98 lakh crore: retail customer assets grew 18 per cent to Rs 79,714 crore, the corporate and institutional book rose 17 per cent to Rs 1.05 lakh crore, and commercial banking led at 25 per cent growth to Rs 32,780 crore. Total deposits rose 19 per cent to Rs 3.43 lakh crore, with retail deposits up 16 per cent to Rs 2.49 lakh crore. The loan-deposit arithmetic stays comfortable, and the deposit franchise — traditionally the bank's calling card — has clearly been strengthened by the NRI franchise that made it a natural beneficiary of the FCNR window.

Asset quality did the kind of work that markets reward most. Gross NPAs fell 51 basis points year-on-year to 1.32 per cent from 1.83 per cent; net NPAs dropped to 0.17 per cent from 0.48 per cent. Fresh slippages declined 28 per cent to Rs 420 crore, the annualised slippage ratio improved to 59 basis points from 94, and credit cost fell to 31 basis points from 50. The provision coverage ratio excluding technical write-offs jumped to 86.78 per cent from 73.45 per cent — a conservative posture that gives future quarters room to breathe. Provisions including tax rose 22 per cent to Rs 839 crore, a deliberate strengthening rather than stress.

Manian also addressed the strategic question that has followed the bank since its leadership transition: inorganic expansion. "We are looking at all opportunities in financial services, including core banking as well as universal banking opportunities," he said, while confirming nothing is imminent. That is the standard careful formulation, but in a season when consolidation talk is running through Indian banking — and when the bank's own capital position and profitability trajectory would support a transaction — it lands as more than boilerplate.

The quarter places Federal Bank in a useful position within the earnings season's narrative. The major private banks showed NII growth in the teens and stable-to-improving asset quality; Federal Bank showed NII growth of 26 per cent and a 45 per cent profit jump, with the FCNR programme's cost acknowledged rather than hidden. If the NIM trajectory Manian promises holds through the December quarter — when the full cost of the swap-funded deposits annualises — the record will have been earned rather than borrowed. For a mid-sized bank that has spent years in the "promising" category, this is the kind of quarter that converts the adjective into a number.

The competitive backdrop makes the print more notable still. Private-bank lending has been accelerating through FY27 — system credit growth has run above 16 per cent — and the banks that combine balance-sheet momentum with credible deposit franchises are pulling away from those still repairing theirs. Federal Bank's 19 per cent deposit growth, powered by a 27 per cent surge in NRE money, puts it firmly in the former camp, while the NIM expansion shows it is not buying growth with pricing. The 45 per cent profit jump will also sharpen the perennial question of scale: at Rs 2.98 lakh crore of advances, Federal Bank remains sub-scale against the private-bank giants, and Manian's openness to inorganic options reads as management acknowledging that arithmetic.

For shareholders, the record quarter carries a straightforward message: the FCNR windfall that flattered system-wide liability numbers this quarter came with a margin cost, and Federal Bank absorbed it while still expanding profitability — evidence that the franchise, not the facility, is doing the heavy lifting. The next test arrives in Q3, when the full-quarter effect of the swap-window deposits lands and the December policy decision sets the tone for margins into FY28.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT WERE FEDERAL BANK'S Q2 HEADLINES?
Record net profit of Rs 1,386 crore, up 45% YoY; NII up 26% to Rs 3,153 crore; NIM at 3.32%.
HOW DID ASSET QUALITY MOVE?
Gross NPAs fell to 1.32% from 1.83%, net NPAs to 0.17% from 0.48%, and credit cost dropped to 31 basis points.
WHAT IS THE FCNR IMPACT?
The bank raised Rs 14,922 crore under the RBI's FCNR window; it pressures margins but lifted NRE deposits 27% to Rs 1.09 lakh crore.
IS FEDERAL BANK LOOKING AT ACQUISITIONS?
CEO KVS Manian said the bank is evaluating financial-services opportunities including core and universal banking, with nothing imminent. GLOSSARY: - Net interest margin (NIM): The difference between what a bank earns on loans and pays on deposits, as a share of assets. - FCNR deposits: Foreign currency non-resident deposits — dollar-denominated savings held by Indians abroad. - Provision coverage ratio: The share of bad loans a bank has already set aside against; higher is more conservative. - Credit cost: Provisions for bad loans as a share of total advances.
