Japan Nikkei 225 Hits Record 65,316 On Iran Peace Deal Optimism

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Nikkei 225 surges 3.12% to close at 65,316.79, breaching 65,000 for the first time in history with intraday peak at 65,408.57
  • 02/Trump says US and Iran have "largely negotiated" peace deal to reopen Strait of Hormuz, though later adds deal is not fully finalized
  • 03/Oil prices tumble and yen strengthens on easing geopolitical fears; analysts warn of execution risks even if agreement is reached

Japan's Nikkei 225 share average closed at a record 65,316.79 on Monday, May 25, 2026. The benchmark index surged 3.12% and touched an all-time intraday high of 65,408.57.

The rally was fueled by optimism that a peace deal could end the nearly three-month war between the United States, Israel and Iran. President Donald Trump said over the weekend that Washington and Tehran had "largely negotiated" a memorandum of understanding.

The deal would reopen the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for global petroleum shipments. Japan, which depends heavily on imported energy, has seen its economy battered by surging oil prices during the conflict.

The news sparked a sharp drop in crude oil prices. Japanese government bonds and the yen also gained, signaling broad improvement in risk sentiment across markets.

The broader Topix index rose 1.39% to 3,946.67. Market breadth was strong, with 144 advancers versus 79 decliners on the Nikkei.

AI and semiconductor stocks led the charge. Lasertec jumped over 11%, while Fujikura climbed more than 11%. SoftBank Group gained over 5% and chip-testing firm Advantest rose nearly 6%.

The largest losers included oil explorer Inpex, which slid 3.9%. Online retailer Rakuten Group dropped 4.4% and Archion fell 4.8%.

"Even if an agreement is reached, there remains uncertainty regarding whether it will be adhered to," said Maki Sawada, equities strategist at Nomura Securities. "The Iranian government may not be united on the issue."

Sawada also noted that 65,000 is a psychological milestone. Reaching this level triggered some caution and selling pressure at such elevated prices.

Trump later walked back his initial optimism. He told reporters he had instructed his representatives not to rush into any deal, saying "time is on our side."

Hours later, Trump posted that the agreement was "not fully finalized." The conflicting messages injected fresh uncertainty, though markets still priced in a higher probability of de-escalation.

The rally adds to a strong run for Japanese equities. The Nikkei has been lifted by robust corporate earnings, yen weakness, and a wave of foreign investor inflows.

Trading was thinner than usual across Asia. Hong Kong and South Korean markets were closed for public holidays, while US markets were shut for Memorial Day.
