Prithu Raises $1.1 Million to Scale Carbon Removal Platform in India

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Prithu raises $1.1 million in funding led by Transition VC to scale its carbon removal platform
  • 02/Targets 500,000 hectares in nature-based solutions over next 12-24 months using blockchain dMRV
  • 03/Founded in 2024, company enables small farmers to generate high-integrity carbon credits globally

Prithu, a climate technology start-up, has raised $1.1 million in a funding round led by Transition VC. The capital will scale its nature-based carbon removal platform across India.

The company was founded in 2024 by Sunny Vaish, Prabal Tomar, and Abhinav Pandey. It builds scalable carbon removal infrastructure rooted in India's agricultural ecosystem.

Prithu will use the fresh capital to onboard small landholder farmers and strengthen its blockchain-powered digital MRV infrastructure. Advanced satellite monitoring, ground truthing, and machine learning power the system.

The proprietary dMRV system combines on-ground soil sampling, satellite monitoring, and biogeochemical modelling. Every carbon credit is aligned with global standards including Verra, Gold Standard, and Puro.

The company focuses on regenerative agriculture practices such as soil organic carbon enhancement, Alternate Wetting and Drying, and biochar. These methods generate high-integrity, verifiable carbon removal credits.

Over the next 12-24 months, Prithu plans to expand to 500,000 hectares in nature-based solutions projects. It will also explore new geographies and secure international offtake partnerships.

"The carbon market is entering a phase of accelerated growth, driven by stronger climate commitments," said Sunny Vaish, co-founder and CEO of Prithu. He added that limited supply of credible nature-based credits will drive long-term value.

Gaurav Patil, VP of Investments at Transition VC, said buyers are prioritising credits with strong data and clear traceability. "Prithu is building the infrastructure required to deliver that," Patil said.
