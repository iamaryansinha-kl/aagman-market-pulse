'Inflation And Unholy Mess:' Nithin Kamath Warns RBI May Hike Rates

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Nithin Kamath says RBI cannot stay dovish if food and energy prices rise together in 2026
  • 02/El Niño may weaken monsoon by 6% while Strait of Hormuz disruption keeps oil above $100 per barrel
  • 03/India imports 80-90% of crude and 50% of natural gas; weak monsoon could hit 60% of farmers dependent on rainfall

Nithin Kamath has warned that India faces a severe inflation risk in 2026. The Zerodha co-founder said the RBI may be forced to abandon its dovish stance and hike interest rates.

In a post on X, Kamath called 2026 "a case of when it rains, it pours." He flagged two converging risks that threaten to push inflation higher simultaneously.

The India Meteorological Department forecasts rainfall 6% below normal this year. Around 60% of El Niño years since 1951 have produced below-average rainfall in India.

Nearly 70% of India's annual rainfall arrives during the monsoon. About 60% of Indian farmers remain dependent on rainfall rather than irrigation for their crops.

A weak monsoon typically reduces output of rice, pulses, sugar and vegetables. This directly feeds into food inflation, which carries heavy weight in India's retail price basket.

Kamath described the Strait of Hormuz situation as an "unholy mess." The channel carries 20% of global oil and 20% of LNG supplies.

India imports 80-90% of its crude oil and nearly half its natural gas. The Indian crude basket averaged $114 per barrel in April and around $106 in May.

The RBI had cut the repo rate by 125 basis points during 2025 to support growth. The rate currently stands at 5.25% after the central bank paused in early 2026.

"When food and energy prices rise together, the RBI cannot stay quiet," Kamath said. Beyond a point, rate hikes become unavoidable and that is when a bad situation starts to feel like a crisis.

Higher interest rates would raise borrowing costs for housing, automobiles and corporate capital expenditure. Sectors sensitive to rate cycles include real estate, banking and non-banking finance companies.
