Gold Nears ₹1.5 Lakh, Silver Drops: What's Driving the Selloff

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/MCX Gold trades at ₹1,50,027 down 1.12%, while MCX Silver drops 1.86% to ₹2,42,763 as crude oil surges 4.16%
  • 02/Rising US rate hike expectations ahead of the April 29 FOMC decision are pressuring non-yielding assets like gold
  • 03/Axis Direct and Kotak Neo maintain a short-term bullish regime for gold despite the daily selloff and bearish stochastics

MCX Gold was trading at ₹1,50,027 on Wednesday, down 1.12% for the day. MCX Silver fell harder, sliding 1.86% to ₹2,42,763.

On the international front, COMEX Gold hovered in the $4,560–$4,626 implied range. Spot gold was last seen near $4,594.

The primary driver behind gold's retreat was a sharp rise in US rate hike probability. The FOMC rate decision is due on April 29, 2026.

Crude oil surged 4.16% to ₹9,485 on MCX. Nymex WTI futures were trading near the $99–$100 zone amid Middle East tensions.

"Persistent energy price inflation, driven by Middle East tensions, has strengthened expectations that central banks may maintain or further raise interest rates... This monetary tightening outlook is exerting downward pressure on gold prices," said Renisha Chainani, Head of Research at Augmont.

Silver was hit harder than gold due to its industrial character. The OTM options skew on Comex showed call options decreasing more than puts, signaling bearish sentiment.

"Gold has broken important support of $4,650 (approximately ₹1,51,000), next target is $4,550 (approximately ₹1,47,500)... Silver is on the verge of breaking $73 (approximately ₹2,35,000)," Chainani added.

Both Axis Direct and Kotak Neo research reports maintain a short-term bullish regime for gold. This reflects tension between the longer structural trend and near-term selling pressure.

For MCX Gold, critical levels to watch are ₹1,52,000 on the upside and ₹1,48,000 on the downside. A breach below ₹1,49,000 could extend losses toward the ₹1,47,000–₹1,45,000 band.

For silver, ₹2,45,000 remains near-term resistance. A break below ₹2,43,000 risks a slide toward ₹2,38,000 and potentially lower.
