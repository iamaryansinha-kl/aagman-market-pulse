Jio Platforms IPO Draws Record Rs 2.5 Lakh Crore in Anchor Demand

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- Aggregate institutional demand reached roughly Rs 2.5 lakh crore, a record for an Indian IPO.
  • •- The Rs 8,110 crore anchor book was covered more than 30 times.
  • •- BlackRock, Capital Group, Fidelity, T. Rowe Price, Schroders, JPMorgan AM, Franklin Templeton, Invesco and Janus Henderson are in the book.
  • •- Sovereign funds include GIC, Abu Dhabi Investment Authority, Qatar Investment Authority and Norges Bank.
  • •- All of India's top 10 mutual funds and majors like LIC, HDFC Life and SBI Pension are participating.
  • •- The issue opens October 21 at Rs 1,065-1,119, valuing the company near Rs 10.3 lakh crore.

Two days before its public issue opens, Jio Platforms has already broken the record that matters most in Indian primary markets. The Reliance Industries digital unit's Rs 30,200 crore initial public offering has drawn aggregate institutional demand of approximately Rs 2.5 lakh crore — the largest such figure ever recorded for an Indian IPO — with the anchor tranche alone, sized at Rs 8,110 crore, covered more than thirty times over, according to people aware of the matter. The book's composition reads like a directory of the world's largest pools of capital, and its breadth is the story: this is not a momentum trade but an institutional allocation event.

The global roster is exhaustive, and its composition is as telling as its size. BlackRock, Capital Group, Fidelity, T. Rowe Price, Schroders, JPMorgan Asset Management, Franklin Templeton, Invesco and Janus Henderson feature among the asset managers. Four sovereign wealth funds — Singapore's GIC, the Abu Dhabi Investment Authority, the Qatar Investment Authority and Norway's Norges Bank Investment Management — are participating alongside them. On the domestic side, all of India's top ten mutual funds, including SBI Mutual Fund, ICICI Prudential, HDFC, Aditya Birla Sun Life, Nippon India and Kotak, are in the book, joined by LIC, HDFC Life, SBI Life and the major pension funds, including SBI Pension, HDFC Pension and ICICI Prudential. People familiar with the demand said foreign long-only funds, sovereign funds and pension funds account for a substantial portion of it — the patient capital that buys IPOs to hold, not to flip, and whose presence in an anchor book is read by the rest of the market as a certification of the price.

The structure of the issue amplifies what the demand means. The offering is entirely a fresh issue of 27 crore shares with no offer for sale, and the company plans to use Rs 27,500 crore of the proceeds to repay or prepay borrowings — meaning anchor demand at this scale is, in effect, a vote on the deleveraging of the Jio Infocomm balance sheet rather than an exit by insiders. That distinction has been central to how the market has read the issue since it was announced: insiders selling would have priced in scepticism about the valuation, while insiders holding and the company deleveraging invites the opposite inference. At the upper end of the Rs 1,065-1,119 price band, Jio Platforms' post-issue market capitalisation is estimated at roughly Rs 10.3 lakh crore, which would place it among India's most valuable listed companies from the first day of trade.

Valuation remains the debate the anchor book has now largely settled. At the upper band, Emkay estimates the issue prices Jio at 31.7 to 33.3 times FY26 earnings and 15 to 15.6 times FY26 EBITDA, while SBI Securities pegs the post-issue FY26 price-to-earnings multiple at 34.4 times and values the company at an enterprise value of Rs 20,117 per subscriber. For context, those multiples price the market leader of the world's largest data market at a premium to most listed telecoms and a discount to pure-play digital platforms — a deliberate middle ground that the anchor demand suggests institutional investors found acceptable. The comparison set matters here: global investors allocating to the issue are not choosing between Jio and a bank stock but between Jio and the other AI-adjacent data infrastructure franchises of Asia, and in that company the subscription multiples look moderate rather than stretched. The grey market, for its part, is pricing the issue at a premium of about Rs 220, implying an indicative listing near Rs 1,339 and a gain of roughly twenty per cent, though grey-market indications are an informal signal rather than a promise.

The record book does more than fill the issue. It arrives in a market that has just watched foreign investors sell a record Rs 52,100 crore of Indian equities in October, and it demonstrates that the domestic bid — plus the global long-only community's strategic allocation to India — can absorb a Rs 30,000 crore offering without strain. For Reliance, the IPO is the first public offering from the group in nearly two decades and the cornerstone of its value-unlocking playbook; for the market, it is a liquidity test being passed in advance. For the thousands of retail investors who will apply when the book opens, the anchor print converts an act of faith into a priced queue: the institutions have already voted, at thirty times cover, and the question they answer by subscribing is no longer whether the asset is worth owning but where in the allocation line they stand. With the main book opening Tuesday and closing Thursday, the anchor print sets a high bar — and, unusually for an IPO of this size, leaves the question of the week not whether the issue gets done but how much of it gets left unfilled.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
HOW LARGE IS JIO PLATFORMS' ANCHOR BOOK?
The Rs 8,110 crore anchor tranche drew demand of around Rs 2.5 lakh crore in aggregate, more than 30 times the shares on offer — a record for an Indian IPO.
WHO ARE THE INVESTORS?
Global asset managers including BlackRock, Fidelity, Capital Group and Schroders; sovereign funds GIC, ADIA, QIA and Norges Bank; all of India's top 10 mutual funds; and LIC, HDFC Life and major pension funds.
WHAT WILL THE PROCEEDS BE USED FOR?
The issue is entirely fresh, and Rs 27,500 crore of proceeds are earmarked to repay or prepay borrowings.
WHEN DOES THE IPO OPEN AND AT WHAT PRICE?
The issue opens October 21 and closes October 23, with a price band of Rs 1,065 to Rs 1,119 per share and an implied post-issue market capitalisation near Rs 10.3 lakh crore. GLOSSARY: - Anchor book: The portion of an IPO allocated to institutional investors a day before the public issue opens. - Grey market premium: The unofficial market price of IPO shares before listing. - Fresh issue: New shares created by the company, with proceeds going to it rather than to selling shareholders. - Enterprise value per subscriber: A valuation metric comparing total company value, net of debt, to its user base.
