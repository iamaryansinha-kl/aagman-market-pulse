Iran Stock Market Reopens May 19 After 80-Day War Closure, Extended Hours

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Iran's Tehran Stock Exchange reopens May 19 after an 80-day closure since US-Israel missile strikes began on February 28, 2026
  • 02/TEDPIX benchmark had hit a record 4.5 million points in January before tumbling on protests and a 20-day internet blackout
  • 03/Trading hours extended by one hour to let war-damaged firms disclose key information; Strait of Hormuz remains shut

Iran's stock market reopened on May 19 after an 80-day shutdown. The Tehran Stock Exchange had been closed since February 28, when US and Israeli forces carried out missile strikes on Tehran and other cities.

Trading in shares, equity funds, and equity-linked derivatives resumed on Tuesday and Wednesday. Market hours were extended by one hour to give major companies extra time to disclose material information.

The extension targets firms that suffered war damage or held shareholder meetings while trading was suspended. This aims to improve transparency before normal order flow returns.

Hamid Yari, deputy at Iran's Securities and Exchange Organization, told state media the reopening was designed to "protect investors' assets, prevent emotional behaviour, and create conditions for trading with more accurate and transparent information."

The TEDPIX benchmark index had climbed to a record high of nearly 4.5 million points at the start of 2026. It later tumbled after nationwide protests intensified on January 8 and 9, followed by a government-imposed 20-day internet shutdown.

Iran's market is largely cut off from global indexes due to Western sanctions. It is not the primary source of economic financing, but its resumption offers a real-time gauge of domestic investor confidence and liquidity conditions.

Global oil prices have surged since the war began, with retail fuel costs now more than 50% higher than pre-war levels. The Strait of Hormuz remains effectively closed, blocking roughly 20% of global petroleum consumption and a fifth of LNG exports daily.
