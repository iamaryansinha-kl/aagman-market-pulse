Nifty Falls 97 Points as Crude Surges Past $110, RBI Norms Hit Banks

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Nifty 50 fell 97 points, or 0.40%, to settle at 23,995.70 on April 28 as crude surged past $110 per barrel
  • 02/Bank Nifty plunged 864 points, or 1.45%, to 55,400.35 — its fifth straight decline — on RBI's new Expected Credit Loss framework
  • 03/Broader markets bucked the trend: Nifty Midcap gained 0.28% and Smallcap rose 0.42%, hitting fresh highs relative to benchmarks

Equity benchmarks closed lower on Tuesday, April 28, 2026. The Nifty 50 fell 97 points, or 0.40%, to settle at 23,995.70.

The BSE Sensex dropped 416.72 points, or 0.54%, to close at 76,886.91. The session was volatile from the start.

The Nifty opened at 24,050.65 and climbed to an intraday high of 24,181.80. It failed to hold gains and drifted to a low of 23,957.05 before closing.

On the daily chart, the index formed a small-bodied candle with a prominent upper wick. This marks the fourth consecutive session of such formations.

Banking stocks were the session's biggest drag. The RBI finalised the Expected Credit Loss framework, shifting banks to forward-looking provisioning.

PSU Banks declined nearly 2% and private banks slipped close to 1%. Axis Bank and State Bank of India were among the notable laggards.

The Bank Nifty fell 863.95 points, or 1.45%, to close at 55,400.35. It marked a fresh breakdown below last week's low.

Oil & Gas emerged as the top sectoral gainer, rising around 1.5%. ONGC and Adani Enterprises led the charge.

Metals, Chemicals and select pharma stocks also attracted buying. The broader market notably decoupled from the benchmarks.

The Nifty Midcap index gained 0.28% after breaking above the 60,350–60,400 resistance zone. The Smallcap index advanced 0.42%, clearing the 17,900–17,920 range.

Geopolitics remained the dominant macro overhang. Disruptions at the Strait of Hormuz entered the ninth week of the Iran conflict.

Domestic crude futures rose over 3% to near ₹9,400. Natural gas jumped more than 7%.

Foreign institutional investors have been net sellers of ₹44,281 crore through April. This added to the liquidity pressure on domestic markets.

Precious metals came under pressure despite the risk-off backdrop. MCX gold fell over 0.8% and silver dropped around 1.8%.

Analysts noted crude oil was competing for the same pool of inflation-hedge capital. This pulled flows away from metals.

The Indian rupee weakened further, crossing the 94.5 level against the US dollar. This reflected elevated energy costs and persistent geopolitical uncertainty.

India VIX eased marginally by 1.80% to close at 18.04. Yet 293 of the Nifty 500 stocks ended in the red.

The near-term outlook hinges on geopolitics. The Nifty is consolidating in a broad 23,600–24,400 band.

A move above Friday's high of 24,206 could open upside toward 24,400. A break below 23,813 risks a slide toward 23,600.
