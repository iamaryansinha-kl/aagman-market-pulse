India's Botanical Wellness Gap: Farmers See 1/10th of Final Value

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/The price gap between farm-gate ashwagandha and branded retail is over 10x, leaving farmers with less than 10% of final value
  • 02/India can grow nearly every medicinal plant the global wellness industry demands, yet brands import extracts from abroad due to quality inconsistency
  • 03/Existing clusters in MP, Rajasthan, Kerala and Karnataka prove the model works, but scaling remains the critical challenge for the next decade

India's farmers grow ashwagandha, turmeric, tulsi, and moringa that the global wellness industry pays premium prices for. The country can cultivate almost every medicinal plant in current demand.

The price a farmer receives for ashwagandha root and what a brand pays for the same ingredient differs by a factor of ten or more. The plant grows on Indian farms. The value is captured almost everywhere else.

Most medicinal plants are still treated as secondary crops or harvested wild. Quality varies wildly batch to batch. Active compound content swings unpredictably. Serious buyers, including Indian brands, often import extracts from abroad despite native availability.

The premium is not paid for the plant. It is paid for verified content like withanolides in ashwagandha, curcumin in turmeric, and eugenol in tulsi. Testing currently happens far from the farm. So the margin stays far from the farm too.

A single farmer selling 200 kilograms has zero negotiating power. Brands need consistency, traceability, and volume. One farmer cannot offer any of this. A collective of 500 farmers can.

There are working ashwagandha clusters in Madhya Pradesh and Rajasthan with contract buyers already locked in. Turmeric and cardamom clusters in Kerala and Karnataka are further along. The template exists. It simply has not been built at the scale the next decade will demand.

The intense wellness demand wave is arriving whether farms are ready or not. Brands will need serious volumes of clean, tested, and traceable botanical ingredients over the next five to ten years.

If infrastructure encompassing cultivation practice, shared testing, and aggregation gets built, farmers become the direct beneficiaries. If not, raw material leaves as commodity and returns as finished product at ten times the price.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT IS THE BOTANICAL OPPORTUNITY FOR INDIAN FARMERS?
INDIA CAN GROW ALMOST EVERY MEDICINAL PLANT THE GLOBAL WELLNESS INDUSTRY IS PAYING PREMIUM PRICES FOR, INCLUDING ASHWAGANDHA, TURMERIC, TULSI, MORINGA, BRAHMI AND SHATAVARI. THE DEMAND IS REAL AND THE MARKET NUMBERS ARE SERIOUS.
WHY ARE FARMERS NOT BENEFITING FROM THE BOTANICAL BOOM?
THE PRICE A FARMER RECEIVES FOR ASHWAGANDHA ROOT AND WHAT A BRAND PAYS FOR THE SAME INGREDIENT DIFFERS BY A FACTOR OF TEN OR MORE. MOST MEDICINAL PLANTS ARE TREATED AS SECONDARY CROPS OR HARVESTED FROM THE WILD, WITH QUALITY VARYING BATCH TO BATCH.
WHAT ARE THE THREE MAIN PROBLEMS HOLDING BACK FARMERS?
FIRST, LACK OF QUALITY CONSISTENCY AS MOST PLANTS ARE SECONDARY CROPS. SECOND, ABSENCE OF STANDARDISATION AND TESTING INFRASTRUCTURE NEAR FARMS FOR ACTIVE COMPOUNDS LIKE WITHANOLIDES AND CURCUMIN. THIRD, LACK OF COLLECTIVE BARGAINING POWER AS INDIVIDUAL FARMERS SELLING 200 KILOGRAMS CANNOT MATCH BRAND REQUIREMENTS FOR VOLUME AND TRACEABILITY.
WHAT IS THE 'AMUL MOMENT' REFERENCED IN THE ARTICLE?
IT REFERS TO VERGHESE KURIEN'S MODEL FROM THE 1980S THAT CLUSTERED FARMERS INTO COLLECTIVES. THE AUTHOR ARGUES MEDICINAL PLANT FARMERS NEED A SIMILAR AMUL-STYLE AGGREGATION MODEL TO GAIN NEGOTIATING POWER AND CAPTURE VALUE IN THE SUPPLY CHAIN.
WHERE ARE WORKING EXAMPLES OF THIS MODEL ALREADY IN OPERATION?
ASHWAGANDHA CLUSTERS IN MADHYA PRADESH AND RAJASTHAN ARE ALREADY RUNNING WITH CONTRACT BUYERS LOCKED IN. TURMERIC AND CARDAMOM CLUSTERS IN KERALA AND KARNATAKA ARE FURTHER ALONG, PROVING THE TEMPLATE WORKS BUT HAS NOT YET BEEN SCALED.
