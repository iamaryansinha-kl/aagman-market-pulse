Nvidia Q1 FY27 Earnings Preview: Can the AI Rally Continue?

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Nvidia reports Q1 FY27 earnings on May 20 with consensus at $78B revenue and $1.77 EPS, data center near $73B
  • 02/Stock has traded sideways for 3 months after a 65% FY2026 revenue surge; options market prices an 8-10% move
  • 03/Q2 guidance, gross margin direction, and China commentary are the three critical numbers to watch

Nvidia Corp reports fiscal Q1 2027 earnings after US market close on Wednesday, May 20, 2026. The print is the single largest AI infrastructure data point this quarter.

Wall Street consensus expects roughly $78 billion in revenue and $1.77 in non-GAAP EPS. That implies approximately 77-78% year-on-year revenue growth.

Buy-side whispers run higher than published estimates. Some sell-side desks model a print closer to $79 billion, with the most aggressive houses above $80 billion.

Nvidia has beaten the Street every quarter of this AI cycle. A beat alone is already priced in. A print between $78 and $79 billion meets consensus but may disappoint buy-side desks.

Q2 FY27 guidance will drive the after-hours tape more than the headline beat. Consensus expects $85 to $87 billion, with whisper numbers near $90 billion.

Data center revenue is expected near $73 billion for Q1, roughly 90% of total sales. The Blackwell platform now drives the majority of data center compute.

Gross margin direction matters as much as revenue. Nvidia guided to mid-70s gross margins, and any slippage would signal pricing pressure or yield issues on Blackwell.

China commentary remains the highest-variance line. Nvidia is not assuming any data center compute revenue from China in its outlook, but policy shifts could change that.

CFO Colette Kress said Nvidia remains "disappointed" by export restrictions. The company views its ability to sell to China as key to America's AI competitiveness.

The options market is pricing an implied move of 8-10% on the print. That is in line with recent quarters and sets up for significant volatility.

Fiscal 2026 revenue totaled $215.9 billion, up 65% year-over-year. Data Center alone generated $193.7 billion, roughly 90% of total revenue.

The top four customers — Microsoft, Meta, Google, and Amazon — represent approximately 50% of data center revenue. Customer concentration remains a structural risk.

CEO Jensen Huang has said Blackwell sales are "off the charts." The next-generation Rubin platform remains on track for a 2026 rollout.

Analysts note that Nvidia trades at roughly 47x forward earnings, compressed from a peak near 65x. The multiple already embeds high growth expectations.

A guidance beat above $87 billion for Q2 would confirm the Blackwell ramp is accelerating. Below $85 billion gives bears a deceleration narrative.
