Trump Extends Jones Act Waiver 90 Days Amid Iran War Energy Crisis

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Trump extends Jones Act waiver for 90 days starting May 18, 2026, after an initial 60-day waiver issued March 18
  • 02/The move aims to ease energy prices as the Iran war cut off one-fifth of global oil supply; gasoline now averages $4.044 per gallon
  • 03/U.S. maritime groups strongly oppose the extension, arguing it harms American workers and national security without lowering pump prices

The Trump administration announced on April 24, 2026, that it will extend the Jones Act waiver for an additional 90 days. The extension begins at 12 a.m. ET on May 18, 2026.

The original 60-day waiver was issued on March 18, 2026. It allowed foreign vessels to transport oil and natural gas between U.S. ports amid the Iran war.

White House press secretary Taylor Rogers said new data showed significantly more supply reached U.S. ports faster under the waiver. The administration said more than 40 tankers have used, or will use, the waiver.

The national average gasoline price was $4.044 per gallon on April 27, up $0.324 from March 16, according to the U.S. Energy Information Administration.

The Jones Act, passed in 1920, requires goods shipped between U.S. ports to use U.S.-built, -flagged, and -crewed vessels. Of nearly 7,500 oil tankers worldwide, only 54 are Jones Act-compliant.

The law supports nearly 650,000 American jobs and over $154 billion in annual economic output, according to the American Maritime Congress.

U.S. maritime trade associations and labor unions responded with unified opposition. "This broad Jones Act waiver is a gut punch to American workers and should be terminated immediately," said Jennifer Carpenter, president and CEO of the American Waterways Operators.

"The facts speak for themselves: the original waiver has done nothing to lower prices at the pump for the American consumer," said Adam Vokac, president of the Marine Engineers' Beneficial Association.

The Seafarers International Union warned that repeated waivers erode investor confidence. "Capital does not flow into uncertainty," the union said in a statement.

The American Maritime Congress called for alternative measures including maximizing direct imports, expanding pipeline and rail capacity, and using Defense Production Act authorities.
