Brent Crude Surges Past $108 as Strait of Hormuz Supply Risks Intensify

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Brent crude is trading near $108-110 per barrel as of May 4, 2026, with WTI above $100, reflecting acute supply anxiety in the market.
  • 02/Roughly 20% of global oil supply — 15 to 20 million barrels per day — transits the Strait of Hormuz, now under escalating US-Iran confrontation and a naval blockade cutting ~2 million barrels/day.
  • 03/Watch whether the disruption persists through end of May; if so, analysts warn Brent could reprice toward $110 and the 2026 forward-implied average near $94 may shift materially higher.

Brent crude is trading between $108 and $110 per barrel as of May 4, 2026, while WTI has climbed above $100. The rally reflects a market increasingly convinced that supply losses from the Strait of Hormuz will not resolve quickly.

The flashpoint is a direct confrontation between the United States and Iran. A US naval blockade of Iranian ports has removed approximately 2 million barrels per day from circulation. Alternative pipeline routes have proven unable to offset the shortfall.

The Strait of Hormuz remains the single most critical oil chokepoint on the planet. About one-fifth of global supply, or 15 to 20 million barrels per day, passes through the narrow waterway. Even a partial disruption cascades across every consuming economy.

Analysts are scrambling to revise their 2026 Brent forecasts. Barclays lifted its target to $100 from $85 on the assumption of a prolonged disruption. Goldman Sachs moved its call to $85 from $77. Bank of America now sees $77.50, up from $61.

The consensus shift is not speculative. The market deficit is currently estimated at approximately 6.6 million barrels per day. That gap is large enough to drain inventories at a pace that forces sustained price strength rather than a temporary spike.

The forward curve tells its own story. The Brent forward-implied average for 2026 sits near $94 per barrel, embedding expectations that the crisis will linger. Traders are no longer pricing a quick resolution.

If the disruption extends through the end of May, several desks have warned that Brent could fully reprice toward $110. The threshold is not arbitrary — it represents the level where demand destruction and accelerated strategic releases would begin to balance the market mechanically.

Equity markets have already voted. The Energy Select Sector SPDR Fund (XLE) is up 38.2% year-to-date, making energy the best-performing S&P 500 sector by a wide margin. The move reflects both spot oil strength and a repricing of long-term energy geopolitical risk.
