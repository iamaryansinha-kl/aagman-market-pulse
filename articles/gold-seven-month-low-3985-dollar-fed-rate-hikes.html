Gold Hits Seven-Month Low at $3,985 as Dollar Surges, Fed Rate Hikes Loom

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Spot gold drops 0.4% to $3,985.89 per ounce, touching its lowest level since November 2025
  • 02/U.S. dollar surges to 13-month high for third straight day, making gold expensive for foreign buyers
  • 03/Markets price in three Fed rate hikes this year with 67% probability of a September increase

Spot gold fell 0.4% to $3,985.89 per ounce on Thursday. It hit the lowest level since November 2025 a day earlier.

U.S. gold futures for August delivery lost 0.2% to $4,001.60. Bullion slipped below the key $4,000 mark for the first time in over seven months.

The U.S. dollar advanced for a third consecutive day. It reached a 13-month high, pressuring gold prices globally.

A stronger dollar makes gold costlier for buyers holding other currencies. This has driven consistent selling across bullion markets.

Traders now expect three Federal Reserve interest rate hikes this year. The CME FedWatch Tool shows a 67% chance of a September increase.

U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent backed Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's plan to reduce forward guidance. Bessent said policymakers must keep an open mind on inflation from the Iran conflict.

Investors are watching the U.S. Personal Consumption Expenditures data due later Thursday. This is the Fed's preferred inflation gauge and will shape policy expectations.

Spot silver fell 0.2% to $57.33 per ounce. Platinum lost 0.2% to $1,575.85, while palladium gained 0.3% to $1,170.25.
