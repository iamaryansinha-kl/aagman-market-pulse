Fossil Fuel Power in India, China Falls for First Time This Century

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Fossil fuel electricity generation fell in both India and China in 2025, the first simultaneous decline this century
  • 02/India's fossil output dropped 52 TWh (-3.3%) while China's fell 56 TWh (-0.9%), both driven by record clean energy additions
  • 03/India's coal share fell to 71% from 75% as solar surged 53 TWh; watch if mild weather or structural shift sustains the trend

Fossil fuel electricity generation fell in India and China during 2025. This marks the first simultaneous decline this century for the world's two largest power consumers.

India's fossil generation dropped by 52 TWh, a 3.3% decrease. This reversed four years of strong post-pandemic growth in coal and gas output.

China saw fossil output fall by 56 TWh, down 0.9%. It was China's first fossil generation decline since 2015, driven by solar additions exceeding demand growth.

India's renewable generation grew by 98 TWh in 2025, twice the pace of demand growth. Solar led with a 53 TWh jump (+37%), followed by wind at 22 TWh (+28%) and hydro at 21 TWh (+14%).

India added 38 GW of solar capacity in 2025, up 54% from 2024. For the first time, India installed more solar capacity than the United States in a single year.

Coal's share of India's electricity mix dropped to 71% in 2025 from 75% in 2024. Coal-fired generation fell by 44 TWh, a 2.9% decline from the previous year.

India's power demand rose only 49 TWh (+2.4%) in 2025, less than half the 118 TWh (+6.2%) growth seen in 2024. Mild summer temperatures cut an estimated 32 TWh of cooling demand.

Ember's Global Electricity Review 2026 calls this a structural shift at a vulnerable moment. The world has faced two major fossil fuel shocks in four years: Russia's Ukraine invasion and the US-Israel conflict with Iran.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
HOW MUCH DID FOSSIL FUEL GENERATION FALL IN INDIA AND CHINA IN 2025?
INDIA'S FOSSIL GENERATION FELL BY 52 TWH (-3.3 PERCENT) AND CHINA'S FELL BY 56 TWH (-0.9 PERCENT). THIS WAS THE FIRST YEAR THIS CENTURY THAT BOTH COUNTRIES RECORDED A SIMULTANEOUS DECLINE IN FOSSIL FUEL POWER OUTPUT.
WHAT DROVE THE DECLINE IN INDIA'S FOSSIL FUEL GENERATION?
THREE FACTORS CONTRIBUTED: RECORD RENEWABLE GROWTH (+98 TWH), MIDER SUMMER TEMPERATURES THAT REDUCED COOLING DEMAND BY AN ESTIMATED 32 TWH, AND SLOWER INDUSTRIAL DEMAND GROWTH. INDIA'S GDP GREW 6.6 PERCENT IN 2025, SIMILAR TO 6.5 PERCENT IN 2024.
HOW MUCH SOLAR AND WIND CAPACITY DID INDIA ADD IN 2025?
INDIA ADDED A RECORD 38 GW OF SOLAR CAPACITY, UP 54 PERCENT FROM 2024. WIND CAPACITY ADDITIONS REACHED 6.3 GW AND HYDRO ADDITIONS HIT 4 GW. FOR THE FIRST TIME, INDIA INSTALLED MORE SOLAR CAPACITY THAN THE UNITED STATES IN A SINGLE YEAR.
WHAT HAPPENED TO COAL'S SHARE IN INDIA'S ELECTRICITY MIX?
COAL'S SHARE OF INDIA'S ELECTRICITY GENERATION FELL TO 71 PERCENT IN 2025 FROM 75 PERCENT IN 2024. COAL-FIRED GENERATION ITSELF DECLINED BY 44 TWH, A 2.9 PERCENT DROP FROM THE PREVIOUS YEAR.
IS THIS DECLINE STRUCTURAL OR TEMPORARY?
THE OUTLOOK IS MIXED. CHINA'S DECLINE APPEARS STRUCTURAL, AS CLEAN POWER HAS MET ALL NEW DEMAND FOR SIX QUARTERS. INDIA'S DROP WAS AIDED BY UNUSUALLY MILD WEATHER; IF TEMPERATURES RETURN TO NORMAL LEVELS, DEMAND AND FOSSIL GENERATION COULD REBOUND. SUSTAINED RENEWABLE GROWTH WILL DETERMINE WHETHER INDIA'S COAL PEAK IS PERMANENT.
