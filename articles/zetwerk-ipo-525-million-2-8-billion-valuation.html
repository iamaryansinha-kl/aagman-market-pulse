Zetwerk Plans $525 Million IPO Next Week at $2.8 Billion Valuation

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • •- The Bengaluru manufacturing platform is preparing to launch its IPO as early as next week, raising as much as $525 million, or nearly Rs 5,000 crore.
  • •- The targeted valuation is Rs 270 billion ($2.8 billion), with a listing targeted for October 23.
  • •- The offer combines a Rs 26 billion fresh issue with a secondary sale of up to 96.84 million shares by existing shareholders.

Zetwerk Pvt Ltd, a Bengaluru-based manufacturing platform, is preparing to launch an initial public offering as early as next week that may raise as much as $525 million, people familiar with the matter said.

That is nearly Rs 5,000 crore at current exchange rates. The company has already met potential investors ahead of the planned share sale.

The targeted valuation is Rs 270 billion, or $2.8 billion. The sale is likely to open on October 15, with a targeted listing date of October 23, the people said.

Talks are ongoing, and details including the size and timing of the IPO may still change. A representative for Zetwerk did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

The company filed confidentially for an IPO in March and received regulatory approval in July. That timeline means the machinery has been turning quietly for months.

The bankers are marquee. Managers on the offering include Kotak Mahindra Capital, JM Financial Services, Avendus Capital, Pantomath Capital Advisors, and the Indian units of HSBC, Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs, according to the prospectus.

A seven-firm syndicate spanning domestic and global banks is the standard configuration for a deal of this size, and signals the company is targeting the full range of institutional demand.

The structure of the deal tells two stories at once. The IPO comprises a fresh issue worth Rs 26 billion and a secondary sale of up to 96.84 million shares by existing shareholders.

At the midpoint of the Rs 5,000 crore raise, the fresh component covers just over half. Existing investors are cashing out a meaningful slice alongside the new capital.

Zetwerk operates 26 manufacturing sites across India, the United States, Spain and Germany. It manufactures products for utilities, renewable energy, consumer electronics and artificial-intelligence infrastructure.

That mix places the company at the intersection of two of the market's favourite themes: energy-transition hardware and the AI buildout. Both are procurement-heavy supply chains where scale manufacturers have been winning long contracts.

The timing tests the market. Indian companies have raised about $14 billion through IPOs this year, after a record $22 billion in 2025, according to data compiled by Bloomberg.

That pipeline has held up even as secondary-market volatility spiked this month, with benchmark indices swinging sharply on foreign selling and rate-hike worries.

For Zetwerk, a successful listing at $2.8 billion would rank among the largest by an Indian manufacturer in recent years and would validate the made-in-India contract manufacturing theme at scale.

The test for investors will be in the prospectus details. How much of the secondary component early backers are selling, and what the fresh proceeds will fund, will matter more than the headline valuation.

If the company can show order-book depth across its four end-markets and a credible path from custom manufacturing to higher-margin programme ownership, the valuation has support.

If the secondary component dominates, the message flips: this becomes an exit dressed as a growth raise, and investors have seen enough of those to be picky.

For now, the calendar is the anchor: a launch as early as October 15 puts Zetwerk in front of investors within days, and the pricing will tell the market what it really thinks about new paper in a nervous tape.

Confidential filing allows a company to submit draft offer documents to regulators without immediate public disclosure. For Zetwerk, that process began in March and ended with approval in July.

The quiet period since then has been spent on pre-IPO conversations with institutional investors. Those meetings typically test demand at various valuation points before the price band is fixed.

Zetwerk runs a contract-manufacturing model rather than owning consumer brands. It builds to customer specification across heavy electricals, renewables equipment, electronics and infrastructure components.

That model lives and dies on utilisation and order books. Fixed costs across 26 plants in four countries must be covered by contract wins, which makes long-duration orders from utility and energy customers strategically important.

The fresh issue of Rs 26 billion gives the company capital to expand capacity and consolidate its supplier base. The secondary component gives early investors partial liquidity after years of private holding.

That balance is increasingly common in Indian new listings. Pure secondary issues draw scepticism, while pure fresh issues leave early backers locked in; a mix lets both sides claim a win.

The market backdrop adds stakes. With roughly $14 billion raised this year against a record $22 billion in 2025, the window is open but crowded, and every large deal now competes for the same institutional allocations.

A stumble on pricing would be read well beyond Zetwerk itself. The contract-manufacturing theme has drawn a queue of issuers, and the first big print sets the reference point for those behind it.

GLOSSARY: IPO (initial public offering): The first sale of a company's shares to public investors, leading to a stock-exchange listing. Fresh issue: New shares created by the company in an IPO; the proceeds go to the company itself. Secondary sale (offer for sale): Existing shareholders selling their own shares in an IPO; the proceeds go to them, not the company. Valuation: The total worth assigned to a company, here Rs 270 billion ($2.8 billion) before listing. Bookrunner: The investment banks that manage an IPO, set the price band and place shares with investors. Contract manufacturing: Producing goods to another company's designs and specifications rather than selling under your own brand.
