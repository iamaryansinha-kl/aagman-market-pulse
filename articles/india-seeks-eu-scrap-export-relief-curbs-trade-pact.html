India Seeks EU Scrap Export Relief as Curbs Threaten Trade Pact Gains

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/India has formally asked the EU for relief from planned metal scrap export curbs set to begin May 2027
  • 02/India imported 366,000 tons of aluminium scrap from the EU in 2025 and was the bloc's top buyer in Q1 2026
  • 03/The India-EU trade deal is set to take effect early next year, but scrap curbs could raise costs for domestic steel and aluminium makers

India has urged the European Union to reconsider planned restrictions on metal scrap exports. The curbs are set to take effect from May 2027 under the EU's revised waste shipment regulation.

The new rules will bar exports of non-hazardous waste to countries outside the OECD group. The EU must approve non-OECD nations by November 2026 to allow continued access.

India was the EU's top buyer of aluminium scrap in the first quarter of 2026. The country imported about 366,000 tons of aluminium scrap from the EU in 2025.

The EU's aluminium scrap exports hit a record 1.27 million metric tons in 2025. This is roughly 50% above 2019 levels, according to European Aluminium.

"The EU is a key supplier of high-quality ferrous scrap to India and any export curbs could tighten supply and raise costs for domestic steelmakers," said Pankaj Chadha, chairman of the Engineering Export Promotion Council of India.

A government official, speaking anonymously, said export quotas rather than a complete ban could be one relief measure. India has formally applied to retain access to recyclable materials under the new regime.

The Material Recycling Association of India warned that growing "resource nationalism" could disrupt supplies. This would boost procurement costs and fuel competition among importing nations.

The India-EU trade pact is expected to be signed by December and implemented from February-March next year. However, the deal does not provide direct relief from EU carbon emission levies.

HOW MUCH ALUMINIUM SCRAP DOES INDIA IMPORT FROM THE EU?
INDIA IMPORTED ABOUT 366,000 TONS OF ALUMINIUM SCRAP FROM THE EU IN 2025. INDIA WAS ALSO THE BLOC'S TOP BUYER OF ALUMINIUM SCRAP IN THE FIRST QUARTER OF 2026.

INDIA HAS FORMALLY APPLIED TO RETAIN ACCESS TO RECYCLABLE MATERIALS UNDER THE NEW REGIME. A GOVERNMENT OFFICIAL SUGGESTED EXPORT QUOTAS RATHER THAN A COMPLETE BAN COULD BE ONE RELIEF MEASURE.
WHY DO THE CURBS MATTER FOR INDIAN STEEL AND ALUMINIUM MAKERS?
THE EU IS A KEY SUPPLIER OF HIGH-QUALITY FERROUS SCRAP TO INDIA. EXPORT CURBS COULD TIGHTEN SUPPLY AND RAISE COSTS FOR DOMESTIC STEELMAKERS, UNDERMINING GAINS FROM THE UPCOMING TRADE PACT.
