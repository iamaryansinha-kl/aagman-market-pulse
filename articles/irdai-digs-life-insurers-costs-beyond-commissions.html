Irdai Digs Into Life Insurers' Costs Beyond Commissions

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

Irdai is digging deeper into life insurance costs.

The Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India has launched a probe into expenses beyond commissions that life insurers charge policyholders. The investigation covers a range of costs that may be inflating premiums and reducing net returns for consumers.

Life insurance in India operates on a cost-heavy model. Premiums include commissions to agents, administrative expenses, marketing costs, and other charges. While commissions are regulated, other expenses have received less scrutiny, and some insurers may be passing on excessive costs to policyholders.

The regulator is concerned about opacity. Policyholders often do not understand the full cost structure of their insurance products. Expenses are embedded in the premium and deducted from the fund value in unit-linked plans, making them invisible to most consumers.

Irdai has been pushing for greater transparency. The regulator has already mandated that insurers disclose commission rates and has capped charges on unit-linked insurance plans. The new probe extends this oversight to the broader expense structure.

The investigation could lead to caps on non-commission expenses. If Irdai finds that certain costs are excessive or unjustified, it may impose limits, similar to how it regulates commissions. This would squeeze margins for insurers but benefit policyholders.

The life insurance industry has been growing steadily but faces challenges from mis-selling, high lapse rates, and low persistency. Many policies are sold on the basis of exaggerated returns, and high costs contribute to the disappointment when actual returns fall short.
