Infosys Cuts FY27 Guidance to 1-2%, Shares Fall 4%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

Mumbai: Infosys shares fell 4% on Monday, three days after the IT major delivered the news investors had been bracing for: a cut to its full-year revenue guidance.

The Bengaluru-based company now expects FY27 revenue growth of 1-2% in constant currency terms, down from the 1.5-3% range it had guided to earlier. It retained its operating margin guidance at 20-22%.

The September quarter that triggered the downgrade was soft across most parameters. Revenue came in at Rs 49,244 crore, up 2.1% sequentially — broadly in line with analyst estimates. But the composition of that growth matters: a meaningful share came from the Optimum Healthcare acquisition consolidating for a full quarter rather than underlying client demand expanding.

Constant currency revenue growth of 1.2% quarter-on-quarter missed the 1.4% the Street had pencilled in. Dollar revenue rose 1.2% to $5,145 million, a whisker below the $5,151 million analysts had expected. The rupee's weakness through the quarter provided a modest translational cushion, but not enough to disguise the softness in underlying client budgets.

Profitability slipped. EBIT rose 1.6% sequentially to Rs 10,330 crore, below the estimated Rs 10,470 crore, and the EBIT margin came in at 21% against expectations of 21.3%.

Net profit edged up 0.7% quarter-on-quarter to Rs 7,823 crore, falling short of the Rs 7,917 crore consensus estimate.

Management attributed the downgrade to a softer demand environment, subdued volumes and pricing pressure, with weakness concentrated in retail and high tech, where discretionary spending has run below expectations. Strength in financial services, energy and utilities, and manufacturing was not enough to offset those two verticals.

CFO Jayesh Sanghrajka said higher productivity expectations from clients are also weighing on revenue, a theme that has run through the IT sector all year as generative AI tools let clients do more with fewer outsourced hours. CEO Salil Parekh countered that the company continues to gain market share and remains a preferred AI strategy and deployment partner for its largest clients — a claim the deal pipeline supports, even if the income statement has yet to reflect it.

Margins held at 21%, helped by the company's cost-efficiency programme, Project Maximus. A 70-basis-point hit from post-sales customer support provisions and 20 basis points from acquisitions were offset by currency benefits, calendar effects and the cost programme. Management flagged that employee compensation costs and continued pricing pressure will weigh on margins in the second half, with Project Maximus expected to absorb part of the impact.

There were genuine bright spots. Large deal wins totalled $5.2 billion in total contract value, with 62% classified as net new business — a healthy number by any historical standard, and evidence that clients continue to consolidate spending onto fewer, larger partners even as overall budgets stay tight.

AI is becoming a real revenue line, not just a talking point. AI-related revenue accounted for 10.5% of total revenue in Q2, up from 8.2% in the June quarter — a 29% sequential jump, with growth across all six areas of the company's AI strategy.

The company also signed a new $2 billion mega deal on a net-new basis, which is expected to contribute around 1% to revenue next fiscal year.

Headcount rose by 4,480 sequentially, and the company is onboarding more than 1,000 employees this month under an expanded collaboration with US financial services firm Truist to build a global capability centre in Hyderabad. The Truist engagement, which consolidates technology, data, AI and operations work previously spread across multiple suppliers into a single operating model, is emblematic of how large deals are being structured in the AI era — fewer vendors, larger scopes.

The competitive contrast sharpened the disappointment. TCS reported constant currency growth of 0.5% with revenue of Rs 73,188 crore and profit of Rs 13,884 crore, while HCLTech actually raised its FY27 guidance to 2.5-4% after a 21% year-on-year profit jump. HCLTech's advanced AI services revenue grew 28% sequentially, underscoring that execution — not demand — is the variable separating the sector's winners from its laggards this quarter.

Infosys is the sector's guidance bellwether, and a second consecutive guidance cut — the upper end has now come down from 3.5% in April to 2% — resets revenue assumptions across the IT services complex. Rivals with stronger momentum, notably HCLTech, will absorb some of the revenue that Infosys cannot execute, but the sector-wide signal is unmistakable: clients are spending cautiously, and AI is as much a pricing headwind as a growth opportunity for the traditional outsourcing model.

For investors, the question is whether the stock's 29% YTD decline already prices in a guidance range that implies near-flat organic growth. At its Friday close of Rs 1,154.50, the stock traded well below the target prices of most brokerages that maintained buy ratings after the Q1 disappointment — targets that will now be revised down to reflect the new guidance reality. Deal wins and AI revenue suggest the pipeline is intact; the conversion of that pipeline into billed revenue is what the market will judge the company on next quarter.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS //
WHAT GUIDANCE DID INFOSYS CUT?
Infosys lowered its FY27 revenue growth guidance to 1-2% in constant currency from the earlier 1.5-3%. It retained its operating margin guidance at 20-22%. This is the second cut this fiscal year.
HOW DID Q2 NUMBERS COMPARE WITH ESTIMATES?
Revenue of Rs 49,244 crore was broadly in line, but constant currency growth of 1.2% missed the 1.4% estimate. EBIT margin of 21% fell short of the 21.3% forecast, and net profit of Rs 7,823 crore came in below the Rs 7,917 crore consensus.
WHAT WERE THE BRIGHT SPOTS IN INFOSYS' Q2?
Large deal wins of $5.2 billion TCV (62% net new), AI revenue rising to 10.5% of total from 8.2%, a new $2 billion mega deal expected to add 1% to revenue next fiscal, and headcount growth of 4,480.
WHY IS THE STOCK FALLING?
The guidance cut implies near-flat organic growth for the rest of the fiscal year, with pricing pressure and weak retail and high-tech demand offsetting strong deal wins. The stock is down about 29% year-to-date before Monday's 4% decline. GLOSSARY: CONSTANT CURRENCY (CC) GROWTH: Revenue growth calculated using unchanged exchange rates, stripping out the effect of currency movements between the rupee and the dollar. GUIDANCE: A company's official forecast for its own financial performance, which management updates each quarter. IT investors treat Infosys' guidance as a sector benchmark. TOTAL CONTRACT VALUE (TCV): The full value of deals signed in a period, including future revenue. Net-new TCV excludes renewals of existing contracts. EBIT MARGIN: Earnings before interest and tax as a percentage of revenue — the standard profitability measure for IT services companies. PROJECT MAXIMUS: Infosys' internal cost-efficiency programme, designed to protect margins through automation and productivity gains. AI-LED DEFLATION: The compression of IT services pricing as AI tools let clients accomplish the same work with fewer outsourced hours, pressuring traditional billing models.
