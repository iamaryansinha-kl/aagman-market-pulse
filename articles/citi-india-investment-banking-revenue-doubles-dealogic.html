Citi India IB Revenue Doubles to $52M, Bank Tops Dealogic League Table

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Citigroup's India investment banking revenue nearly doubled year-on-year to $52 million in 2026 YTD, ranking #1 in Dealogic's India league table
  • 02/The bank advised on $36.81 billion of announced M&A across just 10 transactions and helped Indian companies raise close to $16 billion
  • 03/Total India IB revenue pool is flat at $780 million versus $796 million in 2025, meaning Citi's gains are share gains, not a rising tide

Citigroup (NYSE: C) has nearly doubled its India investment banking revenue. The bank earned $52 million in 2026 year-to-date, up sharply from $29 million in the same period of 2025, according to Dealogic data cited by Citi.

The jump lifted Citi to the top of Dealogic's India investment banking revenue league table. Its market share climbed to 7%, from 4% a year earlier.

The context matters. Total India IB revenue across the league table stood at $780 million, versus $796 million in the same period last year. Citi's surge came against a flat fee pool, meaning these are share gains rather than the product of a rising tide.

Citi also dominated the M&A rankings. The bank advised on $36.81 billion of announced M&A deal value across just 10 transactions, giving it a 28% market share in announced deal value.

That concentration in a small number of very large deals sets Citi apart from the field. Arpwood Capital ranked second with $25.05 billion across 19 transactions, a 19% share. JPMorgan was third at $17.55 billion, or 13%.

By IB revenue, Citi's $52 million comfortably led JPMorgan's $43 million. Kotak Investment Bank and HSBC followed, each posting $35 million.

Deal activity has been concentrated in the technology sector. Indian companies are expanding their international presence and raising financing to fund growth ambitions across global markets.

Equity capital markets work has been a key contributor. Citi helped Indian companies raise close to $16 billion so far this year, supporting listings and capital raises across sectors.

The league table composition underlines how concentrated the top end of the market has become. The top three banks by M&A value controlled roughly 60% of announced deal value between them, leaving smaller advisers to fight over the remainder.

For Citi, the India performance fits a broader strategic picture. The bank has consistently described India as a key global market, and the 2026 league table results show that commitment translating into fee income.

"Citi is growing with our clients in India as we deliver the full power of the franchise to clients both locally and across Citi's global network," said Rahul Saraf, India head of investment banking at Citi.

Saraf added that India is a key market for Citi globally. "We have a very strong pipeline of business ahead," he said, pointing to expectations of continued strong deal flow through the rest of the year.

The outlook is not without risk. The flat overall fee pool signals fierce competition for mandates, and Citi's reliance on a small number of large-ticket M&A deals means quarterly revenue can swing sharply if a single mandate slips.

Still, with a full pipeline, a 28% M&A market share, and technology sector deals continuing to expand, Citi enters the final stretch of 2026 as the clear leader in India's investment banking market.

GLOSSARY: Investment banking: The division of a bank that helps companies raise capital (equity and debt issues) and advises on mergers, acquisitions and restructurings, earning fees per deal. League table: A ranking of banks by deal activity — revenue or deal value — compiled by data firms such as Dealogic. Banks cite them to win mandates. Market share: A bank's slice of the total fee or deal-value pool. Citi's India IB revenue share rose to 7% from 4% in 2025. M&A (mergers and acquisitions): Deals in which companies combine or buy each other. Announced deal value counts signed deals even if not yet completed.
