Delhi Airport T3 Pier C International Ops Begin May, Capacity Up 50%

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Pier C at Delhi Airport Terminal 3 will commence international operations from the first week of May 2026, marking a domestic-to-international conversion
  • 02/The repurposed facility increases T3 international passenger handling capacity by around 50%, adding nearly 10 million passengers annually
  • 03/DIAL CEO Videh Kumar Jaipuriar said the conversion reflects a strategy of building capacity through existing infrastructure while preparing for future demand

Pier C at Terminal 3 of Delhi Airport will begin international operations from the first week of May 2026. The conversion from domestic to international use was completed earlier this month and is now awaiting final operational rollout.

Delhi International Airport Limited (DIAL) said the repurposed Pier C will increase international passenger handling capacity by around 50 per cent. The facility will add nearly 10 million passengers annually to Terminal 3.

This expansion is expected to take T3's total international capacity to approximately 30-32 million passengers per annum. The move strengthens Delhi Airport's position as an international aviation transit hub.

Pier C has been part of Terminal 3 since its inception. DIAL converted the facility through repurposing existing infrastructure without any new construction.

According to Videh Kumar Jaipuriar, Chief Executive Officer of DIAL, the conversion reflects the airport's forward-looking strategy. "The transformation of Pier C, from domestic to international, reflects our philosophy of building capacity with foresight—anticipating demand while keeping the passenger experience at the centre," he said.

The upgraded pier includes 12 additional self-baggage drop counters and 20 emigration counters including Trusted Traveller Programme counters. It also features five Automatic Tray Retrieval System lanes and dual-view X-BIS machines.

The facility includes 16 boarding gates converted for international use and 40 additional Bureau of Immigration counters in arrivals. New escalators and lifts have also been installed.

The conversion improves readiness for wide-body aircraft including the Airbus A350. Wide-body stand availability for international operations increases by around 40 per cent, aligning with fleet expansion plans of domestic and international carriers.
