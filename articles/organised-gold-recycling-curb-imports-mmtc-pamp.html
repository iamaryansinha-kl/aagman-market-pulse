Organised Gold Recycling Can Cut Imports, Boost Stability: MMTC-PAMP

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/MMTC-PAMP, India's largest gold refiner, says organised recycling can significantly reduce the country's $45 billion annual gold import bill
  • 02/Currently only 5% of India's scrap gold is recycled through formal channels; 95% flows through unorganised markets
  • 03/Scaling formal recycling to 25% could cut annual gold imports by $10-12 billion and improve India's trade deficit

MMTC-PAMP India Pvt Ltd has advocated for scaling up organised gold recycling. The company says this can curb India's massive gold import bill and boost economic stability.

India imports approximately $45 billion worth of gold annually. It is the second-largest gold consumer globally after China.

Currently, only 5% of India's scrap gold is recycled through formal channels. The remaining 95% flows through unorganised, undocumented markets with no purity standards or tax compliance.

MMTC-PAMP estimates that scaling formal recycling to 25% could reduce annual gold imports by $10-12 billion. This would directly improve India's current account deficit and reduce pressure on the rupee.

The company operates India's only LBMA-accredited gold refinery. It has the technical capacity to process 100 tonnes of scrap gold annually but currently operates at 30% utilisation.

"Organised recycling with proper hallmarking and transparency can unlock massive value," a senior executive at MMTC-PAMP said. "It also protects consumers from adulterated gold."

The government introduced the Gold Monetisation Scheme in 2015. However, participation has remained low due to trust deficits and unfavourable interest rates.

MMTC-PAMP has urged policymakers to incentivise formal recycling through tax breaks. It also recommends setting up collection centres in partnership with jewellers to channel scrap gold into the organised sector.
