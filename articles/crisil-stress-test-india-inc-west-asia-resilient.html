Crisil Stress Test: India Inc Resilient Despite West Asia Conflict, 8 Sectors At Risk

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Crisil stress test of 34 sectors covering 65% of rated corporate debt finds India Inc broadly resilient despite prolonged West Asia conflict
  • 02/Operating profitability could fall 200 bps to 10% this fiscal from pre-conflict expectation of 12%; 22 sectors to see profit hit over 10%
  • 03/Only 8 sectors accounting for 10% of rated debt face material credit impact; strong balance sheets and steady domestic demand provide cushion

A Crisil Ratings stress test of 34 sectors, representing 65% of its rated corporate debt, shows India Inc will remain broadly resilient. Strong balance sheets, steady domestic demand, and government-led capital expenditure are the key buffers.

The rating agency assumed supply-chain disruptions could last nine months this fiscal. Crude oil prices are assumed to average $110 per barrel, up from a $95 base case.

The prolonged disruptions could shave 200 basis points off corporate operating profitability. Profitability would drop to 10% this fiscal from the pre-conflict expectation of 12%.

Of the 34 sectors stress-tested, 22 would see operating profitability culled by more than 10%. Higher inventory costs and inability to fully pass on burden to consumers immediately are the main drivers.

Credit profiles will be cushioned by controlled gearing levels and sustained domestic demand. Only eight sectors, accounting for 10% of rated corporate debt, face material credit quality impact.

Over the past decade, corporate India's median gearing has halved to ~0.5 times as of March 2026. Interest coverage has doubled to over 5 times, providing sufficient headroom.

The ceramic sector will be the hardest hit. Gas shortages could reduce its revenue by a third and profitability by half. Airlines could see profitability drop by around 50% due to airspace closures, higher fuel costs, and rupee depreciation.

"For companies, managing costs and profitability will be a bigger challenge than achieving topline growth," says Subodh Rai, Managing Director, Crisil Ratings. "Credit profiles will be cushioned by controlled gearing levels and sustained domestic demand."

"While our outlook for India Inc's credit quality remains stable, we maintain a cautious stance because of the uncertain trajectory of the West Asia conflict," says Somasekhar Vemuri, Senior Director, Crisil Ratings.
