RBI Issues Basel III Norms on Capital Requirements for Market Risk: Key Changes

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • Key points unavailable

The Reserve Bank of India has issued directions on minimum capital requirements for market risk under the revised Basel III framework. The directions were released on Monday.

The move aims to align India's market risk guidelines with the updated Basel III framework. The RBI said the directions ensure simplicity of regulations while providing flexibility and ease of adoption.

No regulatory arbitrage A key provision prohibits banks from reclassifying instruments between the trading book and the banking book for regulatory arbitrage. Specifically, banks cannot switch instruments between the two books with the intention of achieving lower capital requirements.

"A bank shall not reclassify instruments between the trading book and the banking book for regulatory arbitrage, i.e., with the intention of achieving lower capital requirements," the RBI said.

This addresses a common practice where banks could shift assets to whichever book required less capital, reducing their overall capital adequacy requirements without reducing actual risk.

Simplified standardised approach Banks will have to use the simplified standardised approach (SSA) for computing risk-weighted assets for market risk.

Under this approach, risk-weighted assets will be determined by multiplying the capital requirements calculated under the framework by a factor of 12.5.

The specific risk tables for interest rate risk have been revised. These align with the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS) guidelines and provide a more concise and clean treatment.

Debt fund treatment revised On debt mutual funds and exchange-traded funds (ETFs) held in the trading book, the RBI said the capital treatment has been revised. The changes ensure capital computation is based on the underlying risk drivers while maintaining sufficient guardrails.

This closes a potential loophole where banks could hold debt instruments through mutual funds or ETFs and receive more favourable capital treatment than holding the underlying securities directly.

Applicability and timeline The directions apply to all commercial banks, except small finance banks, payments banks and local area banks.

The new rules will take effect from April 1, 2027. The RBI said this provides sufficient lead time for banks to prepare for the transition.

What is market risk? Market risk refers to the risk of losses arising from movements in market prices. This includes interest rate risk, equity risk, foreign exchange risk and commodity risk.

Under Basel III, banks are required to hold capital against market risk exposures. The revised framework, finalised by the BCBS in 2019, updates the original market risk rules to make them more risk-sensitive.

The RBI's directions implement these updated standards in the Indian banking context.
