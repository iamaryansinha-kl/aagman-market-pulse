India Bonds Jump 4th Day as Oil Drops to $72, FPIs Buy ₹236 Billion

BY AAGMAN DESK | EST. READ: 3 MIN
EXECUTIVE TLDR //
  • 01/Indian government bonds rallied for a fourth straight day on June 25 as Brent crude fell to $72.24 per barrel
  • 02/Foreign portfolio investors bought a net ₹236 billion of Indian government bonds in June, the highest monthly inflow in two years
  • 03/RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra said it was premature to talk about rate hikes, capping yield rise expectations

Indian government bonds rallied for a fourth consecutive day on Thursday, June 25. The benchmark 6.94% 2036 bond yield dropped 2 basis points to 6.7648% by 10:45 a.m. IST.

The rally was capped ahead of New Delhi's ₹280 billion debt sale scheduled for later in the day. Investors remained cautious despite the positive momentum.

Brent crude oil fell to $72.24 a barrel in Asian trade. Shipping through the Strait of Hormuz resumed, returning prices to late February levels.

The 10-year bond yield is now just 10 basis points above pre-war levels. It had peaked at nearly 50 basis points higher earlier.

Foreign portfolio investors bought a net ₹236 billion of Indian government bonds so far in June. This marks the highest monthly inflow in two years if sustained.

Foreign inflows into Asian bonds hit a three-month high of $5.61 billion in May. South Korea, Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand and India led the pack.

"As flows continue from FPIs with the increased likelihood of index inclusion, overall momentum is also expected to continue," said Basant Bafna, head of fixed income at Mirae Asset Investment Managers.

RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra said it was premature to talk about rate hikes. The one-year OIS rate dropped 2 bps to 5.75% on Thursday.
